## Executive Summary

Every phase of Nex to date has built a capability. Phase 31 is the phase that turns those capabilities into an **experience a new merchant can start with**. A carpenter with no digital presence should be able to have a conversation with Nex and, within an hour, hold a complete operational business — brand, tradesite (canteen), CRM, quoting, marketplace profile, Trade Centre store, marketing campaigns drafted, social channels connected, financial dashboard live, Trade Brain (Phase 27) activated, memory (Phase 26) primed with regional benchmark defaults, market intelligence (Phase 30) tuned to their region.

The user brief calls this an "AI Business Builder." That framing is right but incomplete. Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, GoDaddy already build websites. Bolt.new and Lovable already build web apps. What none of them build is a **construction business** — with trade-specific expertise, regional regulation awareness, honest reviews handling, market-intelligence-informed pricing, and a lifetime path from onboarding to Digital Twin handover.

The strategic result is that Nex becomes the fastest, most rigorous, and most respectable way to launch a construction business. Not the cheapest — the most complete. A carpenter finishing an apprenticeship, a tradesperson going solo after redundancy, an established firm consolidating fragmented tools onto one platform — Phase 31 is their landing point.

The key discipline: everything the Builder produces is a **draft the merchant reviews before publishing**. Nothing goes live without deliberate merchant approval. This is the difference between a Wix template that looks fake because it feels autogenerated, and a Nex tradesite that reads like the merchant wrote it themselves because they signed off on every line before it went out.

## 1. The Business Creation Journey

### 1.1 The five-step conversation

Conversational, not form-driven. Nex asks; merchant answers naturally. Each step is a checkpoint that unlocks the next.

**Step 1 — Trade identity**

Nex: "What trade are you?"

Merchant answers freely. Nex pattern-matches to the trade catalog (Phase 24 + Phase 27 Brains — 40+ specialists including builder, plumber, electrician, bricklayer, landscaper, roofer, painter, kitchen installer, bathroom installer, steel fabricator, plus supplier, manufacturer, developer, architect, engineer).

If the answer is ambiguous ("I do property maintenance"), Nex asks the disambiguating question with named options.

**Step 2 — Business shape**

Sole trader · 2-5 employees · growing (6-20) · established (20+) · enterprise · not-yet-registered.

Business shape drives which modules are activated first. A sole trader gets estimating + CRM immediately; a 20-person firm gets workforce + scheduling promoted higher.

**Step 3 — Country + region + regulatory context**

Country (UK / IE / AU / US / CA / NZ / AE — Phase 21 global) → sub-region → operating radius. Nex applies:

- Currency
- VAT / GST / sales-tax framework
- Building regulation set (Approved Documents UK, TGDs IE, NCC AU, IBC US, etc.)
- Language + local vocabulary (Phase 27 translation)
- Trade certification bodies (Gas Safe, NICEIC, MCS, etc.)

**Step 4 — Services offered**

Nex asks. Where the merchant is uncertain ("I mainly do rewires but sometimes small commercial"), Nex proposes a taxonomy from the Trade Brain (Phase 27) — sub-specialisations. The merchant ticks. Every service becomes a first-class object with pricing, portfolio slot, SEO landing page.

**Step 5 — Business goals**

Weighted preferences: Growth · Lead generation · Project management · E-commerce · Trade Centre listings · Retention · Efficiency.

Weights drive module prioritisation (growth-first onboarding surfaces marketing + Trade Centre; project-management-first surfaces SiteBook + Twin).

### 1.2 Existing-context detection

Nex offers to import from existing signals:

- Public URL (existing website) → Vision AI + scraper reads current content
- Companies House number (UK) → verified business name, address, incorporation date, directors
- Instagram / Facebook handle → posts + portfolio to seed the gallery
- Google Business Profile → reviews (with the merchant's consent to import — never fabricated)

Every imported asset is labelled with its source. Merchant reviews before it enters the tradesite.

### 1.3 The reveal

At the end of the conversation, Nex presents the drafted business as a live preview. Every panel is editable. Nothing is published yet. Merchant clicks through Yes/Edit/No on each:

- Brand identity
- Tradesite (canteen)
- Services catalogue
- CRM + quoting setup
- Trade Centre store
- Marketplace profile
- Social channels (draft posts queued)
- Financial dashboard
- Trade Brain configuration
- Regulatory profile
- Onboarding checklist for post-launch

When Yes on all, Nex publishes. Merchant is live.

Every generated asset is a **draft**, marked visually as such until merchant approves. Never auto-published.

| Brand colours                | Palette drawn from Phase 27 Trade Brain regional norms + tone preference  | Enforces accessibility contrast (Phase 26 WCAG rule)                          |
| Typography                   | Reading-hierarchy pair (heading + body) from Studio design system         | Enforces 12px floor per platform rule                                         |
| Business profile             | Merchant answers 4 questions; Nex drafts the paragraph                   | Merchant edits; never claims credentials the merchant hasn't provided         |
| About page                   | Story arc drafted from onboarding conversation                            | No fake "family business since 1970" — asks for real founding year            |
| Services                     | Trade Brain sub-spec list × merchant selections                           | Each service links to Phase 7 est engine for real quotes                      |
| Service areas                | Postcode radius drawn from merchant declared operating region             | Honestly reflects merchant's actual reach                                     |
| Price guides                 | Regional bands from Phase 30 market intelligence + Phase 27 Brain         | Marked "from £X — final quote based on site survey"                          |
| Gallery structure            | Layout scaffolded; merchant uploads real photos                           | **Never fills gallery with stock images.** Empty = "gallery coming soon."     |
| Testimonials                 | Merchant imports real reviews from Google / Trustpilot / Trade Centre     | **Never generates fake testimonials.** Empty = honest.                        |
| FAQs                         | Trade Brain FAQ pack per trade + region                                  | Grounded in real regulations; every answer cited                              |
| Terms & Conditions           | Regional template drafted; **prominent "have a solicitor review"** flag  | Nex does not provide legal advice                                              |
| Privacy Policy               | GDPR / regional-privacy-law template                                     | Same solicitor-review flag                                                    |
| Contact page                 | Merchant details + WhatsApp routing (per platform rules)                 | No voice AI on customer contact path                                          |
| SEO structure                | Category + location page fan-out (city × service)                        | Phase 30 signals pick which pairs are worth generating                        |
| Knowledge articles           | Trade Brain seeds 5 articles; merchant edits                              | Every claim carries evidence                                                  |
| Construction blog            | Roll-forward calendar with Brain topic suggestions                       | Merchant approves each post before publish                                    |
| Location pages               | One per city in operating radius, drafted with regional signals          | Enforces schema markup for local SEO                                          |
| Lead capture forms           | Form fields tuned to the merchant's declared services                    | Fields become the qualifier (per merchant memory rule — not washer-gated)     |

### 2.2 The generation stack

- **Structured content** (services list, contact, service area) — deterministic from the conversation answers
- **Long-form copy** (about page, blog articles, service descriptions) — LLM-drafted through Claude Opus 4.7 (per merchant memory pin) with Trade Brain vocabulary layer applied
- **Visual assets** (logo, palette) — design engine with Trade Brain style priors per trade
- **Legal templates** — regional templates; never bespoke drafting by AI without solicitor review

### 2.3 What is explicitly forbidden

- Fake reviews or testimonials
- Fabricated credentials (Gas Safe number, insurance policy number, trade body membership)
- Fabricated portfolio images (stock or AI-generated as if real jobs)
- Fabricated stats ("500+ happy customers")
- Voice AI on the customer purchasing path (per constitutional rule)

Every one of these has a technical guardrail: the schema does not accept these values without merchant-provided evidence + verifiable source.

## 3. Studio Integration

Studio is already the single merchant interface (per merchant memory notes). Phase 31 does not build a parallel interface — it **generates a Studio configuration for a new merchant**.

### 3.1 The manifest-first pattern

Studio Apps are manifest-first (per ADR-0001 + merchant memory). Phase 31 emits a **starter manifest bundle** for the new merchant:

- Tradesite canteen manifest (`src/apps/canteen/manifest.ts` with merchant-scoped configuration)
- Trade Centre store manifest
- CRM manifest with the merchant's declared customer categories
- SiteBook manifest (with Twin readiness)
- Marketing manifest with drafted campaigns
- Financial manifest

Each manifest ships with sensible defaults driven by the onboarding answers. Merchant customises through the Studio App Store surface (existing pattern) after launch.

### 3.2 App Store surfaces (honouring existing rule)

Per merchant memory: **Studio App Store never disables premium Apps — it shows upgrade CTAs.** Phase 31 respects this. A new merchant on Free tier sees the full App catalog with clear upgrade paths, not a stripped-down view.

### 3.3 Layout + widget generation

The starter layout is not templated. Trade Brain design priors drive layout choices:

- Roofing tradesites lead with hero + safety-first visual language
- Kitchen installer tradesites lead with completed-work carousel
- Plumbing tradesites lead with 24/7 contact + emergency handling

The **structural** decisions are Brain-driven. The **visual** decisions (palette, typography, exact hero text) are drafts the merchant edits.

### 3.4 Container + brand style

The Studio design system (`src/platform/design`) provides the primitives. Phase 31 generates a merchant-specific theme file (colours, typography, spacing) that flows through every container. Change the theme once — every surface updates.

Trade Brain pricing_model modules feed Phase 28 estimator from day one. First quote a new merchant produces uses regional peer defaults (Phase 30) since the merchant has no history yet. As their memory (Phase 26) fills, defaults are replaced by the merchant's own calibrated numbers.

### 4.4 Brain-driven troubleshooting

Trade Brain fault-finding decision trees (Phase 27) become available in chat immediately. Even day-one merchants get the expert answer.

## 5. AI Marketing Engine

### 5.1 What's generated

Per platform (with honest coverage — voice-only surfaces are excluded per constitutional rule):

- Facebook / Instagram / TikTok — short-form content ideas + captions + schedule
- LinkedIn — long-form business-oriented posts
- YouTube — script outlines only (production is merchant's job)
- Google Business Profile posts
- Seasonal campaigns (autumn boiler-service, winter roof-check, spring garden landscaping)
- Email newsletters (opt-in-list required; never scraped or synthesised)
- Promotions + lead magnets (trade-specific "5 things to check before you hire an electrician" etc.)
- Referral campaigns
- Educational content (Brain-authored, merchant-edited)
- Community engagement (local groups; commented as the merchant with consent, not automated)

### 5.2 The publishing workflow

1. Nex generates 30 days of drafts
2. Merchant reviews in a calendar view (Studio surface)
3. Merchant approves individually or in bulk
4. Approved posts publish on schedule
5. Performance data (reach, engagement, conversion) flows back into Memory + Phase 30 signals
6. Next batch of drafts is refined by what worked

### 5.3 Honesty guardrails

- Never uses fake customer names or fake testimonials in generated copy
- Never makes claims the merchant hasn't verified (e.g., "trusted by 500+ customers")
- Never uses AI-generated "before/after" images as if they were real jobs (real photos only)
- Every draft is marked as such until merchant approves
- Merchant can edit voice / tone / disallowed phrases; the engine respects them

### 5.4 Regional intelligence

Phase 30 tells the marketing engine which categories are trending in the merchant's region. Marketing prioritises high-conversion topics automatically. Merchant sees the reasoning: "posting about heat pumps this month because regional search interest is up 24% in your area (Phase 30 signal)."

3. **Approved** with edits

Content is never one-off. It's an ongoing loop.

## 8. AI Business Optimiser

Every Monday morning, alongside Phase 25 BOS morning brief, the merchant sees a Business Optimiser slice.

### 8.1 What's recommended

- **New services** — Phase 30 signals show a category the merchant doesn't offer that's trending in their region
- **Pricing adjustments** — merchant is drifting below regional median; suggested uplift
- **Marketing improvements** — which channels underperform; which content types outperform
- **Profit opportunities** — high-margin services the merchant should double down on
- **Expansion areas** — adjacent regions with capacity gaps
- **Equipment investments** — plant purchases that Phase 23 twin scenario supports affordability for
- **Staff hiring** — Phase 30 labour intelligence + Phase 26 memory show when hiring pays back
- **Training / CPD** — Trade Brain module tracks which certifications increase win rate
- **Supplier changes** — supplier ranking (Phase 17 MP + Phase 26 memory) shows better options
- **Technology upgrades** — Nex features the merchant hasn't turned on that peers use successfully

### 8.2 The recommendation shape

Every recommendation:

- Cites the underlying signal
- States confidence
- Shows expected outcome + magnitude
- Names the tradeoff
- Includes an explicit "here's what would change my recommendation"

Merchant never sees "you should do X because Nex thinks so." Always signal + framing.

## 12. Competitive Analysis

### 12.1 vs. Wix / Squarespace / Weebly

**Their strength:** template-driven website builders. Ubiquitous. Cheap.

**Their gap:** they build a website. Nothing else. No CRM, no estimating, no Trade Brain, no memory, no Twin, no market intelligence.

**Nex advantage:** the tradesite is one of many surfaces. Merchants who start with Wix find themselves buying 8 additional tools; Nex ships all 8 on day one.

### 12.2 vs. Shopify

**Their strength:** e-commerce depth. Ecosystem of apps.

**Their gap:** oriented to product sellers, not service businesses. A construction firm's revenue is primarily services with materials as an input, not products off a shelf.

**Nex advantage:** Nex handles services AND product sales (Trade Centre) in a construction-native way.

### 12.3 vs. GoDaddy

**Their strength:** cheap domain + basic site + hosting.

**Their gap:** none of the vertical intelligence. No trade-specific anything.

**Nex advantage:** we don't compete on price of hosting; we compete on operating the business.

### 12.4 vs. HubSpot / Salesforce

**Their strength:** enterprise CRM depth.

**Their gap:** enterprise pricing + horizontal focus. Not construction-native. No Trade Brains. No Twin.

**Nex advantage:** vertical specificity and small-merchant pricing.

### 12.5 vs. ServiceTitan / Buildertrend / Housecall Pro / Jobber

**Their strength:** established vertical workflow players.

**Their gap:** they onboard by having merchants fill 40 forms. They don't generate the business — the merchant builds it manually. They don't ship the tradesite, the Trade Brain, the estimator, and the marketing engine as one connected launch.

**Nex advantage:** Nex is what those platforms would look like if they were designed from scratch in 2026 with AI-first onboarding.

### 12.6 vs. Lovable / Bolt.new

**Their strength:** AI-generated web apps + sites in minutes.

**Their gap:** they generate *code*. They don't generate a *business*. A Lovable-generated site has no CRM, no estimator, no Brain, no memory of prior jobs, no market intelligence, no Twin. It's a page.

**Nex advantage:** a business, not a page.

### 12.7 vs. ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini as builders

**Their strength:** conversational AI + broad reasoning.

**Their gap:** no ecosystem. No published tradesite output. No merchant tenant. No integrated marketplace. A generic LLM can suggest a business plan; it can't stand up a business.

**Nex advantage:** we're the ecosystem, not just the chat. The Business Builder converses (like an LLM) + instantiates (like a full platform).

### 12.8 The moat

Three durable advantages:

1. **Composition depth over years.** Phases 1-30 are prerequisites. Copying Phase 31 requires copying everything under it.
2. **Trade specificity.** Every generalist AI builder produces generic construction pages. Nex Trade Brains write with real trade voice.
3. **Real-integration on day one.** New merchant's Trade Centre store is a real store. Their marketplace profile is a real profile. Their CRM is a real CRM. Nothing is a Potemkin village. Competitors take months to wire what Nex does in one hour.

## 14. Final Strategic Assessment

### 14.1 Is this Nex's strongest customer acquisition tool?

Yes, in the following precise sense: Phase 31 is the point at which everything Phases 1-30 built becomes usable *without* the merchant having to figure out how to configure it. Every prior phase's value is unlocked by the Business Builder for every new merchant. The Builder is not customer acquisition marketing; it's customer acquisition *conversion*. Merchants who talk to Nex leave with a business. Merchants who talk to Wix leave with a shopping cart.

### 14.2 How does it increase subscriptions?

The Free tier does exactly what ADR-0004 says: viral loop. Every business Nex generates is a small billboard. Free merchants who fully use the platform end up on Starter or Professional inside 60-90 days because 10 washers a month is a proof-of-concept, not a livelihood. The Builder's *quality* determines conversion — a beautiful, honest, functioning Free tier business converts. A templated feels-fake Free tier does not.

### 14.3 How does it strengthen every other module?

Every new merchant adds:

- Memory rows (Phase 26)
- Regional signals (Phase 30)
- Trade Brain corrections (Phase 27)
- Twin activity (Phase 29)
- Marketplace listings (Phase 17)
- Trade Centre inventory
- Network graph edges (Phase 14)

Each strengthens the substrate for every other merchant. The 1000th merchant onboarded gets a better product than the 100th because of what the 100-999 contributed.

### 14.4 Network effects

Three overlapping loops:

1. **Merchant-to-merchant** — more merchants → richer benchmarks → better Advisor → higher merchant retention.
2. **Merchant-to-homeowner** — more merchants → thicker marketplace → more homeowner engagement → more leads back to merchants.
3. **Merchant-to-industry** — more merchants → richer Phase 30 signals → wholesale revenue possible → more platform investment → better product.

### 14.5 Replication difficulty

For a competitor to match Phase 31, they need:

- Phase 27 Trade Brain equivalent (multi-year build)
- Phase 26 Memory Engine (multi-year build)
- Phase 30 Market Intelligence (multi-year build)
- Phase 28 Estimator (multi-year build)
- Phase 29 Twin (multi-year build)
- Phase 24 mesh (multi-year build)
- Studio App Store equivalent (already shipped, multi-year build)
- Trade Centre marketplace equivalent
- Merchant density to justify all of the above

Every prerequisite is by itself a multi-year competitor project. In combination, they are a decade of construction focus. Any competitor who did all of this instead of pursuing other opportunities is Nex; any competitor who does part of it doesn't have Phase 31.

### 14.6 Breakthrough ideas that make this definitive

Beyond the core V0-V3:

1. **Voice-conversational onboarding (merchant-side only).** New merchant walks the streets recording their vision on a walk — Nex generates the business from the walk-and-talk. Respects the constitutional constraint (no voice on customer purchasing path; merchant-side scope capture is fine).
2. **Public-record grounding.** Every claim the Builder makes about the merchant traces back to a verifiable public register (Companies House, Gas Safe register, insurer register). "As verified against register X on date Y." A homeowner clicking any claim can prove it.
3. **Adjacent-service growth pathways.** Day-one Nex shows the merchant which adjacent services successful peer merchants in their region moved into (roofer → solar; electrician → EV; plumber → heat pump). Growth advice on the onboarding screen, not deferred to month 12.
4. **Regional-language localisation.** Merchant serving Welsh-speaking areas gets bilingual tradesite drafts. Merchant serving Gaeltacht regions gets Irish-language options. Multi-lingual customer surfaces from day one.
5. **Founder-story capture.** Some of the most powerful marketing content is the founder's own story. Nex conducts a short interview during onboarding and drafts a founder page from it. Merchant edits. Real story, professionally framed.
6. **Ecosystem-day-one revenue.** From the moment onboarding completes, the merchant is a paid participant in Nex's regional intelligence contribution (Phase 26 opt-in). Small revenue share for contributing merchants; ensures the ecosystem economics compound.

### 14.7 Non-negotiables

1. Every generated asset is a **draft** until merchant approves. Nothing auto-publishes.
2. No fake reviews, no fake testimonials, no fake credentials, no fake portfolio, no fake stats. Ever.
3. Every content piece cites its source (Trade Brain, Phase 30 signal, imported public register, merchant-provided).
4. Legal templates carry solicitor-review flags.
5. Voice AI never on customer purchasing path.
6. Real photos only for portfolio (never stock, never AI-generated as-if-real).
7. Verification badges are earned not granted.
8. Merchant can export their business (data portability rule).
