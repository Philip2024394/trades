# Staircase Author Portfolio Evaluation Rubric

**Draft V1 · 2026-07-24**
**Purpose:** consistent scoring of the 5 portfolio pieces each applicant submits (per intake spec Section C). Prevents "vibes-based" filtering. Two reviewers score independently, discuss, agree.

---

## How to use

- Two reviewers (CEO + one delegate — Product Lead or trusted trade contact) score each portfolio piece independently on the 6 axes below
- Score 1-5 per axis · total per piece 6-30
- Average scores across all 5 pieces = candidate portfolio score
- Discuss any divergence >4 points before agreeing final
- Combine with intake Section E answers ("misunderstood thing" question) for shortlist decision

---

## The 6 axes

### Axis 1 — Technical accuracy (1-5)
Does the content match current UK regulations and standards?
- **5:** cites Approved Doc K by paragraph · references BS 5395 · no factual errors
- **4:** cites regulations broadly · minor imprecisions
- **3:** technically correct but no regulation citations
- **2:** small factual errors (e.g. wrong rise/going numbers)
- **1:** significant factual errors OR relies on outdated regs

### Axis 2 — Clarity of explanation (1-5)
Can a non-expert follow it?
- **5:** every technical term defined or shown · logical flow · progressive depth · homeowner could follow the reasoning
- **4:** mostly clear · one or two terms assumed
- **3:** clear to a trade audience but not homeowners
- **2:** jargon-heavy · assumes too much
- **1:** confusing structure or unclear reasoning

### Axis 3 — Evidence + sourcing (1-5)
Does the author cite sources properly?
- **5:** every claim tied to a source (reg · standard · industry body · own trade experience clearly flagged as such)
- **4:** most claims sourced · clear when personal experience is the basis
- **3:** some sources · some unsupported claims
- **2:** claims presented as authoritative without support
- **1:** cites unreliable or promotional sources

### Axis 4 — Voice + originality (1-5)
Is this the author's own voice — or generic copy?
- **5:** distinctive voice · specific to their trade experience · genuine insight
- **4:** clear voice · some genuine insight
- **3:** competent writing but interchangeable with any generalist
- **2:** feels ghost-written or lightly rewritten from other sources
- **1:** obviously derivative or promotional content

### Axis 5 — Practical depth (1-5)
Would a working tradesperson learn from this?
- **5:** covers the "obvious but never explained" details a master would notice · includes edge cases · warns of common mistakes
- **4:** solid working-level detail · a few edge cases
- **3:** covers the basics well · little beyond textbook
- **2:** overview-level only
- **1:** superficial

### Axis 6 — Suitability for AI-Brain authoring (1-5)
Does the piece show they can write in a way that structured extraction will handle well?
- **5:** discrete verifiable claims · clear one-fact-per-sentence when it matters · uses "when · then · unless" structure · avoids waffle
- **4:** mostly structured · a few flowing paragraphs
- **3:** structured in places · could be reworked
- **2:** flowing narrative that's hard to break into facts
- **1:** stream-of-consciousness or heavily rhetorical

## Scoring guide (candidate portfolio total)

- **135-150** (avg 4.5-5.0/axis · 27-30/piece · exceptional across 5 pieces) — likely hire · fast-track to interview
- **105-134** (avg 3.5-4.5/axis) — good candidate · shortlist for interview
- **75-104** (avg 2.5-3.5/axis) — borderline · shortlist only if Section E answer is exceptional
- **Below 75** (avg <2.5/axis) — polite decline

## Reviewer discipline

- Never score without reading the piece in full
- Never inflate scores because the applicant is likeable in cover note (score portfolio strictly; other factors go into interview stage)
- Note any red flags separately (undeclared commercial interest hints · plagiarism suspicion · outdated regulations) — flag to CEO before final decision
- Second reviewer is a genuine check, not a rubber stamp — disagreements are the signal, not the problem

## Red flags (auto-decline regardless of score)

- Any evidence of plagiarism (run Copyscape or Turnitin on submitted pieces)
- Outdated regulation citations passed off as current (e.g. cites Approved Doc K 1998 without noting it's superseded)
- Any piece that is essentially product promotion for a specific manufacturer (bias risk)
- Portfolio dominated by a single client (over-specialisation risk for Trade Brain neutrality)

## What to look for in the "misunderstood thing" answer (Section E)

This is the single most predictive filter. Rank each answer:
- **Original insight** (grade A): specific to the applicant's trade experience · would surprise a homeowner or generalist joiner · shows they think about their craft deeply
- **Solid working answer** (grade B): a legitimate misconception addressed clearly · not novel but useful
- **Textbook answer** (grade C): correct but any competent joiner would give the same answer
- **Generic answer** (grade D): could be about any trade

Grade A alone is a strong signal to interview even with a borderline portfolio score.
