# Panel System Knowledge Base — Decorative Staircase Wall Panelling

## Purpose

Decorative staircase panelling is a non-structural architectural finish used to enhance staircase walls, staircase enclosures, under-stair areas, landings and entrance halls.

The system gives the appearance of traditional handcrafted joinery while normally being manufactured from MDF, veneered MDF or plywood with applied moulding profiles.

Decorative panelling should always be treated as an architectural finish rather than part of the staircase structure.

## Panel System Definition

A decorative panel system consists of:

- backing substrate
- decorative mouldings
- perimeter trims
- skirting integration
- architrave integration
- paint or stain finish
- optional concealed storage
- optional LED lighting

The finished installation appears as bespoke architectural joinery.

## Primary Applications

Decorative panelling can be installed on:

- staircase side walls
- under-stair walls
- staircase enclosures
- staircase string covers
- hallway walls
- entrance halls
- landings
- feature walls
- cloakroom walls
- living rooms

It is suitable for both new-build and renovation projects.

## Panel Styles

### Raised and Fielded

Characteristics: traditional · deep mouldings · shadow lines · period appearance · luxury finish.

Best suited for: Georgian · Victorian · Edwardian · heritage homes · luxury properties.

### Shaker

Characteristics: flat recessed centre · clean lines · minimal moulding · timeless appearance.

Best suited for: contemporary homes · Scandinavian interiors · modern renovations · painted finishes.

### Beaded

Characteristics: decorative bead profile · softer appearance · cottage style.

Best suited for: country homes · farmhouse interiors · traditional properties.

### Contemporary

Characteristics: square mouldings · minimal shadow lines · geometric layouts · flush appearance.

Best suited for: luxury modern homes · architectural interiors.

## Panel Layouts

Panels should never be identical if the wall geometry changes. Instead they should be proportionally resized.

Possible layouts include: rectangular · square · vertical · horizontal · triangular · trapezoid · angled · stair pitch · landing · under-stair.

Good panelling maintains equal border spacing even when panel sizes change.

## Staircase Wall Principles

When panelling follows a staircase the mouldings should generally follow the stair pitch.

This creates:

- visual flow
- balanced proportions
- bespoke appearance

Avoid forcing horizontal panels onto angled walls unless a dado rail design has been selected.

## Under-Stair Panelling

Under-stair panelling should:

- follow the stair angle
- align with wall panels
- continue moulding spacing
- integrate with skirting
- hide access doors where required

The enclosure should appear as one continuous architectural feature.

## Materials

### Common backing materials

- Moisture Resistant MDF
- Standard MDF
- Oak Veneered MDF
- Walnut Veneered MDF
- Ash Veneered MDF
- Birch Plywood

### Decorative mouldings

- MDF mouldings
- Pine mouldings
- Hardwood mouldings
- Flexible mouldings for curves

Premium projects often use veneered MDF with matching hardwood mouldings.

## Construction Method

Typical construction sequence:

1. Measure wall.
2. Produce panel layout.
3. Cut backing sheet.
4. Cut mouldings.
5. Mitre corners.
6. Fix mouldings.
7. Fill joints.
8. Sand.
9. Prime.
10. Paint or stain.
11. Install.
12. Caulk edges.

This method creates the appearance of solid timber joinery.

## Moulding Rules

All mouldings should:

- maintain equal width
- have mitred corners
- align with neighbouring panels
- terminate neatly against skirting
- terminate neatly against architraves

Consistency is more important than absolute panel size.

## Panel Proportions

Good panel design maintains:

- equal border spacing
- balanced rectangles
- symmetrical appearance
- consistent moulding widths

Panels may become taller · shorter · wider · narrower to suit the available wall.

## Storage Integration

Decorative panelling may conceal:

- cupboards
- shoe storage
- coat storage
- cleaning cupboards
- utility access
- electrical cupboards

Hidden doors should:

- match surrounding panels
- use push-to-open hardware
- minimise visible joints

The storage should not interrupt the panel rhythm.

## LED Integration

Lighting may include:

- under handrail
- under tread
- stair string
- panel reveals
- under cabinet
- newel lighting

Recommended colour temperature: **2700K–3000K**.

Lighting should appear concealed. Visible LED strips should be avoided.

## Period Matching

- **Georgian** — raised and fielded · bolection mouldings · symmetrical layouts
- **Victorian** — larger decorative mouldings · deeper profiles · ornate details
- **Edwardian** — simplified raised panels · elegant proportions
- **Modern** — Shaker · square mouldings · minimal decoration
- **Scandinavian** — simple recessed panels · pale timber · clean geometry

## Staircase Compatibility

Decorative panelling is suitable for:

- straight staircases
- quarter-turn staircases
- half-turn staircases
- winder staircases
- curved staircases
- floating staircases
- enclosed staircases
- open staircases

Each staircase requires a custom panel layout.

## Design Principles

A successful panel installation should:

- look built with the house
- align with the staircase
- align with door frames
- align with windows
- maintain proportional balance
- conceal construction joints
- integrate with skirting and architraves
- appear as bespoke joinery

## Nex Recommendation Logic

**If staircase is modern oak** → recommend Shaker · Contemporary · Oak veneered MDF · Slim mouldings · Optional LED lighting.

**If staircase is Georgian or Victorian** → recommend Raised and Fielded · Bolection mouldings · Hardwood mouldings · Traditional proportions.

**If staircase is Scandinavian** → recommend Minimal Shaker · Pale oak veneer · Flush mouldings · Hidden storage · Soft warm lighting.

**If staircase is luxury contemporary** → recommend Contemporary panels · Veneered MDF · Concealed LEDs · Large-format geometric layouts.

## Core Principle

Decorative staircase panelling is not simply wall decoration. It is a bespoke architectural joinery system that integrates with the staircase, surrounding walls, skirting, architraves, lighting and storage to create a unified, high-quality interior feature. It should always be designed as part of the overall staircase rather than as an afterthought.
