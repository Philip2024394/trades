# My Staircase Has Scratches – Should I Paint It White or Fit Carpet?

Scratches are a common part of owning a timber staircase and, in many cases, they can be repaired without changing the staircase's overall appearance.

Changing a staircase from a natural timber finish to a painted finish, or covering it with carpet, should normally be an interior design choice rather than a solution to surface damage. If you like the appearance of your timber staircase, it is often worth considering repair before deciding to hide it.

## Light Surface Scratches

If the scratch has only damaged the protective lacquer or varnish and has not penetrated into the timber itself, it may be possible to repair the affected area.

A light sanding with a fine abrasive can remove minor surface marks and prepare the finish for a new protective top coat. To maintain a consistent appearance, the surrounding tread or affected section may also require light sanding before refinishing.

The aim is to blend the repaired area with the rest of the staircase rather than creating an obvious patch.

## Deeper Scratches

If the scratch has cut through the protective finish and into the timber, more preparation is usually required.

The damaged area may need to be sanded back until the scratch is removed before the finish is rebuilt. Depending on the depth of the damage, this may involve refinishing the entire tread or timber component to achieve an even colour and sheen.

Although this requires more time and effort, many deeper scratches can still be successfully repaired without replacing the timber.

## Should I Paint the Staircase?

Painting can completely change the appearance of a staircase and is an excellent choice if it suits the overall interior design of your home.

However, painting purely to hide scratches may not always be the best solution. Once painted, returning the staircase to its original natural timber appearance can involve considerable work.

If you already enjoy the look of the natural timber, repairing the finish is often worth considering before making a permanent design change.

## Would Carpet Be a Better Option?

Fitting carpet or a stair runner can hide existing cosmetic damage while also providing additional grip and reducing wear on the timber.

For some homeowners, this is a practical choice. For others, it changes the appearance of the staircase more than they would like. The decision should be based on your preferred style, maintenance requirements, and how you use the staircase, rather than simply covering scratches.

## Expert Tip

A scratch usually indicates that the **protective finish has been damaged**, not necessarily the staircase itself.

* If the damage is only within the lacquer or varnish, repairs are often straightforward.
* If the timber beneath has been damaged, more extensive sanding and refinishing may be required.

Before deciding to paint or carpet your staircase, consider whether the existing finish can be restored. Many scratched timber staircases can be successfully repaired with careful preparation, patience, and the correct finishing products while retaining their original natural appearance.
