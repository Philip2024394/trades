# Springing or Bounce in a Staircase

A staircase that springs or feels bouncy when walked on indicates that movement is occurring somewhere within the structure. This movement should not be present in a correctly designed and adequately supported staircase.

On a closed-string staircase, one possible cause is excessive movement in the string. This can occur if the string has insufficient stiffness for the loads it is carrying or if the staircase has not been adequately designed or supported for the application.

On a cut-string staircase, a similar issue can occur if the string beneath the treads and risers does not provide sufficient structural support, allowing the staircase to flex under normal use.

The appropriate repair depends on the cause of the movement. In some cases, additional reinforcement may be fitted to increase the stiffness of the string. One method is to install a steel reinforcement plate along the inside face of the string, securely fixed to reduce deflection. This solution is generally more suitable where the reinforcement can be concealed, such as on staircases with enclosed or backed strings.

For open-backed staircases, concealing reinforcement can be more difficult. A structural steel channel or similar reinforcement fitted beneath the string may provide additional strength, although this can affect the appearance of the staircase and may not be suitable for every design.

Where reinforcement is not practical or would compromise the appearance or structural integrity of the staircase, replacing the affected string may be the most appropriate solution. This may require refinishing to match the existing staircase. Painted staircases can usually be blended successfully, whereas stained or varnished timber may show visible colour and grain differences between the original and replacement components.

> **Important:** Springing can have several causes, including inadequate structural design, insufficient support, poor installation, loose joints, or deterioration over time. The source of the movement should be identified before selecting a repair method.
