# Staircase Maintenance Guide

A well-built staircase can last for many decades when it is properly maintained. Regular cleaning and simple inspections can help preserve its appearance, reduce wear, and identify small issues before they become expensive repairs.

## Regular Cleaning

Dust and grit act like fine sandpaper when walked across timber stairs. Regular vacuuming or sweeping removes abrasive particles that can gradually wear protective finishes.

Clean timber surfaces using a soft cloth that is only lightly dampened. Avoid soaking the timber, as excessive moisture can damage both the finish and the wood over time.

Always wipe away spills as soon as possible, particularly on stair treads where water can penetrate joints and affect the finish.

## Choosing the Right Cleaning Products

Not all furniture polishes and sprays are suitable for staircases.

Some furniture sprays leave an attractive temporary shine, but certain products contain silicones, solvents, waxes, or oils that can gradually break down lacquer or varnish finishes. Over time, this may dull the surface, soften the protective coating, or make future refinishing more difficult.

Always follow the staircase manufacturer's maintenance recommendations. If no recommendations are available, test any cleaning product on an inconspicuous area before applying it across the entire staircase.

Avoid harsh chemicals, abrasive cleaners, or products intended for other surfaces unless they are specifically suitable for finished timber.

## Protecting the Finish

The protective finish is the staircase's first defence against everyday wear.

To help extend its life:

* Keep stair treads free from grit and dirt.
* Wipe away water immediately.
* Avoid dragging furniture or heavy objects across the stairs.
* Use protective pads when moving large items.
* Trim pet claws where appropriate to reduce scratching.
* Replace worn carpet runners or stair protectors before they expose finished timber to excessive wear.

If the finish begins to wear through in high-traffic areas, consider refinishing before bare timber becomes exposed.

## Check for Movement

A staircase should feel solid under normal use.

Periodically check for:

* Loose handrails.
* Loose newel posts.
* Movement in balustrades.
* Squeaking or springing treads.
* Gaps opening between treads, risers, or strings.
* Loose fixings or visible signs of movement.

Small issues are often easier and less expensive to correct before they develop into larger structural problems.

## Control Moisture and Humidity

Timber naturally expands and contracts as humidity changes throughout the year.

Maintaining a reasonably stable indoor environment helps minimise seasonal movement.

Avoid exposing timber staircases to excessive moisture, leaks, or prolonged high humidity. Likewise, extremely dry conditions can increase shrinkage and the appearance of small gaps between components.

## Protect During Building Work

If decorating or carrying out renovation work, protect the staircase from paint, plaster, dust, and impact damage.

Use breathable protective coverings where possible and avoid taping directly onto finished timber surfaces unless the tape is specifically designed for delicate finishes.

## Inspect the Finish Regularly

Inspect your staircase at least once or twice each year.

Look for:

* Worn lacquer or varnish.
* Surface scratches.
* Chips or dents.
* Water marks.
* Areas where the protective coating has become thin.

Addressing minor cosmetic damage early can significantly extend the life of the original finish.

## When Professional Advice May Be Needed

If your staircase develops significant movement, excessive springing, structural cracking, loose structural joints, or other signs of deterioration, seek advice from the original staircase manufacturer or another suitably qualified staircase specialist before carrying out repairs.

Correctly identifying the cause of the problem is essential before selecting an appropriate repair.

## Expert Tip

The best staircase maintenance is preventative rather than reactive. Regular cleaning, gentle care, and periodic inspections will usually preserve both the appearance and performance of a timber staircase far better than waiting until visible damage occurs.
