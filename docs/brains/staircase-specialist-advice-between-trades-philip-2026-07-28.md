# "My Carpenter Says It's Wrong" – Understanding Specialist Advice During Your Staircase Installation

One of the most common statements staircase companies hear during installations is:

> *"My carpenter says it's wrong."*

For homeowners, hearing this can naturally cause concern. After all, construction projects involve significant investments, and nobody wishes to discover that something within their home has been installed incorrectly.

Before becoming alarmed, however, it is important to understand that construction projects involve many different specialist trades, each possessing their own knowledge, experience, and professional opinions.

The key word here is specialist.

## Poor Workmanship Is Usually Easy to Identify

Where a staircase has genuinely been installed incorrectly or the workmanship falls below acceptable standards, this can usually be identified relatively easily.

Examples may include:

* Poor finishing.
* Loose components.
* Damaged materials.
* Incorrect dimensions.
* Visible manufacturing defects.
* Incomplete installations.
* Obvious structural concerns.

These issues are generally quite clear and should always be discussed immediately with your staircase company.

Professional companies welcome genuine concerns and will usually work quickly to investigate and resolve any problems identified.

## Not Every Concern Means Something Is Wrong

Construction projects are rarely straightforward.

Walls move slightly. Floors vary in level. Plaster thickness changes. Flooring specifications evolve throughout the project.

What may appear unusual to one trade may simply represent the correct solution to a particular site condition.

For example, an installer may decide to:

* Use a cover slip.
* Pack fixing positions.
* Alter flooring allowances.
* Change fixing methods.
* Adjust decorative details.

These decisions are often made because of conditions found on site rather than because something has been manufactured or installed incorrectly.

Without understanding the complete picture, it can sometimes be difficult for another trade to understand why particular decisions have been made.

## Every Trade Has Its Own Specialist Knowledge

Professional carpenters are exceptionally skilled at what they do.

Likewise:

* Electricians understand electrical installations.
* Plasterers understand finishes and wall preparation.
* Flooring specialists understand floor systems.
* Builders understand construction management.
* Staircase specialists understand staircase manufacture and installation.

None of these trades diminish the knowledge of another.

Construction projects are successful because specialist knowledge is shared and respected throughout the build.

This article is not intended to discriminate against or diminish the skills of any trade. Quite the opposite—it is intended to recognise that specialist trades exist because they possess specialist knowledge developed over many years of experience.

## There May Be Information You Cannot See

Occasionally, another trade may express concerns because they are considering future stages of the build.

For example, a carpenter may say:

> "I don't think that's correct."

What they may actually mean is:

> "I'm concerned this could affect the flooring later."

or perhaps:

> "I'm wondering how this detail will work once we install the wall panelling."

These observations can often prove extremely valuable.

Rather than treating differing opinions as disagreements, they should become opportunities for professional discussion between trades.

Trade communication remains one of the most important parts of successful construction projects.

## Ask One Simple Question

If another trade raises concerns regarding your staircase, consider asking:

> *"Could you explain specifically what concerns you?"*

Then ask your staircase company:

> *"Could you explain why this has been installed in this manner?"*

Once both explanations have been provided, homeowners are usually in a much better position to understand the situation properly.

Professional discussions should always be welcomed.

## Daily Events Within Construction

Situations like these occur every single day throughout the construction industry.

Examples include:

* Builders questioning flooring details.
* Electricians questioning plaster finishes.
* Flooring installers questioning wall levels.
* Staircase companies questioning staircase openings.
* Carpenters questioning decorative finishes.

Construction projects involve many professional opinions coming together to achieve the best possible result.

This should never automatically be interpreted as conflict between trades. More often than not, it simply represents professionals discussing how their work affects the next stage of the build.

## Communication Produces Better Results

The most successful projects are rarely those without problems—they are usually the projects where communication has been strongest throughout the build.

If concerns are raised:

* Ask questions.
* Remain level-headed.
* Seek specialist advice where appropriate.
* Encourage trades to communicate openly.
* Consider all professional opinions carefully before reaching conclusions.

Professional advice should never become a cause for unnecessary conflict.

## A Final Thought

If another trade advises that something may be wrong with your staircase, take the advice seriously—but also take it calmly.

Listen carefully to the concerns raised and discuss them openly with your staircase company. In many situations there will be perfectly reasonable explanations based upon manufacturing requirements, site tolerances, or installation methods that may not be immediately obvious during construction.

Equally, genuine concerns should never be ignored simply because they come from another trade. Professional observations are often extremely valuable and can help deliver better outcomes for everyone involved.

Construction has always been a partnership between specialist trades working together.

Every trade brings valuable knowledge to a project, and every trade occasionally benefits from the knowledge of another.
