# Under-Stair Storage Integration

Converting the triangular void beneath a staircase into practical, furniture-quality built-in storage.

## Why It Matters

Every staircase creates a triangular void beneath it. When left empty, this space is wasted. When properly designed, it becomes some of the most valuable storage in the home — cleaner hallways, reduced need for separate cupboards, better organisation and increased property value. The goal is a clean furniture-quality appearance that reads as an integral part of the staircase joinery, not an afterthought bolted on.

## Storage Types

### Double-Door Cupboard

Located beneath the tallest part of the staircase. Ideal for: vacuum cleaners · mops · brooms · coats · cleaning products · household equipment · sports equipment · folding chairs · suitcases. Doors follow the staircase angle while remaining symmetrical and neatly proportioned.

### Integrated Drawer Stack

Placed next to (or instead of) the cupboard. Ideal for: shoes · gloves · hats · keys · pet accessories · charging cables · children's toys · documents · everyday household items. Drawers maximise usable volume beneath the staircase.

### Combined Systems

The strongest under-stair layouts combine both — one or two large cupboards (bulky items) plus a drawer stack (frequently accessed everyday items). The face of the storage assembly stays symmetrical even when the depth changes behind it.

## Matching Side Panelling

The visible staircase side is fully sheeted using matching timber panelling. Purpose: conceal the structural staircase framework · create a clean furniture-style appearance · provide a fixing surface for the storage units · strengthen the overall visual design · make the staircase appear custom-built rather than simply installed.

The panelling follows the staircase pitch precisely, producing clean diagonal lines from floor level to the upper landing.

## Joinery Quality Signals

Purpose-built storage (not modular) reads as premium. Look for:

- Flush-fitted doors
- Matching timber grain across all faces
- Consistent panel spacing
- Hidden structural framing
- Recessed cabinet construction
- Integrated plinth
- Matching skirting details
- Furniture-quality finish

## Construction Sequence

Storage is typically built **after** the staircase installation, not during.

1. **Timber framing** fixed between floor and staircase — provides the internal skeleton.
2. **MDF or plywood cabinet carcasses** — the box behind each cupboard/drawer.
3. **Timber face frames** — the visible surround that panels + doors mount into.
4. **Matching panel doors** — hung on soft-close concealed hinges.
5. **Full-extension drawer runners** — for the drawer stack.
6. **Magnetic catches or concealed latches** — no visible handles preferred.
7. **Decorative mouldings where required** — to tie the storage into surrounding skirting/architraves.

**Structural rule:** the staircase remains the structural element; the storage cabinetry is non-structural and is fixed independently to the surrounding walls, floor and underside of the staircase. Do not load-bear from the storage carcasses.

## Suitable House Styles

- Family homes
- New builds
- Renovation projects
- Smaller hallways where storage is scarce
- Traditional interiors
- Scandinavian interiors
- Country homes
- Modern farmhouse designs

## Enhancement Opportunities

- Concealed warm-white LED lighting beneath the handrail.
- Motion-sensor cupboard lighting.
- Pull-out shoe racks.
- Hidden charging station (wireless pads + USB-C sockets inside a drawer).
- Umbrella storage compartment.
- Wine storage.
- Oak Shaker panel doors (or raised-and-fielded traditional doors).
- Push-to-open concealed cupboards.
- Secret hidden storage compartments.
- Ventilated drying cupboard.
- Pull-out recycling bins.
- Integrated seating bench within the storage.
- Bespoke oak handles or recessed finger pulls.
- Matching wall panelling to create a continuous architectural feature.

## Access Panel Rule (composes with the panelling brain doc)

Whenever an LED transformer, electrical junction, isolator or serviceable component sits behind an under-stair panel or door, that panel/door must be REMOVABLE / hinged / push-to-open — never a fixed panel. Same rule that applies to decorative panelling elsewhere.

## Nex Knowledge Summary

Nex should recognise that under-stair storage is one of the highest-value enhancements a homeowner can make to a traditional staircase. The best installations use matching timber panelling for the visible staircase side, combine cupboards with drawers to suit both bulky and everyday items, and are built after the staircase itself so the storage cabinetry can be fitted independently without altering the structural staircase. When advising customers, Nex should also flag the future-proofing options (LED, hidden charging, motion sensors) that are inexpensive at first fit but expensive to retrofit later.
