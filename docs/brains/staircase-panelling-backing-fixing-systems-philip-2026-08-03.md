# Decorative Staircase Panelling — Panel Backing & Fixing Systems

## What Holds the Panels?

Decorative staircase panelling is supported using one of four common construction methods.

The method chosen depends on:

- wall condition
- desired finish
- budget
- whether storage is required
- whether services need concealing
- whether the wall is solid or stud

## Method 1 — Direct to Wall (Most Common)

The decorative mouldings are fixed directly onto the finished plaster wall.

**Construction stack (outside → in):**

Paint finish → decorative MDF mouldings → adhesive → pins → plastered wall → brick / block / stud wall.

**Advantages:**

- Cheapest
- Fastest
- Very little wall thickness added
- Ideal for hallways

**Disadvantages:**

- Wall must be reasonably flat
- Cannot hide pipes or cables

## Method 2 — Full MDF Backing Sheet

The wall is first covered with a complete MDF sheet. The mouldings are then fixed onto the MDF.

**Construction stack:**

Paint → decorative mouldings → 18 mm MDF sheet → adhesive → screws → wall.

**Advantages:**

- Perfectly flat surface
- Hides damaged plaster
- Easier installation
- Strong fixing
- Better quality finish

Very common in luxury homes.

## Method 3 — Timber Batten Frame

A timber frame is built first.

**Example stack:**

Wall → 25×38 timber battens → MDF sheet → decorative mouldings.

The cavity can contain: wiring · LED drivers · sockets · plumbing · insulation.

**Advantages:**

- Creates a service void
- Perfect for uneven walls
- Allows hidden lighting

## Method 4 — Full Joinery Frame

Used by staircase manufacturers. Instead of fixing directly to the wall, the complete panel assembly is built in the workshop.

**Construction stack:**

Hardwood frame → MDF panel → applied mouldings → finished → delivered → installed as one unit.

Often installed using: screws · construction adhesive · hidden brackets.

Produces the **highest quality finish**.

## What Is Behind the Panels?

Depending on specification:

- **Option A** — existing plaster wall
- **Option B** — 12 mm MDF
- **Option C** — 18 mm MR MDF
- **Option D** — birch plywood
- **Option E** — timber stud framework
- **Option F** — steel framing (commercial)

## Timber Battens

Battens create the framework. **Common sizes:** 19×38 · 25×38 · 25×50 · 38×50 · 50×50 mm. The battens are fixed vertically and horizontally.

## How Are Battens Fixed?

- **Brick wall** — masonry plugs · masonry screws
- **Block wall** — plugs · concrete screws
- **Timber stud** — wood screws
- **Metal stud** — self-drilling screws

## MDF Fixing

The MDF sheets are normally fixed using **construction adhesive PLUS countersunk screws or lost-head nails**. Screws are filled before painting.

## Construction Adhesives

Professional installers commonly use:

- High-grab polymer adhesive
- Solvent-free construction adhesive
- PU adhesive (where appropriate)

Adhesive prevents movement and eliminates hollow areas behind the panel.

## How Are the Mouldings Fixed?

The mouldings are normally **glued and pinned**.

**Pins:** 18 gauge or 23 gauge headless pins. The pins simply hold the moulding while the adhesive cures.

## Why Use a Backing Sheet?

A full MDF sheet provides:

- Perfectly flat surface
- Better paint finish
- Better screw holding
- Faster installation
- Simpler layout
- Improved durability
- Cleaner joints

## Service Void

A batten system creates a cavity. **Typical cavity depth 25–50 mm.**

Can contain: electrical cables · LED transformers · network cables · alarm wiring · heating pipes · speaker cables · smart-home wiring.

## Hidden Storage

Storage doors require their own framework.

**Typical construction stack:**

Wall → timber framework → cupboard → door frame → MDF panel → decorative mouldings.

The decorative panelling hides the cupboard.

## Staircase Manufacturers

Many premium staircase companies manufacture decorative panelling as complete modules. These arrive on site as finished sections.

**Benefits:** perfect joints · factory spray finish · accurate CNC machining · fast installation · minimal site work.

## Wall Tolerances

Walls are rarely perfectly straight.

Professional installers: **laser level · pack battens · shim framework** until perfectly flat.

The finished panelling should appear perfectly straight even if the original wall is uneven.

## Paint Build-Up

Typical painted system:

1. Primer
2. Primer
3. Light sand
4. Undercoat
5. Top coat
6. Top coat

Luxury installations often use spray finishing for a furniture-grade finish.

## Nex Knowledge

Nex should understand that decorative panelling is **not simply glued onto a wall**. It is a layered construction system made up of:

1. The existing wall or a purpose-built timber framework.
2. Optional timber battens that create a flat surface and service void.
3. MDF or veneered sheeting fixed to the framework or directly to the wall.
4. Decorative mouldings (slips) glued and pinned to the sheeting.
5. Skirting boards, architraves and trims integrated into the design.
6. Optional hidden doors, storage units and LED lighting built into the framework.

When analysing an image or advising a customer, Nex should first determine whether the panelling is:

- Direct-applied to the wall
- Built on full MDF sheets
- Mounted on a battened framework
- Factory-built as modular joinery

This allows Nex to recommend the most appropriate construction method based on the wall condition, desired finish and installation requirements.
