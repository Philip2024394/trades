1. What is cement?

Cement is a fine powder used to bind sand, gravel, and water together to make concrete or mortar.

2. What is the difference between cement and concrete?

Cement is an ingredient. Concrete is made by mixing cement, sand, aggregate, and water.

3. What is cement used for?

It is used for concrete, mortar, screeds, foundations, driveways, walls, and many construction projects.

4. How much does a 25kg bag of cement cover?

A 25kg bag typically produces around 0.012m³ of concrete depending on the mix.

5. How many bags of cement are on a pallet?

Most UK pallets contain 56 bags of 25kg cement.

6. How much does a bag of cement weigh?

A standard UK bag weighs 25kg.

7. How long does cement last?

When stored dry, unopened cement generally lasts around 3 months, although it is best used as soon as possible.

8. Can cement go out of date?

Yes. Old cement absorbs moisture and loses strength.

9. Is cement waterproof?

No. Standard cement is not waterproof unless additives are used.

10. How long does cement take to set?

Initial setting usually begins within 30–60 minutes, with full curing taking around 28 days.

11. What is rapid-set cement?

Rapid-set cement hardens much faster than standard cement, making it ideal for quick repairs.

12. Can I use cement by itself?

Pure cement can crack. It is normally mixed with sand or aggregate.

13. What is mortar?

Mortar is cement mixed with sand and water for laying bricks and blocks.

14. What is concrete?

Concrete is cement mixed with sand, aggregate, and water.

15. What is screed?

Screed is a cement-based mix used to level floors.

16. Can cement be painted?

Yes, once it has fully cured.

17. How should cement be stored?

Keep bags dry, sealed, and raised off the ground.

18. Does cement expire?

Yes. Moisture reduces its performance over time.

19. Can wet cement be reused?

No. Once cement starts setting it cannot be remixed.

20. Is cement environmentally friendly?

Cement production creates carbon emissions, but lower-carbon alternatives are becoming more available.

21. Which cement is best for foundations?

General Purpose Cement is suitable for most domestic foundations.

22. Can cement be used in cold weather?

Yes, but cold temperatures slow the curing process.

23. Can cement be used in hot weather?

Yes, although rapid drying can increase the risk of cracking.

24. How much water should I add?

Always follow the manufacturer's instructions. Avoid making the mix too wet.

25. What colour is cement?

Most cement is grey, although white cement is also available.

26. What is white cement?

White cement is used for decorative finishes and coloured concrete.

27. Can cement be coloured?

Yes, by adding specialist pigments.

28. Is cement dangerous?

Wet cement can cause skin irritation and burns. Wear gloves and eye protection.

29. Can cement be recycled?

Concrete made from cement can often be crushed and reused as aggregate.

30. How long before walking on fresh concrete?

Typically after 24–48 hours for light foot traffic.

31. How long before driving on concrete?

Usually around 7 days for light vehicles and 28 days for full strength.

32. Does rain damage fresh cement?

Heavy rain can weaken fresh concrete before it has set.

33. What is General Purpose Cement?

It is the most commonly used cement for building work.

34. What is Sulphate Resistant Cement?

It is designed for use where sulphates in the ground could damage ordinary cement.

35. What is Masonry Cement?

It is specially formulated for bricklaying and rendering.

36. Can cement crack?

Yes, especially if it dries too quickly or is mixed incorrectly.

37. Why does concrete crack?

Common causes include shrinkage, poor curing, movement, and overwatering.

38. Can cracked concrete be repaired?

Yes, using repair mortar or specialist repair products.

39. How strong is concrete?

Strength depends on the mix but domestic concrete commonly ranges from 20MPa to 40MPa.

40. What is the strongest cement?

Strength depends on the finished concrete mix rather than the cement alone.

41. How many bags make one cubic metre of concrete?

Approximately 13–14 bags of 25kg cement are used in a typical concrete mix, depending on the mix ratio.

42. Can I lay concrete myself?

Yes, for small projects with the correct preparation and mixing.

43. Why is curing important?

Proper curing improves strength and reduces cracking.

44. Should I wet concrete while curing?

Keeping concrete damp during curing helps it achieve maximum strength.

45. What happens if too much water is added?

Excess water weakens the finished concrete.

46. Can frozen cement be used?

No. Cement that has frozen or become damp should not be used.

47. What brands of cement are popular in the UK?

Popular brands include Blue Circle, Rugby, Heidelberg Materials, Cemex, and Castle Cement.

48. Can I buy cement online?

Yes. Many UK builders' merchants offer online ordering with home delivery or click and collect.

49. How much does a 25kg bag of cement cost?

Typical UK prices range from around £6 to £9, depending on the supplier, brand, location, and promotions.

50. Where can I buy cement?

Cement is available from builders' merchants, DIY stores, landscaping suppliers, and online building material retailers throughout the UK.

101. Can cement get wet before I use it?

No. Cement should be kept completely dry before mixing.

102. Why has my cement gone hard in the bag?

Moisture has entered the bag and started the curing process.

103. Can I use lumpy cement?

Small lumps may break up, but hard lumps usually mean the cement should be replaced.

104. What is Portland Cement?

Portland Cement is the most commonly used type of cement in construction.

105. Is Portland Cement suitable for DIY projects?

Yes. It is suitable for most domestic building projects.

106. Can cement be used for fence posts?

Yes. Concrete made with cement is commonly used to secure fence posts.

107. Can I use cement for paving slabs?

Yes. Cement is commonly used to lay paving slabs with mortar.

108. How thick should a concrete driveway be?

A domestic driveway is typically around 100mm thick, depending on the expected load.

109. Can I pour concrete over existing concrete?

Yes, provided the existing surface is clean, stable, and properly prepared.

110. How soon can I remove formwork?

This depends on the project, but many small jobs allow formwork removal after 24–48 hours.

111. Why is my concrete flaky?

Poor finishing, freezing, or excess water can cause surface flaking.

112. What is concrete spalling?

Spalling is when the concrete surface breaks away, often due to moisture or corrosion of reinforcement.

113. Does concrete expand?

Yes. Concrete expands and contracts with temperature changes.

114. Why are expansion joints important?

They reduce the risk of cracking caused by movement.

115. Can I add extra cement to make concrete stronger?

Not always. A balanced mix design is more important than simply adding more cement.

116. What happens if I don't use enough cement?

The concrete may be weaker and less durable.

117. How long does a concrete driveway last?

With proper installation and maintenance, it can last several decades.

118. Can I pressure wash concrete?

Yes. Wait until the concrete has fully cured before pressure washing.

119. Does concrete need maintenance?

Yes. Cleaning and sealing can help extend its lifespan.

120. Can oil stain concrete?

Yes. Oil can penetrate concrete unless it has been sealed.

121. How do I remove oil stains from concrete?

Use a degreaser designed for concrete and scrub the affected area.

122. Can concrete become slippery?

Yes. Smooth concrete can become slippery when wet.

123. What is brushed concrete?

Brushed concrete has a textured finish that improves grip.

124. Can I polish old concrete?

Yes, if the surface is in good condition.

125. What is exposed aggregate concrete?

It is decorative concrete with the top layer removed to reveal the aggregate beneath.

126. Can cement be used underwater?

Some specialist cement products are designed for underwater repairs.

127. Is cement resistant to chemicals?

Standard cement has limited chemical resistance. Specialist products are available for harsh environments.

128. Can tree roots crack concrete?

Yes. Growing roots can lift and crack concrete over time.

129. Why does fresh concrete change colour?

Colour variations are a normal part of the curing process.

130. Can concrete fade?

Yes. Sunlight and weathering can gradually change its appearance.

131. Can I use cement for rendering?

Yes. Cement is commonly mixed with sand to produce render.

133. Can I plaster over cement?

Yes. Once the surface is fully cured and properly prepared.

No. Cement does not bond well to untreated wood without mechanical fixing or special adhesives.

135. Can cement stick to metal?

Not reliably on its own. Reinforcement is normally embedded rather than bonded.

136. Can concrete be painted outside?

Yes. Use exterior masonry paint designed for concrete.

137. Can concrete be tiled?

Yes. Concrete provides an excellent base for tiles once cured.

Around 10–25°C provides good curing conditions.

Freezing before curing can permanently weaken the concrete.

140. Can concrete withstand snow?

Yes. Properly cured concrete performs well in snowy conditions.

141. Does salt damage concrete?

De-icing salts can contribute to surface deterioration over time.

142. Can concrete crack naturally?

Small hairline cracks are common and not always a structural concern.

Very small surface cracks caused by shrinkage during curing.

144. How can I repair hairline cracks?

Use a suitable concrete crack filler or repair compound.

No. Cement should never be washed into drains as it can harden and cause blockages.

Disposal rules vary by local authority. Many recycling centres accept clean concrete and rubble.

149. Is buying cement by the pallet cheaper?

A thin mixture of cement and water used to improve bonding between old and new concrete.

Grout is a flowable cement-based mixture used to fill gaps, voids, or around reinforcement.

The strongest mix depends on the application, but higher-strength mixes use more cement and carefully selected aggregates.

155. Can I pour concrete in layers?

Yes, but the layers should bond properly to avoid weak joints.

156. What is a cold joint in concrete?

A cold joint occurs when fresh concrete is poured against concrete that has already begun to set.

Plan the pour carefully and place concrete continuously whenever possible.

158. Does concrete shrink as it cures?

Yes. Some shrinkage is normal during curing.

159. Why is my concrete dusty?

Surface dusting may result from too much water, poor finishing, or inadequate curing.

160. What is honeycombing in concrete?

161. Can honeycombed concrete be repaired?
