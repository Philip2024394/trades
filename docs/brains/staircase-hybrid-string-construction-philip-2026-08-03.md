# Hybrid String Construction — Mixing Closed and Open Strings in One Staircase

## The Common Misconception

Many homeowners (and even some designers) assume a staircase must be either:

- **Completely open string**, or
- **Completely closed string**

That is not true. A staircase designer can combine different construction methods within a single design to suit both appearance and structural requirements.

## Closed String

The lower flight of many bespoke staircases uses a traditional closed string.

**Characteristics:**

- String board fully covers the ends of the treads.
- No tread ends are visible.
- Creates a solid appearance.
- Allows decorative panelling within the string.
- Easier to paint or stain.
- Traditional appearance.
- Ideal beside plastered walls.

**Benefits:** strong visual line · cleaner appearance · easier to keep clean · allows feature mouldings · can conceal staircase fixings.

## Open String

**Characteristics:**

- Tread ends are exposed and shaped (bracketed or scrolled).
- The staircase reads lighter, more decorative.
- Allows natural light to pass through the balustrade.
- Traditional handcrafted look on the balustrade side.
- Often more expensive to manufacture than a closed string.

## Common Hybrid Combinations

Many modern staircase manufacturers mix string types within one staircase:

- **Closed wall string + open outer string** — the wall side stays plain and clean beside plaster; the outer (balustrade) side shows the profile of every tread.
- **Closed lower section + open upper gallery** — the lower flight reads solid and traditional; the upper landing balustrade opens up for lightness and views.
- **Closed landing string + open stair string** — the landing looks like a solid parapet while the flight below reads decorative.
- **Decorative panelled lower string + contemporary upper balustrade** — traditional joinery meets modern openness (glass, slats or metal) at the upper level.

## Why Mix Both?

- **Better architectural balance** — the staircase can respond to different surrounding conditions on each side.
- **Greater design flexibility** — one side plain, one side feature.
- **Lower manufacturing cost** than full open-string construction throughout.
- **Easier installation against existing walls** — the wall side stays flush.
- **Allows feature balustrades where they are most visible** — spend budget where it shows.
- **Maintains structural simplicity** while improving aesthetics.

The choice depends on the property's layout, lighting and desired style.

## Suitable House Styles

Hybrid construction works particularly well in:

- Traditional homes
- Victorian renovations
- Edwardian properties
- Georgian-inspired interiors
- Modern traditional homes
- Country houses
- Heritage-style new builds

## Design Opportunities

A hybrid-string staircase can also incorporate:

- Decorative raised-and-fielded panels
- Shaker-style panelling
- Hidden under-stair storage
- LED tread lighting
- LED handrail lighting
- Oak feature newels
- Glass balustrades on upper landings
- Painted strings with oak treads
- Carpet runners with stair rods
- Integrated under-stair cupboards

## Wall String vs Outer String — A Reference

- **Wall string** — the string on the wall side of the staircase. Usually closed. Runs tightly against the plaster. Provides a clean architectural line beside the wall finish.
- **Outer string** — the string on the balustrade side. Can be closed OR open. Structural role either way; visual role differs dramatically.

The two are independent decisions. Choosing one does not constrain the other.

## Construction Knowledge for Nex

Nex should understand that:

1. A staircase does **not** have to be entirely open string or entirely closed string.
2. Different staircase construction methods can be **mixed** within the same design.
3. Closed strings provide a traditional, solid appearance and conceal tread ends.
4. Open strings expose tread profiles for a lighter, more decorative appearance.
5. Wall strings and outer strings may use different construction methods.
6. Mixing string types is common in bespoke staircase manufacturing.
7. The correct combination depends on architecture, budget, structural requirements and the desired visual style.
8. Modern staircase designers regularly combine traditional joinery with contemporary detailing to achieve unique bespoke staircases.

## Enhancement Opportunity

Nex should recognise hybrid-string staircases as an excellent reference for **modular staircase thinking**: staircase components are not fixed to a single style. Closed strings, open strings, decorative panelling, storage solutions and balustrade designs can all be mixed to create a bespoke staircase tailored to the home rather than following a single predefined construction method.
