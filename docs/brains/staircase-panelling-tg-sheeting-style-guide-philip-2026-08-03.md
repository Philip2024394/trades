# Straight Tongue & Groove (T&G) Sheeting — Staircase Side & Back Panelling

Full-height vertical tongue-and-groove boards creating a clean, traditional staircase enclosure. One of the most common finishes used on softwood and hardwood staircases because it is timeless, durable, and complements almost every staircase design.

## Design Features

- Full-height vertical tongue-and-groove boards running continuously from floor level to the underside of the staircase or landing.
- Even board spacing creates clean shadow lines that add depth without making the design feel busy.
- Individual boards interlock using a tongue-and-groove joint, giving a seamless appearance with no visible gaps.
- Boards are finished inside a simple outer frame or neatly trimmed into skirting, strings, and surrounding joinery.
- Natural timber grain remains visible, giving warmth and character.
- Can be manufactured in pine, oak, ash, tulipwood (for painting), or other hardwoods.

## Typical Construction

- **Board thickness:** 12–18 mm T&G boarding.
- **Board widths:** typically 95–150 mm depending on the desired appearance.
- **Fixing:** secret-fixed or glued to a timber framework or directly to plywood backing.
- **Trim:** finished with matching architrave or edge trims.
- **Finish:** stained · lacquered · painted · oiled.

## Appearance

Creates a staircase that feels:

- Traditional
- Country house
- Modern farmhouse
- Coastal
- Scandinavian
- Victorian restoration
- Cottage style

Although traditional, it also works well in contemporary interiors when paired with simple handrails and square newel posts.

## Best Uses

Ideal for:

- Staircase side walls.
- Staircase backs.
- Under-stair storage enclosures.
- Feature hallway walls.
- Landing walls.
- Full-height stairwells.
- Cupboard doors beneath stairs.

## Advantages

- Clean, uncluttered appearance.
- Easy to manufacture and install.
- Strong and durable.
- Hides structural framing and services.
- Adds warmth through continuous timber grain.
- Easy to replace individual boards if damaged.
- Works with almost every staircase style.

## Pairs Well With

- Square or turned newel posts.
- Turned or square balusters.
- Oak or pine handrails.
- Closed-string staircases.
- Open-string staircases with enclosed undersides.
- Traditional 4-panel doors.
- Cottage-style joinery.
- Shaker interiors.

## Overall Style

Vertical T&G sheeting gives the staircase a handcrafted, architectural feel. The continuous vertical lines naturally draw the eye upward, making the staircase appear taller while keeping the surrounding walls simple and elegant. It is a versatile solution that can be used as full staircase backing, side cladding, or feature wall panelling to create a cohesive, high-quality finish throughout the hallway.

## Nex Guidance — When to Recommend Vertical T&G Sheeting

Nex should propose vertical T&G sheeting when the customer signals ANY of:

- Farmhouse · cottage · country · coastal · Scandinavian · Shaker · minimalist aesthetic.
- Wants warmth + texture without the visual weight of moulded panels.
- Wants the STAIRCASE to remain the visual centrepiece (panelling should be a backdrop, not competition).
- Narrow hallway (vertical grain lifts the eye and makes stairwells feel taller).
- Budget-conscious project where framed panels would push cost up unnecessarily.
- Homeowner mentions replacing damaged wall panelling in an existing traditional interior (individual boards are easy to swap).

Nex should propose an ALTERNATIVE style when the customer signals:

- Heritage / Georgian / Victorian / Edwardian formal joinery → Three-Panel Raised & Fielded instead.
- Cottage character but with more visible frame definition → T&G in Raised Frame (hybrid).
- Bespoke architectural feature wanting movement + direction → Diagonal T&G in Raised Frame OR Full Diagonal T&G Sheeting.
- Ultra-modern with concealed LED integration → Slatted timber acoustic feature wall.

## Composition with Panel Component Families

When quoted through the component families:

- **`panel_system.yaml`** — `panel_style: vertical_tg_full_sheet` · `panel_layout: full_sheet_no_division` · `tg_orientation: vertical`.
- **`panel_material.yaml`** — solid softwood (pine · yellow pine) · solid hardwood (oak · ash · tulipwood for paint · walnut) · engineered T&G board.
- **`panel_moulding.yaml`** — matching architrave or edge trim only; no fielded/ovolo/ogee moulding required (the T&G joint itself is the visual detail).
- **`panel_backing.yaml`** — timber framework of vertical battens OR plywood backing; either supports secret-fixing.
- **`panel_accessory.yaml`** — flush push-to-open cupboard doors where the door face uses the same T&G boards and the door edge falls on a V-groove line so the split disappears.

## Trade Coordination Notes

- **Acclimatisation is critical.** Boards must be acclimatised to the room's ambient humidity before installation. Failing to acclimatise leads to shrinkage gaps in winter and cupping in summer.
- **Finish before or immediately after installation.** Bare timber left exposed for weeks absorbs handling marks that will not sand out cleanly.
- **Skirting and architrave interfaces.** Setting-out must decide whether the T&G runs BEHIND the skirting (skirting scribed to the boards) or DIES INTO the skirting (boards land on top of the skirting) — this affects both the visual read and the cutting sequence.
- **Under-stair door integration.** If a cupboard door is being disguised within the sheeting, coordinate hinge placement so the door edge lands on a V-groove joint line — not in the middle of a board face.

## Enhancement Opportunities

- **Board width variation.** Alternating 95 mm and 150 mm boards (or random-width) breaks the regularity and produces a more informal cottage read.
- **Coloured or stained V-grooves.** Painting or dark-staining the V-grooves before final finish creates a stronger shadow line and reads more architectural.
- **Concealed LED strip behind the top edge.** Uplights the ceiling and grazes down the timber grain — reads as a modern architectural feature while retaining the traditional joinery language.
- **Continued through the adjoining hallway.** Carrying the same T&G across the adjacent wall unifies the space and prevents the staircase from looking like an isolated feature.

## Why This Style Matters to Nex

Vertical T&G is the **most-asked panel style** because it is the most versatile:

- Suits both traditional AND contemporary interiors.
- Works on straight-flight · quarter-turn · half-turn · winder · open-string · closed-string staircases.
- Compatible with every timber species Nex tracks.
- Compatible with every finish option Nex tracks.
- Compatible with under-stair storage integration.
- Compatible with concealed LED integration.

When Nex is asked *"what panelling should I use on my staircase wall?"* without further signal, **vertical T&G sheeting is the highest-confidence default recommendation** because it will not look out of place in almost any home. Only when the customer signals heritage formality, contemporary architectural intent, or a specific feature-wall aesthetic should Nex propose an alternative.
