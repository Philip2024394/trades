# Starting Step Design + Newel Post Relationship

How the first tread of a staircase meets the first newel post — and why the choice matters.

## Why the Starting Step Matters

The first step is the one visitors see when they walk in. It sets the perceived quality of the whole staircase before anyone has climbed a single stair. There are three common patterns, and the difference between "builder-grade" and "bespoke" is often visible in this single detail.

## Type 1 — Square Starting Step (Rebated Into Newel Face)

The simplest and most economical option. Widely used on traditional builder staircases.

**Characteristics:**

- Full-width rectangular first tread.
- No curved projection.
- No rounded entrance platform.
- Clean square front edge.
- Step finishes neatly into the front face of the newel post.

**Newel relationship:** the front tread returns INTO the face of the newel post rather than extending AROUND it. Standard joinery practice: the front edge of the first tread is housed into the newel · the newel remains fully visible from the front · the tread is rebated or jointed into the side of the post · the staircase has a clean, symmetrical appearance.

**Best for:** compact hallways · builder-grade specifications · budget-conscious projects · homes where the staircase should not dominate.

## Type 2 — Bullnose Starting Step (Wraps Around Newel)

A feature first tread that projects forward into the hallway with a large curved front edge.

**Characteristics:**

- Curved or splayed tread projecting beyond the string.
- Rounded outer front corner.
- Creates a welcoming entrance.
- Provides additional standing space at the bottom of the stairs.
- Higher-end appearance.

**Newel relationship (variant A — common):** the tread wraps around approximately half of the newel post's front face. The newel appears to rise from within the tread. The entrance becomes wider and more decorative.

**Newel relationship (variant B — premium):** the 90mm newel post sits ON TOP OF the bullnose starting step, rather than directly on the floor. The bullnose wraps around the front portion of the newel. Unlike variant A, this places the newel almost entirely BEHIND the bullnose projection, creating a clean symmetrical appearance while maintaining structural support. A common arrangement on traditional softwood staircases supplied by bespoke manufacturers.

**Best for:** family homes with generous hallways · renovation projects wanting a step up in quality · properties where the staircase is a visual feature.

## Type 3 — Oversized Bullnose / Feature Starting Step

An even larger version of type 2 — extra-large, sometimes semi-circular, tread that acts almost like a welcoming platform rather than another stair.

**Characteristics:**

- Larger than every other tread.
- Rounded front outer corner for safer circulation.
- Creates a softer entrance into the staircase.
- Makes the staircase appear wider and more substantial.
- Often specified on premium staircases.
- Provides additional standing space before ascending.
- Improves the visual balance of the staircase.
- Accommodates decorative newel arrangements.
- Gives the staircase a more bespoke appearance.

**Best for:** double-height entrance halls · bespoke oak or hardwood staircases · properties where the staircase is the architectural centrepiece.

## Comparison Table

| Aspect | Square (Type 1) | Bullnose (Type 2) | Oversized Bullnose (Type 3) |
| ------ | --------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------- |
| Tread shape | Rectangular | Curved front | Extra-large, often semi-circular |
| Newel visibility | Full newel visible | Half newel covered | Newel sits within/behind tread |
| Footprint | Compact | Larger | Largest |
| Manufacturing | Simplest | More complex machining | Bespoke joinery |
| Cost | Lowest | Mid-range | Highest |
| Perceived quality | Standard/builder | Premium/family home | Bespoke feature |
| Best use | Any hallway | Renovation upgrade | Feature entrance |

**Both are structurally sound.** The choice is primarily aesthetic and depends on available entrance space, budget and the desired visual impact — not structural requirements.

## Standard Sizing Conventions (Traditional Softwood Staircases)

The most common combination on domestic pine staircases is a **90 mm newel post + 41 mm turned baluster**.

**90 mm newel post:**

- Approximately 90 × 90 mm section.
- Turned centre detail.
- Square structural base.
- Square upper section supporting the handrail.
- Decorative ball finial (traditional).
- Natural pine or stained finish.

**41 mm turned balusters:**

- Turned softwood profile.
- Even spacing.
- Traditional profile.
- Matching upper landing balustrade.
- Fixed into the baserail below and mortised beneath the handrail above.

These sizes have become the domestic default because they balance visual weight against practical stiffness. Larger newels (100 mm, 125 mm) read more substantial and are common on hardwood staircases; smaller balusters (32 mm square) read more contemporary.

## When to Choose Each

**Choose square starting step when:**

- The hallway is narrow.
- Budget is tight.
- The staircase is intentionally understated.
- The property style favours minimalism.

**Choose bullnose starting step when:**

- The hallway can accept a projecting tread without obstructing the entrance door swing.
- The customer wants a more prestigious entrance without a full bespoke commission.
- Renovating a period property.

**Choose oversized bullnose / feature starting step when:**

- The staircase is intended as the architectural centrepiece.
- Ceiling height is generous.
- Budget supports bespoke joinery.
- The entrance space can accommodate a welcoming platform.

## Enhancement Opportunities

A square starting step can be upgraded later by:

- Replacing the first tread with a bullnose or oversized feature tread.
- Adding an oak handrail on painted balusters.
- Fitting a larger, decorative newel cap.
- Adding a carpet runner with stair rods.
- Adding LED lighting beneath the handrail or tread nosings.

## Construction Knowledge for Nex

Nex should recognise:

1. Three common starting-step types exist — square, bullnose, oversized bullnose.
2. The relationship between the starting tread and the first newel is a key visual and structural detail — the tread either abuts the front face, wraps half the front face, or the newel sits on top of the tread.
3. Both are structurally sound; choice is primarily aesthetic + budget-driven.
4. Standard sizing for traditional pine staircases: 90 mm square newel + 41 mm turned baluster.
5. Bullnose designs read more prestigious and provide additional standing space at the bottom.
6. Square designs are more compact, cheaper and widely used in builder-grade construction.
7. Upgrading from square to bullnose is possible during renovation but requires manufacturing the new tread and re-jointing the first newel.
