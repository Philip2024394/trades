# Staircases and Building Regulations · A Nuanced Q&A

These are excellent questions, and they're exactly the sort of nuanced issues a Staircase Reference Brain should eventually answer with jurisdiction-specific guidance. For now, here's the general position (assuming England/Wales; Scotland and Northern Ireland differ in some respects).

## Can I order a staircase that doesn't comply with Building Regulations?

Yes, you can ask a staircase company to manufacture almost any design. There is generally no law preventing a company from making a bespoke staircase simply because it doesn't comply with current Building Regulations.

However, there is an important distinction between:

* Manufacturing a staircase, and
* Installing it as part of building work that must comply with Building Regulations.

If the staircase is intended for work that requires Building Regulations approval (for example, a new house, loft conversion, or many structural alterations), then the completed building work is normally expected to comply with the applicable regulations.

There are also legitimate situations where a non-standard staircase may be appropriate, such as furniture access, display installations, stage sets, or some historic buildings where different considerations apply.

## If the staircase is wrong, is the staircase company automatically at fault?

Not automatically.

Responsibility depends on several factors, including:

* Who designed the staircase?
* Who specified the dimensions?
* Who took the site measurements?
* What did the contract say?
* Who installed it?
* What information was provided to the manufacturer?
* Was the company asked to manufacture something they advised against?

For example:

* If you supplied the design and instructed the company to manufacture it exactly as drawn, responsibility may be different than if the company designed and specified the staircase.
* If the company designed, manufactured, and installed it as a compliant staircase, they may bear greater responsibility if it fails to meet contractual or regulatory requirements.
* If the company clearly warned that the design would not comply and you insisted on proceeding, that warning could become important evidence.

This is ultimately a legal question that depends on the facts and the contract.

## Will home insurance cover a staircase that isn't built to regulations?

There is no universal answer.

Insurance depends on:

* the wording of your policy,
* the circumstances of any claim,
* whether the non-compliance is related to the loss,
* whether there was any misrepresentation when obtaining the policy.

For example:

* If a staircase contributed to someone's injury because it did not meet required standards, the insurer may investigate whether that affected coverage or liability.
* If the staircase has nothing to do with the claim (for example, storm damage to the roof), Building Regulation compliance may have little or no relevance.

Whether a claim is paid is determined by the policy terms and the facts of the case, not simply by whether a staircase complies with Building Regulations.

## Do Building Regulations only apply to new builds?

No.

Building Regulations have existed in various forms for many decades and apply to certain types of building work, not just new houses.

Examples include:

* new dwellings,
* loft conversions,
* many extensions,
* certain structural alterations,
* work requiring Building Control approval.

Many older houses have staircases that were perfectly acceptable when built but would not satisfy today's standards. That does not automatically make them illegal or require them to be replaced simply because standards have changed.

## From what year do they apply?

There isn't a single "start year."

In England and Wales:

* Building Regulations have evolved over many decades.
* The modern regulatory framework stems from the Building Act 1984, with the Building Regulations 2010 consolidating later regulations.
* Guidance such as Approved Document K has been revised multiple times, with standards changing over the years.

When assessing an existing staircase, the relevant question is often:

> *What regulations applied when the work was carried out?*

rather than

> *Does it meet today's standards?*

## For the Staircase Brain (structured reasoning framework)

These questions are exactly the type that should be answered using a structured reasoning approach, for example:

1. What country/jurisdiction?
2. Is this an existing staircase or a new installation?
3. Who designed it?
4. Who manufactured it?
5. Who installed it?
6. What type of building work is involved (new build, loft conversion, renovation, replacement)?
7. Is the question about legal compliance, insurance, liability, or design?

Only after establishing that context should Nex provide guidance. That approach is more reliable than giving a single blanket answer because responsibility and compliance often depend on the specific facts of the project.
