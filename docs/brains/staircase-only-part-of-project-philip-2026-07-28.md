# Remember – Your Staircase Is Only Part of the Project

One of the most common misunderstandings during staircase installations is assuming that every detail surrounding the staircase falls under the responsibility of the staircase company.

In reality, your staircase is only one component within a much larger construction project.

Before manufacture begins, staircase companies are usually provided with site dimensions at a particular stage of construction. However, between manufacture and installation, many things may have changed, including:

* Flooring specifications.
* Wall build-ups.
* Plaster thicknesses.
* Staircase openings.
* Ceiling finishes.
* Decoration allowances.
* Structural alterations.
* Site tolerances between trades.

Even where measurements have been taken professionally, construction sites continue to evolve throughout the building process.

## Your Staircase Company Cannot Control Other Trades

Professional staircase companies take responsibility for manufacturing and installing their product to the highest possible standard. What they cannot control are changes made elsewhere within the build.

For example:

* A wall moved by 10 mm.
* Additional plaster build-up.
* Flooring specifications changing after manufacture.
* Alterations to staircase openings.
* Timber floor levels changing due to floor preparation.

Individually, these may appear insignificant, but when combined they can affect the final installation considerably.

Sometimes, the installation team may arrive on site and immediately identify challenges that simply did not exist when the staircase was manufactured.

Their responsibility then becomes finding the most professional solution available.

## Sometimes There Isn't a Perfect Solution

Homeowners understandably expect perfection when investing in their homes. Professional staircase companies expect the same of themselves.

However, construction occasionally presents situations where there is no perfect solution—only the best available one.

The installer may have several options available:

* The most attractive finish.
* The strongest fixing method.
* The least invasive alteration.
* The quickest installation method.

Occasionally, achieving all four simultaneously is simply impossible.

Professional experience is knowing which solution provides the best overall result while maintaining both structural integrity and appearance.

What may appear to be a compromise is often the product of years of specialist knowledge and careful consideration.

## Ask Your Installer One Important Question

Rather than asking:

> "Why isn't this perfect?"

try asking:

> "If this was your own staircase, how would you install it?"

Professional installers take enormous pride in their work and their reputation. The answer to that question will often provide valuable insight into why particular decisions have been made.

If they explain that this is the method they would use within their own home given the circumstances, it may provide reassurance that the solution presented is both practical and professional.

## Professional Advice Should Never Feel Like Conflict

It is perfectly acceptable to ask questions during installation. In fact, professional companies welcome them.

Good communication protects everyone involved.

If something concerns you:

* Ask for an explanation.
* Ask to see the area causing the problem.
* Request photographs if appropriate.
* Ask what alternative solutions were considered.
* Seek specialist staircase advice if necessary.

These conversations should never become confrontational.

Remember, both parties are usually working towards exactly the same goal—a beautiful staircase finished to the highest possible standard.

## A Final Thought

Construction projects are rarely the result of one trade working in isolation. Every completed staircase represents the combined efforts of designers, manufacturers, builders, plasterers, flooring installers, and specialist installation teams.

Sometimes, the staircase company is solving problems they did not create simply because they are the final trade asked to bring everything together.

Before assuming that an installation issue belongs solely to the staircase company, take the time to understand the wider construction picture.

The very best results are achieved when professional advice is listened to, questions are encouraged, and solutions are found together.
