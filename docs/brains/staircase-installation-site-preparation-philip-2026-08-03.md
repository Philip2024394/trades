# Staircase Installation Site Preparation Guide

## Preparing Your Home for a New Staircase Installation

A new staircase is one of the largest bespoke joinery items installed within a home. Proper site preparation is essential to ensure a smooth installation, minimise delays and allow the installation team to work safely and efficiently.

This guide explains the recommended site conditions before staircase installation begins.

## The Property Should Be Weather-Tight

Before a staircase is delivered, the building should be protected from the weather. Ideally the property should have:

- Roof completed
- External doors installed
- Windows fitted
- Building made watertight

This protects the staircase from moisture, excessive humidity and water damage. Timber staircases should **never** be stored or installed in buildings exposed to rain or persistent damp conditions.

## Internal Walls Should Be Complete

Where possible, surrounding walls should already be constructed:

- Blockwork completed
- Stud walls erected
- Structural openings finished

The staircase installer requires the final structural opening to accurately position and secure the staircase.

## Walls Should Be Plastered

For most domestic installations, plastering should be completed before the staircase is installed.

**Benefits:**

- Cleaner finished appearance
- Easier decoration afterwards
- Reduced risk of damaging the staircase during plastering
- More accurate wall dimensions

Some new-build projects may install staircases before final plaster skim, depending on the builder's programme.

## Walls Should Be Dry

Fresh plaster contains a significant amount of moisture. Before installing a timber staircase:

- Plaster should be fully cured.
- Walls should feel dry.
- Excessive building moisture should be removed through ventilation.

Installing timber into damp conditions may lead to movement, swelling or delayed decoration.

## Flooring Preparation

The installation area should be prepared before the fitting team arrives. Ideally:

- Concrete floors should be complete.
- Timber floor structures should be installed.
- Floor levels confirmed.
- Finished floor height agreed.

Installers work from the **finished floor level (FFL)**, so accurate floor heights are important.

## Finished Floor Coverings

Floor finishes such as carpet · engineered timber · laminate · vinyl · tile are often installed after the staircase.

However, installers need to know the **thickness of the finished flooring** so the staircase can be set at the correct height.

## Access Routes

Safe access should be available. Remove: furniture · building materials · tools · waste · packaging · obstacles.

A clear route allows the staircase to be carried safely into the property.

## Working Space

The installation crew requires sufficient room to manoeuvre large staircase sections. The working area should be free from: plasterboard stacks · timber packs · kitchen units · appliances · scaffolding · stored materials.

Good working space reduces installation time and helps prevent accidental damage.

## Lighting

Adequate lighting is essential. If permanent electrical lighting is unavailable, temporary site lighting should be provided.

Good visibility improves: installation accuracy · safety · finish quality.

## Power Supply

Where practical, a suitable electrical supply should be available for power tools. This allows installers to complete: final trimming · drilling · fixing · adjustments · finishing work.

## Stairwell Opening

Before installation, confirm that:

- floor openings are complete
- stairwell dimensions match approved drawings
- supporting walls are complete
- structural beams are installed

**Any changes to the opening should be communicated before manufacture.**

## Dry Environment

Timber staircases should be installed in a dry internal environment. Avoid installation where: standing water is present · leaks remain · high humidity exists · wet trades are still working heavily.

Excess moisture can affect timber movement and finished joinery.

## Decoration

Painting and decorating are normally completed **after** the staircase installation.

This allows decorators to: fill minor fixing holes · caulk joints · prepare timber · achieve a seamless finish.

Protective coverings should be used if decorating continues after installation.

## Protection After Installation

Once installed, the staircase should be protected until the project is complete.

**Recommended protection:**

- stair protection board
- tread covers
- edge protection
- temporary handrail protection
- dust sheets

This helps prevent scratches, dents, paint splashes and impact damage caused by other trades.

## Site Visit Before Manufacture

Many bespoke staircase companies carry out a site survey before manufacturing begins. During the survey the installer or surveyor typically checks:

- Stairwell dimensions
- Floor-to-floor height
- Finished floor levels
- Wall construction
- Plaster thickness
- Structural openings
- Ceiling heights
- Headroom clearance
- Landing sizes
- Access into the property
- Delivery route
- Installation method
- Existing services that may interfere with the staircase

A final survey helps identify any issues before production, reducing the risk of delays or costly alterations.

## Customer Checklist

Before your staircase installation date, please ensure:

- ✓ Roof, windows and external doors are installed.
- ✓ The building is weather-tight.
- ✓ Walls are built and, where possible, plastered.
- ✓ Plaster has dried sufficiently.
- ✓ Structural floor openings match the approved drawings.
- ✓ Finished floor levels have been confirmed.
- ✓ A clear access route is available for delivery.
- ✓ The installation area is free from stored materials and obstructions.
- ✓ Adequate lighting and power are available.
- ✓ Other trades have completed work that could damage the staircase.
- ✓ The staircase will be protected after installation until the project is finished.

## Nex Knowledge Summary

Nex should advise customers that a staircase installation is most successful when the property is **dry**, **weather-tight** and **structurally complete**. Walls should ideally be plastered and sufficiently dry, finished floor levels confirmed and the installation area kept clear of obstacles. A **pre-installation site survey** is recommended to verify dimensions, access and structural conditions before manufacture, helping to ensure the staircase fits correctly and can be installed safely and efficiently.
