# Fitting Staircases When Walls Are Off Square – One of the Most Common Site Challenges

One of the most common issues encountered during staircase installation is walls that are slightly off square. It is something every staircase manufacturer, installer, block layer, plasterer, site manager, and homeowner will come across at some point during construction.

The first thing to remember is that nothing in construction is ever 100 percent perfect. A construction site involves many different trades, each working to their own standards and tolerances while relying on the workmanship of the previous trade. The goal is not perfection—it is achieving the best possible standard at every stage of the build.

When planning a staircase that will fit tightly against walls, particularly an L-shaped dogleg staircase or quarter-turn staircase, it is extremely important that the corners are as square as possible. Even small discrepancies can have a significant effect on the finished installation.

## Planning Begins Before the Staircase Arrives

Homeowners, project owners, and site managers should discuss staircase locations with the block layer or bricklayer before work begins. If a staircase is being fitted into a corner, everyone involved should understand that the staircase area requires particular care during construction.

Professional block layers and bricklayers understand the requirements for staircase installations and will always aim to produce the best possible results. However, there will always be small tolerances within construction.

At the early stages of a build, walls can easily be adjusted if required. If block walls are found to be dramatically out of square at a height of approximately two metres, rebuilding and correcting the work may only take a few hours. Making adjustments early in the project is almost always quicker, easier, and more cost-effective than attempting to resolve problems once the staircase arrives on site.

> **Installation Tip:** The earlier an issue is identified, the easier and less expensive it will be to correct.

## The Importance of Plastering

After the block work is completed, the plasterers become the next important part of the process.

If walls are slightly off square, plasterers should notify the site manager or homeowner where possible and use their build coat to achieve the best possible corners. In many cases, they can significantly improve wall alignment before decoration begins.

However, plasterers can only work within the limitations of the existing construction. Sometimes the amount of plaster required simply will not allow them to achieve perfectly square corners without causing additional problems elsewhere.

It is also important to understand how small measurements can become larger problems over distance.

A corner showing only 1 cm out of square on a roofing square can become 2 cm, 3 cm, or even 4 cm over several metres of wall length. What appears to be a very small discrepancy during construction can become highly visible once a staircase is installed.

For this reason, achieving the best possible corner alignment should always be the goal.

## When the Problem Reaches the Staircase Company

If the walls remain out of square once plastering has been completed, the issue is ultimately passed to the staircase manufacturer and installer.

By this stage, there are usually only a few options available:

* Add matching cover slips to conceal the discrepancy.
* Rebuild sections of the staircase opening.
* Pack and adjust fixing points during installation.
* Modify particular components where possible.
* Work closely with the customer to agree upon the most acceptable visual solution.

Every staircase company has a responsibility to maintain both their workmanship standards and their reputation. Finding solutions that satisfy the customer while maintaining structural integrity is part of professional installation.

## Quarter Landings and Winders

Quarter landing staircases generally allow greater flexibility when walls are slightly off square.

Winder staircases can present more challenges because the changing angles and tapered treads make discrepancies more noticeable. Even small wall alignment issues may become visible once mouldings, strings, and plaster lines meet one another.

In these situations, cover slips are often the preferred solution. When carefully installed and overlapped down the side of the string, they can provide an excellent finish.

Backing blocks can also be installed within larger gaps, allowing the staircase string to be securely fixed back to the wall using suitable anchor bolts through the string and into the blockwork.

The result is a solid fixing while maintaining a professional appearance.

## Straight Flight Staircases

Straight flight staircases are generally easier to accommodate than L-shaped installations.

However, they present their own challenges.

If the wall directly in front of the staircase is not aligned with the adjoining wall, the top of the staircase may not sit tightly against the trimming joist or staircase well opening.

In most situations this is not considered a major issue and can usually be packed and adjusted during installation.

The greater consideration is often the finished flooring.

Timber flooring, laminate flooring, and other floor finishes installed at first floor level will follow the angle of the top step. Where minor adjustments are possible, the top tread may be trimmed slightly to provide a cleaner alignment between the staircase and the finished floor covering.

Careful planning between the staircase installer and flooring contractor will usually provide the best results.

## Construction Is a Team Effort

Every site owner, foreman, and project manager has an obligation to monitor the quality of the build throughout construction.

While achieving absolute perfection is rarely possible, acceptable construction standards should never adversely affect the trade that follows. Every trade relies upon the previous one to provide the best possible starting point for their own work.

Professional tradespeople will always do their best to minimise discrepancies and provide practical solutions when problems arise. Occasionally, however, some tolerances will remain and must simply be managed appropriately.

The most successful staircase installations are rarely the result of one trade alone—they are the result of block layers, plasterers, flooring installers, staircase manufacturers, and site managers all understanding how their work affects the next stage of construction.
