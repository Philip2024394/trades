# Decorative Staircase Panelling — Panel Layout, Slip Sizes & Material Selection

## Design Philosophy

**There is no universal panel size.**

Professional joiners do not begin by deciding the panel size. Instead, they start with the wall dimensions and work backwards to create balanced, symmetrical panels.

The objective is visual proportion rather than achieving fixed measurements. Every staircase wall should be treated as a bespoke layout.

## Step 1 — Measure the Available Area

Measure only the area that will receive panelling. Record:

- overall wall width
- overall wall height
- stair pitch angle
- skirting height
- architrave positions
- door openings
- window openings
- ceiling height
- newel post positions
- string line position

These measurements define the usable panel area.

## Step 2 — Establish the Panel Margin

Leave a consistent border around the outside of the layout.

Typical border widths: **50 mm · 60 mm · 75 mm · 90 mm · 100 mm**.

The chosen border should remain consistent throughout the project.

## Step 3 — Select the Panel Style

The panel style determines the moulding width and visual proportions.

**Shaker** — slim, square mouldings · clean contemporary appearance.

**Raised & Fielded** — wider mouldings · deeper shadow lines · traditional appearance.

**Beaded** — softer detailing · cottage style.

## Step 4 — Choose the Moulding (Slip)

A "slip" is the decorative timber or MDF moulding fixed to the wall to create the panel frame.

**Common widths available** from builders' merchants, timber merchants, local joinery shops and staircase manufacturers:

- 18 mm × 9 mm
- 22 mm × 9 mm
- 25 mm × 12 mm
- 30 mm × 12 mm
- 32 mm × 15 mm
- 38 mm × 15 mm
- 44 mm × 18 mm
- 50 mm × 18 mm
- 63 mm × 18 mm
- 69 mm × 18 mm

Exact sizes vary by supplier and profile.

## Common Moulding Profiles

- square edge
- chamfered
- ogee
- bolection
- beaded
- torus
- lamb's tongue
- contemporary shadow-gap
- Shaker profile

## Materials

Most painted staircase panelling uses:

- Moisture Resistant MDF mouldings
- Moisture Resistant MDF sheet

Natural timber projects often use: **oak · ash · walnut · tulipwood · pine**.

Veneered projects commonly use: **oak veneered MDF · walnut veneered MDF · ash veneered MDF**.

## Where Materials Are Sourced

Decorative panelling materials are commonly available from:

- local builders' merchants
- timber merchants
- specialist MDF suppliers
- local joinery workshops
- staircase manufacturers
- CNC machining companies
- bespoke cabinet makers

Many joinery workshops can also machine custom moulding profiles to match existing period details.

## MDF Sheet Sizes

**Common sheet sizes:**

- 2440 × 1220 mm (8' × 4')
- 3050 × 1220 mm (10' × 4')
- 3660 × 1220 mm (12' × 4')

**Typical thicknesses:** 6 mm · 9 mm · 12 mm · 15 mm · 18 mm · 25 mm.

For decorative wall panelling, **9 mm, 12 mm and 18 mm** are the most commonly used.

## How Joiners Calculate Panel Sizes

A professional joiner usually works in this order:

1. Measure the wall.
2. Deduct the outer border.
3. Decide how many panels will fit.
4. Deduct the moulding widths and gaps.
5. Divide the remaining space equally.
6. Adjust panel widths slightly until they look visually balanced.

The final dimensions are driven by proportion rather than strict mathematics.

## Example

- Wall width: **3600 mm**
- Outer borders: **75 mm each side**
- Remaining width: 3600 − 150 = **3450 mm**

Assume:

- three panels
- two gaps between panels
- 25 mm moulding/slip width

Calculation:

- 3450 − (2 × 25) = 3400 mm
- 3400 ÷ 3 = 1133 mm

Each panel would be approximately **1133 mm wide**.

The joiner may then adjust the dimensions slightly to achieve better symmetry or alignment with doors, windows or the staircase.

## Under-Stair Triangles

Triangular wall sections are not divided into equal triangles. Instead, the panel layout follows the stair pitch.

**Good practice:**

- maintain the same border widths
- keep moulding widths consistent
- enlarge or reduce panel sizes proportionally
- avoid narrow or awkward triangular panels

## Golden Proportion Guidelines

Although not mandatory, many joiners naturally produce panels close to these ratios:

- 1 : 1.5
- 1 : 1.6
- 1 : 1.8
- 1 : 2

These proportions generally appear more elegant than perfect squares.

## Visual Balance Rule

When two possible layouts are available:

- Choose the one with fewer, larger panels over many small panels.
- Avoid creating panels narrower than approximately **150–200 mm** unless matching an existing design.
- Keep panel borders and moulding widths consistent throughout the installation.

A professional installation prioritises balance, rhythm and proportion over exact equal dimensions.

## Nex Design Rule

Nex should not recommend fixed panel sizes. Instead, it should:

1. Measure the available wall area.
2. Deduct the chosen border.
3. Select a moulding/slip profile and width appropriate to the style.
4. Divide the remaining space into visually balanced panels.
5. Adjust panel dimensions to align with architectural features such as doors, windows, skirting, architraves and the staircase pitch.
6. Recommend commonly available materials from builders' merchants, timber suppliers, local joinery workshops or staircase manufacturers, while noting that exact stock sizes and profiles vary by supplier and region.

This approach reflects how experienced joiners and staircase manufacturers typically design decorative panelling in practice.
