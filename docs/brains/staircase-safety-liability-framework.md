# Staircase Safety & Liability Framework

**Purpose:** Give NEX the vocabulary and workflow to advise staircase makers, installers and homeowners on liability, evidence collection and complaint handling when something goes wrong.

**Not legal advice.** This framework describes industry practice and evidence discipline. NEX must always recommend qualified legal advice for active claims.

---

## The responsibility map

A staircase project touches multiple parties. When an accident or defect occurs, responsibility is not automatic — it depends on who did what.

```
Customer
   ↓ commissions
Architect / Designer          ← design responsibility
   ↓ specifies
Staircase Manufacturer        ← product / manufacturing responsibility
   ↓ builds
Installer                     ← installation responsibility
   ↓ hands over to
Property Owner                ← maintenance responsibility
```

For each party, NEX identifies:
- **What they did** (design / manufacture / install / maintain)
- **When they did it** (before / during / after the accident)
- **What evidence exists** (drawings / photos / records)

## The four-question liability decision

When an incident is reported, NEX walks through:

### 1. What happened?
- Slip
- Trip
- Fall through opening
- Handrail failure
- Structural movement

### 2. When did it happen?
- During construction
- Immediately after installation
- Weeks to months after installation
- Years later

### 3. What changed?
- Nothing — as installed
- Carpet removed / floor finish changed
- Stair or fixings modified
- Damage ignored
- Regular use only

### 4. Who controlled the staircase at the time?

The four-question output routes toward the most likely responsibility:

| Signal | Likely responsibility |
|---|---|
| Defect visible from installation day + no change since | Manufacturer or installer |
| Failure appears after alteration | Whoever altered it |
| Failure appears after known-ignored damage | Property owner (maintenance) |
| Structural failure with no visible cause | Structural engineer + designer + manufacturer investigation |
| Slip caused by finish + finish is as-installed | Finish specification (manufacturer / spec-writer) |
| Slip caused by contamination on tread (water / wax) | Property owner |

This is a triage — NEX suggests where investigation should start, not a legal determination.

## The defect responsibility matrix

Cross-referenceable in `data/staircase-defect-responsibility-matrix.json` (see below). Structured as:

| Symptom | Likely cause | Likely responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Loose handrail | Poor fixing at install | Installer |
| Loose handrail | Wall plug failure over time | Property owner (maintenance) |
| Loose handrail | Wrong bracket spec for load | Designer / specifier |
| Cracked tread | Wrong species / grade | Manufacturer |
| Cracked tread | Impact damage | Property owner |

| Squeaking stairs | Poor wedging at manufacture | Manufacturer |

## Evidence discipline

The single most valuable protection for a staircase business is **documented evidence**. If it isn't documented, it didn't happen (from a claims perspective).

### For every job, create a project file containing:

- Customer name + property address
- Site survey drawings and measurements
- Design approval (customer signature)
- Material specification list with supplier certificates
- CAD drawings signed off
- Installation photos (see next section)
- Completion photos
- Handover pack signed by customer
- Warranty document
- Post-install snagging record
- Any subsequent visits (repair, adjustment, complaint)

### Installation photos — the minimum viable set

**Before:**
- Existing staircase (if replacement)
- Floor levels top and bottom
- Wall condition where fixings will land
- Stair opening

**During:**
- Hidden fixings before they are covered
- Steel supports (floating stairs) before cladding
- Structural connections
- Adhesive applications

**After:**
- Completed staircase (multiple angles)
- All balustrade fixings
- Newel bases
- Handrail brackets
- Under-stair area
- Final finish quality

Time-stamped, geolocated (if possible), backed up. Prove the state at handover.

### Customer sign-off document

At handover the customer confirms:
- ✓ Staircase inspected
- ✓ Appearance approved
- ✓ Installation complete
- ✓ Care instructions received

Signed. Dated. Copy retained.

## Insurance categories

A professional staircase business typically carries:

**Public Liability Insurance**
- Covers injury and property damage claims arising from the business's operations
- Standard cover: £1m minimum, £2-5m typical for trade

**Product Liability Insurance**
- Covers claims arising from products supplied
- Critical for manufacturers and specifiers — separate from public liability
- Standard cover: £1-5m

**Professional Indemnity**
- Covers claims arising from advice, design or specification errors
- Important when providing design services, CAD drawings, or technical recommendations
- Standard cover: £250k-£2m

**Employer's Liability**
- Legally required in the UK if employing anyone (even one apprentice)
- Standard cover: £5m minimum (£10m typical)

NEX advises the maker to check what their current policy actually covers — many small-business policies exclude product liability unless specifically added.

## Timber movement education — pre-emptive protection

Many complaints are about normal timber behaviour. Setting expectation at handover prevents the complaint entirely.

**Normal (not a defect):**
- Small seasonal gaps opening in winter, closing in summer
- Slight colour change over months in strong sunlight
- Very small (hairline) shrinkage cracks in wide solid boards
- Occasional creaks or ticks under heating on/off cycles

**Not normal (defect):**
- Structural cracking
- Dangerous movement of components
- Failed joints
- Gaps opening and staying open across seasons
- Movement that increases over time

Include this distinction in the handover care guide. A customer who understands normal-vs-defect does not file a complaint about a seasonal gap.

## Complaint workflow — the four-stage professional response

### Stage 1 — Receive

- Record customer details, issue, date reported
- Ask for photographs
- Do not admit fault
- Do not dismiss

### Stage 2 — Classify

- Safety issue → prioritise, may need urgent action
- Cosmetic issue → schedule inspection
- Maintenance issue → advise
- Installation defect → return under warranty
- Warranty exclusion → explain kindly

### Stage 3 — Inspect

- Compare original installation photos to current state
- Check for modifications
- Identify probable cause
- Photograph current condition

### Stage 4 — Resolve

- Repair
- Adjustment
- Replacement
- Explanation (if the "defect" is normal behaviour)

**Never argue before investigation.** A professional company separates the customer's concern from the actual cause — validates the concern, investigates the cause, communicates both.

## The "if someone says I fell on your stairs" response

- **Stay calm.** Do not admit fault.
- **Record the complaint** in writing (date, time, injured party, injury described, staircase details).
- **Inspect the staircase** and photograph its current condition immediately.
- **Compare against your installation photos** — is it in the same condition?
- **Contact your insurer** before responding further to the claimant. Insurance policies have notification requirements — silence can void cover.
- **Do not modify the staircase** before the insurer's assessment. Preservation of evidence matters.
- **Refer legal questions to a solicitor.** Do not sign or agree to anything without advice.

NEX repeats: **not legal advice.** These are the professional-practice steps; the specifics of an active claim require a solicitor and the insurer's claims team.

## The NEX safety inspection checklist

Before signing off a completed installation:

**Structural**
- ☑ Staircase fixed securely (push-test every component)
- ☑ No excessive movement under load
- ☑ Strings supported correctly at trimmer
- ☑ Landing connections secure

**Tread**
- ☑ Correct depth (matches design spec)
- ☑ Consistent riser heights (within 5mm across whole flight)
- ☑ No loose boards
- ☑ No cracking

**Riser**
- ☑ Secure fixing
- ☑ No movement
- ☑ Correct alignment

**Handrail**
- ☑ Firm fixing (push and pull test)
- ☑ Comfortable height (900-1000mm above pitch line per Doc K UK domestic)
- ☑ Continuous grip
- ☑ No sharp edges

**Balustrade**
- ☑ Secure newels
- ☑ 100mm sphere cannot pass (UK/US) / 125mm (AU)
- ☑ Strong connections
- ☑ No dangerous gaps

**Finish**
- ☑ No glue squeeze-out
- ☑ No visible pin holes
- ☑ No damage or scratches
- ☑ Consistent sheen

Photograph each checkpoint. Store with the project file.

## The final rule

A professional staircase company does not only build beautiful stairs. It creates a **complete safety record**:

```
Design + Manufacturing + Installation + Inspection + Documentation + Customer Handover
                                                                            ↓
                                                                       Protection
```

If a claim ever comes, the company that documented everything is the company that survives it.
