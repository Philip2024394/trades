# Staircase Panel Design Catalog

The visual pattern library Nex uses when recommending panel styles for a staircase. Every entry below is backed by an authored specimen image in the manifest — Nex can retrieve the reference image + description to show the customer what each option actually looks like.

## How to Read This Catalog

Panel choices break into two independent axes:

1. **Panel STYLE** — the moulding/detail language (raised & fielded, Shaker, T&G, diagonal, slatted, etc.).
2. **Panel LAYOUT** — the way panels divide the wall (three vertical, three horizontal, single large, full continuous sheet).

A given wall design combines one from each axis. E.g. *"Three-panel horizontal raised & fielded"* = style `RAISED_FIELDED` + layout `THREE_HORIZONTAL`.

The eight specimens below cover the most-asked combinations for staircase walls.

## Style 1 — Three-Panel Vertical Raised & Fielded

**Specimen:** manifest URL `ChatGPT Image Jul 26, 2026, 12_28_47 PM.png`.

Three equal vertical raised panels within a solid frame. Deep shadow lines from the fielded moulding. Traditional Georgian/Victorian/Edwardian character. Excellent for staircase side string cladding, panelling that follows the pitch, under-stair storage doors and feature walls.

**Best when:** the wall is TALLER than it is wide · you want traditional heritage character · the staircase itself is oak or painted classical joinery.

## Style 2 — Three-Panel Horizontal Raised & Fielded

**Specimen:** `ChatGPT Image Jul 26, 2026, 12_37_56 PM.png`.

Same fielded moulding language as Style 1, but rotated 90° — three large horizontal panels stacked vertically. Reads calmer and more substantial than narrow verticals. Excellent for LONG or TALL staircase walls where vertical panels would look busy.

**Best when:** the wall is TALL · you want fewer moulding lines · the surrounding architecture already has strong horizontal features (dado, picture rail, window band).

## Style 3 — Three-Panel T&G in Raised Frame (Hybrid)

**Specimen:** `ChatGPT Image Jul 26, 2026, 12_40_08 PM.png`.

Traditional raised-and-fielded frame with vertical **tongue-and-groove boards** inside each panel instead of a plain flat panel centre. The frame provides classical definition; the T&G centres add texture, movement and softer heritage character.

**Best when:** cottage · farmhouse · Scandinavian traditional · country home · a homeowner wants heritage character with more visible texture than a plain fielded panel.

**Why it works:** grooves hide slight timber movement better than large flat panels; vertical boards emphasise ceiling height; less formal than plain raised-and-fielded but still elegant.

## Style 4 — Diagonal T&G in Raised Frame (Hybrid, 45°)

**Specimen:** `ChatGPT Image Jul 26, 2026, 12_43_09 PM.png`.

Three framed raised panels with **45° diagonal T&G boards** inside each. The angled grain introduces movement and direction — critically important — **the diagonal mirrors the rising angle of the staircase**, visually connecting the staircase to the surrounding architecture.

**Best when:** premium residential · you want a bespoke joinery signature · the surrounding hallway is long or flat and needs visual movement · the staircase is the architectural focal point.

**Why it works:** the diagonal grain flows in the same direction as the stair pitch; adds movement and direction that vertical/horizontal panels can't; catches light differently through the day.

## Style 5 — Full Diagonal T&G Sheeting (No Framing)

**Specimen:** `ChatGPT Image Jul 26, 2026, 12_48_22 PM.png`.

Continuous 45° diagonal T&G boards running across the entire wall surface. **No intermediate rails or panel divisions.** Just a clean perimeter frame and unbroken diagonal boarding.

**Best when:** contemporary architectural · you want the diagonal movement of Style 4 but without the visual weight of raised panel frames · narrow hallways that need to feel wider · full-height stairwell feature walls.

**Why it works:** the uninterrupted diagonal pattern naturally follows the angle of the staircase; makes narrow hallways feel wider and taller; feels more contemporary than framed variants while retaining traditional joinery craftsmanship.

## Style 6 — Vertical T&G Sheeting (No Framing)

**Specimen:** `Untitledsdfsdfdff-removebg-preview.png`.

Full-height vertical T&G boards running continuously from floor to ceiling. No raised panels, no intermediate framing. Just crisp V-groove joints and a straight vertical grain pattern.

**Best when:** Scandinavian · Shaker · modern minimalist · coastal · homeowners who want warmth + texture without the visual weight of moulded panels · staircases that should remain the main visual feature (the panelling is a backdrop, not competition).

**Why it works:** vertical boards draw the eye upward making stairwells feel taller · calm balanced backdrop for the staircase itself · easy to continue across adjoining walls for a cohesive design.

## The Decision Matrix

| Homeowner Signal | Recommend Style | Recommend Layout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heritage / Georgian / Victorian / period property | Raised & Fielded | Three Vertical (if wall is average) or Three Horizontal (if wall is tall) |
| Cottage / farmhouse / country / rustic | T&G in Raised Frame (hybrid) | Three panels |
| Bespoke / high-end / architectural feature | Diagonal T&G in Raised Frame (hybrid) | Three panels |
| Contemporary architectural / narrow hallway | Diagonal T&G Full Sheet | Full sheet, no division |
| Scandinavian / Shaker / minimalist / coastal | Vertical T&G Full Sheet | Full sheet, no division |
| Modern painted new build | Shaker | Three vertical or three horizontal |
| Ultra-modern / feature wall / with LED | Slatted timber acoustic | Full sheet, LED-integrated |

## Hidden Under-Stair Storage — Compatible with EVERY Style

All eight styles above can accept flush push-to-open under-stair storage doors within the panel layout. Cupboard doors adopt the same panel style and layout as the surrounding walls so they disappear when closed:

- **Framed styles (1–4, 7)** — cupboard door face mimics one panel · the door edge aligns with a moulding line so the split reads as a joint rather than a break.
- **Full-sheet styles (5, 6)** — cupboard door face uses the same T&G boards · the door edge falls on a V-groove line so it disappears.

## Full Sheeting vs Framed Panels — When to Choose Each

- **Framed panels** provide more visual weight, shadow depth and traditional character. Best for heritage/traditional/period + when the panelling is the primary feature.
- **Full sheeting** provides warmer texture with less visual weight. Best for contemporary/minimalist + when the STAIRCASE should remain the primary feature.

Both are equally valid. Framed panels cost more (more mouldings, more mitres, more setting-out); full sheeting installs faster and reads more contemporary.

## Materials + Finishes (Reference)

Every style above can be executed in any of the panel material variants — see `panel_material.yaml`. Painted MR-MDF is the default for painted schemes; oak/ash/walnut veneered MDF or solid hardwood are the natural-finish options; birch ply is a premium alternative.

Finish options match — clear lacquer · satin varnish · hardwax oil · light/medium/smoked oak stain · walnut stain · white · off-white · cashmere · stone · sage green · olive green · heritage blue · navy · dark green · charcoal · oxblood · black.

## Nex Recommendation Sequence

When a customer asks *"what panelling should I use on my staircase wall?"*, Nex should:

1. **Identify the property style** — heritage / modern / farmhouse / Scandinavian / contemporary.
2. **Measure the wall proportions** — narrow, wide, tall, short.
3. **Match style to property** using the decision matrix above.
4. **Match layout to wall proportion** — tall walls → horizontal panels or vertical T&G; wide walls → three vertical or diagonal; narrow hallways → full-sheet diagonal or vertical.
5. **Confirm hybrid opportunities** — offer T&G-in-raised-frame or diagonal-in-raised-frame when the customer wants heritage character with more texture.
6. **Show the reference specimen** from the manifest so the customer can see exactly what the recommendation looks like.
7. **Flag under-stair storage integration** — every style above supports flush push-to-open doors within the panel layout.
