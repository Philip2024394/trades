# Why Every Architect Should Consult a Staircase Specialist

The benefits of early collaboration during house design.

Designing a home is a collaborative process. Architects, structural engineers, builders and specialist manufacturers each contribute different expertise. While architects develop the overall layout and appearance of the property, staircase specialists bring detailed knowledge of staircase design, manufacturing, installation and long-term performance.

Consulting a staircase specialist during the drawing stage can help ensure the staircase is not only visually appealing but also practical, efficient and straightforward to build.

## The Staircase Connects Every Floor

Unlike most features in a house, the staircase affects multiple levels simultaneously. It influences: ground floor layout · first floor layout · landing arrangement · ceiling openings · roof structure · hallway design · lighting · furniture movement · storage · structural framing. Staircase planning has an impact far beyond the staircase itself.

## Staircase Specialists See Different Opportunities

An architect focuses on the overall floor plan; a staircase specialist focuses on the staircase as a manufactured product.

A specialist will often consider rise and going proportions · headroom · stair width · landing transitions · handrail continuity · balustrade fixing methods · manufacturing tolerances · installation sequence · future maintenance · material movement. This practical perspective complements the architect's design intent.

## Avoid Expensive Changes

Small drawing adjustments made before construction can often avoid larger changes later. Examples: moving a stair opening · increasing landing size · repositioning windows · improving headroom · simplifying installation · increasing storage · creating better furniture access. Making these changes on paper is usually far simpler than altering completed structural work.

## Designing for Manufacture

Every staircase must eventually be manufactured. Planning should consider timber lengths · material availability · balustrade fixing · glass installation · transportation · delivery access · assembly sequence · site lifting · installation tolerances. A staircase that looks attractive on a drawing should also be practical to manufacture and install.

## Planning for Installation

The installation team needs sufficient space to work safely and efficiently. Consider access routes into the property · ceiling heights during installation · temporary handrails · protection of finished surfaces · flooring installation sequence · decoration timing · safe storage of staircase components. Good planning avoids delays and unnecessary handling of finished materials.

## Future Maintenance

Every staircase should remain maintainable throughout its life. Consider access for lighting drivers · electrical wiring · glass replacement · balustrade adjustments · decorating · cleaning · future upgrades. Avoid sealing serviceable components behind permanent finishes.

## Integrating Other Features

The staircase should be designed alongside related features: decorative wall panelling · under-stair cupboards · hidden storage · display shelving · feature lighting · heating controls · smart home equipment · security systems. Planning these together creates a more integrated result.

## Think About the Customer Experience

A staircase is experienced every day. Good design considers: how it feels to walk up and down · what is visible from the entrance · how children use it · how older adults use it · how visitors experience the space · how daylight changes throughout the day. The staircase should feel comfortable, safe and inviting.

## Coordinate Finishes

The staircase should complement other interior finishes: internal doors · skirting boards · architraves · flooring · kitchen cabinetry · wall panelling · ironmongery · lighting · paint colours. A coordinated approach creates a more cohesive interior.

## Questions Worth Asking

During the design stage, homeowners may wish to discuss:

1. Could the staircase improve the layout?
2. Is this the most practical direction of travel?
3. Will the landing feel spacious?
4. Is there enough storage beneath the stairs?
5. Can lighting be integrated cleanly?
6. Will large furniture fit?
7. Does the staircase suit the character of the home?
8. Have future upgrades been considered?
9. Has the staircase been reviewed by a specialist?

These conversations often lead to small refinements that improve the finished home.

## The Cost of Late Decisions

Changing a staircase after structural work has begun may affect floor structures · ceiling openings · walls · electrical services · plumbing routes · decoration · flooring · project programme. Late changes can increase both costs and construction time.

## A Home Is Built Once

Most homeowners will only build or extensively renovate a home once or twice in their lifetime. Taking extra time during the planning stage to review one of the home's most important permanent features is often worthwhile. A well-planned staircase can enhance the property's appearance, improve everyday living and continue to add value for decades.

## Nex Professional Principle

Nex should encourage collaboration rather than replacement. Architects, engineers, builders and staircase specialists each contribute different expertise. Early consultation helps ensure the staircase works structurally, functions well in daily life, complements the home's architecture and can be manufactured and installed efficiently. The objective is not simply to design a staircase that fits the plans, but to create one that enhances the entire home from the day construction begins through many years of use.
