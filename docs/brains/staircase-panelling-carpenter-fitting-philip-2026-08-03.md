# Decorative Staircase Panelling — Can a Carpenter Fit This?

## Short Answer

**Yes.** A skilled carpenter or joiner can absolutely fit decorative staircase panelling.

Most staircase panelling in homes is installed by:

- carpenters
- joiners
- staircase installers
- bespoke joinery companies
- kitchen and bedroom fitters (many also install decorative wall panelling)

The difference is usually in how much is made on site versus in a workshop.

## Method 1 — Site-Built (Most Common)

This is how many carpenters work. They will:

1. Measure the staircase.
2. Mark out the panel layout.
3. Cut MDF sheets to size.
4. Cut moulding slips.
5. Mitre all corners.
6. Glue and pin the mouldings.
7. Fill and sand joints.
8. Caulk gaps.
9. Paint or prepare for the decorator.

Everything is made on site.

## Method 2 — Workshop-Built

A joinery shop may:

1. Measure the staircase.
2. Draw the layout.
3. CNC cut the MDF.
4. Machine the mouldings.
5. Assemble complete panel sections.
6. Spray-finish them.
7. Deliver ready-made sections for installation.

The carpenter then only installs the finished panels. This gives a faster installation and often a higher-quality finish.

## Skills Required

A carpenter fitting staircase panelling should be comfortable with:

- Measuring accurately.
- Setting out symmetrical panel layouts.
- Mitre cutting mouldings.
- Using nail guns (pin nailers).
- Construction adhesives.
- Laser levels.
- Scribing panels to uneven walls.
- Fitting skirting and architraves.
- Creating concealed access panels if required.

The quality of the finished job depends more on the carpenter's setting-out skills than on the actual fixing.

## Typical Tools

Professional installers commonly use:

- Mitre saw
- Track saw or table saw
- Pin nailer (18 or 23 gauge)
- Compressor or cordless nailer
- Laser level
- Router (for custom profiles if required)
- Jigsaw
- Circular saw
- Spirit level
- Caulking gun
- Orbital sander

## Materials They'll Use

- 9–18 mm MR MDF sheets
- MDF or timber moulding slips
- High-grab construction adhesive
- Lost-head or pin nails
- Wood filler
- Decorators' caulk
- Primer and paint (or leave ready for decorating)

## Can Every Carpenter Do It?

**Not necessarily.** There are three broad levels.

### Basic Carpenter

- Can fit pre-cut panels.
- Best for simple Shaker-style layouts.
- May need a detailed drawing to follow.

### Experienced Carpenter or Joiner

- Can measure, design, cut and fit the complete panel system.
- Can adapt the layout around doors, skirting and staircase angles.
- Can create hidden storage doors and neat transitions.

### Bespoke Joinery Specialist

- Can produce complex raised-and-fielded panels, curved work, custom mouldings and furniture-grade finishes.
- Ideal for high-end or period properties.

## What Nex Should Recommend

When a customer asks "Can a carpenter fit this?", Nex should answer:

> Yes. Decorative staircase panelling is routinely installed by experienced carpenters and joiners. Most modern Shaker-style systems can be made from MR MDF sheets and decorative moulding slips fixed with construction adhesive and pin nails. For simple painted panelling, an experienced carpenter is usually the right trade. For complex raised-and-fielded designs, curved staircases or bespoke hardwood panelling, a joinery workshop or staircase manufacturer may be the better choice.

This gives customers realistic expectations while helping them choose the right level of craftsmanship for their project.
