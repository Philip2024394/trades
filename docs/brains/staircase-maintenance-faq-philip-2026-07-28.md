# Staircase Maintenance Knowledge Library

## 1. How Often Should I Maintain My Staircase?

Most homeowners only notice their staircase when something goes wrong. A simple inspection every six months can identify loose fixings, worn finishes, movement, squeaks, or damage before repairs become more extensive. Annual cleaning and inspection should form part of every home's maintenance routine.

---

## 2. Why Does My Staircase Squeak?

Squeaks usually occur when two components move against each other under load. This movement may develop through natural timber movement, wear, loose fixings, seasonal moisture changes, or settlement. The source of the noise should always be identified before attempting repairs.

---

## 3. Why Does My Staircase Feel Loose?

A staircase should feel solid under normal use. If movement develops, it may indicate loose fixings, movement within joints, inadequate support, or changes within the surrounding building structure. Continued use without investigating the cause may allow the problem to worsen.

---

## 4. Looking After Lacquered Timber Staircases

Modern lacquer provides a durable protective finish, but it is not indestructible. Avoid abrasive cleaners and harsh chemicals.

Some furniture sprays leave an attractive shine but contain solvents, waxes or silicones that may gradually soften or break down lacquer finishes over time. Always use products recommended for lacquered timber surfaces.

---

## 5. Looking After Varnished Staircases

Traditional varnish provides good protection but can wear in high traffic areas.

As with lacquer, avoid furniture polishes that promise instant shine without confirming they are compatible with varnished timber. Some products may leave residues that affect the finish or complicate future refinishing.

---

## 6. Caring for Painted Staircases

Painted staircases should be cleaned using mild cleaning solutions and soft cloths.

Avoid impact damage from heavy objects, as chipped paint exposes the timber beneath. Small chips are easier to repair than widespread paint failure.

---

## 7. Protecting Staircases During Decorating

Always cover staircases before decorating.

Paint splashes, plaster dust and dropped tools can permanently damage finished timber.

Use breathable protective coverings and avoid aggressive adhesive tapes directly on finished surfaces.

---

## 8. Seasonal Timber Movement

Timber naturally expands during humid weather and contracts during drier periods.

Small seasonal changes are normal and should not automatically be considered a defect. Large gaps, excessive movement or structural changes should be investigated.

---

## 9. Can I Steam Clean My Staircase?

Steam cleaning is generally unsuitable for most finished timber staircases.

Heat and moisture may affect finishes and encourage moisture penetration into joints. Follow the manufacturer's maintenance recommendations.

---

## 10. Preventing Wear on Stair Treads

The front edge of each tread receives the greatest amount of foot traffic.

Keeping shoes clean, removing grit regularly and repairing worn finishes early helps preserve both appearance and service life.

---

## 11. Can Sunlight Damage My Staircase?

Direct sunlight may gradually alter the colour of natural timber.

Some species darken while others lighten over time. Rugs, furniture and stair runners may leave colour differences where sunlight reaches exposed timber unevenly.

---

## 12. Looking After Stair Handrails

Handrails are touched every day.

Body oils, dirt and cleaning chemicals gradually affect finishes.

Regular gentle cleaning helps preserve both appearance and hygiene.

---

## 13. Looking After Balustrades and Spindles

Check balusters regularly for looseness or movement.

Even slight movement should be investigated, particularly where children regularly use the staircase.

---

## 14. Protecting Your Staircase During House Moves

Large furniture is one of the most common causes of staircase damage.

Use protective blankets where practical and avoid dragging heavy items against strings, newel posts and handrails.

---

## 15. When Should a Staircase Be Refinished?

Refinishing should normally take place before the protective coating has worn completely through.

Once bare timber becomes exposed, staining, moisture penetration and deeper wear become more likely.

---

## 16. Can Pets Damage Timber Staircases?

Pet claws may gradually scratch finished timber surfaces.

Keeping claws trimmed and cleaning grit regularly helps reduce unnecessary wear.

---

## 17. Moisture Around Staircases

Water should never be allowed to remain on timber surfaces.

Leaks from bathrooms, windows or roofs should be repaired promptly to minimise the risk of swelling, staining or movement.

---

## 18. Should I Wax My Staircase?

Not every staircase finish is suitable for wax.

Applying wax over modern lacquer or varnish systems may create maintenance problems and could affect future refinishing. Always identify the existing finish before applying additional products.

---

## 19. How Long Should a Timber Staircase Last?

A well-designed, correctly manufactured and properly maintained timber staircase can provide many decades of service.

Regular maintenance is often more important than expensive repair work later.

---

## 20. Signs Your Staircase Needs Professional Attention

Seek advice if you notice:

• Excessive springing or bounce.
• Significant structural movement.
• Loose newel posts.
• Loose handrails.
• Cracks developing in structural members.
• Persistent squeaking that becomes worse.
• Visible separation of joints.
• Water damage.
• Signs of decay or insect attack.
• Any issue affecting the safe use of the staircase.

Early investigation is usually less costly than major repairs after damage has progressed.
