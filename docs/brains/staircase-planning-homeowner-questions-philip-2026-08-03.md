# Designing the Perfect Staircase — Questions Every Homeowner Should Ask Before Finalising Their House Plans

The staircase is one of the few features that every member of the household will use every day. It connects every floor, influences movement throughout the home and becomes a permanent architectural feature that cannot easily be changed.

Before approving your architect's drawings, take time to discuss the staircase in detail. Asking the right questions early can prevent costly alterations later and help create a home that is both practical and beautiful.

## Is This the Best Position for the Staircase?

The first question should never be:

> "Will a staircase fit here?"

Instead ask:

> "Is this the best location for the staircase?"

Sometimes moving the staircase only a small distance on the plans can improve room layouts · increase usable floor space · create larger hallways · improve natural light · increase under-stair storage · create better furniture access · improve the appearance of the entrance hall.

## Does the Staircase Feel Welcoming?

Imagine opening the front door.

Ask yourself:

- Does the staircase create a welcoming entrance?
- Does it feel spacious?
- Is it bright?
- Is it one of the home's best features?
- Does it reflect the style of the property?

The staircase often creates a lasting first impression.

## Can More Natural Light Reach the Staircase?

Natural light makes staircases feel safer and more inviting. Discuss whether windows could be repositioned · a rooflight could be added · a stairwell window would improve the space · glass balustrades would allow more light through · internal borrowed light could be increased. A brighter staircase often makes the whole house feel larger.

## Is the Staircase the Correct Width?

A staircase that meets minimum requirements may not always provide the best day-to-day experience.

Consider carrying furniture · moving mattresses · children passing each other · everyday comfort · future accessibility. Where space allows, a slightly wider staircase can improve usability and the overall sense of quality.

## Will Furniture Fit Upstairs?

Before construction begins, think about moving beds · wardrobes · sofas · desks · washing machines · large televisions. The staircase should allow these items to be moved without unnecessary difficulty.

## Is There Enough Headroom?

The staircase should feel open and comfortable. Consider landing headroom · sloping ceilings · roof trusses · dormer windows · ceiling bulkheads. Adequate headroom contributes to both comfort and safety.

## Could the Staircase Become a Feature?

Rather than hiding the staircase, ask whether it could become a design highlight: oak feature treads · glass balustrades · decorative wall panelling · LED lighting · slatted timber walls · feature pendant lighting · statement newel posts. Treating the staircase as a feature can add character to the home.

## How Will the Staircase Look From Upstairs?

Many people focus only on the view from the hallway. Also consider looking down from the landing · views into the stairwell · connections between floors · lighting from above. The staircase should look attractive from every angle.

## Have We Planned Enough Storage?

The area beneath the staircase offers valuable storage opportunities: coat cupboards · utility storage · cleaning equipment · pantry space · wine storage · shoe storage · hidden cupboards · home office space. Planning these spaces early usually leads to better results.

## Can Future Features Be Added Easily?

Even if you don't want additional features now, ask whether the staircase can accommodate them later. Examples: decorative panelling · stair lighting · smart home controls · charging points · security equipment · built-in shelving. Simple preparation during construction can make future upgrades much easier.

## Has the Staircase Been Coordinated With Other Trades?

Ensure coordination with structural engineer · electrician · plumber · heating installer · flooring contractor · joiner · kitchen designer · interior designer. Helps prevent conflicts during construction.

## Is the Staircase Suitable for Long-Term Living?

Think beyond the day you move in. Ask:

- Will this staircase still work in twenty years?
- Is it comfortable to use every day?
- Will it suit changing family needs?
- Can it be maintained easily?
- Does it provide good lighting?
- Is the handrail comfortable?
- Is the landing spacious enough?

A staircase is a long-term investment.

## Have We Spoken to a Staircase Specialist?

Architects create excellent overall building layouts, but staircase specialists focus on stair geometry · manufacturing · materials · installation · detailing · practical use · future maintenance. Reviewing the staircase with a specialist before finalising the plans can identify opportunities for refinement and help ensure the design is practical to build.

## Final Planning Checklist

Before construction begins, confirm:

- ✓ Staircase position is agreed.
- ✓ Direction of travel is correct.
- ✓ Natural light has been considered.
- ✓ Furniture access has been checked.
- ✓ Under-stair space has a planned use.
- ✓ Lighting routes have been planned.
- ✓ Future conduit and wiring have been considered.
- ✓ The staircase complements the kitchen and the overall home design.
- ✓ Structural requirements have been coordinated.
- ✓ A staircase specialist has reviewed the drawings where appropriate.

## Nex Design Philosophy

Nex should encourage homeowners to think of the staircase as a key architectural investment rather than a standard building element. The staircase should be planned with the same care as the kitchen, because both define how the home is experienced every day. By asking thoughtful questions during the drawing stage and involving the right professionals early, homeowners can create staircases that improve circulation, maximise natural light, support future upgrades and become lasting architectural features that add beauty and value to the property.
