# I Don't Want a Newel Post Running to the Floor on My Free-Standing Staircase

On some free-standing staircases, a full-height newel post extending to the floor may not suit the desired appearance. Where the structural design allows, alternative support methods may be available.

One possible solution is to use concealed structural steel, such as square hollow section (SHS) steel, positioned behind the staircase and extending beneath the landing. The steel can be securely fixed to the floor structure and the wall, with the upper section concealed within the landing floor construction.

In many designs, the vertical steel support can be hidden behind the staircase or enclosed using timber panelling, plasterboard, or other suitable finishes, while the landing structure conceals the horizontal section. When completed, the structural support may be largely or completely hidden from view.

Whether this approach is suitable depends on the staircase design, the building structure, and the loads that the support must carry. Structural calculations and connection details should be determined by the staircase manufacturer or an appropriately qualified structural engineer.

**Expert Tip:** If you prefer the appearance of a free-standing staircase without a floor-mounted newel post, discuss this with your staircase manufacturer during the design stage. Concealed structural supports are often easier and more cost-effective to incorporate before manufacture than to add after installation.
