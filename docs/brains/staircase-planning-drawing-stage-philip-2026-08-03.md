# Planning Your Staircase — Why the Staircase Should Be Designed at the Drawing Stage

When planning a new home, extension or major renovation, the staircase should never be treated as an afterthought. Along with the kitchen, the staircase is one of the largest and most important permanent features in a home. It influences how the house functions, how people move through the space and how the interior feels.

Early collaboration between the homeowner, architect, staircase designer and builder helps ensure the staircase is positioned correctly and designed to complement the entire property.

## The Staircase Is More Than a Way to Reach the Next Floor

A staircase is often one of the first features people notice when entering a home. It acts as a central architectural element, connecting different levels while contributing to the overall character and flow of the building.

A well-designed staircase should:

- Complement the style of the home.
- Create a welcoming first impression.
- Maximise available space.
- Provide safe and comfortable access.
- Allow natural light to flow through the building.
- Integrate with the surrounding rooms and circulation spaces.

## The Staircase and Kitchen Are the Two Largest Built-In Features

In most homes, the staircase and the kitchen are the two biggest bespoke installations. Unlike furniture, they are permanent features that are difficult and expensive to relocate once construction is complete.

Both should be considered together during the design stage because they have a major impact on daily living · property value · interior appearance · storage opportunities · lighting design · future flexibility.

## Work With Your Architect Early

The architect will develop the layout of your home, but valuable input from a staircase specialist during the design stage can help refine the staircase before drawings are finalised.

Early discussions may cover: staircase location · stair width · direction of travel · landing positions · ceiling openings · headroom · structural support · feature walls · under-stair storage · lighting · balustrade style · future accessibility.

Resolving these details early is usually easier than making changes after structural work has begun.

## Choosing the Best Staircase Location

Important considerations: entrance hallway layout · relationship to kitchen and living spaces · access to upstairs rooms · circulation routes · privacy · natural light · views through the home · furniture movement · emergency escape routes.

A staircase should feel like a natural part of the home's layout rather than something fitted into the remaining space.

## Staircase Direction

The direction the staircase turns can influence room layouts · landing positions · window placement · furniture access · under-stair storage · hallway circulation · visual impact. A staircase turning towards a feature window can create a dramatic focal point; a different orientation may free up valuable storage space below.

## Think About Natural Light

The staircase often connects multiple floors and can help distribute natural light through the home. Consider roof windows · skylights · stairwell glazing · side windows · feature glazing · open balustrades · glass panels. A bright stairwell can make the entire property feel larger and more welcoming.

## Plan for Future Features

Many homeowners add features after moving in. Planning ahead allows easier installation of decorative wall panelling · under-stair storage · wine storage · display shelving · hidden cupboards · staircase lighting · smart home wiring · security systems · audio systems · charging points.

Including conduit, service voids or spare cable routes during construction is far easier than retrofitting them later.

## Consider the View

Think about what you see: looking up the staircase · looking down the staircase · from the entrance door · from the landing · from the living room · from upstairs. A staircase often becomes a visual centrepiece, so these sightlines deserve careful consideration.

## Match the Staircase to the Home

Contemporary floating staircases for modern homes · oak staircases for traditional family homes · painted staircases for period properties · glass balustrades for minimalist interiors · heritage designs for Georgian or Victorian homes. The design should feel consistent with the rest of the house.

## Think Beyond Today's Needs

Consider: carrying furniture upstairs · children's safety · ease of cleaning · future maintenance · ageing in place · space for future upgrades. Planning for long-term usability can increase both comfort and value.

## Coordinate With Other Trades

Coordinate with architect · structural engineer · staircase manufacturer · builder · electrician · joiner · plasterer · flooring installer · kitchen designer · interior designer. Good communication between trades helps avoid delays and costly alterations.

## Questions to Ask During the Drawing Stage

Before approving the plans:

1. Is this the best location for the staircase?
2. Could the staircase improve the entrance to the home?
3. Does the direction of travel make sense?
4. Is there enough headroom?
5. Is the staircase wide enough?
6. Can natural light reach the stairwell?
7. Will furniture fit up the stairs?
8. Can lighting be integrated?
9. Is there space for under-stair storage?
10. Does the staircase complement the kitchen and overall interior design?
11. Have future upgrades been considered?
12. Has a staircase specialist reviewed the design?

## Common Planning Mistakes

- Leaving staircase decisions until construction has started.
- Choosing the staircase purely to save space.
- Ignoring natural light.
- Not consulting a staircase specialist.
- Forgetting under-stair storage opportunities.
- Overlooking future lighting or panelling.
- Designing narrow landings that restrict movement.
- Failing to coordinate with flooring and ceiling layouts.

## Professional Recommendation

The best staircase projects begin with collaboration. An architect brings expertise in the overall building design, while a staircase specialist understands proportions, structural requirements, manufacturing, installation and practical everyday use.

By involving both at the drawing stage, you can ensure the staircase is not only compliant and functional but also becomes a defining architectural feature that enhances the entire home.
