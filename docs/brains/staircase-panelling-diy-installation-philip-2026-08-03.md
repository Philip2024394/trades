# DIY Decorative Staircase Panelling — Professional Installation Process

## Core Principle

Decorative panelling is not difficult because of the woodworking. **It is difficult because of the planning.**

Professional joiners often spend more time measuring and setting out than actually fixing the mouldings. A perfectly planned layout usually produces a professional installation.

## Is This a DIY Project?

**Yes.** Modern staircase panelling is one of the most achievable carpentry DIY projects.

Most homeowners can install: Shaker panelling · Contemporary panelling · square mouldings · under-stair panelling · hallway panelling — without specialist woodworking skills.

## What Makes It DIY-Friendly?

The materials are forgiving. MDF is:

- flat
- stable
- easy to cut
- easy to sand
- easy to paint
- inexpensive
- readily available

Small mistakes can usually be filled and painted.

## DIY Difficulty Guide

### ⭐ Easy (Beginner DIY)

Simple square wall panelling · one flat wall · no staircase angle · pre-cut mouldings.

**Typical project time:** 1–2 days.

### ⭐⭐ Moderate (Confident DIY)

Straight staircase · under-stair triangle · Shaker panelling · painted finish.

Requires: accurate measuring · mitre cutting · filling and caulking.

**Typical project:** 2–4 days.

This is the level most homeowners successfully complete.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Advanced

Hidden storage doors · LED lighting · multiple walls · landing panelling.

Requires: carpentry experience · careful setting out.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Professional

Curved staircases · raised-and-fielded panels · hardwood joinery · steam-bent mouldings.

Usually completed by a joiner or staircase specialist.

## DIY Skill Level

A homeowner should be able to:

- measure accurately
- use a tape measure
- use a spirit level
- cut timber
- use adhesive
- use a drill
- use a nail gun (optional)

No advanced joinery techniques are normally required.

## Tools You'll Need

You don't need a full workshop. A typical DIY setup includes:

- Tape measure
- Pencil
- Spirit level or laser level
- Mitre saw (manual or powered)
- Jigsaw or circular saw
- Pin nailer (helpful but not essential)
- Caulking gun
- Orbital sander
- Drill/driver
- Clamps

## Materials

Most DIY staircase panelling uses:

- 9 mm or 12 mm MR MDF
- MDF moulding slips
- Construction adhesive
- Decorators' caulk
- Wood filler
- Primer and paint

Everything is commonly available from builders' merchants and DIY stores.

## Most Important Skill

The most valuable skill is **NOT cutting**. It is learning how to "set out" the wall.

Professional carpenters call this **Setting Out**. Everything depends upon it.

## What Is Setting Out?

Setting out means designing the panel layout before cutting anything.

The installer should determine:

- borders
- panel positions
- panel widths
- panel heights
- moulding locations
- skirting positions
- architraves
- sockets
- switches

**Nothing should be cut until the entire design has been marked on the wall.**

## Professional Method

Professional carpenters rarely start cutting immediately. Instead they:

Measure → Draw → Adjust → Stand back → Look again → Adjust again → Only then begin cutting.

## Why Professional Panels Look Better

Professionals rarely use mathematics alone. Instead they use **visual balance**.

If one panel looks slightly narrow, they adjust all neighbouring panels. The eye notices poor proportions more than small dimensional differences.

## Mock-Up Method

Many joiners temporarily stick masking tape onto the wall. The tape represents the mouldings.

This allows the customer to approve the design before anything is cut.

**Benefits:** zero waste · instant visualisation · easy adjustments.

## Card Template Method

Some installers make cardboard templates.

**Advantages:** quick checking · easy marking · useful around awkward stair angles · perfect for repeated panel sizes.

## Laser Method

Modern installers often use a **laser level** to project horizontal lines, vertical lines and reference lines. This speeds up layout dramatically.

## Marking Out

Professional marking usually uses: pencil · spirit level · laser · combination square · tape measure.

Never rely solely on measurements. Always visually inspect.

## Cutting MDF

Use: track saw · table saw · circular saw · fine-tooth blade.

Support both sides of the sheet. Prevent breakout.

## Cutting Mouldings

Always cut on a mitre saw. Typical mitres: **45°** for internal corners and external corners. Always dry-fit first.

## Dry Fitting

Before glue · before pins · before paint — professionals often dry-fit every moulding. Adjustments are much easier before fixing.

## Adhesive

Construction adhesive carries most of the load. Pins simply hold the moulding while the adhesive cures. The adhesive is the permanent fixing.

## Pin Nailer

A pin nailer does **not** provide structural strength. It simply prevents movement.

**Typical pin sizes:** 23 gauge · 18 gauge. Headless pins leave almost invisible holes.

## Filling

Professional finishers fill: mitres · pin holes · minor gaps — before sanding.

## Caulking

Caulk is applied where mouldings meet walls · ceilings · skirting · architraves.

It hides tiny movement and creates crisp shadow lines.

## Painting

Most luxury projects use: spray primer · spray undercoat · spray topcoat.

Brushes are generally reserved for small touch-ups.

## Why Shaker Is Popular

Shaker panelling is forgiving. Flat mouldings · simple mitres · minimal profiles · easy painting. Excellent for beginners.

## Why Raised & Fielded Is Harder

Requires large mouldings · deep profiles · complex mitres · sometimes router work. Often better suited to professional joiners.

## DIY Time Estimate

- Simple hallway: **1 day**
- Straight staircase: **2–3 days**
- Under stair: **1–2 days**
- Landing: **1 day**
- Painting: **1–2 days**

**Total:** approximately one weekend for a confident DIYer.

## Cost Savings

DIY staircase panelling typically costs:

- **Materials** — £150–£500
- **Professional installation** — £800–£2,500+
- **Luxury bespoke work** — £3,000–£10,000+

DIY can therefore reduce the overall cost substantially, provided the homeowner is confident with measuring and finishing.

## When to Hire a Professional

Recommend a carpenter or joiner if:

- The staircase is curved.
- Existing walls are significantly uneven.
- Hidden storage is being built.
- Hardwood panelling is required.
- The project includes bespoke raised-and-fielded panels.
- The customer wants a factory-quality sprayed finish.

## Common DIY Mistakes

Most problems come from planning rather than construction:

- Starting without drawing the layout.
- Unequal panel sizes.
- Very narrow end panels.
- Inconsistent border widths.
- Poor mitre cuts.
- Forgetting to account for skirting boards or architraves.
- Rushing the caulking and painting.

## A Good DIY Rule

Professional joiners often spend **30–40% of the project time measuring and marking out**, and 60–70% installing and finishing.

If you take your time with the layout, the fitting becomes much easier.

## Nex DIY Advice

When asked "Can I do this myself?", Nex should respond:

> Modern staircase panelling is one of the most accessible architectural joinery projects for a capable DIYer. The materials are widely available, the construction techniques are straightforward, and most of the work involves careful measuring, setting out, cutting mouldings, fixing them with adhesive and pins, then filling, caulking and painting. The quality of the finished result depends far more on the planning and panel layout than on advanced woodworking skills. Taking time to design balanced panel proportions before making any cuts is the single most important step toward achieving a professional-looking finish.

This gives Nex practical, experience-based guidance rather than simply stating that the project is "easy" or "hard."
