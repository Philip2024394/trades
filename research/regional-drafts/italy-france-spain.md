## Executive Summary

Across the three countries, the anchor codes are **UNI 10804 / UNI 10803** and Italian building regulations (Italy), **NF DTU 36.3** with **NF P01-012** for guardrails (France), and the **CTE DB-SUA** (Spain). All three languages have far richer, more differentiated stair vocabulary than English — and machine translation systematically merges concepts that local industry keeps separate.

**Key findings:**
- **Italy** rigorously distinguishes `scala a chiocciola` (spiral around a central column) from `scala elicoidale` (helical with an open well, no column). English uses "spiral" for both — merging them in a sales taxonomy is a mistake.
- **France** distinguishes `limon` (solid stringer, top edge straight) from `crémaillère` (cut/notched stringer, top edge stepped). This is exactly the same distinction UK trade calls "closed string" vs "cut string / open string" — and `crémaillère` is often called `escalier à l'anglaise`.
- **Spain** treats `meseta`, `descansillo` and `rellano` as full synonyms for landing. CTE uses `meseta`.
- **Balustrade vocabulary triples in all three languages**: IT `ringhiera` / `parapetto` / `balaustra`; FR `garde-corps` / `rampe` / `balustrade` (code term is `garde-corps`, the NF P01-012 anchor); ES `barandilla` / `pasamanos` / `balaustrada` — with `pasamanos` doubling as the strict word for handrail.

**Notable false friends:**
- IT `scala a giorno` ≠ FR `escalier à giorno`. Italian `a giorno` = open-riser. French uses `escalier à jour` / `escalier à claire-voie`; `escalier à l'italienne` = **different** (a straight flight between two walls).
- FR `escalier à l'anglaise` = `escalier à crémaillère` = cut-stringer stair.
- ES `escalera helicoidal` in Spain = open-well helix (no column); in Latin American Spanish often used for the central-column type. For international taxonomy: ES `caracol` = column, ES `helicoidal` = open well.

## Spiral / Helical / Winder — canonical distinctions

| Concept | Italian | French | Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central-column spiral | scala a chiocciola | escalier en colimaçon (popular) / escalier hélicoïdal (professional; FR often merges) | escalera de caracol |
| Open-well helix | scala elicoidale | escalier hélicoïdal (often used for both — verify with product photo) | escalera helicoidal (Spain; ambiguous in LatAm) |
| Winder | scala a piè d'oca / con gradini compensati | escalier balancé / quart tournant balancé | escalera compensada / con peldaños compensados |

**Catches:** French merges spiral/helical linguistically — cannot infer column-vs-no-column from label alone. Italian keeps the distinction rigidly. Spanish keeps distinction in Spain but not always in LatAm.

## Ambiguity Flags (10)

1. **FR escalier autoportant** — industry warns term is misused; treat as marketing synonym of flottant/suspendu.
2. **IT scala autoportante** — same problem ("forzatura commerciale").
3. **ES pasamanos** — strict CTE = handrail; colloquially can mean whole assembly.
4. **FR rampe** — dangerous polysemy: (a) ramp/slope, (b) balustrade+handrail in stair context.
5. **IT ringhiera vs balaustra vs parapetto** — commercial vs code registers.
6. **NF P01-012:2026** — transitioning umbrella term from `garde-corps` to `EDP`. Existing pages still say `garde-corps`.
7. **ES meseta vs descansillo vs rellano** — RAE lists as synonyms; regional preference varies.
8. **IT alzata** — technically riser face; colloquially can mean whole step-height dimension.
9. **ES escalera japonesa** — no code definition; commercial term for alternating-tread.
10. **IT scala a incasso** — commercial only, no code definition.
