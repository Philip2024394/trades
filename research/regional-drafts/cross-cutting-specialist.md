## Executive Summary

**Definitively resolved:**
- **Spiral vs Helical** — Codes and industry converge: spiral = central column (winders radiating from a common centre with a load-bearing column at that centre); helical = around an open well/void with no central column. Confirmed by BS 5395-2, IBC 1011.10, Approved Doc K, NCC, DIN 18065 (Spindeltreppe as spiral subtype of Wendeltreppe), Italian/Spanish/French usage.
- **Winder** — Individual tapered/wedge-shaped tread that turns direction. NOT synonymous with spiral or helical.
- **Curved** — Continuous smooth radius (large diameter), not a helix pattern. Distinct.
- **Open string = Cut string** — Two names for the same UK concept.
- **Floating ≠ Cantilever** — Floating is a visual/appearance category; cantilever is one structural way to achieve it. Mono-stringer floating stairs are floating but not cantilevered.
- **Balustrade (UK/AU/IE) vs Guardrail/Railing (US/CA)** — regional labels, same whole-assembly concept.

**Remains ambiguous:**
- Casual and marketing usage of "spiral" for helical stairs is widespread even in DE/IT/ES/FR technical literature.
- "Grand" vs "Monumental" — no code definitions; used interchangeably.
- "String" vs "stringer" — no substantive semantic difference; UK/IE prefer "string", US/CA/AU/NZ prefer "stringer".
- Open-riser + cantilever combinations require jurisdiction-specific review for commercial egress in the US.

## Section 2 · Spiral / Helical / Winder / Curved International Lookup

**Anchor definitions:**
- **Spiral** = winders radiating from a common centre WITH a central column (IBC 1011.10; NCC; Approved Doc K; BS 5395-2)
- **Helical** = winds around an open central void (no column) (Approved Doc K; BS 5395-2; NCC "tapered treads without a centre post")
- **Winder** = individual tapered/wedge tread used in an otherwise straight stair to turn direction (Approved Doc K; NCC 11.2)
- **Curved** = continuous smooth-radius plan, not a helical wrap

| Concept | Central column? | Open well? | Wedge treads? | Continuous radius? | UK | US | DE | IT | FR | ES | NL | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spiral (central column) | YES | NO | YES | N/A | Spiral stair | Spiral stairway | Spindeltreppe | Scala a chiocciola | Escalier en colimaçon | Escalera de caracol | Spiltrap | HIGH |
| Helical (open well) | NO | YES | YES | N/A | Helical stair | Curved / helical stair | Wendeltreppe (ohne Spindel) / Bogentreppe | Scala elicoidale | Escalier hélicoïdal | Escalera helicoidal / escalera de ojo | Wenteltrap | HIGH (technical); MEDIUM (colloquial) |
| Winder (single turn) | N/A | N/A | YES (1-3) | NO | Winder / kite winder | Winder | Wendelstufen | Gradino a ventaglio | Marche balancée | Peldaño compensado | Wenteltreden | HIGH |
| Curved (smooth radius) | N/A | N/A | Sometimes | YES | Curved stair | Curved stair | Bogentreppe | Scala curva | Escalier courbe | Escalera curva | Gebogen trap | MEDIUM |
| Balanced (evenly-tapered turn) | N/A | N/A | YES | N/A | Balanced / continuous winder | Balanced stair | Ausgeglichene Wendelung | Scala compensata | Escalier balancé | Escalera compensada | Gebalanceerde trap | HIGH |

**Ambiguity flags:**
- German everyday: **Wendeltreppe** covers both spiral and helical; technical usage reserves **Spindeltreppe** for column type.
- French: **colimaçon** ≈ everyday "spiral"; **hélicoïdal** = professional term for either but strictly = with central pivot in trade catalogues.
- Italian: **scala a chiocciola** vs **scala elicoidale** rigidly distinguished professionally.
- Spanish: **caracol** vs **helicoidal** rigidly distinguished in Spain; often merged in Latin America.

**Concept-level canonical:**
- **Whole-assembly barrier** = balustrade (UK/AU/IE/NL/DE-informal) OR guardrail/guard (US/CA) OR garde-corps (FR) OR barandilla (ES) OR ringhiera/parapetto (IT) OR Geländer (DE-formal)
- **Vertical component** = baluster (formal EN) OR spindle (informal UK) OR picket (US) OR balustre/barreau (FR) OR balaustro (IT) OR balaustre/barrote (ES) OR Stab (DE) OR spijl (NL)
- **Top rail (graspable)** = handrail (EN) OR main courante (FR) OR corrimano (IT) OR pasamanos (ES) OR Handlauf (DE) OR leuning (NL)

**Ambiguity flag:** In US, "handrail" and "guardrail" are DISTINCT code concepts (IBC 1014 vs 1015). Guardrail prevents falls; handrail is graspable. In UK, "handrail" often serves both meanings colloquially because "balustrade" covers fall-prevention separately.

## Section 5 · Compatibility Matrix (JSON-friendly rules)

**Status classes:** `common` · `conditional` · `specialist_review` · `rare`

### Material × Geometry
- timber × straight: common
- timber × winder: common
- timber × spiral: common
- timber × helical: conditional (large timber helical stairs need engineered laminated members)
- timber × curved: conditional
- concrete × straight: common
- concrete × spiral: common
- concrete × helical: common (widely built as in-situ RC)
- concrete × curved: common
- steel × spiral: common
- steel × helical: common
- glass × straight: common
- glass × spiral: specialist_review (glass treads on spiral central column feasible but rare and bespoke)
- glass × helical: specialist_review
- stone × spiral: common (historic Wendeltreppe idiom)
- stone × cantilever: common (historic geometrical stone stair)

### Material × Structural
- timber × cantilever: specialist_review (requires concealed steel armature; historic 17th-C hollow timber examples exist but engineered steel today — IStructE)
- timber × mono_stringer: conditional (usually steel spine with timber treads)
- timber × two_string: common
- steel × cantilever: common
- steel × mono_stringer: common
- concrete × cantilever: common (each tread anchored into RC wall)
- concrete × mono_stringer: conditional
- glass × cantilever: specialist_review (glass treads on concealed steel cantilever arms is common; glass itself as cantilever element is bespoke)
- stone × cantilever: common (classical geometrical stair)

### Riser × Structural
- open × cantilever: common (open riser is visual default for cantilever)
- open × mono_stringer: common
- open × two_string: conditional — code flag: US commercial IBC 1011.5.5.3 requires solid risers on required-egress unless 4-inch-sphere rule met; UK Approved Doc K limits 100mm gap
- closed × two_string: common
- closed × cantilever: conditional (closed riser reduces floating aesthetic)

### Balustrade × Handrail
- glass_laminated_toughened × none: conditional — code flag: BS 6180:2011 §8.5.2 permits no handrail if laminated toughened; toughened-monolithic requires handrail
- glass_toughened_only × required: common
- glass × timber: common
- glass × metal_top_cap: common
- timber_spindles × timber: common
- metal_spindles × timber: common
- cable_horizontal × metal: conditional — code flag: DIN 18065 prohibits horizontal climbable elements in residential; cables ≤5mm permitted with tension

### Geometry × Application
- spiral × primary_access: conditional — code flag: UK Approved Doc K allows spiral as main private stair per BS 5395-2 Cat A/B/C; US IBC 1011.10 restricts spiral to certain occupancies with 60-inch min diameter
- spiral × secondary_access: common
- alternating_tread × primary_access: specialist_review — code flag: UK Approved Doc K §1.29 permits only in loft conversion serving one habitable room; US IBC 1011.14 restricts to specific occupancies
- ships_ladder × primary_access: rare — code flag: IBC 1011.15 permits only to spaces ≤200 sqft not exclusive to kitchen/bath

## Section 6 · "I'm Not Sure" Primary Option Sets

### 1. Geometry (shape in plan)
- **Straight** — one flight, no turn; needs longest run of floor space
- **Quarter turn (L-shape)** — one 90° turn using a landing
- **Half turn (U-shape/dogleg)** — two 90° turns with landing between; compact footprint
- **Winder** — turns using wedge-shaped treads instead of landing; saves space
- **Spiral** — winds tightly around a central column; smallest footprint, steepest walk
- **Curved / helical** — sweeps around a smooth curve or open well; most spacious, statement option

### 2. Material Family (what it's made of)
- **Timber** — warm, traditional, widest range of prices
- **Timber + glass** — timber structure with glass balustrade; adds light
- **Metal + glass** — steel structure, glass panels; contemporary look
- **All glass** — glass treads and balustrade on hidden steel; premium bespoke
- **Concrete** — poured or precast; permanent, high thermal mass, heavy
- **Mixed** — combining stone treads, metal frame, timber handrail etc.

### 3. Structural System (how it holds itself up)
- **Two strings** — traditional stringers either side; classic construction
- **Mono-stringer (central spine)** — single steel beam under centre; open, modern
- **Cantilever** — each tread anchored into wall, no visible support; needs engineered wall
- **Floating (mono-stringer or cantilever)** — visual category for either
- **Spiral central column** — single vertical post carries every tread
- **Bolt-fixed (Bolzentreppe)** — treads bolted to wall and/or structural handrail; German engineered pattern

### 4. Riser (vertical face between treads)
- **Closed** — solid riser; traditional and most compliant everywhere
- **Open** — no riser; see through; more light, more air
- **Partial / feature bar** — reduced riser with slim bar to meet "4-inch sphere" rule

### 5. Tread Material (what your foot lands on)
- **Solid timber** — most common; warm; can be sanded and refinished
- **Engineered timber** — layered/veneered for stability; matches wide-plank floors
- **Stone** — very durable, heavy, cold underfoot
- **Metal** — industrial or contemporary; often perforated on external stairs
- **Glass** — laminated toughened; premium look; needs specialist detailing
- **Carpet insert** — timber tread with runner; softens the tread

### 6. Balustrade (side barrier)
- **Timber spindles** — traditional; widest style range
- **Metal spindles** — slim uprights; contemporary or Victorian idioms
- **Glass** — frameless or clamped; maximum sightline
- **Cable / wire** — thin horizontal cables; industrial look; check residential climbability rules
- **Mixed** — e.g. timber newels with glass infill

### 7. Handrail (what you grip)
- **Timber** — most common; warm; profiled or square
- **Metal** — brass, stainless, powder-coated steel; modern look
- **Glass-mounted top cap** — sits on top of glass balustrade
- **Wall-mounted** — brackets straight into wall on closed-side stair

## Ambiguity Flags (9)

1. **Wendeltreppe scope (DE)** — Wiktionary/consumer includes both spiral+helical; DIN 18065 practitioners reserve Spindeltreppe for central-column variant.
2. **Colimaçon vs Hélicoïdal (FR)** — Escarmor treats as synonyms; Univ-Escaliers/PBM insist on technical distinction.
3. **Chiocciola vs Elicoidale (IT)** — Rizzi/Sandrini flag daily-language conflation.
4. **Caracol vs Helicoidal (ES)** — Ferrovial treats caracol as general term; strict technical usage reserves helicoidal for open-well.
5. **Grand vs Monumental** — no code definitions; used interchangeably.
6. **Double staircase** — Bob Vila uses = bifurcated; other sources = two independent flights. needs_review.
7. **Open riser + commercial cantilever (US)** — IBC 1011.5.5.3 requires solid risers on required egress; exceptions apply.
8. **Handrail-less glass balustrade** — BS 6180:2011 permits with laminated toughened; contested by some Building Control officers.
9. **SMA "String" vs "Stringer"** — SMA accepts both (parenthetical); US trade defaults to stringer.
