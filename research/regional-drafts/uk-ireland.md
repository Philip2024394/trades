## 1. Executive Summary

UK and Ireland staircase terminology is closely aligned, both derived from the same British Standard (BS 5395) lineage, EN 14076 (European stair terminology), and near-identical Approved Document K / Technical Guidance Document K wording.

Key findings:
1. UK trade uses "spindle" colloquially; "baluster" is the standards term. Both are current.
2. "Cut string" and "open string" are treated as synonyms in essentially every UK trade source, but "open string" is occasionally misused to mean "open riser" — a genuine ambiguity flag.
3. "Spiral" vs "helical" has a hard technical distinction (central column present / absent) that trade sources agree on.
4. Ireland's TGD K is textually near-identical to English AD K terminology; a **proposed** Irish amendment introduces "gait" to replace "pitch/degree" but this is not yet enacted.
5. Ireland's Irish-owned suppliers (57 Stairs, McNally, George Quinn, Stairs Dublin) use identical vocabulary to UK trade with no Ireland-specific term evidenced.
6. "Cantilever" and "floating" are used interchangeably in UK trade even though they are structurally different.
7. EN 14076 is the referenced glossary for BWF Stair Scheme members — worth citing as the definitive term list once accessed.

Notable gaps: exact BS 5395-1 clause text, TGD K 2014 clause 1.0 definitions, and EN 14076 term list all sit behind paywalls / PDFs the agent could not extract. Marked as `needs_review` where reliance was on secondary sources quoting them.

## 4. Ambiguity Flags

1. **"Open string" vs "cut string" vs "open riser"** — UK trade treats "cut" and "open" strings as synonyms; "open string" is occasionally misused by customers/product listings to mean "open riser". These are structurally different. **Flag as needs_review at customer-input parsing**: treat "open string" as cut-string by default but confirm.
2. **"Cantilever" vs "floating"** — trade uses interchangeably. Strictly, cantilever = each tread structurally cantilevered; floating = broader visual class incl. cantilever, mono-stringer, suspended. Recommend recording both a marketing tag and a structural tag.
3. **"Spindle" vs "baluster"** — both current in UK. Spindle dominates consumer speech; baluster is the standards term. No confusion in meaning; hold both as synonyms.
4. **"Volute" vs "monkey's tail"** — some sources treat as synonyms, others (KnoStairs) treat monkey's tail as one specific form of wreathing volute. Treat monkey's tail as subtype of volute; needs_review for strict use.
5. **"Dog-leg" scope** — UK/IE defines as half-turn (180°). Some US and secondary sources use "dog-leg" to include quarter-turn. In UK/IE terminology, restrict to 180° with half-landing.
6. **"Gait" (Ireland)** — appears in a **proposed** TGD K amendment. Not yet enacted. Do NOT ship as canonical.
7. **BS 5395 vs AD K measurement of "going"** — for straight stairs identical; for spiral/helical, BS 5395-2 defines going measured at centre of tread and at 270mm inboard, differing from AD K's straight-stair definition.
8. **BWF glossary** — 14 terms indexed but definitions did not render for extraction; corroborated with BWF Design Guide 2. Mark BWF-only definitions as needs_review until full glossary is fetched.
9. **EN 14076** — cited by BWF but not directly accessed. Term-list should be reconciled against EN 14076 before final freeze.
