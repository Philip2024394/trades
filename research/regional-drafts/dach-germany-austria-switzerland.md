## 1. Executive Summary

German staircase terminology is markedly more precise than English and is anchored by **DIN 18065:2020-08 "Gebäudetreppen – Begriffe, Messregeln, Hauptmaße"**, which defines terms, measurement rules and main dimensions. Two terms consistently confused in translated English literature — **Wendeltreppe** and **Spindeltreppe** — are NOT synonyms in trade usage: every Spindeltreppe is a Wendeltreppe, but not vice versa. The distinguishing feature is the central axis: Wendeltreppe has an open stair-eye (Treppenauge); Spindeltreppe has a solid central column (Spindel). Multiple mainstream German trade sources confirm this distinction consistently — **NOT needs_review**.

Structural family terms (Wangentreppe / Bolzentreppe / Kragarmtreppe / Mittelholmtreppe / Sattelholmtreppe / Faltwerktreppe) each denote a distinct load-bearing principle and MUST NOT be merged. Notably, **Faltwerktreppe is not defined in DIN 18065** and requires an ETA (European Technical Assessment) instead.

DACH divergences are relatively small: Austria and southern Germany/Switzerland historically used **Stiege** for Treppe; ÖNORM B 5370 (Stiegen) was renamed and largely harmonised with DIN terminology after 1982, and modern ÖNORM B 5371 is coordinated with DIN 18065. Swiss SIA 358 covers railings/parapets only, not stair geometry.

The industry body is **BVTG (Bundesverband Treppen- und Geländerbau)**, founded 1981 — **"BDT" as an acronym does not exist**.

## 3. The Wendeltreppe vs Spindeltreppe Question — RESOLVED

**Definitive answer:** Not synonyms in German trade terminology.

- **Wendeltreppe** = umbrella term for a curved stair that turns ≥ once completely around itself. The middle is **open** — the **Treppenauge** (stair-eye). Load path: cantilever or perimeter stringer.
- **Spindeltreppe** = a **special case** of Wendeltreppe with a **solid central column** (the **Spindel**) around which treads radiate. No stair-eye. Because of tight winding, most space-efficient; typically secondary/auxiliary use.

**Set relationship:** Every Spindeltreppe IS a Wendeltreppe; not every Wendeltreppe is a Spindeltreppe.

**Consumer confusion warning:** English "spiral staircase" is often marketed for both — English does not carry the eye/no-eye distinction reliably. For international DB, keep them as two separate canonical concepts and flag the mapping asymmetry.

**Sources agreeing:** daibau.ch, bauspot.de, stadler.de blog, bau-wiki.de, treppen-intercon.de, schaefers.de. **No contradicting source found.** Confidence: HIGH.

## 4. Structural Family Map

Per BauNetz Wissen / trade sources (DIN 18065 does not enumerate these as named families):

- **Wangentreppe** — treads BETWEEN two lateral stringers; closed, traditional; self-supporting (no wall needed)
- **Holmtreppe** (Ein-/Zwei-/Mittelholm) — treads ON TOP of one or two longitudinal beams beneath; open, floating look; self-supporting
- **Sattelholmtreppe / aufgesattelte Treppe** — descriptive of Holm construction where treads sit on saw-tooth or Z-folded stringers
- **Bolzentreppe** — treads carried by bolts fixed into a load-bearing wall (+ optional light stringer / handrail structure on open side); requires structural wall
- **Kragarmtreppe** — pure cantilever from a structural wall; treads carry internal steel; requires massive wall
- **Faltwerktreppe** — folded-plate self-supporting structure (tread + riser form a zig-zag plate); wall-anchored; **not in DIN 18065 — requires ETA**

**Distinguishing question tree for a database:**
1. Does the stair need a load-bearing wall? → No: Wangen or Holm. Yes: continue.
2. Are treads bolt-fixed with visible bolts? → Bolzentreppe.
3. Are treads freely floating with no visible bolts? → Kragarmtreppe.
4. Is the structure a folded plate? → Faltwerktreppe.
5. If self-supporting: are treads BETWEEN side beams (Wangen) or ON TOP of beams (Holme)?

## 5. DACH Divergences

### Austria (AT)
- Colloquial/traditional word for stair is **Stiege**, not Treppe. ÖNORM B 5370 (Stiegen) was renamed after 1982 to harmonise; current **ÖNORM B 5371** uses "Treppe" and is broadly coordinated with DIN 18065.
- ÖNORM B 5371 explicitly **excludes** stairs steeper than 45° (Wendeltreppen, Raumspartreppen), reflecting a stricter safety framing than DIN 18065.
- Building-authority umbrella body: **ÖIB** (Österreichisches Institut für Bautechnik, OIB-Richtlinie 4 for stairs/railings).

### Switzerland (CH)
- German-speaking Switzerland uses **Treppe** in modern trade, with **Stiege** as regional/colloquial.
- **SIA 358 "Geländer und Brüstungen"** covers **railings and parapets only** — NOT stair geometry (no Swiss equivalent to DIN 18065 for geometry per se; SIA norms apply for structural aspects, and DIN 18065 is often referenced by Swiss practitioners).
- SIA 358 railing height: ≥ 90 cm along stairs; ≥ 100 cm elsewhere up to 12 m fall height; openings < 12 cm where unaccompanied children can access.
- French cantons use French terminology (escalier, limon, contremarche…); Italian cantons use Italian — different SIA language editions.

### Germany (DE)
- **DIN 18065:2020-08** is the primary anchor.
- Landesbauordnungen (state building regs) impose additional (occasionally divergent) requirements per Bundesland.
- Industry body: **BVTG** (Bundesverband Treppen- und Geländerbau e.V., founded 1981; ~70 member companies). **Treppenmeister** is a trade cooperative / brand — not the industry body.

## 6. Ambiguity Flags

- **Katzentreppe** — dual meaning (stepped gable in architecture literature; loft-ladder colloquial). **needs_review** for consumer-facing use.
- **Schiffstreppe** — historical/nautical, not defined in DIN 18065. Near-synonymous with steep/paddle stair. Confidence: Medium.
- **Hilfstreppe** — used loosely; overlaps with *Nebentreppe* (secondary stair) and *Raumspartreppe*. Not a DIN term.
- **Steiltreppe / Raumspartreppe / Sambatreppe** — trade classifications, overlap. Sambatreppe is a specific shape (alternating half-treads); Steiltreppe is a gradient descriptor; Raumspartreppe is a footprint umbrella. All can coexist for one stair.
- **Faltwerktreppe** — visually similar to Kragarmtreppe but structurally different (folded plate vs cantilever from wall). Do not merge.
- **Mittelholm vs Einholm** — often used interchangeably; strictly, *Einholm* = one beam (position variable), *Mittelholm* = one beam centrally located. Trade usage conflates them.
- **"BDT"** as a German staircase industry body — **does not exist**. The body is **BVTG**. Recommend removing "BDT" from the source spec.
- **Wangenholz** — refers only to timber Wange; not applicable to steel/concrete stringers.
- German "Podesttreppe" vs "Treppe mit Zwischenpodest" — largely synonymous in trade; strictly a Podesttreppe is any multi-flight stair with landing.
