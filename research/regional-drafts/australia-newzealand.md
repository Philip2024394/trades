## Executive Summary

Australia and NZ inherit UK vocabulary but diverge in specific, verifiable ways. Both countries authoritatively use tread, riser, going, flight, landing, nosing, balustrade, baluster, handrail, newel, winder, kite winder following UK usage. Key divergences:

- **String vs stringer:** Both terms used interchangeably by AU and NZ manufacturers. Trade shifts toward "stringer" in technical writing; "string" persists in `cut-string / closed-string / open-string` phrasing.
- **Loft ladder vs attic ladder:** AU market strongly favours **attic ladder** (US-style), though "loft ladder" is understood.
- **Space-saver vs paddle vs alternating tread:** All three exist in AU; "space-saving stair" is dominant customer-facing term. Under NCC these are typically permitted only as secondary access, unlike UK where common as primary in small homes.
- **Timber species:** AU centres on native hardwoods — **Jarrah** (WA), **Blackbutt**, **Spotted Gum**, **Tasmanian Oak**, **Ironbark**, **Merbau** (external). NZ centres on **Rimu**, **Matai**, **Kauri**, **Tawa**, **Beech**.
- **Cable/wire balustrade:** Very common in AU (stainless 3.2 mm 1×19 or 7×7 standard); marketed as "wire balustrade" not "cable".
- **Regulatory anchors differ:** AU = NCC 2022 + AS 1657 (industrial/commercial) + AS 1288 (glass) + AS 1926 (pool). NZ = NZBC clauses D1 (access) and F4 (safety from falling) + NZS 3604 (timber framing) + NZS 4223 (glass).
- **"Carpet-to-timber staircase conversion"** is a live commercial pitch anchor in AU, but the vocabulary is **"staircase makeover" / "staircase renovation"**, not "refacing".

## Australia · Per-Layer Terminology

**Layer A — Material:** timber (also "wooden" in marketing) · steel · aluminium (mostly external/marine) · glass · concrete · mixed/composite (hybrid stair).

**Layer B — Geometry:** straight · quarter-turn (L-shape, NCC Part 11.2) · half-turn (U-shape) · winder (NCC allows max 3 winders per quarter, 6 per half) · kite winder · spiral (central column) · helical/curved (classed as tapered-tread stair under NCC) · floating · cantilever · alternating tread (secondary access only under NCC) · space-saver (dominant AU customer term) · ships ladder (AS 1657-2018) · attic ladder (dominant AU term) · external stair / deck stair.

**Layer C — Structural:** string/stringer (both used) · closed string · cut string / open string · central stringer / mono stringer · double stringer · cantilever · concrete substructure.

**Layer D — Riser:** closed · open (NCC 125 mm sphere rule in Class 1a).

**Layer E — Tread:** tread · going (NCC glossary) · nosing (AS 1657 slip-resistance rule) · bullnose nosing · square nosing.

**Layer G — Balustrade:** balustrade · baluster · glass balustrade (AS 1288; ≥1m barriers require interlinked handrail) · **wire balustrade** (3.2 mm 316 stainless, 1×19 or 7×7) · metal balustrade · timber balustrade. Heights: 865 mm min on stair pitch · 1000 mm on landings/balconies · 125 mm sphere rule on openings.

**Layer H — Handrail:** handrail · wall-mounted · continuous (AS 1428.1 commercial/accessible) · over-the-post · post-to-post.

**Layer I — Newel:** newel post · landing newel · half newel.

**Layer J — Landing:** landing · quarter landing (90°) · half landing (180°) · intermediate landing. NCC clarifies quarter landings sufficient to halt a fall.

**Layer K — Specialist:** alternating tread (AS 1657 steep pitch, industrial; NCC restricts to secondary access in dwellings) · attic ladder (big market via Attic Group, AM-BOSS, Attic Ladders Aus) · pool-area stair (AS 1926.1-2012 for surrounding barriers) · **fire-isolated stairway** (NCC-defined: stairway within fire-resisting shaft).

## New Zealand · Per-Layer Terminology

**Layer A — Material:** Same as AU. Timber species differ (see below).

**Layer B — Geometry:** Same categories as AU. **NZBC D1 differentiates stair "types" by USE**: accessible stairways · common stairways · main stairways · private stairways · service stairways — each with different pitch/riser/tread limits. **NZ-specific classification** not used the same way in AU.

**Layer C — Structural:** Confirmed from Stylecraft NZ + BRANZ:
- **String / stringer** — used interchangeably; BRANZ external-stair bulletin uses "string or stringer"
- **Closed string, cut string, open string** — same UK/AU convention
- **Central stringer / mono stringer**
- **Apron** — "a facia covering the ends of rough strings, carriage pieces, and the joists of landings" (Stylecraft NZ). Not typically used in AU customer language.
- **Kite** — same as AU (kite winder)

**Layer D — Riser:** Same as AU. NZBC D1/AS1 permits open risers subject to sphere rule.

**Layer E — Tread:** tread · riser · going · nosing — same terminology. Native timber species differ (see below).

**Layer G — Balustrade (regulatory anchor NZBC F4):**
- Barrier (F4 term)
- Balustrade (industry common)
- Baluster
- Glass balustrade (must comply with NZS 4223.3:2016, Grade A)
- Barrier heights: 900 mm min on stairs (from nosing) · 1000 mm on housing decks/balconies/landings · 1100 mm commercial.

**Layer H — Handrail:** 900 mm on stair, transitioning to 1100 mm at landings where it forms part of the barrier. Continuous-handrail requirement aligns with AS/NZS 1428.

**Layers I, J, K:** Newel, landing (quarter/half/intermediate), spiral, curved, floating, cantilever — same terminology as AU.

**NZ-specific term:** "External stairs" (BRANZ canonical term for outdoor housing stairs; treads 900–1000 mm should be nominal 50 mm min thickness, 5 mm board gaps).

## Space-Saver / Paddle / Alternating-Tread — AU Specifics

- **"Space-saver stair"** = dominant AU customer-facing marketing term.
- **"Paddle stair"** = understood but less common than UK.
- **"Alternating tread stair"** = AS 1657 industrial term.
- **NCC 2022 constraint:** alternating-tread stairs are **generally not permitted as primary access** in Class 1a dwellings. Secondary access (loft/attic) subject to council + certifier approval.
- **AS 1657-2018** covers industrial/commercial alternating-tread; increased max pitch above 45° for steep "ships-ladder-style" stairways under specific conditions.
- **Ships ladder ≠ alternating tread**: ships ladder = parallel treads at steep pitch; alternating tread = offset half-treads.

## Ambiguity Flags (10)

1. **String vs stringer** — both used by same AU manufacturers on same page; not resolvable to one canonical.
2. **Floating vs cantilever** — used interchangeably in AU marketing; not always structurally identical.
3. **Curved vs helical vs spiral** — NCC classifies by central-column presence; marketing collapses.
4. **Attic ladder vs loft ladder** — AU uses both; attic dominant.
5. **Wire vs cable balustrade** — AU trade says "wire"; imported/US docs say "cable".
6. **Refacing** — NOT an AU customer-facing term. AU says "makeover" / "renovation" / "carpet-to-timber conversion".
7. **NZ-specific stair types (D1)** — accessible/common/main/private/service stairways is NZBC classification without direct AU/UK equivalent.
8. **Tasmanian Oak** = trade name covering three eucalypts (E. delegatensis, E. obliqua, E. regnans) — not one species.
9. **Australian White Oak** = marketing name; verify actual species commercially.
10. **AS 1657 exact clause definitions** could not be verified verbatim (paywall). Confidence high for term existence, medium for exact wording.
