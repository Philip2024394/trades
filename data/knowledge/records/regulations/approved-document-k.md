# Approved Document K

## Summary

Approved Document K is the England statutory guidance to the Building Regulations covering protection from falling, collision, and impact. Part K1 sets the dimensional and safety rules for stairs, ramps, and ladders — rise, going, pitch, headroom, handrails, guarding, baluster spacing. Every UK residential and commercial staircase specification must satisfy the relevant K1 provisions.

---

## Structured Knowledge

### What Approved Document K Is

Approved Document K is one of the Approved Documents issued by the UK Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (or its successor department) to provide practical guidance on how to satisfy the requirements of the Building Regulations 2010 (as amended) in England. Each Approved Document maps to a Part of Schedule 1 of the Regulations; Approved Document K corresponds to Part K, which covers protection from falling, collision, and impact.

Approved Document K is not itself law. The legal requirement is set out in the Building Regulations Part K. The Approved Document sets out one way of satisfying that legal requirement — following the Approved Document guidance is a route to compliance, but alternative approaches that meet the underlying Regulations are also permitted, subject to Building Control approval.

For NEX trade purposes, the practical position is that Approved Document K is treated as the compliance target for staircase, ramp, and guarding work in England unless an alternative approach has been explicitly approved for a specific project.

### Six Sections of Approved Document K

Approved Document K is organised into six sections, each addressing a distinct risk domain:

- **K1** — Stairs, ladders and ramps. Dimensional and safety rules for interior and exterior stairs, ramps, and ladders used in buildings.
- **K2** — Protection from falling. Requirements for guarding (guards, balustrades, barriers) where a person could fall from an edge.
- **K3** — Vehicle barriers and loading bays. Impact-resistance requirements for barriers protecting people from vehicle impact.
- **K4** — Protection from collision with open windows, skylights and ventilators. Requirements to prevent injury from projecting glazing elements.
- **K5** — Protection against impact from and trapping by doors. Powered and manual door safety requirements.
- **K6** — Protection against impact with glazing. Where safety glass is required and how impact protection is achieved.

For staircase work, K1 and K2 are the primary sections. K6 is relevant where glass balustrades are used. K3 through K5 are typically outside the scope of a staircase project.

### Jurisdictional Scope · England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland

Approved Document K applies to England. The three other UK jurisdictions have parallel but distinct provisions:

- **Wales** — Building Regulations for Wales have been devolved since 2011. Wales issues its own Approved Documents that closely mirror the English versions but may diverge on specific provisions. Approved Document K (Wales) is the direct counterpart.
- **Scotland** — The Building (Scotland) Regulations 2004 apply, supported by the Technical Handbooks (Domestic and Non-Domestic). Section 4 of the Technical Handbooks covers Safety, including stairs, ramps, and guarding. The Scottish provisions differ in some specific dimensions from the English rules.
- **Northern Ireland** — The Building Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2012 apply, supported by Technical Booklets. Technical Booklet H covers stairs, ramps, guarding and protection from impact.

A NEX quotation or specification issued for construction in a particular jurisdiction must reference the correct regulatory instrument. A specification stating "compliant with Approved Document K" is by default an England-scoped statement.

### K1 Stair Categories

Approved Document K1 classifies stairs into three categories based on the building type and usage. The dimensional rules differ between categories.

- **Category 1 · Private stair** — a stair intended for use in a single dwelling by the occupants of that dwelling. This is the domestic house staircase.
- **Category 2 · General access stair** — a stair intended for use as the main route in a building other than a dwelling (e.g., offices, retail, hospitality). This is the commercial main stair.
- **Category 3 · Utility stair** — a stair used only for access to purposes such as maintenance and inspection, rarely used by the general public. This is the plant-room or service stair.

The distinction matters because the same dimensional rule (for example, minimum going) has different values depending on category. The NEX specification for a project must identify which category applies before dimensional rules are applied.

### Rise · Going · Pitch (Category 1 Private Stair)

For a private stair (Category 1), the widely-established Approved Document K1 dimensional rules are:

- **Rise** (height of each step): between 150mm minimum and 220mm maximum.
- **Going** (horizontal depth of each step, measured on the walking line): 220mm minimum.
- **Pitch** (angle of the stair): 42° maximum.
- **Comfort formula** (guidance): 2R + G should be between 550mm and 700mm, where R is rise and G is going. This is a comfort criterion, not a strict compliance rule.
- **Consistency**: all rises in a single flight should be equal; all goings in a single flight should be equal. Variation within a flight increases trip risk.

### Rise · Going · Pitch (Category 2 General Access Stair)

For a general access stair (Category 2), the widely-established rules are:

- **Rise**: between 150mm minimum and 170mm maximum (some sources cite 180mm).
- **Going**: 250mm minimum.
- **Pitch**: shallower than a private stair, typically 33-38° maximum.

Buildings with heavy footfall or public access should verify the exact rule against the current text before specification.

### Rise · Going · Pitch (Category 3 Utility Stair)

For a utility stair (Category 3):

- **Rise**: between 150mm minimum and 190mm maximum.
- **Going**: 250mm minimum.
- **Pitch**: 38° maximum.

Utility stairs are only for maintenance/inspection access and are not intended for general use.

### Headroom

Minimum headroom above a stair or ramp is **2000mm (2 metres)**, measured vertically from the pitch line of the stair (the imaginary line joining the tread nosings) up to the underside of any obstruction above.

Special provisions exist for loft conversions where full headroom is not always achievable; reduced headroom over a stair to a loft may be permitted in specific circumstances subject to Building Control agreement.

### Handrails

- **Every stair must have at least one handrail** on at least one side.
- **Stairs wider than 1000mm** must have handrails on both sides.
- **Handrail height**: between 900mm and 1000mm above the pitch line of the stair (measured vertically to the top of the handrail).
- **On landings**: handrails and guarding may be a different height than on the flight itself (typically 900mm domestic, 1100mm non-domestic — see Guarding section below).
- **Grip**: the handrail must be graspable in a way that allows a user to close the hand fully around it (functional grip requirement).
- **Continuity**: where practical, handrails should be continuous across the length of the flight and turn horizontal at the top and bottom for the user's grip transition.

### Guarding · Balustrades

Guarding is required wherever there is a drop that could cause injury (typically a drop of more than 600mm).

- **Domestic stairs and landings**: guarding height minimum **900mm** from pitch line (stairs) or floor (landings).
- **Non-domestic stairs and landings**: guarding height minimum **1100mm** (some sources cite 1000mm for stair sides).
- **Non-climbable design**: for buildings likely to be occupied by children under 5 years, guarding must be designed so that a child cannot easily climb it. Horizontal rails at heights climbable by a young child are typically avoided.

### The 100mm Sphere Rule (Baluster Spacing)

The most widely-cited safety rule in K1 is the **100mm sphere test**: no opening in the guarding of a stair or landing should be large enough to allow a 100mm sphere to pass through. This is intended to prevent a young child's head from becoming trapped or the child from falling through the opening.

The rule applies to:
- Gaps between balusters
- Gaps between the bottom rail and the tread
- Gaps between the handrail and the guarding infill
- Any opening in the guarding system

The rule applies at all points along the balustrade, so tapered gaps (where the gap widens at one point) must satisfy the 100mm sphere test at the widest point.

### Landings

- **Every flight must have a landing** at its top and its bottom.
- **Landing depth** (measured perpendicular to the direction of travel): minimum equal to the width of the flight, and typically minimum 400mm for a private stair.
- **Landings must be level** (unless specifically permitted otherwise).
- **Landings cannot contain doors that swing across** the landing in a way that reduces the effective landing depth below the minimum, unless the door is at the bottom of the flight and there is at least 400mm of clear landing beyond the door swing.

### Flight Length and Rest Landings

- **Private stair**: maximum number of rises in a single flight without an intermediate landing is generally 16 (though longer flights can be provided subject to design justification).
- **General access stair**: maximum typically 16 rises without a rest landing.
- **Utility stair**: similar limits, verified against current text.

Long flights are broken by intermediate rest landings both for accessibility and for the safety of a fall.

### Winders and Tapered Treads

Winders (tapered treads used to turn a stair around a corner without an intermediate landing) are permitted subject to specific measurement rules. The going of a winder tread is measured at a specific reference line depending on stair width:

- **Stairs less than 1000mm wide**: going measured at the centre.
- **Stairs 1000mm or wider**: going measured at 270mm from the inside face of the stair.
- **Minimum going at the narrower end**: typically 50mm; the tread must not narrow to a point.

Kite winders (three winders that turn a stair 90°) are a common private-stair configuration for space-saving. NEX staircase records that specify winder configurations must satisfy these rules.

### Spiral and Helical Stairs

Spiral and helical stairs have their own design guidance. Approved Document K1 references **BS 5395-2: Code of practice for the design of helical and spiral stairs** as the applicable design standard. NEX records specifying spiral or helical geometries reference both Approved Document K1 and BS 5395-2.

### Alternating Tread Stairs

Alternating tread (paddle-tread) stairs are permitted only in specific space-constrained circumstances (typically loft conversions where a conventional stair cannot fit). They are not permitted as the sole means of access to a habitable room, and specific dimensional rules apply.

### Interaction With Other Approved Documents

Approved Document K does not stand alone. A compliant staircase specification typically interacts with:

- **Approved Document B (Fire safety)** — staircases in escape routes have additional fire-safety requirements (fire-resistant construction, protected shafts, self-closing doors).
- **Approved Document M (Access to and use of buildings)** — accessibility requirements. A public-access stair may need to satisfy Approved Doc M provisions on step consistency, tactile paving at top/bottom, contrast for step nosings.
- **Approved Document Q (Security)** — some staircase-related security requirements for accessible windows.
- **Approved Document B6 or the Fire Safety Order 2005** — commercial buildings.

A staircase specification is not fully compliant until all applicable Approved Documents are satisfied. NEX system records specifying a staircase should list the applicable Approved Documents.

## Advantages

- **Widely-established compliance target.** Approved Document K is the default compliance route for staircase work in England; specifying against it is a common professional standard.
- **Category-based rules match project types.** The Private/General Access/Utility split maps to the customer type NEX System records already differentiate.
- **The 100mm sphere rule is testable in the workshop.** Baluster spacing compliance can be verified with a physical gauge, not requiring specialist inspection.
- **Interaction with other Approved Documents is well-mapped.** The relationship to Approved Doc B (fire) and Approved Doc M (accessibility) is documented in the trade literature.
- **Comfort formula (2R+G) provides a design target beyond bare compliance.** Specifying to the comfort range produces a stair that feels right to use, not just one that meets the minimum.

## Considerations

- **Guidance, not law.** The Approved Document is a route to compliance, not the legal requirement itself. Alternative approaches that meet the underlying Regulations are permitted subject to Building Control agreement.
- **England-scoped by default.** Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland have parallel but distinct provisions. Cross-border projects require correct jurisdictional referencing.
- **Amendments happen.** Approved Document K is periodically updated. NEX records must be reviewed and superseded when substantive amendments are issued.
- **Loft conversions have specific exceptions.** Reduced headroom and alternating tread stairs are permitted in specific loft-conversion contexts but not elsewhere.
- **Category 2 and Category 3 numbers vary between sources.** For non-domestic work, always verify the exact rule against the current text.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying Category 1 (private) rules to Category 2 (commercial) work.** A stair specified with 220mm rise for a private house is non-compliant if built as a commercial main stair.
- **Overlooking Category 2 pitch limits on commercial work.** A steeper stair permitted for domestic use is not permitted for commercial main use.
- **Missing the 100mm sphere rule on decorative balustrades.** Cut-out patterns or feature balustrades must satisfy the sphere test at every point, not just the "typical" spacing.
- **Insufficient handrail on stairs wider than 1000mm.** Both sides are required, not one.
- **Assuming Approved Document K covers the whole staircase specification.** Fire (Doc B) and accessibility (Doc M) may also apply, and interaction with each other is not always obvious.
- **Applying English rules to a Scottish or NI project.** Scotland uses Section 4 of the Technical Handbook; NI uses Technical Booklet H. Both differ in some specifics.
- **Treating this record as the authoritative text.** It is a NEX-authored summary. The current published Approved Document K is the authoritative source; this record defers to it.

## Concepts

### Industry Knowledge

The following are established construction and regulatory concepts referenced by this record.

- **Building Regulations 2010** — the UK statutory instrument that sets the legal requirements for building work in England (parallel instruments apply in Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland).
- **Approved Document** — statutory guidance issued by the UK government explaining one way to satisfy a Part of Schedule 1 of the Building Regulations.
- **Part K** — the Part of Schedule 1 of the Building Regulations 2010 that Approved Document K addresses.
- **Pitch line** — the imaginary line joining the nosings of successive treads on a stair; the reference line for measuring rise and going.
- **Nosing** — the projecting front edge of a tread.
- **Going** — the horizontal depth of a tread measured on the walking line.
- **Rise** — the vertical height between the top of one tread and the top of the next.
- **Pitch** — the angle between the pitch line and horizontal.
- **Headroom** — the vertical clearance above the pitch line to any obstruction above.
- **Guarding** — a general term for guards, guardrails, balustrades, and barriers that prevent people from falling from an edge.
- **Balustrade** — a specific form of guarding consisting of balusters (vertical members) between a base rail and a handrail.
- **Baluster** — a vertical member in a balustrade.
- **Landing** — a horizontal platform at the top, bottom, or intermediate point of a stair.
- **Winder** — a tapered tread used to turn a stair around a corner without an intermediate landing.
- **Kite winder** — a set of three winders that turn a stair 90°.
- **Alternating tread stair** — a stair with alternating half-treads (paddle treads) permitted only in specific space-constrained contexts.
- **BS 5395-2** — British Standard code of practice for the design of helical and spiral stairs.
- **BS 6180** — British Standard code of practice for barriers in and about buildings.
- **Building Control** — the local authority function responsible for enforcing the Building Regulations.

### NEX Concepts

The following are NEX-proprietary concepts that reference this regulation record.

- **Compliance Target Reference** — NEX System records (staircase System, ramp System, guarding System) reference Approved Document K as the compliance target where England scope applies. The reference is by section and rule, not by embedding text.
- **Deferral to Authoritative Text discipline** — every NEX regulation record explicitly defers to the current published statutory instrument. Records summarise; they do not replace.
- **Jurisdiction-Aware Specification** — NEX System records that call out a compliance target also identify the applicable jurisdiction (England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland) so the correct parallel provisions apply.
- **Regulatory Interaction Map** — Approved Doc K interacts with Approved Doc B (fire), Approved Doc M (accessibility), Approved Doc Q (security), Fire Safety Order 2005. NEX System records must reference every applicable Approved Document, not just K.
- **Category-First Specification** — NEX staircase specifications identify Category 1, 2, or 3 before dimensional rules are applied. Getting the category wrong is a common non-compliance root cause.

## Canonical Q&A (Composed From Structured Knowledge · Constitutional Clause 7)

The following Q&A pairs are grounded in the Structured Knowledge above. Philip did not supply verbatim regulation Q&As in the 2026-08-05 authoring session; these are composed by Research Claude from the source knowledge in this record. Every answer traces to a claim or structured section within this record.

**Q1 · What is Approved Document K?**
A: Approved Document K is UK statutory guidance issued by the UK government explaining one way of satisfying Part K of Schedule 1 of the Building Regulations 2010 in England. It covers protection from falling, collision, and impact, with dedicated sections on stairs and ramps (K1), guarding (K2), and impact protection.

**Q2 · Does Approved Document K apply everywhere in the UK?**
A: Approved Document K applies to England. Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland have parallel provisions: Wales has its own version of Approved Document K; Scotland uses Section 4 of the Technical Handbook; Northern Ireland uses Technical Booklet H. A specification for a project must reference the correct instrument for the jurisdiction.

**Q3 · Is Approved Document K the law?**
A: No. The law is the Building Regulations Part K itself. Approved Document K is statutory guidance that sets out one way to satisfy the legal requirement. Alternative approaches that meet the underlying regulation are permitted subject to Building Control agreement.

**Q4 · What are the categories of staircase in Approved Document K?**
A: Three categories. Category 1 is a private stair for use in a single dwelling. Category 2 is a general access stair for use in a building other than a dwelling. Category 3 is a utility stair used only for maintenance access. Different dimensional rules apply to each category.

**Q5 · What is the maximum rise for a private staircase?**
A: For a Category 1 private stair, the maximum rise is 220mm. The minimum is 150mm. All rises within a single flight should be equal.

**Q6 · What is the minimum going for a private staircase?**
A: For a Category 1 private stair, the minimum going is 220mm. All goings within a single flight should be equal.

**Q7 · What is the maximum pitch for a private staircase?**
A: For a Category 1 private stair, the maximum pitch is 42°.

**Q8 · What is the 2R+G comfort formula?**
A: 2R+G is a comfort criterion where R is the rise and G is the going. For a private stair, the formula should give a value typically between 550mm and 700mm. It is a design guidance target rather than a strict compliance rule.

**Q9 · What is the minimum headroom above a stair?**
A: 2000mm (2 metres), measured vertically from the pitch line of the stair to the underside of any obstruction above. Special provisions apply in loft conversions where full headroom is not achievable.

**Q10 · Where do I need handrails on a stair?**
A: Every stair must have at least one handrail. Stairs wider than 1000mm must have handrails on both sides. Handrail height should be between 900mm and 1000mm above the pitch line.

**Q11 · How high should the guarding be on a domestic stair?**
A: Minimum 900mm on the flight (measured from the pitch line) and on landings (measured from the floor).

**Q12 · How high should the guarding be on a commercial staircase?**
A: Non-domestic stairs and landings typically require 1100mm guarding on landings and balconies. The exact figure for the stair itself should be verified against the current text.

**Q13 · What is the 100mm sphere rule?**
A: No opening in stair or landing guarding should be large enough to allow a 100mm sphere to pass through. This applies to gaps between balusters, between rails, and anywhere else in the guarding system. It prevents young children from being trapped or falling through.

**Q14 · Do decorative balustrades need to satisfy the 100mm sphere rule?**
A: Yes. Decorative and feature balustrades must satisfy the sphere test at every point, including at the widest opening if the gap tapers. Cut-out patterns and feature designs must be checked at every opening.

**Q15 · Can I have winder treads?**
A: Yes. Winders (tapered treads used to turn a stair without a landing) are permitted subject to specific measurement rules. The going of a winder is measured at the centre of the stair for stairs less than 1000mm wide, or at 270mm from the inside face for wider stairs. The narrow end of the winder cannot be too narrow.

**Q16 · Are spiral staircases regulated by Approved Document K?**
A: Approved Document K references BS 5395-2 (British Standard code of practice for the design of helical and spiral stairs) as the applicable design standard. Spiral and helical stairs should be designed to BS 5395-2 while still satisfying the general safety principles of Approved Doc K.

**Q17 · Are alternating tread stairs allowed?**
A: Alternating tread (paddle-tread) stairs are permitted only in specific space-constrained contexts, typically loft conversions where a conventional stair cannot fit. They are not permitted as the sole means of access to a habitable room. Specific dimensional rules apply.

**Q18 · How many rises can there be in a single flight?**
A: The general guidance is a maximum of 16 rises in a single flight without an intermediate rest landing, though longer flights can be provided subject to design justification.

**Q19 · Do I need a landing at the top and bottom of every stair?**
A: Yes. Every flight must have a landing at its top and its bottom. The landing depth (perpendicular to the direction of travel) should be at least equal to the width of the flight, and typically at least 400mm on a private stair.

**Q20 · Can a door swing across a landing?**
A: A door can be at the bottom of a flight and swing away from the stair, provided there is at least 400mm of clear landing beyond the door swing. A door that swings across a landing in a way that reduces effective landing depth below the minimum is not permitted.

**Q21 · Does Approved Document K cover fire safety on stairs?**
A: No. Fire safety on staircases in escape routes is covered by Approved Document B. A stair in an escape route must satisfy both Approved Doc K (dimensional and safety rules) and Approved Doc B (fire protection).

**Q22 · Does Approved Document K cover accessibility?**
A: No. Accessibility of stairs is covered by Approved Document M. A public-access stair typically must satisfy both Approved Doc K and Approved Doc M provisions on step consistency, tactile paving, and contrast for step nosings.

**Q23 · What happens if my design cannot meet the Approved Document K dimensions?**
A: The Approved Document is guidance, not law. An alternative approach that meets the underlying Building Regulations Part K may be permitted subject to Building Control agreement. Non-standard configurations should be discussed with Building Control before final specification.

**Q24 · How do I know if I'm designing to the current Approved Document K?**
A: The current published version should be obtained from gov.uk (or its successor). NEX records referencing Approved Document K defer to the current published text as the authoritative source. Where a discrepancy is identified between a NEX record and the current text, the current text supersedes.

**Q25 · Does this NEX record contain the full text of Approved Document K?**
A: No. This is a NEX-authored practical summary. The current published Approved Document K is the authoritative source and is not reproduced here. NEX records reference the section and specific rule; downstream System records must verify against the current text at specification time.
