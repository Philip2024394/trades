# Is MDF Suitable for Kitchen Carcasses?

MDF (Medium Density Fibreboard) is a strong, smooth engineered wood product made from fine wood fibres bonded with resin under heat and pressure. It has excellent machining properties and provides a smooth surface for painted finishes.

While MDF **can** be used for cabinet carcasses, it is usually reserved for specific applications rather than the entire cabinet. Most quality kitchens use **MFC (Melamine Faced Chipboard) or plywood** for the carcass, while **MDF is more commonly used for doors, end panels, fillers, and painted components**.

## Advantages of MDF

- Very smooth surface
- Excellent for painted finishes
- Machines cleanly
- Stable and consistent
- No knots or grain
- Easy to rout decorative profiles
- Good screw holding when fixed correctly
- Produces accurate joints

## Disadvantages of MDF

- Heavier than chipboard
- More expensive than standard MFC
- Can swell if exposed to prolonged moisture (unless moisture-resistant MDF is used)
- Not as stiff as plywood for long spans
- More difficult to handle because of its weight

## Where MDF Is Commonly Used

MDF is an excellent choice for painted cabinet doors · Shaker doors · slab doors · end panels · decorative panels · cornices · pelmets · plinths (kickboards) · light pelmets · decorative mouldings.

These parts benefit from MDF's smooth surface and ability to achieve a high-quality painted finish.

## Common Carcass Materials

| Material | Typical Use | Advantages |
|---|---|---|
| MFC (Melamine Faced Chipboard) | Most factory-made kitchens | Cost-effective · durable · easy to clean |
| Moisture-Resistant MFC | Premium kitchens | Better resistance to humidity |
| Plywood | Bespoke and luxury kitchens | Strong · lightweight · excellent durability |
| MDF | Some bespoke carcasses and painted furniture | Smooth finish · accurate machining |
| Solid Timber | Traditional bespoke furniture | Premium appearance · long lifespan |

## Moisture-Resistant MDF

Where MDF is used in kitchens, MR-MDF is usually preferred: better resistance to humidity · less swelling than standard MDF · suitable for painted cabinetry · ideal near sinks when properly sealed. **MR-MDF is moisture resistant, not waterproof.**

## Is MDF Better Than MFC?

Depends on the application.

**Choose MDF when:** painted finish · decorative routed profiles · cabinet is custom made · appearance matters more than keeping weight low.

**Choose MFC when:** standard cabinet carcasses · durability and easy cleaning are priorities · cost and weight are important · a melamine finish is preferred.

## What Do Premium Kitchen Manufacturers Use?

Many premium manufacturers use combinations:

- **Cabinet carcasses:** 18mm moisture-resistant MFC or plywood.
- **Painted doors:** moisture-resistant MDF.
- **Timber doors:** solid timber or veneered MDF.
- **Decorative panels:** MDF or veneered MDF.

The best material depends on the purpose of each component rather than using one material throughout.

## Nex Recommendation

Rather than asking *"Is MDF good?"*, ask:

- Is the material for a carcass, door, or decorative panel?
- Will it be painted or wood finished?
- Is it close to a sink or dishwasher?
- Is this a budget, mid-range, or luxury kitchen?

For most kitchens, a practical combination is:

- **Carcasses:** 18mm moisture-resistant MFC (or plywood for premium bespoke).
- **Painted doors + decorative components:** moisture-resistant MDF.
- **Natural wood doors:** solid timber or veneered MDF.

This approach combines durability · appearance · value while using each material where it performs best.
