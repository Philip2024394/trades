# Kitchen Door Styles

The cabinet door style largely determines the kitchen's overall character. This guide covers all 14 door styles Nex tracks, when each suits which interior, and the trade-offs between them.

## Modern & Minimal

### Slab

Flat door with no framing, no panel, no detail.

- Reads pure modern · minimal · clean.
- Available in painted MDF (any colour), timber veneer, high gloss, or matt.
- Best for: contemporary interiors · handleless designs · Scandinavian.

### Handleless (True Handleless)

Recessed grip channel routed into the top edge of doors and drawer fronts.

- The cleanest look — no visible hardware at all.
- Most expensive handleless option.
- Best for: pure modern · minimalist · luxury contemporary.

### Handleless (J-Pull)

A J-shaped profile integrated into the top edge providing an angled grip point.

- Simpler and cheaper than true handleless.
- Best for: contemporary kitchens where budget matters.

### Handleless (Push-to-Open)

No handle or channel at all — cabinets open with a light press.

- Ultra-minimal appearance.
- Not always practical near sinks (wet hands trigger accidentally).
- Best for: statement contemporary + luxury.

### High Gloss

Reflective lacquered finish — usually applied to slab or handleless doors.

- Bright, reflective, feels premium.
- Shows fingerprints and dust · needs regular wiping.
- Best for: small kitchens (reflects light and space).

### Matt Finish

Non-reflective finish — applied to slab or handleless doors.

- Sophisticated, hides marks better than gloss.
- Popular in dark colours (navy, charcoal, forest green).
- Best for: contemporary · sophisticated interiors.

## Traditional & Heritage

### Raised Panel

Traditional five-piece door with a raised (usually beveled) central panel inside a frame.

- Reads formal · heritage · classical.
- Best for: Georgian · Victorian · country · formal traditional.

### In-Frame

Doors sit INSIDE the cabinet frame (not on the front). You see the cabinet frame around every door.

- The most traditional and craftsman-quality construction.
- 20-40% more expensive than equivalent Shaker.
- Best for: premium traditional · heritage properties · homes wanting the most bespoke look.

### Beaded Shaker

Standard Shaker construction with a decorative bead detail routed around the panel edge.

- Adds heritage character to standard Shaker.
- Best for: traditional and cottage kitchens.

### Tongue & Groove

Vertical or horizontal tongue-and-groove boarding forming the door face.

- Cottage · farmhouse character.
- Best for: cottage · farmhouse · coastal kitchens.

## Bridge (Traditional-Modern)

### Shaker

Flat panel inside a five-piece frame (top rail · bottom rail · two stiles · centre panel). The door sits on the FRONT of the cabinet with a small overlay.

- The most popular door style in the UK.
- Works in Victorian townhouses · Georgian country homes · modern new-builds · contemporary flats.
- Slim Shaker (45-55mm frame) reads more modern; wider Shaker (70-90mm) reads more traditional.
- The safest choice if you may sell within 10 years.
- Best for: virtually every interior style.

### Narrow / Slim Shaker

Shaker with a slim 40-55mm frame.

- Bridges pure Shaker heritage with contemporary minimalism.
- Best for: modern kitchens wanting warmth without full-Shaker traditionalism.

## Specialty

### Fluted Door

Vertical concave-groove detail across the door face.

- Currently trending (as of 2026).
- Adds texture and shadow interest to flat modern kitchens.
- Best for: contemporary kitchens wanting distinctive detail.

### Reeded Door

Vertical convex-ridge detail across the door face (opposite of fluted).

- Growing in popularity alongside fluted.
- Best for: contemporary kitchens · statement islands.

### Glass Display Door

Cabinet door with a glass insert (clear, frosted, or leaded).

- Used sparingly for display units, not full runs.
- Best for: showcasing crockery · adding lightness in wall-cabinet runs.

## The Shaker vs In-Frame Comparison (most-asked question)

| | Shaker | In-Frame |
|---|---|---|
| Door position | On front of cabinet | Inside cabinet frame |
| Visible cabinet frame | No | Yes, around every door |
| Cost | Baseline | +20-40% |
| Construction complexity | Standard | Premium |
| Best for | Modern-traditional bridge · most homes | Premium traditional · heritage properties |
| Longevity of look | Timeless | Timeless (heritage-leaning) |

## Which Door Style Wins by Interior

| Interior Style | Recommended Doors |
|---|---|
| Modern / Contemporary | Slab · Handleless · Matt · Slim Shaker |
| Traditional / Heritage | Raised Panel · In-Frame · Beaded Shaker · Shaker |
| Cottage / Farmhouse | Shaker · Beaded Shaker · Tongue & Groove |
| Scandinavian | Slab (light timber) · Slim Shaker (white/pale) |
| Industrial | Slab (matt dark) · High Gloss (dark) |
| Luxury | In-Frame · Handleless (true) · Fluted |
| Coastal | Shaker (painted white/blue) · Tongue & Groove |
| Victorian Restoration | In-Frame · Raised Panel · Beaded Shaker |
| Georgian | In-Frame · Raised Panel |
| Modern Farmhouse | Slim Shaker · Shaker (painted sage/olive/navy) |

## Nex Recommendation Logic

When advising, Nex asks:

1. How would you describe your interior — modern, traditional, or somewhere between?
2. What's the age and style of your property?
3. Painted finish or natural timber?
4. Handleless or with handles?
5. Do you want the doors to be the visual statement, or should they be a quiet backdrop?
6. Do you plan to sell within 10 years (informs timelessness weighting)?
7. Budget tier?

The answers narrow 14 options to typically 2-3 recommendations.
