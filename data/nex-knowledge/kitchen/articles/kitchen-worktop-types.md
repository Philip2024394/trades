# Kitchen Worktop Types

A kitchen worktop is the main preparation and working surface within a kitchen. The right worktop balances durability · appearance · maintenance requirements · budget · and lifestyle. This guide covers all 19 worktop materials Nex recommends across those criteria.

## Economy Tier (£20-£120/m² supplied)

### Laminate

The most popular and affordable worktop.

- **Features:** decorative laminate bonded to engineered board · thousands of colours and patterns · can replicate stone/wood/concrete.
- **Advantages:** budget-friendly · huge design choice · easy to clean · lightweight · quick installation.
- **Best for:** first homes · rental properties · budget renovations · family kitchens where cost matters more than premium finish.

### Compact Laminate

Thin, high-density laminate worktops.

- **Advantages:** waterproof core · slim contemporary profile · affordable · modern appearance.
- **Best for:** minimalist kitchens · budget contemporary designs.

## Mid Tier (£120-£300/m² supplied)

### Solid Wood

Made from real hardwood joined into full-length boards. Common woods: oak · walnut · maple · ash · beech · iroko.

- **Advantages:** natural appearance · can be sanded and refinished · warm traditional feel · ages beautifully.
- **Maintenance:** requires regular oiling to protect against moisture.
- **Best suited to:** farmhouse and traditional kitchens.

### Bamboo

Manufactured from compressed bamboo strips.

- **Advantages:** renewable resource · modern appearance · strong · eco-friendly.
- **Maintenance:** needs periodic sealing.

### Solid Surface (acrylic/mineral composite)

Made from acrylic or mineral composite materials.

- **Advantages:** seamless joints · repairable · hygienic · curved shapes possible · integrated sinks available.
- **Best for:** modern kitchens and commercial spaces.

### Recycled Glass

Made from recycled glass set in resin or cement.

- **Advantages:** sustainable · unique appearance · colourful finish · durable.
- **Best for:** eco-conscious homes.

### Reclaimed Timber

Made using reclaimed wood from old buildings.

- **Advantages:** sustainable · character-rich · unique appearance · eco-friendly.
- **Maintenance:** requires regular maintenance.

## Premium Tier (£300-£600/m² supplied)

### Quartz

Engineered stone made from approximately 90-95% natural quartz mixed with resins.

- **Advantages:** extremely durable · scratch resistant · stain resistant · hygienic · non-porous · no sealing required.
- **Popular colours:** white · Carrara marble effect · black · grey · concrete effect · beige · Calacatta · pure white.
- **Best for:** modern kitchens · luxury homes · busy family kitchens.

### Granite

Natural stone cut directly from granite slabs.

- **Advantages:** every slab unique · heat resistant · very hard wearing · premium appearance · long lifespan.
- **Considerations:** requires sealing periodically.
- **Best for:** luxury homes · households that want natural stone character.

### Marble

Natural stone famous for elegant veining.

- **Advantages:** premium luxury appearance · naturally cool surface · excellent for baking.
- **Considerations:** softer than granite · can stain · requires sealing · scratches easier.
- **Best suited to:** statement kitchens · pastry-baking enthusiasts · homes where beauty matters more than practicality.

### Porcelain

Ultra-compact ceramic surface fired at very high temperatures.

- **Advantages:** heat resistant · UV resistant · waterproof · scratch resistant · suitable indoors and outdoors.
- **Popular looks:** marble · concrete · stone · industrial finishes.
- **Best for:** luxury contemporary kitchens · outdoor kitchens · slim-profile designs (6mm and 12mm options).

### Sintered Stone

High-performance engineered surface manufactured under extreme pressure and heat.

- **Advantages:** extremely durable · heat resistant · UV resistant · stain resistant · very low maintenance.
- **Best for:** luxury contemporary kitchens · flagship specification.

### Ceramic

Made from fired ceramic slabs.

- **Advantages:** heat resistant · scratch resistant · waterproof · lightweight · UV stable.
- **Best for:** modern kitchens with slim-profile requirements.

### Dekton

Ultra-compact engineered surface (Cosentino brand · similar family to sintered stone).

- **Advantages:** heat resistant · UV resistant · scratch resistant · waterproof · suitable outdoors.
- **Best for:** luxury contemporary homes.

### Corian

Premium solid-surface material (DuPont brand).

- **Advantages:** invisible joints · repairable · smooth finish · hygienic · integrated sinks.
- **Best for:** high-end contemporary kitchens with curves and integrated sinks.

## Specialist Tier

### Stainless Steel

Commercial-grade worktops used in professional kitchens.

- **Advantages:** heat resistant · hygienic · waterproof · easy to disinfect · extremely durable.
- **Best for:** restaurant-style kitchens · industrial-style home kitchens.

### Concrete

Can be cast in place or manufactured off-site.

- **Advantages:** industrial appearance · fully bespoke · very durable · unique finish.
- **Considerations:** requires sealing · heavy weight requires substantial cabinet + floor support.
- **Best for:** industrial-style kitchens.

### Slate

Natural slate stone.

- **Advantages:** dark contemporary appearance · heat resistant · waterproof · low maintenance.
- **Best for:** industrial kitchens · dark contemporary kitchens.

## Best Worktop by Lifestyle

**Busy Family Kitchen** — Quartz · Porcelain · Sintered Stone.

**Luxury Kitchen** — Marble · Quartz · Granite · Dekton.

**Traditional Kitchen** — Oak · Walnut · Beech · Granite.

**Modern Kitchen** — Quartz · Porcelain · Corian · Compact Laminate.

**Commercial Kitchen** — Stainless Steel · Solid Surface.

**Eco-Friendly Kitchen** — Bamboo · Reclaimed Timber · Recycled Glass.

## Nex Recommendation Logic

When advising, Nex asks in this order:

1. What is your budget?
2. Do you cook every day?
3. Do you want low maintenance?
4. Modern or traditional style?
5. Do you have children (heavy wear expected)?
6. Do you place hot pans directly on the surface?
7. Do you want natural stone or engineered?
8. What colour or finish do you prefer?
9. Home or commercial?
10. How important is longevity vs initial cost?

The answers narrow the 19 options to typically 2-3 recommendations with reasoning.
