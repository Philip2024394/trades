# Understanding Kitchen Design

A kitchen is more than a room for cooking. It is one of the most-used spaces within a home and should balance functionality · storage · durability · comfort · and visual appeal. A well-designed kitchen improves daily living, increases property value, and creates a central gathering place for family and guests.

## The Five Main Kitchen Zones

Every successful kitchen is built around five working zones.

### 1 · Food Storage

Contains: refrigerator · freezer · pantry cupboards · tall larder units · dry food storage.

The storage zone should be positioned close to the main entrance of the kitchen, allowing groceries to be unloaded quickly. Tall larder units are more space-efficient than the equivalent base + wall cabinet combination.

### 2 · Preparation Zone

The main workspace. Typical features: large worktops · chopping boards · pull-out bins · knife storage · small appliance storage.

The preparation area should ideally sit BETWEEN the sink and hob — you wash the ingredient, walk to the prep space, then walk to the cooker. Never place the preparation space at the far end away from either.

### 3 · Cooking Zone

Contains: oven · hob · extractor hood · microwave · warming drawer.

Heat-resistant worktop space should be provided on BOTH sides of the hob whenever possible — one side for the pan going ON, the other for the pan coming OFF.

### 4 · Cleaning Zone

Contains: sink · dishwasher · waste bins · recycling · drying area.

This zone benefits from being located close to the preparation area — you're constantly moving between prep and clean during cooking.

### 5 · Storage Zone

A mixture of: base cabinets · wall cabinets · tall units · corner storage · drawer systems · internal organisers.

Well-designed storage makes a kitchen easier to use every day. Drawers hold more accessibly than cupboards; corner solutions (Le Mans, magic corner, carousel) rescue otherwise dead space.

## The Work Triangle (still relevant, still limited)

The classic "work triangle" (sink · hob · fridge should sit at the corners of an imaginary triangle with each leg 1.2m-2.7m long) remains a useful concept for small-to-medium kitchens. But it BREAKS in modern large open-plan kitchens where multiple cooks share the space and appliances are more numerous. For anything above ~15m², prefer the FIVE-ZONE model.

## Kitchen Design Principles (the eight rules)

A successful kitchen provides:

1. **Efficient workflow** — you shouldn't cross the room to complete a common task.
2. **Plenty of storage** — 300mm base cabinet per person + 300mm per regularly-used appliance minimum.
3. **Comfortable circulation space** — 1000mm+ walkways · 1200mm+ where two people need to pass.
4. **Good lighting** — three layers (ambient · task · feature) on separate switches.
5. **Durable materials** — worktops and doors matched to the wear the household will impose.
6. **Easy maintenance** — non-porous worktops · handleless where practical · easy-to-clean plinths.
7. **Safe appliance positioning** — 300mm+ worktop each side of the hob · minimum 650mm above hob to extractor · sockets 150mm+ from sink.
8. **Long-term flexibility** — cabinet interiors reconfigurable · appliance apertures standard-size for future replacement.

## Zone Positioning Rule of Thumb

- **Food Storage** near the door (you unload groceries here).
- **Preparation** central (this is where you spend most time).
- **Cooking** near ventilation (external wall or under an extractor route).
- **Cleaning** near services (water supply, waste).
- **Storage** everywhere else — filling any wall run that doesn't hold appliances.

## Composition with Layout Choice

The Five Zones apply REGARDLESS of layout (Galley · L · U · Island · Peninsula). The layout dictates the SHAPE of the room; the Zones dictate the FUNCTION within that shape. A galley kitchen still has five zones — they're just arranged linearly rather than in a triangle.
