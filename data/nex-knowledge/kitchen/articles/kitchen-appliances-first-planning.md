# Should I Choose Appliances Before Finalising My Kitchen Design?

**Yes. You should choose your major appliances BEFORE finalising your kitchen design.**

Changing appliances later is often possible, but depends on what you're changing.

## Why Appliances Come First

A kitchen is designed AROUND the appliances, not the other way around.

The designer needs to know the exact size and specification of oven · hob · extractor · fridge · freezer · dishwasher · washing machine · tumble dryer · wine cooler · microwave · coffee machine · warming drawer · boiling water tap · sink.

**Even differences of 10-20 mm can affect the cabinet design.**

## The Correct Planning Order

1. Kitchen layout
2. **Appliance list**
3. **Appliance models** (specific brand + model number)
4. Cabinet sizes
5. Worktops
6. Electrical plan
7. Plumbing
8. Lighting
9. Colours and finishes

## Why Exact Models Matter

Different manufacturers have different dimensions. A 60cm oven ISN'T always exactly 600mm.

Some require different ventilation gaps · different cabinet depths · different shelf positions · different fixing methods. The cabinet maker normally works from the manufacturer's installation drawings.

## What Happens If I Change My Mind?

### Changing Before Manufacturing

**✓ Usually easy.** Cabinets can often be resized or redesigned before production begins.

### Changing During Manufacturing

**⚠️ Sometimes possible.** Depends on whether the cabinet has already been cut · whether doors have been made · whether worktops have been templated. Changes at this stage can increase cost and delay the project.

### Changing After Installation

Depends on the appliance:

**EASY** — Replacing like-for-like (dishwasher · washing machine · fridge · freezer · oven same size · microwave same size).

**MORE DIFFICULT** — Changing from 60cm oven → 90cm range cooker. May require new cabinets · new worktop · new extractor · different electrical supply · altered splashback.

**VERY DIFFICULT** — Changing from standard hob → downdraft extractor hob. May require different cabinet · altered drawers · new ducting · electrical changes · plumbing changes.

## Integrated Appliances

Integrated appliances MUST fit the cabinet precisely. Integrated dishwasher · fridge · freezer · washing machine. The door sizes · hinge positions · ventilation are designed for specific appliance dimensions.

## Future-Proofing

A well-designed kitchen can allow for future changes:

- Leave service voids behind cabinets.
- Use adjustable shelves.
- Choose standard appliance widths where possible.
- Allow spare electrical capacity.
- Provide accessible isolation valves.

## Nex Discovery Questions

**Appliances:** Have you chosen your appliances? · Do you already own any? · Integrated or freestanding? · Which brands? · Any you definitely want to keep?

**Cooking:** Cook every day? · Entertain often? · Bake regularly? · Steam oven? · Combi microwave? · Warming drawer? · Coffee machine? · Wine cooler?

**Future plans:** Could your cooking habits change? · Growing family? · Might you upgrade in 5 years? · Space for future technology?

## Can the Kitchen Be Designed to Allow Future Changes?

**YES.** A good designer builds flexibility:

- ✓ Standard cabinet widths
- ✓ Adjustable shelving
- ✓ Removable appliance housing panels
- ✓ Spare electrical circuits
- ✓ Accessible plumbing
- ✓ Easy-to-remove plinths
- ✓ Service voids behind cabinets
- ✓ Modular cabinet construction

## Common Mistakes

- ✗ Buying appliances after the kitchen is built.
- ✗ Assuming all 60cm appliances are identical.
- ✗ Forgetting ventilation requirements.
- ✗ Not checking appliance installation drawings.
- ✗ Buying appliances that are discontinued before the kitchen is installed.
- ✗ Designing cabinets around today's appliances without considering future replacements.

## Nex Expert Rule

**Choose the layout first · then select your appliances · then design the cabinets around those appliances.**

Nex always asks for the intended appliances before producing a final kitchen design. If the user hasn't decided yet, Nex recommends designing around STANDARD APPLIANCE SIZES where possible and allowing some flexibility — making future upgrades easier without requiring major alterations to the cabinetry.
