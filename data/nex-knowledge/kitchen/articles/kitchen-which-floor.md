# Which Floor Should the Kitchen Be On?

## The Short Answer

**For most modern homes, the kitchen is best located on the GROUND FLOOR.**

This provides easy access to garden · dining area · living room · utility room · garage · driveway. It also makes carrying shopping · entertaining guests · family life much easier.

## Ground Floor Kitchen

**✓ Advantages:** Easy access from the front door · easy to bring groceries inside · direct access to the garden · ideal for entertaining · works well with open-plan living · safer for carrying hot food · easier for children and older adults · no stairs to climb with shopping · better connection to outdoor dining · most popular layout.

**✗ Disadvantages:** Can reduce ground-floor living space · cooking smells may spread in open-plan homes if ventilation is poor · requires careful planning if the room also serves as a main circulation space.

## First Floor Kitchen

Common in townhouses · coastal homes · homes with exceptional views · flood-prone areas · some apartments.

**✓ Advantages:** Better views · more natural light · increased privacy · can free the ground floor for bedrooms · garages · utility spaces.

**✗ Disadvantages:** Carrying shopping upstairs · less convenient garden access · harder for entertaining outdoors · less accessible for some users.

## Basement Kitchen

Usually found in large period homes · restaurants · commercial buildings · secondary kitchens.

**✓ Advantages:** Keeps cooking away from main entertaining spaces · excellent for catering or food preparation · can include large storage and pantry areas.

**✗ Disadvantages:** Less natural light · more reliance on artificial lighting and ventilation · not ideal as the primary family kitchen in most homes.

## Split-Level Homes

Some homes have kitchens on a half level. Good connection between living areas · interesting architectural layouts · can follow sloping sites naturally.

## Open-Plan Ground Floor (most popular layout)

```
Garden
────────────────────────────
Kitchen Island
Dining Area
Living Area

```

The kitchen becomes the centre of family life.

## Best Location by Property Type

| Property Type | Best Kitchen Location |
|---|---|
| Family home | Ground floor |
| Bungalow | Ground floor |
| Cottage | Ground floor |
| Victorian terrace | Ground floor (often extended) |
| Georgian house | Ground floor (or lower ground floor in some layouts) |
| Modern self-build | Ground floor |
| Luxury open-plan home | Ground floor with island |
| Townhouse | First floor can work well |
| Apartment | Main living level |
| Coastal home | Upper floor if views are a key feature |

## Relationship With the Staircase

The kitchen and staircase are often visually connected.

**Modern design:** Front Door → Feature Staircase → Kitchen Island → Garden. The staircase becomes part of the kitchen's visual setting.

**Traditional design:** Front Door → Hallway → Kitchen → Garden. The staircase remains in the hall but should still coordinate with the kitchen if they're visible from one another.

## Nex Discovery Questions

**Lifestyle:** How many people live in the house? · Do you entertain regularly? · Young children? · Elderly family members? · Enjoy outdoor dining?

**Property:** New build? · Renovation? · Townhouse? · Bungalow? · Period property? · Sloping site?

**Access:** Where do you usually enter the house? · Where do you unload shopping? · Where's the garage? · Where's the garden? · Where do guests enter?

**Views:** Exceptional views? · Would an upstairs kitchen make better use of them? · Is privacy important?

## Nex Design Principle

**Recommend the kitchen location based on how people LIVE, not just on floor plans.**

Consider: daily movement through the home · carrying groceries · access to the garden · natural light · views · family life · accessibility · entertaining · future needs.

## Nex Expert Tip

For most homes, the ideal arrangement is:

- **Ground-floor kitchen** as the social hub.
- **Feature staircase** positioned so it enhances the entrance and complements the kitchen if they share sight lines.
- **Dining and living spaces** connected to the kitchen.
- **Utility room** nearby to keep laundry and household tasks separate.
- **Direct access to the garden** from the kitchen or dining area.

This creates a practical workflow while making the kitchen the heart of the home and allowing the staircase to serve as an architectural feature rather than simply a route between floors.
