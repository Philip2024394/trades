# Kitchen Cabinet Hinge Types

Cabinet hinges allow kitchen doors to open and close smoothly while supporting the weight of the door. The correct hinge depends on cabinet construction · door style · opening angle · intended use. Modern kitchens typically use concealed European-style hinges, while traditional furniture may use decorative exposed hinges.

## Concealed (European) Hinge

The most common kitchen hinge worldwide.

Hidden when the cabinet door is closed. A 35 mm cup is drilled into the back of the door, and the hinge is fixed to a mounting plate inside the cabinet.

**Features:** Fully concealed · adjustable in three directions · soft-close available · easy installation · quick door removal.

**Best for:** Modern · contemporary · shaker · handleless kitchens.

## Soft-Close Hinge

A concealed hinge with an integrated damper. Prevents doors slamming · quiet operation · protects cabinets · extends hinge life · premium feel. Standard on many modern kitchens.

## Clip-On Hinge

Allows doors to be removed without tools. Easy installation · quick servicing · fast door adjustment · popular with professional installers.

## Full Overlay Hinge

The cabinet door completely covers the cabinet side panel. Best for modern kitchens · slab doors · handleless designs. Produces minimal visible gaps.

## Half Overlay Hinge

Two cabinet doors share one cabinet side. Each door covers approximately half of the side panel. Common on double-door cabinets.

## Inset Hinge

The door sits inside the cabinet opening rather than covering it. Best for in-frame kitchens · handmade kitchens · traditional furniture. Creates a premium furniture appearance.

## Face Frame Hinge

Designed for cabinets with a face frame. Common in North American cabinetry · less common in Europe.

## Corner Cabinet Hinge

Special hinges used on angled or corner cabinets. Applications: 90° · 135° · 165° · blind corner cabinets.

## Wide Angle Hinge

Allows doors to open further than standard hinges. Opening angles: 110° · 125° · 155° · 165° · 170°. Ideal for pull-out storage systems.

## Zero Protrusion Hinge

The door does not project into the cabinet opening when fully open. Allows internal drawers to operate without obstruction.

## Pivot Hinge

Door rotates around top and bottom pivot points. Used on pantry doors · tall storage · bespoke cabinetry.

## Bi-Fold Hinge

Allows two doors to fold together. Common on corner units · appliance cabinets.

## Lift-Up Door Hinges

Used for wall cabinets opening upwards. Types: Gas strut · spring lift · soft lift · parallel lift · folding lift · vertical lift. Popular in contemporary kitchens.

## Stay Hinges

Hold lift-up cabinet doors open safely. Often combined with gas struts.

## Piano Hinge (Continuous Hinge)

Runs the full length of the door. Continuous support · strong construction · even load distribution. More common in furniture than kitchen cabinets.

## Decorative Butt Hinge

Traditional visible hinge. Often used on handmade kitchens · country kitchens · furniture pieces. Available in Brass · Black · Antique bronze · Stainless steel.

## Barrel Hinge

Hidden cylindrical hinge. Mostly used on bespoke furniture.

## Knife Hinge

Slim concealed hinge often found on luxury cabinetry. Produces a clean appearance.

## Common Opening Angles

| Hinge Type | Opening Angle |
|---|---|
| Standard Concealed | 95-110° |
| Wide Angle | 125° |
| Wide Opening | 155° |
| Extra Wide | 165° |
| Full Access | 170° |

## Hinge Adjustments

Most concealed hinges allow adjustment in three directions:

- **Side** — moves the door left or right for even gaps.
- **Height** — moves the door up or down.
- **Depth** — moves the door closer to or further from the cabinet.

This allows precise alignment after installation.

## Hinge Materials

Nickel-plated steel · stainless steel · solid brass · zinc alloy · black powder-coated steel · chrome-plated steel.

## Premium Hinge Features

Soft-close dampers · clip-on fixing · tool-free adjustment · integrated damping · push-to-open mechanism · silent closing · lifetime cycle testing · corrosion resistance.

## Choosing the Right Hinge

Consider: cabinet type (frameless or face frame) · door style (overlay/half overlay/inset) · door weight · door thickness · opening angle required · soft-close or standard · corner cabinet requirements · frequency of use · moisture resistance · budget.
