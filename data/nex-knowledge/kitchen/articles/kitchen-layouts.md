# Kitchen Layouts — Selection Guide

## The Nine Layouts

### 1 · Straight (Single-Wall) Kitchen

Everything fitted along ONE wall.

- **Best for:** apartments · studios · small homes · open-plan living where the kitchen is one wall of a larger room.
- **Room minimum:** 2.4m wall length.
- **Advantages:** space-saving · cost-effective · simple installation · easy workflow.
- **Trade-offs:** limited storage · workflow along a line rather than a triangle.

### 2 · Galley (Two Parallel Runs)

Two runs of cabinets facing each other with a walkway between.

- **Best for:** narrow homes · townhouses · apartments · rental refurbs.
- **Room minimum:** 2.2m width (allows 1.2m walkway between 600mm units); 2.6m preferred.
- **Advantages:** excellent work triangle · maximum storage per m² · efficient for a single cook.
- **Trade-offs:** hard to fit dining seating · not sociable · needs good ventilation.

### 3 · L-Shaped

Cabinets along two adjoining walls.

- **Best for:** family homes · open-plan spaces · medium-sized kitchens.
- **Room minimum:** 3.0m x 2.4m.
- **Advantages:** flexible layout · good traffic flow · room for a small dining table · works with adjoining living space.
- **Trade-offs:** corner cabinet needs a solution (Le Mans / carousel / diagonal).

### 4 · U-Shaped

Cabinets on three walls.

- **Best for:** larger kitchens · serious home cooks · homes with a dedicated kitchen room.
- **Room minimum:** 3.0m x 3.0m (allows 1.2m centre walkway).
- **Advantages:** maximum worktop · plenty of storage · excellent workflow · natural five-zone layout.
- **Trade-offs:** can feel enclosed · two corner solutions needed · no room for a dining table.

### 5 · G-Shaped

A U-shaped kitchen with an extended peninsula (creating a fourth partial wall).

- **Best for:** large kitchens · households that entertain but don't want an island.
- **Room minimum:** 3.5m x 3.5m.
- **Advantages:** enormous storage capacity · excellent workspace · family-friendly · defines the kitchen zone within an open-plan.
- **Trade-offs:** can feel enclosed · walk-in access via one opening only.

### 6 · Island Kitchen

Cabinets around the perimeter PLUS a freestanding island in the middle.

- **Best for:** large kitchens · open-plan homes · sociable cooking · households that entertain often.
- **Room minimum:** 3.5m x 3.5m (allows 900mm walkway around a 1500mm island); 4.0m x 4.0m preferred.
- **Island can include:** sink · hob · seating · storage · wine cooler · pop-up sockets.
- **Advantages:** luxury appearance · social cooking · additional workspace · natural boundary between kitchen and living zones · seating for 2-4.
- **Trade-offs:** requires generous room size · extractor becomes more complex (ceiling downdraft or island hood).

### 7 · Peninsula Kitchen

Like an island but ATTACHED to the main run of cabinets on one end.

- **Best for:** medium-sized rooms that can't fit a full island · smaller open-plan homes.
- **Room minimum:** 3.0m x 3.5m.
- **Advantages:** additional seating · more worktop · better space efficiency than an island in a smaller room · can hide services in the connected end.
- **Trade-offs:** only accessible from three sides · less flexible than a true island.

### 8 · Broken-Plan Kitchen

Kitchen partially separated from living space using GLASS PARTITIONS · TIMBER SCREENS · HALF-WALLS · FEATURE SHELVING.

- **Best for:** homes wanting the openness of open-plan but with defined zones · households where cooking smells and noise need containing.
- **Advantages:** keeps light and connection · blocks smells/mess better than fully open · creates definition without full separation.
- **Trade-offs:** requires design intention · partitions add cost · not suitable for very small rooms.

### 9 · Double-Island Kitchen

Two islands (typically one for prep/hob, one for seating/entertainment).

- **Best for:** very large luxury kitchens · homes with dedicated entertaining space.
- **Room minimum:** 5.0m x 5.0m.
- **Advantages:** separates cook zone from social zone · maximum flexibility for multiple simultaneous cooks · statement design.
- **Trade-offs:** requires a very large room · high cost · can feel over-designed in a household of only 1-2 people.

## The Space-Around-Island Rule

Every side of an island needs a walkway MINIMUM 900mm. Preferred:

- Cook side (hob or oven side) — **1000mm+** so appliance doors open fully.
- Prep side (sink side) — **1000mm** for comfortable working.
- Circulation side — **900mm** minimum.
- Seating side — **1000mm+** to allow chair pull-back behind seated diners.

Never squeeze below 900mm — the kitchen will feel cramped and appliance/cabinet doors will not open properly.

## Which Layout Wins by Room Size

| Room dimensions | Best layout options |
|---|---|
| Under 2.4m wide | Straight |
| 2.4-3.0m wide × 3-4m long | Galley OR L-shaped |
| 3.0-3.5m wide × 3-4m long | L-shaped OR small U-shaped |
| 3.5-4.5m each side | U-shaped OR Peninsula OR small Island |
| 4.5m+ each side | Island (preferred) OR G-shaped |
| 5.0m+ each side | Island OR Double-island |

## Composition with Broken-Plan Choice

Any of the above layouts can go BROKEN-PLAN by adding partial dividers between the kitchen and adjoining living space. Broken-plan is a treatment applied to a layout, not a separate layout itself.
