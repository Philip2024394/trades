# Kitchen Extractor Fan Types

A kitchen extractor fan (cooker hood · range hood · extraction hood) removes steam · grease · cooking odours · smoke · airborne particles. A correctly sized extractor improves indoor air quality · reduces condensation · protects kitchen cabinets · helps keep the kitchen clean.

## Chimney Extractor Hood

Wall-mounted above the hob with a decorative chimney section extending to the ceiling. Best for modern · traditional · feature cooking areas. Excellent extraction · wide range of styles · easy maintenance · strong visual feature.

## Angled Glass Extractor Hood

Modern wall-mounted hood with angled glass front. Increased head clearance · stylish appearance · easy-to-clean glass · popular in contemporary kitchens.

## Flat Glass Hood

Horizontal glass canopy above the cooking area. Best for modern homes · contemporary kitchens. Provides effective extraction while acting as a design feature.

## Canopy Extractor

Hidden inside a wall cabinet. Almost invisible · maintains clean cabinet lines · space saving · ideal for minimalist kitchens.

## Integrated Extractor

Completely concealed within kitchen cabinetry. Only the underside is visible. Best for handleless · modern · minimalist interiors.

## Telescopic Extractor

Slides out when required. Hidden when not in use · compact design · suitable for smaller kitchens.

## Island Extractor Hood

Suspended from the ceiling above a kitchen island. Central design feature · powerful extraction · ideal for island hobs. Often includes decorative lighting.

## Ceiling Extractor

Installed flush into the ceiling. Invisible appearance · quiet operation · luxury finish · ideal for open-plan living. Common in high-end kitchens.

## Downdraft Extractor

Rises from the worktop behind the hob when activated · retracts back into the cabinet when switched off. Hidden when not in use · uninterrupted sight lines · premium appearance. Popular in luxury island kitchens.

## Hob Integrated Extractor

Extraction system built directly into the induction hob. No overhead hood · modern appearance · excellent for open-plan homes · saves wall space. Growing rapidly in premium kitchen designs.

## Under Cabinet Extractor

Mounted beneath a wall cabinet. Affordable · easy installation · suitable for compact kitchens.

## Visor Extractor

Simple extractor with a projecting visor. Budget friendly · compact · easy installation. Common in apartments and rental properties.

## Built-In Extractor

Installed within bespoke cabinetry. Provides a seamless appearance.

## Extraction Methods

### Ducted Extraction

Air extracted outside through ducting. Best performance · removes heat · removes moisture · removes cooking odours. Recommended whenever external ducting is practical.

### Recirculating Extraction

Air passes through grease and carbon filters before returning to the room. Easier installation · no external vent required · suitable for apartments. Requires regular carbon filter replacement.

## Filter Types

- **Aluminium Grease Filter** — Washable · reusable · standard on most extractors.
- **Stainless Steel Filter** — Durable · dishwasher safe · premium finish.
- **Charcoal Filter** — Removes cooking odours in recirculating systems · must be replaced periodically.
- **Carbon Filter** — High-performance odour filtration where ducting is not available.
- **Plasma Filter** — Premium long-life filtration · excellent air purification · minimal maintenance.

## Extraction Speeds

Most extractor fans provide multiple speed settings: Low · Medium · High · Intensive Boost Mode. Boost mode is useful during heavy cooking.

## Control Types

Push Buttons · Electronic Buttons · Touch Controls · Gesture Control · Remote Control · Smart App Control · Voice Control.

## Lighting Options

Modern extractors may include LED strip lighting · spotlights · dimmable LEDs · adjustable colour temperature · automatic lighting.

## Noise Levels

Typical operating noise: Quiet 40-50 dB · Standard 50-65 dB · High Power 65-75 dB. Quieter models are preferred in open-plan kitchens.

## Common Widths

500 · 600 · 700 · 800 · 900 · 1000 · 1100 · 1200 mm. The hood should generally be at least as wide as the cooking hob.

## Popular Finishes

Stainless Steel · Black Glass · White Glass · Matt Black · Matt White · Brushed Steel · Gunmetal · Bronze · Integrated Cabinet Finish.

## Smart Features

Automatic extraction · hob-to-hood communication · air quality sensors · delayed shut-off · filter cleaning reminders · Wi-Fi connectivity · voice assistant compatibility.

## Choosing the Right Extractor

Consider: kitchen layout · hob location (wall or island) · ducted or recirculating installation · kitchen size · ceiling height · noise preference · cooking frequency · gas or induction hob · budget · preferred design style.
