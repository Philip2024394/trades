# How Much of My Renovation Budget Should Go to the Kitchen vs the Staircase?

There isn't a single *"correct"* percentage — it depends on the value of the house · the size of the renovation · the owner's priorities. But there are useful industry rules of thumb.

## Rule-of-Thumb Allocations

| Project Type | Typical Kitchen Share | Typical Staircase Share |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic refresh | 30-50% | 5-10% |
| Full renovation | 25-40% | 8-15% |
| Luxury renovation | 20-35% | 10-20% |
| New self-build | 15-30% | 5-12% |

## Example 1 · £100,000 Renovation

- **Kitchen:** £25,000-£40,000 (25-40%)
- **Staircase:** £8,000-£15,000 (8-15%)
- Bathrooms · flooring · decorating · electrical · plumbing · windows · landscaping · etc.: the remaining budget.

## Example 2 · Luxury Home (£300,000 Renovation)

- **Kitchen:** £60,000-£90,000
- **Staircase:** £30,000-£60,000

In premium homes, the staircase often becomes a major architectural feature rather than just a way to move between floors.

## Which Adds More Value?

- **Kitchen** usually has the biggest impact on everyday living and buyer appeal.
- **Staircase** often creates the strongest first impression because it's one of the first things people see when entering a home.

**They work best when designed together.**

## Nex Recommendation Logic

Instead of giving a fixed percentage, ask:

1. What is your total renovation budget?
2. Is this your forever home or are you planning to sell?
3. Are you building a new home or renovating?
4. Is the staircase visible from the entrance?
5. Is the kitchen open plan?
6. Which room do you spend the most time in?
7. Are you looking for maximum resale value or your dream home?

Then produce a recommended allocation like:

- **Kitchen:** 32%
- **Staircase:** 12%
- **Bathrooms:** 15%
- **Flooring:** 10%
- **Decorating:** 8%
- **Electrical & Lighting:** 7%
- **Plumbing & Heating:** 6%
- **Internal Doors & Joinery:** 5%
- **Contingency:** 5%

This personalized approach is much more useful than a one-size-fits-all answer.

## An Important Design Principle

A beautiful home is rarely created by spending everything on one feature. Many experienced designers aim for a **balanced investment** where the major visible elements complement each other:

- Kitchen: 25-35%
- Staircase: 8-15%
- Flooring: 8-12%
- Internal doors and joinery: 5-10%
- Lighting: 3-8%

This tends to produce a cohesive result, with the kitchen · staircase · flooring · doors · other joinery sharing the same level of quality and style — instead of one feature looking far more expensive than everything around it.

**This is a valuable design principle** because it encourages users to think about the home as a complete, coordinated project rather than a collection of unrelated purchases.
