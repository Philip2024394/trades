# Matching Your Kitchen With the Rest of Your Home

A kitchen should NOT be designed in isolation. It should complement the architecture · interior style · materials used throughout the home. A coordinated design creates a timeless appearance and often increases perceived quality and value.

## 1 · Internal Doors

**✓ Do:** Match the overall style (modern · shaker · traditional · contemporary) · keep door heights consistent throughout the home · coordinate timber species where exposed · use matching paint colours where appropriate · choose complementary handle finishes · repeat moulding profiles · consider glazed doors if the kitchen has glazed cabinets.

**✗ Don't:** Mix ornate traditional doors with ultra-modern slab kitchens unless intentional contrast · use different timber colours without a clear design reason · mix too many handle styles and finishes · choose doors that look noticeably cheaper than the kitchen.

## 2 · Flooring

**✓ Do:** Continue the same flooring into adjoining rooms where practical · choose flooring that suits the kitchen's style · balance warm timber with cooler worktop materials if needed · consider durability · maintenance · underfloor heating compatibility.

**✗ Don't:** Introduce multiple flooring changes in an open-plan area without a purpose · choose a floor that clashes with cabinet colours or timber tones · prioritise appearance over durability in high-traffic family kitchens.

## 3 · Wall Tiles and Splashbacks

**✓ Do:** Let the splashback complement the worktop and cabinetry · keep grout colours appropriate for the overall design · use feature tiles sparingly to create focal points.

**✗ Don't:** Combine several competing tile patterns in the same space · use very busy splashbacks with highly patterned worktops · follow short-lived trends unless you're happy to update.

## 4 · Staircase

**✓ Do:** Coordinate timber species and stain colours where visible together · repeat design details such as square or turned elements to suit the home's style · match metal finishes if both the staircase and kitchen feature exposed hardware.

**✗ Don't:** Pair a highly traditional staircase with an ultra-minimal kitchen without a broader design concept · treat the staircase as unrelated if it is visible from the kitchen or entrance.

## 5 · Wall Panelling

**✓ Do:** Use similar panel proportions and moulding styles · repeat paint colours or timber finishes where appropriate.

**✗ Don't:** Mix many different panelling styles in connected rooms · overdecorate every wall — allow plain surfaces to balance detailed joinery.

## 6 · Skirting and Architraves

**✓ Do:** Keep profiles consistent throughout the home · match heights and proportions where possible · coordinate paint finishes.

**✗ Don't:** Mix modern square skirting with elaborate Victorian mouldings unless restoring a period home with carefully planned contrasts.

## 7 · Worktops

**✓ Do:** Coordinate worktops with flooring · cabinetry · lighting · consider texture as well as colour · choose materials that suit how the kitchen will be used.

**✗ Don't:** Select a worktop based only on appearance without considering maintenance and durability · combine too many different stone or timber finishes in one space.

## 8 · Handles and Metal Finishes

**✓ Do:** Use one primary metal finish throughout connected spaces where practical · coordinate kitchen handles · taps · door handles · lighting · visible hardware. Examples: Brushed brass · Satin nickel · Matte black · Stainless steel · Bronze.

**✗ Don't:** Mix several unrelated metal finishes in one open-plan area · replace hardware individually without considering the overall look.

## 9 · Lighting

**✓ Do:** Use consistent colour temperature · layer ambient · task · accent lighting · highlight key architectural features such as islands and staircases.

**✗ Don't:** Mix very warm and very cool white lighting in adjoining spaces · rely on one central ceiling light.

## 10 · Paint Colours

**✓ Do:** Create a coordinated palette for the whole home · test colours in natural and artificial light · consider neighbouring rooms before making final choices.

**✗ Don't:** Choose every room independently without considering transitions · ignore how light changes colours throughout the day.

## Common Style Combinations

| Kitchen Style | Best With | Avoid Mixing With |
|---|---|---|
| Shaker | Shaker doors · oak floors · traditional staircases | Ultra-gloss acrylic interiors without a clear design concept |
| Modern slab | Flush doors · large-format tiles · floating staircases | Heavy carved mouldings and ornate period details |
| Traditional | Panelled doors · timber flooring · classic staircases | Minimalist industrial features unless intentionally contrasted |
| Industrial | Black metal · concrete · exposed brick · oak | Highly decorative classical joinery |
| Scandinavian | Light oak · white walls · simple doors | Dark ornate Victorian detailing |
| Farmhouse | Oak · stone · painted cabinetry · traditional joinery | High-gloss contemporary finishes |

## Top 10 Mistakes Homeowners Make

1. Buying the kitchen before planning the whole house.
2. Choosing flooring after the cabinets are installed.
3. Mixing too many timber colours.
4. Mixing unrelated hardware finishes.
5. Following trends without considering long-term appeal.
6. Ignoring how the staircase and kitchen look together.
7. Overusing feature materials.
8. Choosing style before considering function.
9. Not planning storage early enough.
10. Working with different designers who never coordinate.

## The Nex Whole-Home Design Rule

**Every major design decision should be checked against the REST of the home before being recommended.**

Before suggesting a kitchen, Nex asks:

- What style are your internal doors?
- What flooring do you have or plan to install?
- Is the staircase visible from the kitchen?
- Do you have wall panelling?
- What metal finish do you prefer?
- Are you renovating one room or the whole house?
- Do you want a timeless look or a more trend-led design?

By gathering this context, Nex can recommend a kitchen that feels like it belongs to the ENTIRE HOME, not just the room it's in.
