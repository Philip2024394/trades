# Should I Match My Kitchen With My Staircase?

**Yes**, in most homes you should coordinate the style of your kitchen and staircase — especially if they can both be seen from the same area. They don't have to be identical, but they should feel like they belong together.

The kitchen and staircase are often two of the largest design features in a home. When they share similar materials, colours, or design details, the home feels more balanced, cohesive, and professionally designed. You don't need to copy every detail, but using complementary finishes creates a natural flow between spaces.

## Match the Timber

If your staircase is made from natural oak, consider using oak elements in the kitchen: oak cabinet doors · oak breakfast bar · oak shelving · oak end panels · oak worktops (where suitable). If your staircase is walnut · ash · another timber, repeating that species in selected kitchen features helps create continuity.

## Match the Paint Colours

Painted staircases and kitchens work well together when they share a coordinated colour palette. Examples: White staircase with white kitchen · Sage green staircase with sage kitchen island · Navy staircase with navy feature units · Cashmere staircase with matching kitchen cabinetry · Graphite staircase with graphite accents.

Colours do not need to be used in the same proportions — one can be dominant while the other acts as an accent.

## Match the Design Style

Choose a consistent design language throughout the home.

| Staircase Style | Kitchen Style |
|---|---|
| Modern glass staircase | Handleless modern kitchen |
| Oak staircase | Contemporary oak kitchen |
| Painted Shaker staircase | Shaker kitchen |
| Traditional staircase | In-frame kitchen |
| Industrial staircase | Industrial kitchen |
| Farmhouse staircase | Country kitchen |
| Minimalist staircase | Minimalist kitchen |

## Match the Handrails and Worktops

Small details tie spaces together. Walnut handrail with walnut shelving · Oak handrail with oak breakfast bar · Black metal balusters with black kitchen handles · Brushed brass stair fittings with brass taps and handles. Subtle links create a unified look without making the spaces feel repetitive.

## Match Metal Finishes

Repeat metal finishes throughout the home: Matt black · Brushed brass · Satin nickel · Stainless steel · Bronze · Gunmetal. Using the same finish for stair hardware · kitchen handles · taps · lighting · door furniture creates consistency.

## Coordinate Glass

If your staircase features glass panels, echo this in the kitchen with glass cabinet doors · fluted glass units · smoked glass displays · glass splashbacks. Carries the design language from one area to another.

## Match Flooring

Flooring is one of the strongest links between kitchen and staircase. Popular choices: engineered oak · Luxury Vinyl Tile (LVT) · porcelain tiles · natural stone. Using the same flooring, or complementary flooring with a similar tone, creates continuous flow.

## Coordinate Wall Panelling

If your staircase has wall panelling, repeat similar detailing in the kitchen: tongue and groove panels · Shaker wall panels · slatted timber feature walls · painted panelling. Adds character while connecting different rooms.

## Match Lighting

Lighting should feel consistent throughout connected spaces. Examples: Black pendant lights with black stair balusters · Brass pendants with brass stair fittings · Warm LED lighting in both areas · Matching wall lights and under-cabinet lighting.

## When Should They Be Different?

It isn't always necessary to match everything. If the staircase is in a separate hallway, or if you're creating distinct spaces with different personalities, you can vary the design while keeping one or two common elements, such as timber colour or metal finish.

Example: A classic oak staircase leading to a contemporary kitchen can work beautifully if both use the same oak tone and black hardware.
