# Staircase Design · The Do's and Don'ts

## The Golden Rule

**Design the staircase as part of the whole home — not as a standalone product.**

## 1 · Match the Style of the House

**✓ Do:** Choose a staircase that suits the home's architecture · keep the design language consistent throughout the property · consider the age and character of the building.

Examples: Victorian house → traditional staircase + panelled doors + decorative mouldings. Modern home → floating staircase + glass balustrades + flush doors.

**✗ Don't:** Install an ultra-modern floating staircase in a period home without an overall design strategy · copy a design from a magazine without considering your home's architecture.

## 2 · Coordinate With Internal Doors

**✓ Do:** Match timber species where visible · repeat moulding styles · use compatible paint colours · coordinate door heights and proportions · match hardware finishes where practical.

**✗ Don't:** Mix ornate traditional doors with an ultra-minimal staircase unless it's an intentional design statement · use several unrelated timber colours in connected spaces.

## 3 · Coordinate With Flooring

**✓ Do:** Continue flooring through the hallway and into adjoining spaces where appropriate · match or complement the timber tone of the staircase · consider the transition between floor finishes · use stair nosings and trims that suit the flooring.

**✗ Don't:** Create unnecessary changes in flooring around the staircase · pair very warm timber stairs with very cool grey flooring without a deliberate design concept.

## 4 · Match the Kitchen (if visible from it)

**✓ Do:** Coordinate timber finishes · match metal finishes · repeat simple design details · ensure the styles complement one another.

**✗ Don't:** Treat the staircase and kitchen as unrelated if they share the same visual space.

## 5 · Coordinate With Wall Panelling

**✓ Do:** Use similar panel layouts · repeat moulding profiles · match paint finishes where appropriate.

**✗ Don't:** Use different panelling styles in adjoining areas without a reason.

## 6 · Coordinate With Skirting and Architraves

**✓ Do:** Match profile styles · keep heights consistent · use the same paint finish.

**✗ Don't:** Mix square modern skirting with elaborate Victorian staircase details unless restoring a period property.

## 7 · Coordinate With Handrails

**✓ Do:** Match the timber to other exposed woodwork where possible · choose a profile that suits the home's style · ensure the handrail is comfortable to grip.

**✗ Don't:** Select a handrail based only on appearance.

## 8 · Coordinate With Balustrades

**✓ Do:** Keep the balustrade style consistent with other metal or glass features · consider maintenance and safety.

**✗ Don't:** Mix several balustrade styles within one staircase.

## 9 · Coordinate With Lighting

**✓ Do:** Add lighting that improves safety and highlights the staircase · use the same colour temperature as nearby rooms · consider tread · wall · handrail lighting.

**✗ Don't:** Over-light the staircase so it dominates the room · mix warm and cool lighting in connected areas.

## 10 · Coordinate With Paint Colours

**✓ Do:** Choose colours that work with flooring · doors · walls · test paint in natural and artificial light.

**✗ Don't:** Pick a staircase colour without considering adjoining rooms.

## 11 · Coordinate With Metal Finishes

Brushed brass · Satin nickel · Matte black · Bronze · Stainless steel.

**✓ Do:** Repeat the chosen finish on stair brackets · door handles · kitchen handles · light fittings · sockets and switches (if visible).

**✗ Don't:** Mix multiple unrelated metal finishes in one open-plan space.

## 12 · Consider the View

Stand at the front door · the hallway · the kitchen · the landing · the living room. Ask: WHAT CAN I SEE?

If the staircase is visible from these locations, it should coordinate with everything in view.

## 13 · Think About Future Projects

**✓ Do:** Choose a staircase that will still work if you later add a new kitchen · new flooring · wall panelling · new doors · home office · media wall.

**✗ Don't:** Choose something so distinctive that it limits future design choices.

## 14 · Balance Statement and Simplicity

**✓ Do:** Let the staircase be a feature if the rest of the interior is relatively calm.

**✗ Don't:** Compete with too many other bold features: highly patterned flooring · busy wall coverings · elaborate ceilings · strongly veined worktops · multiple accent colours.

**One standout feature often has more impact than several competing ones.**

## 15 · Consider Maintenance

**✓ Do:** Choose finishes that suit your household. Hardwax oil for repairability on timber · durable paint systems for painted staircases · glass that's easy to clean if using glass balustrades.

**✗ Don't:** Select a finish solely for appearance if it won't suit your lifestyle.

## Common Staircase Mistakes

1. Choosing the staircase before planning the overall interior.
2. Mixing three or four different timber colours.
3. Using different metal finishes throughout the house.
4. Ignoring sight lines from the entrance.
5. Forgetting to plan lighting.
6. Overcomplicating the balustrade.
7. Ignoring storage opportunities beneath the stairs.
8. Copying trends that may date quickly.
9. Failing to consider long-term maintenance.
10. Not coordinating with future renovations.

## The Nex Staircase Compatibility Check

Before recommending a staircase, Nex asks:

**Home Style:** What style is your home? When was it built? Renovation or new build?
**Existing Features:** Flooring? Internal door style? Wall panelling? Kitchen style? Window colour?
**Materials:** Timber species preference? Metal finish preference? Painted or natural timber?
**Lifestyle:** Children or pets? Forever home? Resale value or personal taste?

## The Nex Staircase Design Principle

**A staircase should connect more than floors — it should connect the design of the entire home.**

Every recommendation is evaluated against the surrounding flooring · doors · kitchen · wall panelling · lighting · architectural style. When these elements work together, the staircase becomes a natural part of a cohesive home rather than an isolated feature.
