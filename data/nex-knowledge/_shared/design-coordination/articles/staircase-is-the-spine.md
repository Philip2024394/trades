# The Staircase is the Spine of the Home

Think of the staircase as the spine. Everything connects to it.

```
             Bedrooms
                 │
          Landing & Gallery
                 │
      Staircase (The Spine)
                 │
─────────────────────────────
 Kitchen │ Dining │ Living
                 │
             Garden
```

**The staircase physically connects the floors, while the kitchen emotionally connects the family.** Together they become the heart of the home.

## The Customer Journey

A homeowner rarely wakes up wanting *"a staircase."* They usually think:

- We're extending our home.
- We need more space.
- Our kitchen is too small.
- We want open-plan living.
- We want a better first impression.
- We want to increase our home's value.

The staircase becomes part of solving those goals.

## Why People Replace Their Staircase

It's often because they are ALREADY:

- Buying a new kitchen.
- Installing new flooring.
- Extending the house.
- Opening up walls.
- Renovating downstairs.
- Replacing internal doors.
- Modernising the property.

This is why Nex should ask about the WHOLE PROJECT, not just one product.

## Visual Sight Lines

One of the most important design concepts is the VIEW from key positions.

### Standing at the front door

Can you see: the staircase · the kitchen · the dining area · the garden · the island?

If yes → all of these elements should work together.

### Standing at the kitchen island

Can you see: the staircase · the living room · the entrance · the fireplace · the garden?

Everything in that view should feel coordinated.

## Coordinated Materials

Imagine a home where the same design language appears throughout:

| Element | Material |
|---|---|
| Stair treads | European Oak |
| Kitchen doors | European Oak |
| Internal doors | European Oak |
| Window seat | European Oak |
| Floating shelves | European Oak |
| Dining table | European Oak |
| Wall panelling | European Oak |

It feels intentional because the materials repeat consistently.

## Coordinated Details

Small details make a huge difference.

- **Shadow gaps** — same width on kitchen plinths · stair strings · media walls · floating shelves.
- **Radii** — same corner radius on kitchen island · handrail ends · window seats · worktops.
- **Timber grain** — consistent direction. Vertical grain on tall cabinets · newel posts · wall panelling. Horizontal grain on drawer fronts · shelves · bench seating.

## Lighting Layers

Instead of designing lighting room by room, design it for the WHOLE HOME. Ambient (general) · Task (cooking · reading · working) · Accent (display cabinets · artwork · staircase lighting) · Feature (island pendants · feature staircase · wine display).

## Sound

Luxury homes also consider acoustics. Will hard floors create echo? Would timber slats improve acoustics? Should soft furnishings reduce noise? Does the staircase amplify sound? Will the open-plan space be noisy?

## Smell

Often overlooked. If the staircase is OPEN to the kitchen: extractor quality becomes more important · cooking odours travel upstairs · ventilation strategy matters · airflow should be planned.

## Heating

Should underfloor heating continue through hallway · kitchen · dining · beneath the staircase? Consistent heating improves comfort and allows cleaner radiator-free walls.

## Storage Opportunities (staircase supports the kitchen)

Under-stair PANTRY (dry food) · WINE STORAGE (climate-controlled if desired) · COFFEE STATION (hidden behind pocket doors) · UTILITY CUPBOARD (cleaning products) · RECYCLING CENTRE (integrated bins) · PET STATION (food and water storage).

## Smart Home Integration

Staircase and kitchen can share automation: motion-activated stair lights · welcome-home lighting scenes · voice-controlled lighting · whole-home audio · security integration · energy monitoring.

## Accessibility

Design for LIFE, not just today. Will anyone have mobility issues? Is a future stairlift possible? Should handrails be prepared? Are walkways wide enough? Is the island accessible? Can appliances be reached comfortably?

## Sustainability

A coordinated project reduces waste. One timber supplier · one paint system · shared CNC nesting · shared transport · shared installation · less packaging · better material utilisation.

## The Core Principle

**"Design rooms individually, but design the home as one connected experience."**

That's the mindset that many homeowners appreciate and that differentiates bespoke joinery from off-the-shelf products.
