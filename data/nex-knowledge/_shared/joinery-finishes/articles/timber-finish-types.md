# Timber Finish Types

The finish applied to timber protects the wood from wear · moisture · stains · sunlight · everyday use, while enhancing its natural beauty. The choice of finish affects the appearance · durability · maintenance · lifespan of a staircase, kitchen, or piece of furniture.

**This article is shared** — it applies to Staircase Domain 001, Kitchen Domain 002, and every future domain that specifies timber products (doors, windows, wall panelling, furniture, bespoke joinery). The finishing systems and selection principles are the same across all of these domains.

## Natural Finish

A clear finish that preserves the timber's original colour and grain.

Enhances natural beauty · minimal colour change · highlights grain patterns · clean contemporary appearance. Best for oak staircases · ash kitchens · walnut furniture · Scandinavian interiors.

## Clear Lacquer Finish

Transparent protective coating. Durable · easy to clean · protects against moisture · UV resistant · available in different sheen levels. Commonly used on kitchens and staircases.

## Matt Lacquer

Very low sheen finish. Modern · natural looking · soft finish · minimal reflections. Very popular in luxury interiors.

## Satin Lacquer

Medium sheen finish. Easy cleaning · durable · soft elegant appearance. One of the most common finishes for kitchens.

## Gloss Lacquer

High reflective finish. Bright appearance · easy to wipe clean · modern styling. Common on high-gloss kitchen doors.

## Spray Painted Finish

Professional paint sprayed onto MDF or timber. Smooth factory finish · no visible brush marks · available in thousands of colours · excellent durability. Popular for painted Shaker kitchens.

## Hand Painted Finish

Applied using brushes or rollers. Traditional appearance · easy to repair · suitable for bespoke kitchens. Often chosen for period properties.

## Painted Finish

Solid colour finish covering the timber. Popular colours: White · Cashmere · Sage Green · Navy Blue · Graphite · Black · Cream · Stone Grey.

## Wood Stain Finish

Changes the timber colour while allowing the natural grain to remain visible. Common stain colours: Light Oak · Golden Oak · Medium Oak · Antique Oak · Walnut · Dark Walnut · Mahogany · Ebony · Black · Driftwood · Smoked Oak. Ideal for staircases and hardwood doors.

## Oiled Finish

Natural oils penetrate into the timber. Nourishes the wood · natural appearance · easy spot repairs · enhances grain. Common oils: Danish Oil · Hardwax Oil · Tung Oil · Linseed Oil.

## Hardwax Oil Finish

Combination of natural oils and waxes. Excellent durability · water resistant · easy maintenance · natural appearance · suitable for heavy traffic. Widely used on oak staircases and timber worktops.

## Wax Finish

Traditional protective coating. Warm natural feel · soft sheen · easy repairs · traditional appearance. Requires periodic re-waxing.

## Beeswax Finish

Natural wax made from beeswax. Best for antique furniture · traditional staircases · period homes. Produces a rich satin sheen.

## Danish Oil Finish

A penetrating oil finish. Easy application · good moisture resistance · natural appearance. Popular for oak furniture and stair parts.

## Polyurethane Finish

Highly durable clear coating. Excellent wear resistance · chemical resistant · moisture resistant · long lifespan. Ideal for busy family homes.

## Varnish Finish

Protective coating available in Matt · Satin · Gloss. Used on staircases · doors · furniture · timber panelling.

## Water-Based Lacquer

Modern environmentally friendly coating. Low odour · fast drying · non-yellowing · durable. Increasingly common in professional joinery.

## Solvent-Based Lacquer

Traditional high-performance coating. Extremely durable · excellent clarity · high chemical resistance. Often used in commercial joinery.

## UV Cured Finish

Factory-applied coating hardened using ultraviolet light. Extremely durable · consistent finish · scratch resistant · fast production. Common on factory-made kitchens.

## Brushed Finish

The timber grain is brushed before finishing. Textured feel · highlights grain · rustic appearance. Popular on oak.

## Wire Brushed Finish

Deeper brushing exposing the grain. Common in farmhouse and industrial interiors.

## Distressed Finish

Artificial ageing process. Creates worn edges · character marks · rustic appearance. Popular in country kitchens.

## Smoked Finish

Timber chemically treated to darken naturally. Usually applied to oak. Creates a luxury appearance.

## Limed Finish

White pigment emphasises the grain. Popular in Scandinavian interiors.

## Whitewashed Finish

Light white stain maintaining visible grain. Produces a bright, airy appearance.

## Cerused Finish

White pigment forced into the grain while leaving the surface natural. Premium decorative finish.

## Burnished Finish

Polished to create a rich lustre. Often found on luxury handcrafted furniture.

## High Gloss Finish

Mirror-like reflective finish. Popular for contemporary kitchens · designer furniture.

## Super Matt Finish

Ultra-low sheen finish. Creates a soft contemporary appearance.

## Anti-Fingerprint Finish

Special surface treatment reducing visible fingerprints. Common on premium kitchen doors.

## Colour Matched Finish

Timber finished to match flooring · doors · staircases · furniture · wall panelling. Creates a coordinated interior.

## Matching Timber Finishes Across a Home

Nex should recommend matching finishes across staircases · internal doors · kitchens · wall panelling · flooring · window boards · furniture · skirting boards · architraves. A consistent finish creates a harmonious and professionally designed interior.

## Finish Selection Guide

| Finish | Appearance | Durability | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural Oil | Natural | Medium | Regular re-oiling |
| Hardwax Oil | Natural Satin | High | Easy spot repair |
| Wax | Traditional | Medium | Periodic re-waxing |
| Lacquer | Clear | High | Low maintenance |
| Polyurethane | Clear | Very High | Low maintenance |
| Spray Paint | Solid Colour | High | Low maintenance |
| Wood Stain | Visible Grain | High | Low maintenance |
| UV Cured | Factory Finish | Excellent | Very low maintenance |

## Choosing the Right Finish

Consider: timber species · interior style · level of wear · moisture exposure · desired sheen (matt/satin/gloss) · ease of future maintenance · colour coordination · environmental conditions · domestic or commercial use · customer preference for a natural or painted appearance.
