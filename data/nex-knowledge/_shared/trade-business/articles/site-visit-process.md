# Site Visit Process

The site visit is the point at which a trade business goes from a paper enquiry to a real, measurable job. It is one of the most important stages of the workflow — everything downstream (quotation · manufacturing · installation) depends on the accuracy of this visit.

## Purpose of the Site Visit

A site visit answers questions that a customer cannot easily convey by phone or email:

- What are the actual room dimensions?
- What condition is the existing space in?
- What services are running through the wall / floor / ceiling?
- Are there access constraints (narrow doorways · stairs · parking)?
- Is the customer's expected finish realistic given the space?
- Are there any structural, safety, or regulatory concerns?

## Booking the Visit

- Confirm the customer's address, contact number, and preferred window.
- Ask if there are parking restrictions (residents' zones · timed bays · width limits).
- Confirm whether the property is occupied (tenant / owner / vacant).
- Book a time window rather than a specific minute — traffic + prior visits make exact timing hard.

## What to Bring

- Tape measure (5m minimum for most trades · 10m for larger rooms).
- Laser measure for room dimensions and ceiling heights.
- Camera or phone for photographs.
- Notepad + pencil (survey sheets should be pre-printed with the checklist for the trade).
- Level / spirit level.
- Torch (for cupboards, under-stair voids, lofts).
- Business cards / brochures to leave behind.
- ID card if the trade has one.
- Overshoes (protects the customer's flooring · builds trust).

## During the Visit

1. **Introduce yourself** — full name, business name, purpose of visit, expected duration.
2. **Ask the customer to walk you through their vision** before you start measuring. Listen more than you speak.
3. **Take exterior + interior photographs** — every wall, every corner, every existing feature.
4. **Measure systematically** — start at one point, work around the room, never assume symmetry.
5. **Check services** — sockets, switches, radiators, water supply, waste, gas points.
6. **Identify constraints** — floor level, ceiling drops, cornices, chimney breasts, alcoves.
7. **Discuss timeline expectations** — when does the customer need it? Any fixed dates (Christmas · sale of the house · baby arriving)?
8. **Discuss budget openly** — a quotation without a budget conversation is a guess.
9. **Explain the next step** — when will they receive the quotation? By what medium?

## What to Photograph

- Every wall in each direction (four walls per room minimum).
- Existing features (radiators · fireplaces · windows · doors · sockets · switches).
- Floor condition (level · type · protection needed).
- Ceiling (cornices · light fittings · smoke alarms).
- Access route from parking to work area (for materials + deliveries).
- Any specific challenges the customer mentioned.
- Fuse box location.
- Water stop-tap location.
- Boiler location.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not commit to a price on the visit. Prices need calm calculation, not on-the-spot guessing.
- Do not disparage another trade's previous work in front of the customer.
- Do not walk on carpet without overshoes.
- Do not use the customer's toilet without explicit permission.
- Do not touch furniture or personal items without asking.
- Do not leave without confirming the next step (quotation timing).

## Site Visit Fee (position)

Most install trades offer the initial site visit FREE for local jobs. Long-distance visits or complex specifications may carry a survey fee that is later credited against the quotation. Be transparent about this at booking time — surprise fees destroy trust.

## After the Visit

- Send a follow-up email within 24 hours confirming what was discussed.
- Send the quotation within the promised timeframe (2-5 working days is typical).
- If the quotation will be delayed, tell the customer BEFORE the deadline passes.

## The Confidence Rule

At the end of the site visit, the customer should feel:

- **Heard** — you understood their vision.
- **Confident** — you asked the right questions.
- **Respected** — you treated their home with care.
- **Informed** — they know what happens next.

That confidence is what wins the quote against competitors, more than price.

## Trade-Specific Overrides

Domain-specific site visit checklists override this shared process:

- **Staircase** — add floor-to-floor measurement · headroom check · light angle for balustrade viewing.
- **Kitchen** — add appliance survey · services location · ceiling height for tall units · water pressure.
- **Windows** — add opening dimensions · frame condition · glazing type · reveal depth.
- **Flooring** — add subfloor condition · moisture level · door clearance check.
