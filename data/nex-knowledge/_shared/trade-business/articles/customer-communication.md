# Customer Communication

Every install trade job is a relationship as much as a transaction. Customers rate their experience more on HOW they were treated than the perfection of the work. Communication is where that experience is built or broken.

## The Universal Rule

**Update the customer BEFORE they have to chase you.**

Silence is the largest source of trust loss in install trades. A customer who hasn't heard from you assumes the worst. A 30-second WhatsApp message on Wednesday afternoon prevents an angry phone call on Friday morning.

## The Communication Cadence

For every active job, the customer expects:

| Stage | Frequency | Medium |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiry → Site Visit | Same-day acknowledgement | Email + SMS confirming appointment |
| After Site Visit | Within 24 hours | Email summarising what was discussed + expected quote date |
| Quotation | Delivered by promised date | Email with PDF + follow-up call if >£5k |
| After Acceptance | Within 48 hours | Email confirming order + expected timeline + deposit invoice |
| During Manufacturing | Weekly update | Email or SMS · include photo if applicable |
| Pre-Install | 1 week before + 24hr before | Phone call + confirmation SMS |
| During Install | Daily start-of-day + end-of-day | SMS or WhatsApp |
| Completion | Same-day | In-person walkthrough + email confirming completion |
| Aftercare | 1 week · 1 month · 6 months | Email or SMS check-in |

Deviating from this cadence needs proactive communication ("running 3 days late on manufacturing — new install date is X").

## Medium Selection

- **Phone call** — bad news · complex explanations · large amounts of money · scheduling changes.
- **Email** — formal confirmations · quotations · invoices · anything that needs a record.
- **SMS / WhatsApp** — short updates · appointment reminders · quick photos · running-late warnings.
- **In-person** — quote presentation (for large jobs) · completion handover · anything emotional.
- **Never** — leaving voicemails as the only communication for important news · pretending you didn't see a message.

## The Bad News Protocol

When something goes wrong (delay · unexpected cost · material issue):

1. **Tell the customer immediately.** Not tomorrow. Not "when we have more info." Immediately.
2. **Own the problem.** Even if a supplier caused it, you own the customer's experience.
3. **State what happened + what you're doing about it + expected resolution.**
4. **Do not offer excuses.** Explanations are fine; excuses are not.
5. **Update again when the situation changes.**

Example: *"Just got word from our factory — the walnut sheet had a defect and we can't use it. I've re-ordered from a secondary supplier · adds 5 working days · your install date moves from the 15th to the 22nd. I'll confirm the new date by Friday. I'm sorry for the extra wait."*

That's honest, owned, specific, and re-schedules a next touchpoint. Never send anything vaguer than that when things go wrong.

## The Response Time Standard

- Enquiry → response within **4 working hours**.
- Question during active job → response within **2 hours** during working hours.
- Complaint → response within **1 hour**, resolution plan within **24 hours**.
- Never let a customer message sit >24 hours without at least an acknowledgement.

## Handling Complaints

Complaints are a chance to build trust, not lose it. The protocol:

1. **Acknowledge within 1 hour.** Even just "I've seen this — I'll come back to you today with a plan."
2. **Listen fully before responding.** Never interrupt or defend prematurely.
3. **Acknowledge what's fair in their complaint.** Even partial acknowledgement disarms.
4. **Propose a specific remediation.** Not "we'll look into it" — a real action with an owner and date.
5. **Follow through fully.** A promise made during complaint handling that isn't kept destroys the customer permanently.

## The Communication Log

Every customer touchpoint is logged in the Workspace CRM (Phase F). Log entries include:

- Date + time.
- Channel (call · email · SMS · in-person).
- Direction (inbound / outbound).
- Summary of what was said.
- Any actions arising + owner.

The log protects both the trade business AND the customer. Composes with First Law (every commitment becomes a visible object).

## What Customers Actually Want

Not perfect work delivered silently. They want:

- To know what's happening.
- To know when things will happen.
- To be told BEFORE things change.
- To be treated with respect in their home.
- To feel like their job matters to you.

Meeting those five expectations wins referrals more than technical craftsmanship (which they can't judge anyway).

## Trade-Specific Overrides

Domain-specific communication rhythms may override this shared process:

- **Staircase** — during installation, daily photos are essential (customer often can't use the stairs during install and wants to see progress).
- **Kitchen** — pre-install customer briefing on what to expect during the disruption (typically 2-3 weeks of no cooking).
- **Windows** — communication around weather (installations can slip during heavy rain).
