# NEX Knowledge Model — The Birth of a Staircase

> **A staircase does not begin as a staircase.**
>
> **It begins as an idea.**

## LEVEL 1 · VISION

Everything begins with the customer's vision.

Examples:
- I want something beautiful
- I want something traditional
- I want something contemporary
- I want something luxurious
- I want something modern
- I want something suitable for a family home
- I have a very small space
- I want to maximise the natural light
- I don't know what I want

*At this stage there is no staircase. There is only the customer's vision and requirements.*

---

## LEVEL 2 · APPLICATION

The staircase now needs a purpose.

Examples:
- Domestic
- Commercial
- Mezzanine
- Loft conversion
- Basement
- Garden room
- Feature staircase
- Main staircase
- Secondary staircase

*NEX now understands where and why the staircase will be used.*

---

## LEVEL 3 · STAIRCASE TYPE

The staircase now begins to take shape.

Examples:
- Straight Flight
- Quarter Turn
- Half Turn
- Curved
- Spiral
- Helical
- Space Saver

*The staircase now has a form.*

---

## LEVEL 4 · STRUCTURE

How is this staircase constructed?

Examples:
- Closed String
- Single Cut String
- Double Cut String
- Floating
- Cantilever
- Mono Stringer
- Twin Stringer
- Steel Spine
- Open Riser

*NEX now understands how the staircase will be built.*

---

## LEVEL 5 · MATERIALS

What materials will be used?

Examples:
- Oak
- Walnut
- Pine
- Ash
- Beech
- Maple
- Steel
- Glass
- Stainless Steel
- Composite materials

*NEX now understands what the staircase will be made from.*

---

## LEVEL 6 · COMPONENTS

The staircase is now broken down into its individual parts.

Examples:
- Treads
- Risers
- Strings
- Newels
- Handrails
- Balusters
- Base Rails
- Wedges
- Angle Blocks
- Brackets
- Decorative components

*The staircase does not yet exist. The individual parts now exist.*

---

## LEVEL 7 · FEATURES

Additional design features are selected.

Examples:
- Glass balustrades
- Open risers
- LED lighting
- Bullnose steps
- Stainless steel inserts
- Decorative details
- Contemporary handrails
- Feature newel posts

*These are enhancements to the staircase rather than the staircase itself.*

---

## LEVEL 8 · ASSEMBLY

The staircase begins to exist physically.

Typical sequence:
- Strings assembled
- Treads fitted
- Risers fitted
- Wedges fitted
- Angle blocks fitted

*At this stage the staircase has become structurally complete.*

---

## LEVEL 9 · STAIRCASE ASSEMBLY

The remaining assemblies are completed.

Examples:
- Newel posts fitted
- Base rails fitted (where required)
- Handrails fitted
- Balusters fitted
- Decorative features installed
- Lighting installed
- Final adjustments completed

*The staircase is now substantially complete.*

---

## LEVEL 10 · FINISHING

The final appearance is completed.

Examples:
- Sanding
- Painting
- Lacquering
- Oiling
- Waxing
- Protective finishes
- Final detailing

*The staircase is now ready for use.*

---

## LEVEL 11 · FINISHED STAIRCASE

The customer's original vision has become reality.

The finished staircase is not one piece of knowledge. It is the truthful collaboration of every specialist:

- Vision specialist
- Application specialist
- Staircase type specialist
- Structure specialist
- Material specialist
- Component specialist
- Feature specialist
- Installation specialist
- Finishing specialist
- Customer FAQ specialist

---

## CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE

> **NEX must understand that a staircase is never one thing.**
>
> A staircase begins as a customer's vision, becomes a series of truthful design decisions, progresses through stages of construction and assembly, and finally becomes a finished staircase.
>
> **NEX must never think in products.**
>
> **NEX must think in relationships.**
>
> The finished staircase is simply the final expression of many specialists working together.

## Assembly-stage awareness (companion principle)

> **NEX must understand not only what something is, but where it exists within the staircase and at what stage of construction it belongs.**

The String specialist doesn't just know what a string is — it knows where the string belongs in the lifecycle. The Handrail specialist knows when it enters the assembly process. The Installation specialist knows the usual sequence in which those specialists come together.

Every component and specialist carries additional context:
- When they are used
- What they connect to
- Whether they are structural or decorative
- Whether they belong to the staircase assembly or the balustrade assembly
- Whether they are optional or typical for a particular staircase construction

Eventually, NEX stops seeing:

> String + Tread + Newel + Handrail

and starts seeing:

> **A staircase being born, one truthful assembly stage at a time.**

## What this document IS NOT

- Not runtime code
- Not a build directive for assembly-stage-aware routing
- Not a proposal to author "String at Level 8" atoms into existing modules
- Not a specialist creation authorization

## What this document IS

A shared understanding of how a staircase actually comes into being · a compass for future specialist authoring · a reminder that every specialist must eventually know where it fits in the lifecycle.

When future Materials, Components, Balustrade, Style, Application specialists are authored, this model informs the "where does this fit in the lifecycle" awareness each specialist should carry.

The constitution stays the same. The pace stays the same. Reality still speaks. This is the picture the specialists eventually paint together.
