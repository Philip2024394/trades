# Homeowner FAQ List · Refacing & Lighting

Questions homeowners are likely to ask · drawn from evidence sprint · with published source coverage where available.

## section_1_understanding_refacing · what_is_refacing

- Refute: "Refacing = cosmetic change with no regulatory consequence — regulations still apply when balustrades or handrails are replaced."
- Refute: "Refacing = same as replacement in appearance — the underlying carriage geometry is inherited; a poorly-set staircase remains poorly set."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · parts_that_can_be_refaced

- Refute: "All parts are refaceable — in fact, the carriage (stringers/strings) is typically encased rather than replaced, meaning any structural fault is inherited."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · reface_vs_replace_decision

- Refute: "Refacing is always cheaper — for a badly-decayed or non-compliant staircase, refacing over the top can trap defects and force replacement within a few years."
- Refute: "Refacing can fix a squeaky staircase — squeaks originate in the carriage/wedges/joints, not the visible face."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · when_refacing_not_recommended

- Refute: "If it looks OK from above, it's fine to reface. Most structural failure originates in the underside carriage, wedges and joints — invisible from the walking surface."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · structural_defects_hidden_by_refacing

- Refute: "New face = new staircase. Refacing preserves inherited condition of everything beneath."
- Refute: "A rotted tread can be 'sealed in' by cladding. Trapped moisture accelerates decay."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · typical_duration

- Refute: "'One-day' claims typically refer to walkable-after-drying time, not total works including balustrade change and finishing."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · planning_permission

- Refute: "Internal work never needs consent — in listed buildings, ANY alteration to fabric can require consent."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · building_regulations_impact

- Refute: "Cladding = cosmetic = no regs. Adding thickness to treads can change the effective going and rise ratio and may push a compliant staircase out of compliance."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · property_value_impact

- Refute: "Refurbishment always adds value. Poor-quality overlay visible to a buyer's eye may reduce perceived quality of the whole house."

## section_1_understanding_refacing · lifespan_after_refacing

- Refute: "10-year warranty = 10-year expected life. Warranties are minimums, not lifetime estimates."

## section_2_materials · european_oak.maintenance

- Similar to American White Oak: oil or lacquer finish; oil requires periodic re-application; lacquer refinished at wear points. Tannin bleed on new installs discussed by UK trade sources.

## section_2_materials · european_oak.cost_drivers

- Cost driver themes: source (French/German/Baltic/UK), FSC/PEFC certification premium, board length/width, prime-vs-character grade, kiln vs air-dry. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · ash.durability

- Ash described as strong, tough and shock-resistant with excellent bending characteristics. Suitable for high-traffic residential staircases; BS EN 350 classification 'not durable' for outdoor exposure

## section_2_materials · ash.maintenance

- Sources describe ash as taking stain and lacquer readily; open grain may require grain-fill for gloss finish.

## section_2_materials · ash.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: source (European/American), grade, ash dieback affecting European supply chain, board dimension. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · walnut.maintenance

- Sources note walnut oxidises to a slightly lighter warmer tone over years of UV exposure. Oil finishes deepen tone; lacquer preserves original tone.

## section_2_materials · walnut.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: premium species relative to oak/ash/beech; grade (FAS premium significant), figure (curly/burl figure carries significant premium), board width. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · beech.appearance

- European Beech (Fagus sylvatica) described in TRADA and UK trade sources as pale pinkish-cream to biscuit, fine even texture, straight grain, minimal figure. Steamed beech has warmer pink-brown tone.

## section_2_materials · beech.durability

- Beech described as hard and abrasion-resistant; BS EN 350 class 'not durable' for outdoor exposure but very suitable for interior stair use. Sources note high moisture-movement sensitivity so joinery 

## section_2_materials · beech.maintenance

- Beech takes stain, lacquer and oil well; fine grain gives smooth uniform surface, forgiving for sanding and refinishing.

## section_2_materials · beech.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: European supply widely available; sourced from managed forests (sustainable regrowth exceeds harvest); grade and steaming premium. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · maple.durability

- Hard Maple Janka ~1450 lbf — one of the hardest domestic hardwoods (AHEC/Top Tread). Excellent abrasion resistance for high-traffic stair treads.

## section_2_materials · maple.maintenance

- Very dense closed grain gives smooth surface; takes lacquer well; harder to stain evenly (blotching risk) compared with oak.

## section_2_materials · maple.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: North American supply; hard vs soft maple grade differential significant; figure (birds-eye, curly) commands premium. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · pine.durability

- Pine is a softwood, described as significantly softer than oak/ash/beech; not durable in BS EN 350 terms; commonly used for economy staircases where finish will be painted or where treads will be carp

## section_2_materials · pine.maintenance

- Sources describe pine as accepting paint or stain but resin bleed at knots can require knotting solution primer; softer surface dents readily.

## section_2_materials · pine.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: dominant UK domestic staircase softwood; supply from Scandinavia / Baltic; grade (Unsorted vs V/VI) drives price. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · sapele.durability

- Sapele described as very durable and dimensionally stable; harder than oak; BS EN 350 class 'moderately durable' to 'durable'; excellent for high-traffic stair applications.

## section_2_materials · sapele.maintenance

- Polishes to high sheen; deep-toned finishes stain well; interlocked grain can produce tear-out during machining requiring sharp tools and careful sanding.

## section_2_materials · sapele.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: African supply with CITES scrutiny (SAP/CITES II Appendix listing for Sapele since 2022 — supply chain compliance premium); board dimension; figure premium for pronounced ribbon-stripe. 

## section_2_materials · tulipwood.maintenance

- Sources consistently describe tulipwood as the highest-quality paint substrate among affordable hardwoods; smooth surface holds crisp painted finish; softer surface dents more readily than oak.

## section_2_materials · tulipwood.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: AHEC / NHLA graded lumber, widely available in 4/4 to 16/4 thicknesses; economical relative to oak/ash/walnut; USD/GBP freight and grade drive price. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · mdf.durability

- MDF is described as vulnerable to water/moisture and prone to splitting at edges. Not suitable for exposed treads. Adequate for painted risers and hidden substrate under veneer.

## section_2_materials · mdf.maintenance

- Painted MDF surfaces refinish readily; water damage typically irreparable (fibre swelling); edge chip repair requires filling.

## section_2_materials · mdf.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: economical relative to solid timber; moisture-resistant (MR-MDF) and fire-retardant (FR-MDF) grades carry premium; sheet size and thickness. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · veneered_products.maintenance

- Damaged veneer typically requires panel replacement rather than repair. Factory-lacquered surfaces are wipe-clean; scuff points may be spot-refinished.

## section_2_materials · veneered_products.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: veneer species (oak/walnut/maple), veneer thickness, core substrate, factory-finish quality, matched-set premium for grain matching across a full flight. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · engineered_timber.durability

- Engineered timber described as more dimensionally stable than solid timber under humidity swings; wear-layer thickness governs number of refinish cycles; core plywood eliminates warping and cupping.

## section_2_materials · engineered_timber.maintenance

- Wear-layer determines lifetime: 2mm allows one or two light sandings, 3-6mm allows more. Once through wear-layer, panel is spent.

## section_2_materials · engineered_timber.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: wear-layer thickness, wear-layer species, core plywood quality (Baltic birch premium), factory finish, board dimension. Industry-cited savings of 50-65% versus equivalent solid. No speci

## section_2_materials · hardwood_overlays.durability

- System durability governed by wear-layer/veneer specification (see engineered_timber and veneered_products). Manufacturers cite 10-year product guarantees (StairFurb).

## section_2_materials · hardwood_overlays.maintenance

- Pre-finished factory lacquer surface described as low-maintenance; edge/nosing scuff highest wear point; delamination failure if adhesive bond fails.

## section_2_materials · hardwood_overlays.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: species (oak most economical, walnut premium), veneer thickness, kit inclusion (adhesive/nosing/scotia), stair count. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · laminate_systems.durability

- Sources describe laminate as very impact and scratch resistant on the wear layer, but with lower grip than carpet or textured vinyl — slip risk described as higher without anti-slip nosing.

## section_2_materials · laminate_systems.maintenance

- Wipe-clean surface; individual panel replacement possible if seams remain intact; water ingress at seams can swell HDF core.

## section_2_materials · laminate_systems.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: AC rating (AC3-AC6 durability class), print quality/embossing, core density, brand. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · vinyl_coverings.durability

- Sources describe vinyl as waterproof and moderately durable; slip resistance described as good with textured surface finish; not recommended for outdoor stairs.

## section_2_materials · vinyl_coverings.maintenance

- Wipe-clean surface; hygienic in wet/muddy-shoe household context; individual damaged pieces replaceable.

## section_2_materials · vinyl_coverings.cost_drivers

- Cost drivers: wear-layer thickness, LVT vs sheet vinyl, colour/pattern, backing type. No specific prices.

## section_2_materials · stone_stair_cladding.durability

- All natural stones described as extremely durable for stair use; granite hardest and most resistant to scratching; marble softer with etching risk from acidic spills; slate resistant to fire.

## section_2_materials · stone_stair_cladding.maintenance

- Marble requires sealing and pH-neutral cleaning; granite low-maintenance; slate texture aids slip resistance but needs sealing; polished surfaces may need slip-treatment for Part K compliance.

## section_2_materials · metal_components.maintenance

- Stainless and anodised aluminium wipe-clean; powder-coated components very low maintenance; painted mild steel requires periodic repaint at wear points.

## section_2_materials · glass_components.maintenance

- Wipe-clean surface; smudges/fingerprints visible and require regular attention; sealant/gasket at fixing points requires inspection; damaged panels typically require full replacement.
