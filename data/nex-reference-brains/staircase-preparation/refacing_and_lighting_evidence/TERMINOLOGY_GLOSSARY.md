# Terminology Glossary · Refacing & Lighting

Terms encountered across evidence · brief topic-level descriptions of what sources discuss (NOT formal definitions).

**Note:** Formal definitions require certified expert authoring. This glossary maps terms to source coverage only.

## american white oak.appearance

American White Oak (Quercus alba) is described in AHEC/TRADA and UK timber-supplier sources as pale-brown to biscuit-cream sapwood with light-to-medium-brown heartwood, closed pores plugged with tyloses, straight grain and medium-to-coarse texture with a moderate flame figure on quarter-sawn faces.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## american white oak.durability

American White Oak is described as Janka ~1350 lbf; closed-pore structure with tyloses gives high water and decay resistance; BS EN 350 classification 'durable' (similar to European Oak). Sources consistently describe it as suitable for high-traffic domestic staircases.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## american white oak.maintenance

Sources discuss oak stair components as low-maintenance when factory-lacquered or oil-finished; oiled surfaces need periodic reapplication; lacquered surfaces need cosmetic refinishing at scuff points. Water resistance is high due to tyloses.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## american white oak.cost drivers

Cost driver themes documented in UK timber-trade sources: kiln-drying, grade (FAS, Sel & Btr, No.1 Common per NHLA), quarter-sawn vs plain-sawn premium, board width, and stability of USD/GBP freight costs. NO specific prices per NEX HARD LAW.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## european oak.appearance

European Oak (Quercus robur / petraea) described in UK timber-trade and TRADA sources as light-to-medium honey-brown heartwood, more pronounced medullary rays than American White Oak, straight-grained, medium-to-coarse texture. Duffield Timber notes European Oak often carries more character (knots, cathedral figure) than American White Oak.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## european oak.durability

European Oak measured Janka ~1120 lbf (slightly softer than American White Oak per Duffield/Hardtwood); BS EN 350 class 'durable'; high tannin content aids insect/decay resistance. Sources describe European Oak as slightly more resistant to weather/UV than American White Oak but slightly less impact-resistant.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## european oak.maintenance

Similar to American White Oak: oil or lacquer finish; oil requires periodic re-application; lacquer refinished at wear points. Tannin bleed on new installs discussed by UK trade sources.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## european oak.cost drivers

Cost driver themes: source (French/German/Baltic/UK), FSC/PEFC certification premium, board length/width, prime-vs-character grade, kiln vs air-dry. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## ash.appearance

Ash (Fraxinus excelsior / F. americana) is described in Dyfed Richards, Stairway Joinery and TRADA-referenced sources as pale-cream to light-brown, prominent straight open grain, high visual similarity to oak with less pronounced medullary ray. Stains readily to darker tones.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## ash.durability

Ash described as strong, tough and shock-resistant with excellent bending characteristics. Suitable for high-traffic residential staircases; BS EN 350 classification 'not durable' for outdoor exposure but adequate for interior stair use.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## ash.maintenance

Sources describe ash as taking stain and lacquer readily; open grain may require grain-fill for gloss finish.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## ash.cost drivers

Cost drivers: source (European/American), grade, ash dieback affecting European supply chain, board dimension. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## walnut.appearance

American Black Walnut (Juglans nigra) described by AHEC, Stairway Joinery, Dyfed Richards as rich dark chocolate-brown heartwood with cream sapwood, straight-to-wavy grain, medium-fine texture. European Walnut variants are typically lighter with more figure. Dark tone consistently framed as 'luxury/premium'.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## walnut.durability

Walnut is described as slightly softer than oak/ash but more than adequate for domestic staircases; BS EN 350 class 'moderately durable'. Sources describe it as suitable for residential feature staircases rather than heaviest-traffic commercial.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## walnut.maintenance

Sources note walnut oxidises to a slightly lighter warmer tone over years of UV exposure. Oil finishes deepen tone; lacquer preserves original tone.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## walnut.cost drivers

Cost drivers: premium species relative to oak/ash/beech; grade (FAS premium significant), figure (curly/burl figure carries significant premium), board width. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## beech.appearance

European Beech (Fagus sylvatica) described in TRADA and UK trade sources as pale pinkish-cream to biscuit, fine even texture, straight grain, minimal figure. Steamed beech has warmer pink-brown tone.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## beech.durability

Beech described as hard and abrasion-resistant; BS EN 350 class 'not durable' for outdoor exposure but very suitable for interior stair use. Sources note high moisture-movement sensitivity so joinery detailing matters.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## beech.maintenance

Beech takes stain, lacquer and oil well; fine grain gives smooth uniform surface, forgiving for sanding and refinishing.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## beech.cost drivers

Cost drivers: European supply widely available; sourced from managed forests (sustainable regrowth exceeds harvest); grade and steaming premium. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## maple.appearance

Hard Maple (Acer saccharum) described in AHEC and stair-specialist sources as near-white to cream-white heartwood, sometimes with slight reddish-brown tinge, very fine consistent grain, minimal figure. Sugar/Rock Maple is the primary joinery variant.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## maple.durability

Hard Maple Janka ~1450 lbf — one of the hardest domestic hardwoods (AHEC/Top Tread). Excellent abrasion resistance for high-traffic stair treads.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## maple.maintenance

Very dense closed grain gives smooth surface; takes lacquer well; harder to stain evenly (blotching risk) compared with oak.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## maple.cost drivers

Cost drivers: North American supply; hard vs soft maple grade differential significant; figure (birds-eye, curly) commands premium. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## pine.appearance

Scots Pine / Redwood (Pinus sylvestris) described in Multi-Turn UK stair-supplier sources as pale-golden to light-red heartwood with visible resinous knots. 'Whitewood' (spruce group) paler than redwood. Slow-grown Scandinavian pine gives tighter grain.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## pine.durability

Pine is a softwood, described as significantly softer than oak/ash/beech; not durable in BS EN 350 terms; commonly used for economy staircases where finish will be painted or where treads will be carpeted.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## pine.maintenance

Sources describe pine as accepting paint or stain but resin bleed at knots can require knotting solution primer; softer surface dents readily.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## pine.cost drivers

Cost drivers: dominant UK domestic staircase softwood; supply from Scandinavia / Baltic; grade (Unsorted vs V/VI) drives price. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## sapele.appearance

Sapele (Entandrophragma cylindricum) described in Timbersource, iWood and TRADA-referenced sources as medium-to-dark reddish-brown heartwood, interlocked grain giving pronounced ribbon-stripe figure on quarter-sawn faces, fine even texture. Consistently described as a mahogany substitute.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## sapele.durability

Sapele described as very durable and dimensionally stable; harder than oak; BS EN 350 class 'moderately durable' to 'durable'; excellent for high-traffic stair applications.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## sapele.maintenance

Polishes to high sheen; deep-toned finishes stain well; interlocked grain can produce tear-out during machining requiring sharp tools and careful sanding.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## sapele.cost drivers

Cost drivers: African supply with CITES scrutiny (SAP/CITES II Appendix listing for Sapele since 2022 — supply chain compliance premium); board dimension; figure premium for pronounced ribbon-stripe. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## tulipwood.appearance

American Tulipwood / Yellow Poplar (Liriodendron tulipifera) described in AHEC, TRADA (via wooduchoose), British Hardwoods, Timbersource and ProBoard UK sources as pale cream-white sapwood with olive-green to grey-green heartwood streaked purple/brown/pink. Fine straight grain, low visual figure. Often described as the go-to paint-grade hardwood because grain does not raise through paint.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## tulipwood.durability

Tulipwood is described as fairly soft and lightweight (~510 kg/m³ dried) — softer than oak/ash/beech/maple. BS EN 350 class 'not durable' but adequate for interior joinery. Best suited for painted stair components rather than exposed high-traffic treads.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## tulipwood.maintenance

Sources consistently describe tulipwood as the highest-quality paint substrate among affordable hardwoods; smooth surface holds crisp painted finish; softer surface dents more readily than oak.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## tulipwood.cost drivers

Cost drivers: AHEC / NHLA graded lumber, widely available in 4/4 to 16/4 thicknesses; economical relative to oak/ash/walnut; USD/GBP freight and grade drive price. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## mdf.appearance

Medium Density Fibreboard is a manufactured wood-fibre panel; base MDF is uniformly dense biscuit-brown with smooth machined edges. Not intended as a finished surface — always painted, laminated or veneered. Widely used as core substrate for oak-veneered stair risers and cladding panels in UK stair-parts range (Pear Stairs, Cheshire Mouldings).

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## mdf.durability

MDF is described as vulnerable to water/moisture and prone to splitting at edges. Not suitable for exposed treads. Adequate for painted risers and hidden substrate under veneer.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## mdf.maintenance

Painted MDF surfaces refinish readily; water damage typically irreparable (fibre swelling); edge chip repair requires filling.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## mdf.cost drivers

Cost drivers: economical relative to solid timber; moisture-resistant (MR-MDF) and fire-retardant (FR-MDF) grades carry premium; sheet size and thickness. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## veneered products.appearance

Veneered stair products present the visual grain and colour of the face species (typically 0.6mm-3mm veneer) bonded to MDF, plywood or engineered core. UK retailers (Cheshire Mouldings Stair Klad, StairFurb, Pear Stairs, The Door Store) supply factory-lacquered veneered stair components. Ribbon/grain matching across panels is a quality differentiator.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## veneered products.durability

Veneered surface durability is finish-dependent (typical factory clear satin lacquer). Under-veneer swelling if water penetrates seam is discussed as failure mode. Cannot be sanded and refinished more than once (some 0.6mm veneers cannot be sanded at all).

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## veneered products.maintenance

Damaged veneer typically requires panel replacement rather than repair. Factory-lacquered surfaces are wipe-clean; scuff points may be spot-refinished.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## veneered products.cost drivers

Cost drivers: veneer species (oak/walnut/maple), veneer thickness, core substrate, factory-finish quality, matched-set premium for grain matching across a full flight. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## engineered timber.appearance

Engineered timber for stairs consists of a hardwood wear layer bonded to plywood or HDF core. UK sources (Really Cheap Floors, Factory Direct Flooring, Timbersol) describe grain, colour and finish as identical to solid timber of the same species when the wear layer is thick enough (typically 3-6mm on engineered flooring, thicker on engineered stair treads).

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## engineered timber.durability

Engineered timber described as more dimensionally stable than solid timber under humidity swings; wear-layer thickness governs number of refinish cycles; core plywood eliminates warping and cupping.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## engineered timber.maintenance

Wear-layer determines lifetime: 2mm allows one or two light sandings, 3-6mm allows more. Once through wear-layer, panel is spent.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## engineered timber.cost drivers

Cost drivers: wear-layer thickness, wear-layer species, core plywood quality (Baltic birch premium), factory finish, board dimension. Industry-cited savings of 50-65% versus equivalent solid. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## hardwood overlays.appearance

Hardwood overlay systems (Cheshire Mouldings Stair Klad, StairFurb, The Door Store Oak Staircladding System, Stair Parts Direct) are typically pre-finished engineered or solid-hardwood tread and riser panels dimensioned to sit over a standard 200mm-rise existing tread with matching bullnose and extension pieces. Present as full solid-timber stair to the eye once installed.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## hardwood overlays.durability

System durability governed by wear-layer/veneer specification (see engineered_timber and veneered_products). Manufacturers cite 10-year product guarantees (StairFurb).

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## hardwood overlays.maintenance

Pre-finished factory lacquer surface described as low-maintenance; edge/nosing scuff highest wear point; delamination failure if adhesive bond fails.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## hardwood overlays.cost drivers

Cost drivers: species (oak most economical, walnut premium), veneer thickness, kit inclusion (adhesive/nosing/scotia), stair count. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## laminate systems.appearance

Laminate stair systems consist of photograph-printed melamine or high-pressure laminate (HPL) face bonded to HDF/MDF core. UK retailers (Antbs, Storiesflooring, Lowe's UK) describe wide colour/pattern range including realistic wood-look prints. Nosing is typically a matching pre-finished profile.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## laminate systems.durability

Sources describe laminate as very impact and scratch resistant on the wear layer, but with lower grip than carpet or textured vinyl — slip risk described as higher without anti-slip nosing.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## laminate systems.maintenance

Wipe-clean surface; individual panel replacement possible if seams remain intact; water ingress at seams can swell HDF core.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## laminate systems.cost drivers

Cost drivers: AC rating (AC3-AC6 durability class), print quality/embossing, core density, brand. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## vinyl coverings.appearance

Luxury Vinyl Tile / Plank (LVT/LVP) stair systems and Grey Vinyl Stairclad (The Door Store) present as pre-formed vinyl tread and riser sets. Wide colour/pattern range including wood-effect, stone-effect and plain grey/black.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## vinyl coverings.durability

Sources describe vinyl as waterproof and moderately durable; slip resistance described as good with textured surface finish; not recommended for outdoor stairs.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## vinyl coverings.maintenance

Wipe-clean surface; hygienic in wet/muddy-shoe household context; individual damaged pieces replaceable.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## vinyl coverings.cost drivers

Cost drivers: wear-layer thickness, LVT vs sheet vinyl, colour/pattern, backing type. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## stone stair cladding.appearance

Stone stair cladding (Marble Bros, Paramount Stone, Palazzostone, Omega Stone, Polish Granite, ST Stoneworks) covers marble, granite, limestone, slate, quartz varieties. Marble presents veined figure; granite presents crystalline grain; limestone presents softer creamy uniform tone; slate presents dark grey layered texture.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## stone stair cladding.durability

All natural stones described as extremely durable for stair use; granite hardest and most resistant to scratching; marble softer with etching risk from acidic spills; slate resistant to fire.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## stone stair cladding.maintenance

Marble requires sealing and pH-neutral cleaning; granite low-maintenance; slate texture aids slip resistance but needs sealing; polished surfaces may need slip-treatment for Part K compliance.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## stone stair cladding.cost drivers

Cost drivers: stone type (marble/granite/limestone/slate — significantly different price bands), thickness (12mm vs 20mm vs 30mm), edge profile (bullnose grinding), template/bespoke premium, structural loading assessment. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## metal components.appearance

Metal stair components (Richard Burbidge, Stair Parts Direct, Balustrade UK, Black Country Metalworks) include stainless steel handrails and balustrade posts, wrought iron / mild steel spindles (traditional and contemporary patterns), powder-coated aluminium handrails, brass and bronze accent finishes. Wide aesthetic range from Victorian-cast to minimalist stainless.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## metal components.durability

Sources describe stainless steel as maintenance-free and rust-resistant; anodised aluminium as maintenance-free and non-rusting; mild steel/wrought iron requires paint or powder-coat protection against rust; 316-grade stainless required for external or moisture-exposed applications.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## metal components.maintenance

Stainless and anodised aluminium wipe-clean; powder-coated components very low maintenance; painted mild steel requires periodic repaint at wear points.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## metal components.cost drivers

Cost drivers: material (mild steel < aluminium < stainless < brass/bronze), grade (304 vs 316 stainless), finish (mill vs polished vs brushed vs powder-coat), fabrication complexity (mass-produced spindle vs bespoke wrought). No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## glass components.appearance

Glass stair components (frameless balustrade panels, side-fixed panels, hybrid systems with handrail) present transparent open aesthetic maximising light. UK sources (IQ Glass, Balconette, StairFurb Stainless & Glass Kit, Glass Warehouse) describe 8mm toughened glass panels for framed systems and 17.5mm toughened-laminated glass for frameless systems.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## glass components.durability

Toughened glass rated to structural loading per BS 6180:2011; toughened-laminated glass provides post-breakage integrity for frameless systems; 8mm toughened rated to 1.5 kN/m for domestic. Sources note laminate essential for frameless configurations without top handrail.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## glass components.maintenance

Wipe-clean surface; smudges/fingerprints visible and require regular attention; sealant/gasket at fixing points requires inspection; damaged panels typically require full replacement.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## glass components.cost drivers

Cost drivers: glass type (8mm toughened vs 17.5mm laminated toughened), fixing method (channel/base-shoe/side-fix/point-fix), custom vs standard sizes, edge finish, handrail inclusion. No specific prices.

*Source count: 1 · Classification: unknown*

## treads.commonality of replacement

Very commonly replaced or clad during refacing. Manufacturer stair-cladding kits (Cheshire Mouldings StairKlad, Stairbox, George Quinn) are widely marketed as an alternative to full replacement. Full tread replacement requires access to string housings/wedges (housed-string construction) or exposed tread carriage. Cladding overlays glue onto existing treads with pre-formed nosing.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## treads.structural implications

Cladding adds thickness (typically 12–18mm oak veneer) which alters rise/going and nosing projection. Approved Document K limits rise/going/pitch and requires consistent step geometry, so cumulative cladding can push a compliant flight out of tolerance. Full tread replacement in housed-string construction requires disassembly of wedges and possibly string removal.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## risers.commonality of replacement

Commonly clad or replaced during refacing (often together with treads via Cheshire StairKlad / Stairbox / George Quinn cladding kits). Full replacement typical in open-riser conversions where original risers are removed to create modern open-tread appearance. Painted MDF risers commonly overlaid with veneered panels or repainted.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## risers.structural implications

Removing risers to create open-riser aesthetic reduces stiffening of the flight (risers typically tongue into tread underside via 12mm groove and form a diaphragm with the strings — per traditional housed-string construction). Also engages Approved Document K 100mm sphere rule for private domestic stairs (no opening in the stair structure may permit passage of a 100mm sphere).

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## strings.commonality of replacement

Rarely fully replaced (strings are structural spine of a staircase); commonly clad. Stringer cladding kits (Fiximer, Cheshire, Stairbox) are marketed for refacing over existing painted or worn strings. Full string replacement typically requires disassembling wedges and treads — often a complete staircase rebuild.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## strings.structural implications

Strings hold treads, risers and wedges — the primary load-carrying element of a housed-string stair. Full replacement effectively replaces the staircase. Cladding does not affect structural function but does slightly thicken the assembly which may need to be checked against handrail and newel fixing depths.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## handrails.commonality of replacement

Very commonly replaced during refacing. Wide manufacturer catalogue availability (Richard Burbidge, Cheshire Mouldings, Stairbox, Howarth Timber). BWF Design Guide 1 and Approved Document K both cover replacement compliance.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## handrails.structural implications

Approved Document K requires handrail height 900–1000mm above pitch line, continuous across flights and landings, on at least one side (both sides if stair ≥1000mm wide). Replacement engages current regulations (unless considered like-for-like repair). Handrail termination detail at newel affects load transfer.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## newels.commonality of replacement

Commonly replaced or clad during refacing. Richard Burbidge, Cheshire Mouldings, Stairbox all publish newel post catalogues for retrofit. Newel cladding kits (over existing newel) common where full newel replacement would disturb string housing.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

Newels anchor the handrail and baluster assembly and often the flight itself at bottom-tread and half-landing. Full replacement typically requires the newel to be tenoned/bolted into string and floor joist — a structural fixing detail. Cladding over existing newel avoids disturbing anchorage. ## balusters.commonality of replacement

Very commonly replaced. Manufacturer catalogues (Richard Burbidge, Cheshire Mouldings, Stairbox, Black Country Metalworks, British Ironwork Centre) offer wide range in timber (turned, square, chamfered, spiral) and metal (square bar, twisted bar, decorative panels). Fitted by gluing and pinning into handrail groove and baserail groove per trade guidance.

*Source count: 3 · Classification: unknown*

## balusters.structural implications

Approved Document K 100mm sphere rule: no gap greater than 100mm anywhere in guarding (spindle centres therefore max 99mm typical guidance). BS 6180:2011 governs barrier loading (0.36 kN horizontal at handrail height for dwelling stairs — trade-source figure). Replacement typically like-for-like repair unless re-configuring; current regulations apply where scope is significant.

*Source count: 2 · Classification: unknown*

## feature posts.structural implications

Where a feature post is load-bearing (mid-flight or half-landing) same fixing/structural rules apply as newels. Where purely decorative (surface-mounted, no handrail termination) — lighter fixing. Feature posts with integrated LED require Part P competent-person notified electrical work.

## stair runners.structural implications

None structural. Runner thickness plus underlay may add several millimetres to going and reduce effective nosing projection. Fixing via stair rods, gripper strip or stapling. Slip resistance and edge fixing key to safety (Part K requires safe consistent geometry).

None structural. Thickness affects effective rise/going and can shift a marginally compliant flight (Part K requires consistent rise across a flight). Fire class rating may apply where stair is protected escape route in multi-occupancy (Approved Document B, BS 5395-2 for HMOs).

## led systems.structural implications

Notifiable electrical work under Part P — must be by registered electrician per lighting trade sources. Driver location, cable routing, IP rating for open-plan/wet environments all matter. Cutting recesses into strings/risers reduces section — check with joiner/engineer for load-bearing elements.
