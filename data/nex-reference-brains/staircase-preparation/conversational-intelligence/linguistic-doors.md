# Linguistic Doors — many phrasings, one concept

**Philip's rule:** "Eight ways of saying the same thing are not eight pieces of knowledge. They are eight linguistic doors into one concept."

NEX must recognise that different customer phrasings often lead to the same underlying concept — then retrieve the knowledge in that concept, NOT eight separate answers. But when the concept is broad (like "make staircase feel more open"), the correct response is to **ask which axis matters most**, not dump the whole concept cluster.

---

## The linguistic-doors pattern

For each concept below:
- **Doors** — natural phrasings customers use
- **Underlying concept** — the shared trade-level intent
- **Common ambiguity** — what usually needs clarifying
- **Clarifying question** — how NEX narrows the axis
- **Reference Brain source** — where the eventual answer lives

## CONCEPT · "Make the staircase feel more visually open"

### Doors
- "I want the stairs to look bigger."
- "Can we make the staircase feel less narrow?"
- "It's a bit cramped."
- "How can we open this staircase up?"
- "Can you make it feel wider?"
- "The staircase looks heavy."
- "I don't want it boxed in."
- "How do we make this look more open?"
- "The staircase feels dark and closed in."
- "Can we lighten it up?"

### Concept + design-move cluster (from Reference Brain)
Common moves to make a staircase feel more visually open: pale palette (paint/finish) · glass balustrade · open-riser design · cantilever/floating construction · slim balusters · LED integration · mirror on the wall behind (styling · not a NEX build move) · wider tread projection at the base · lighter timber species.

### Ambiguity axes to clarify
- Is the constraint **structural** (staircase actually narrow) or **visual** (staircase reads heavy)?
- Which axis matters most: **balustrade** (glass/slim) · **finish** (pale palette) · **construction** (open riser · cantilever) · **lighting** (LED under-nosing)?

### Clarifying question NEX asks
> "'More open' can go a few directions. Are you thinking mainly about the balustrade feeling lighter (glass or slim metal instead of turned timber), the whole staircase reading paler (paint or lighter timber), the actual construction (open risers or floating treads), or the lighting? Any one of those would take us somewhere different."

## CONCEPT · "Make the first step wider / bigger / more of a feature"

### Doors
- "Make the bottom step bigger."
- "Can I have a wider first step?"
- "I want the first step to stick out more."
- "Can the entry step be more substantial?"
- "Make the bottom look more welcoming."
- "I want something bigger at the base of the stairs."
- "Can the first step wrap around the newel?"
- "How can I make the entry step more of a statement?"

### Concept + design-move cluster
The starting step family has 20 named types (see `starting-steps-types-carpet-and-design-2026-08-14.md`). "Bigger" could mean: wider extended tread · projecting bullnose · curtail with wrap · double-stacked platform · curtail with volute · combined square+bullnose.

### Ambiguity axes
- **Wider** vs **projecting further forward** vs **wrapping around the sides**
- Style: **modern** (square platform / extended square) vs **traditional** (bullnose / curtail / volute)
- Single or double-stacked

### Clarifying question NEX asks
> "'Bigger' points a few ways. Do you mean wider across (extended tread projecting forward), rounder / wrapping around (bullnose or curtail curling around the newel), or a more substantial two-step statement (double-stacked at the base)? Also, is the rest of the staircase leaning modern or traditional?"

## CONCEPT · "Modernise / update the staircase"

### Doors
- "Modernise this staircase."
- "Make it look more modern."
- "Update the look."
- "Refresh the staircase."
- "Bring it into the 21st century."
- "Give it a makeover."
- "Refurb the stairs."
- "Contemporary look."
- "Less old-fashioned."

### Concept + design-move cluster
The "modernise" cascade activates: modern balustrade families (matt-black metal / brushed stainless / glass) · minimal newel caps · paint spindles (charcoal / white) · replace turned components with square/slim · exposed timber over carpet · LED integration · cantilever if structurally viable · slim handrail profile.

### Ambiguity axes
- **Scope**: full replacement · refacing only · single component swap
- **Direction**: architectural-modern (industrial · minimalist) · warm-modern (Scandi / transitional) · luxury-modern (LED + glass)
- **Budget signal**: implied by scope

### Clarifying question NEX asks
> "Modern goes a few ways. Are you thinking architectural-minimalist (matt black metal, glass, exposed steel), warm-modern (light oak, slim spindles, kept-traditional feel but lighter), or luxury-modern (cantilever, frameless glass, LED integration)? And is this a refurb keeping the existing structure, or a full replacement?"

## CONCEPT · "Traditional look"

### Doors
- "Make it look traditional."
- "Something classical."
- "Period style."
- "Old-fashioned staircase."
- "A proper traditional look."
- "Victorian style."
- "Something like a country house."

### Concept + design-move cluster
Traditional cascade: turned newels · turned timber spindles · moulded handrail · bullnose or curtail starting step · volute at bottom newel · carpet runner · oak / mahogany / walnut · scroll brackets on cut-string.

### Ambiguity axes
- **Era** — Georgian · Victorian · Edwardian · cottage · English country · Federal
- **Formality** — grand · restrained · rustic
- **Timber** — oak · mahogany · pine (painted / natural)

### Clarifying question NEX asks
> "'Traditional' can lean a few ways. Grand traditional (turned newels + volute + carpet runner + dark hardwood) reads formal / country house. Cottage traditional (pine + painted spindles + simpler cap) reads warmer / rural. Georgian / Federal (ball finials + white spindles + dark handrail) reads more classical-architectural. Which era or feel are you drawn to?"

## CONCEPT · "Match the landing to the stairs"

### Doors
- "Can I have the same railing around the landing?"
- "Match the landing to the stairs."
- "Same style upstairs as downstairs."
- "Continue the railing around the top."
- "The landing should feel like part of the staircase."

### Concept + design-move cluster
Landing-railing continuity principle (see `landing-railings-continuity-and-construction-2026-08-14.md` §1). Same handrail · same balusters · same newel design · same finish. Nuance: three-way base-rail rule.

### Ambiguity axes
- **Full continuity** (identical detailing) vs **design-language continuity** (same family, adapted for landing geometry)
- Awareness of base-rail construction difference

### Clarifying question NEX asks (only if warranted)
Usually clear enough to answer directly. Only ask if construction type is unclear:
> "Yes — that's the standard approach. One thing worth knowing depending on your staircase construction: is it a closed-string (string covers the tread ends) or a cut-string (tread ends exposed) design? That affects how the base rail continues onto the landing."

## CONCEPT · "Something modern with wood"

### Doors
- "Modern with wood."
- "Contemporary but I still want timber."
- "Warm modern."
- "Not too cold — modern with wood tones."
- "Modern-transitional."

### Concept + design-move cluster
Warm-modern cascade: light oak treads · frameless glass balustrade OR matt-black metal rods · minimal newel caps · exposed timber (no carpet or runner only) · LED integration.

### Ambiguity axes
- **Which balustrade** — glass · slim metal · slim timber
- **Which timber** — oak · walnut · ash · pale · dark
- **Handrail material** — timber · matt-black metal · stainless

### Clarifying question NEX asks
> "Warm-modern is a common combination. Two axes to narrow: (1) balustrade — frameless glass, slim brushed stainless verticals, or matt-black metal rods; (2) timber — light oak (Scandi-lean), walnut (deeper / more formal), or ash (very pale). Any of those pull you?"

## CONCEPT · "Save space"

### Doors
- "Save space."
- "Small footprint."
- "Tight corner."
- "Can I fit a staircase in a small space?"
- "Space-saver staircase."
- "Minimum floor area."
- "Compact staircase."

### Concept + design-move cluster
Space-saver options: winder (folds turn into the flight) · spiral / helical · alternating-tread ship ladder · loft ladder · steep pitch (constrained by regs).

### Ambiguity axes
- **How restrictive** — a couple more inches vs radically constrained
- **Daily use vs occasional** — daily use rules out ship ladders
- **Can it turn** — L-shape / U-shape / winder / spiral

### Clarifying question NEX asks
> "How constrained is the space, and how often will it be used? Daily use rules out alternating-tread ship ladders. Occasional-only opens up ship ladder and spiral. Between those, winder staircases and L-shape landings are the middle-ground compromises. Do you know the floor area available?"

## CONCEPT · "The staircase feels dated"

### Doors
- "The staircase feels dated."
- "It looks old."
- "The current staircase is stuck in a time period."
- "It doesn't fit the rest of the house."
- "Everything else has been updated except the stairs."

### Concept + design-move cluster
Common refacing scope: spindle swap · newel cap swap · handrail replacement · painting the balustrade · sanding + refinishing the treads · adding LED · runner change.

### Ambiguity axes
- **Which era it's stuck in** — determines the direction of update
- **Full modernise vs subtle update**
- **Refacing scope** — cap-only · spindles · full balustrade

### Clarifying question NEX asks
> "What era does the current staircase feel like? If it's brown-and-heavy 1970s, the moves are different than if it's ornate Victorian or blond 1990s. Also, are you thinking full refacing or just tweaking one thing (like the spindles or the finish)?"

## Rules for NEX applying the linguistic-doors pattern

1. **Recognise the door.** Match the input against the doors list for each concept. Multiple doors → one concept.
2. **Do NOT dump the concept.** Recognising the door doesn't mean dumping every design-move in the cluster. It means understanding what the customer probably means.
3. **Ask the clarifying question.** Almost every linguistic-doors concept is ambiguous by construction — the doors are broad; the answer needs an axis.
4. **Retrieve knowledge only for the resolved axis.** Once the customer picks an axis, retrieve just that Reference Brain evidence.

## Meta-observation

**The customer isn't asking for eight different things when they use eight different phrasings.** They're asking for ONE thing — and the shape of the phrasing usually doesn't tell NEX which axis they care about. The correct response is almost always a clarifying question, then the targeted answer.

Adding more doors to this file is expanding NEX's recognition. Adding more concept clusters is expanding NEX's reasoning coverage. Both should grow.
