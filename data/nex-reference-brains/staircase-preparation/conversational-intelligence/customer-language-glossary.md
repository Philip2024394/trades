# Customer-Language Glossary — with ambiguity modelled

Customers do not use trade terminology. They describe what they see, what they want, and what feels wrong — often ambiguously. NEX's job is to **interpret** rather than to correct or force one meaning.

## How to read this glossary

Each row is a customer phrase (or phrase family) mapped to:
- **Possible trade meanings** — one, or several if the phrase is ambiguous
- **Ambiguity tier** — Clear / Likely / Ambiguous
- **What NEX should do** — answer directly, hedge with a follow-up, or ask first
- **Source in existing Reference Brain** — the doc + section that supports each meaning

**NEX never corrects the customer's wording.** It understands the customer's language, then uses the professional terminology naturally in reply.

## Room-context vocabulary

Where the staircase sits changes which options make sense.

| Customer phrase | Room context | Design implication |
|---|---|---|

| "living room" | open-plan visible from social space | design language must match the room · often cantilever or feature |
| "loft" · "attic" · "upstairs conversion" | often space-constrained | alternating tread · space-saver ship ladder · steep pitch |
| "basement" · "cellar" | utilitarian, less visible | straight run · simple finish · non-slip priority |
| "narrow hallway" | limits projecting starting step | flush first tread often the right choice · L-shape or straight |
| "open plan" | staircase visible from multiple angles | double-ended visibility · asymmetric options |
| "small house" · "tight space" | footprint-constrained | winder · spiral · space-saver |
| "hillside" · "steep site" | outdoor / grade change | outdoor timber deck stairs · long run or switchback |
| "outdoor" · "garden" · "deck" | exterior · weather-exposed | pressure-treated timber · non-slip · drainage |
| "pool-facing" | wet · slippery · UV-exposed | non-slip material · UV-stable finish |
| "commercial" · "office" · "public" | high traffic · code-critical | routed to building_codes future brain |
| "period property" · "old house" | traditional cascade · likely refurbishment scope | refacing · turned components · classical newel |
| "new build" · "modern house" | clean-slate · modern cascade | any layout · modern balustrade families |

## Ambiguity is normal · rules to remember

1. **A single customer phrase can legitimately mean several things.** That's not an error; it's how humans talk.
2. **NEX's job is to interpret gently, not to correct.** Never say "you mean X" — say "if you're thinking about X..." or "that could go a couple of ways."
3. **When a phrase is ambiguous, ASK.** Do not pick a meaning and run with it.
4. **The customer's own words are correct English.** The trade terminology is the specialist form. NEX uses the trade term naturally in its reply — never as a correction.
5. **New entries only from real evidence.** If a customer phrase has no source in the Reference Brain, it doesn't get added here yet.
