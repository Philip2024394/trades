# Three-Rule Author Reminder

**One page. Read before every authoring session.**

> The purpose of every Brain is to become the most trusted professional reference in its field.
> — ADR-0040 · The Prime Sentence

You are about to author knowledge that will be quoted by professionals and applied on real projects. When you sign a module, your name is on it forever. The three rules below are the discipline that earns that trust.

---

## Rule A · Anti-Fabrication

**No invented facts. Ever. For any reason.**

You are the STRUCTURE and the CITATION. You are not the SOURCE.

- If a section is blank because nothing was captured, leave it blank and record it as a `known_gap`.
- If a claim is uncertain, mark it `confidence: uncertain` and cite where the uncertainty comes from.
- If you catch yourself typing "generally", "usually", "most professionals" without a citable source, delete the sentence.
- If pressure to fill space produces prose, STOP. Return to `interview_template.md` and capture what is actually known from a named expert.

The brain has permission to say **"I don't know."** The brain does NOT have permission to guess convincingly.

---

## Rule B · No AI-Authored Content

**No AI-generated technical claims may enter the brain.**

AI tools are permitted for:
- Grammar and readability edits that preserve meaning
- Reformatting citations into the standard layout
- Cross-referencing existing entries you already authored
- Detecting missing sections against the template

AI tools are forbidden for:
- Writing the substance of an entry body
- Supplying technical claims from model knowledge
- Filling gaps in captured interview material
- Answering the "why" section of a body
- Deciding what belongs in `known_limits` or `known_gaps`

If a technical claim in your draft was not spoken by (or written by) a named certified expert, or does not come from a regulation, standard, manufacturer specification, or reviewed field observation, it does not belong in the brain — regardless of how plausible it sounds.

**The test:** for every sentence in body copy, you must be able to point at the specific human or document that is the source. If you cannot, the sentence is a violation of Rule B.

---

## Rule C · Attributable Origin

**Every fact carries a citable origin.**

Every entry declares one or more of five origin types:

- **E** · Named expert — a specific, named, credentialed practitioner captured under interview_template.md
- **R** · Regulation — a legal instrument, cited by document · edition · clause · jurisdiction
- **S** · Standard — an industry standard, cited by body · number · year · clause
- **M** · Manufacturer specification — a datasheet, cited by manufacturer · product · version · date
- **F** · Reviewed field observation — a practitioner's observation, endorsed by a second named expert

Every declared origin must have a matching populated reference block — see `citation_requirements.md` for the exact shape.

**If you cannot classify a claim into one of the five, you cannot publish it.** No exceptions for tribal knowledge, no exceptions for common practice, no exceptions for "everyone in the trade knows this". Either it can be sourced, or it does not enter the brain.

---

## What you are authorising when you sign a module

By setting your name in `module_identity.primary_author` and running the publish-readiness checklist, you are personally asserting all of the following:

1. Every technical claim in this module was captured from a named source you can identify on demand.
2. Nothing in this module was invented by you or generated by an AI tool.
3. Every entry carries an origin classification that resolves to a real, verifiable source.
4. Every entry accurately represents its source — no drift, no unsupported extension.
5. You have read every entry in this module and are willing to be publicly identified as its author.
6. You accept that a professional in the field will hold you accountable for what this module says.
7. You have identified the module's `known_gaps` honestly — no gap has been hidden by convenient omission.
8. You have not approved your own work — an independent reviewer has co-signed every entry.

This is not a formality. In a courtroom, a workshop, or a peer conversation, your name on this module is your professional word. Author accordingly.

---

## The Professional Test — the question at the bottom

Before you submit the draft, hold this question:

> **Would a master tradesperson recommend this module to another master tradesperson?**

Not: would a customer find it useful.
Not: would an interviewer find it impressive.
Not: would an executive be satisfied with the coverage.

**Would a peer stake their own reputation on it?**

If the honest answer is "yes", submit. If "not yet", return to the sections that gave you pause.

Peer respect is earned by being right, repeatedly, verifiably, over time. The three rules above are how you earn it in this session.
