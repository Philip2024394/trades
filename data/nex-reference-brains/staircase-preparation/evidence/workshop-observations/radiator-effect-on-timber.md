# Radiator effect on a timber staircase

The kind of household detail that causes complaints six months after installation. Not a regulation issue — an environmental one.

## Claim (Philip · 2026-07-29)

**A radiator positioned directly beneath a timber staircase produces continuous warm, dry air that rises through the staircase. Over time this dries the timber unevenly, increases seasonal movement, and stresses the joints, glue lines, and fixings.**

Even at a low radiator temperature, the continuous heat differential between the underside of the stair (warm/dry) and the room above (ambient) causes uneven moisture loss.

## Consequences

Prolonged local heat may contribute to:

- **Opening joints** — housed treads and risers gap at the shoulder.
- **Squeaks and movement** — timber movement fractures the glue line.
- **Shrinkage around strings and newels** — visible dark lines at joint edges.
- **Visible gaps** — especially at the top riser / trimmer face.
- **Distortion of timber components** — cupping on wider boards.

## Recommendation

Per Philip:

1. **Where possible, avoid installing a radiator directly beneath a timber staircase** at the design stage.
2. **If a radiator is already installed**, reduce or turn off during periods when the room is unoccupied.
3. **Keep the home's ambient temperature reasonably stable** — sudden changes stress the timber more than steady warmth.

Stable environmental conditions are generally better for a timber staircase than exposure to a constant local heat source.
