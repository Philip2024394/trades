# Lifted top step — safety principle and correct repair

The top step of any staircase is a distinct engineering element (see `top-tread-machining.md`). It also receives repeated loading because every person using the staircase steps onto it when reaching the landing. When it lifts, it creates a serious trip hazard.

## Claim (Philip · 2026-07-29)

**The top step of any staircase must always be securely fixed. If it lifts, it can catch footwear and cause a fall.**

### Correct repair

The recommended repair for a lifted top step is:

1. **Drill** through the top step down into the trimming joist (trimmer).
2. **Screw** using suitable timber screws — mechanical fixing that resists lifting over time.
3. **Plug** the countersink with a matching timber plug so the fixing is invisible after finishing.

Once correctly fixed, the top step should remain secure and safe for many years.

### Wrong repair

**Do not rely on nails alone.**

Nails can gradually loosen through:

- normal use (repeated impact loading)
- timber movement (seasonal expansion/contraction)
- vibration (each footstep creates micro-movement)

A nailed repair may hold for months, then lift again.

**Do not rely on adhesive alone.**

Glue between an already-loose tread and its bearing is a temporary bond that will fail once loading resumes.

### Follow-up checks

After the repair, always confirm:

1. The top tread does not lift when stepped on.
2. The top riser is firmly fixed against the trimmer face.
3. There is no movement between the staircase and the landing.

**Any movement at the top of the staircase should be investigated immediately — it is a safety issue, not a cosmetic one.**

## Author's note (Philip · 2026-07-29)

The reason the top step is a hazard when it lifts (and not the intermediate treads) is that people step onto it at the exact moment their gaze shifts from the stair to the landing — attention is off the foot. A lifted intermediate tread is more noticeable during ascent. A lifted top tread catches on the way UP into a room, at the moment concentration is lowest.

Regular homeowner inspection of the top step is worth naming as a habit — it receives more loading than any other tread over the life of the staircase.
