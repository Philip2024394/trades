# Learning + career

For the apprentice, the career-changer, the parent asking on behalf of a teenager, or the qualified joiner curious about specialising in stairs.

---

## A · Getting into the trade

### Q176 — How do I become a staircase maker in the UK?
- What's the route to becoming a stair maker?

**Notes:** typical path = joinery apprenticeship → workshop or site experience → specialise in staircases. Often 6+ years from start to skilled.

### Q177 — Do I need a joinery apprenticeship to make staircases?
- Can I skip the apprenticeship and go straight to stairs?

**Notes:** formal apprenticeship gives the foundation. Some enter via general carpentry then specialise.

### Q178 — How long is a UK joinery apprenticeship?
- How many years does the apprenticeship take?

**Notes:** typically 2-4 years depending on qualification level + employer.

### Q179 — Do I go to college every week during my apprenticeship?
- Is it like normal college?

**Notes:** typically one day per week or block weeks — most time is with the employer, not in college.

### Q180 — How does an apprentice get paid?
- Do you earn while training?

**Notes:** yes — employer pays wages. Government + employer support training costs. Wage rises with year of apprenticeship.

---

## B · Earnings

### Q181 — What does a UK apprentice joiner earn?
- What's the starting wage?

**Notes:** minimum apprentice rate applies; many employers pay more. Typical starting range £7-15k depending on age + year.

### Q182 — What does a qualified joiner earn in the UK?
- What can I earn once I'm skilled?

**Notes:** employed newly qualified £25-35k; experienced £35-50k; specialist £50-65k+ (approximate; regional variation).

### Q183 — What does a specialist staircase maker earn?
- Do stair specialists earn more than general joiners?

**Notes:** typically yes — narrower skill set + higher demand. Bespoke specialists highest.

### Q184 — Can I earn more self-employed than employed as a joiner?
- Is going self-employed worth it?

**Notes:** day rates £180-500+ depending on specialism + reputation. Higher potential but requires business skills + customer acquisition.

---

## C · Progression + specialisation

### Q185 — At what age do joiners typically reach their peak skill?
- When are you actually good at the trade?

**Notes:** foundational skills 20-40; strongest judgement + specialism 40+; master craftsman later still.

### Q186 — Can an older joiner still work in the trade?
- What happens as you get older in the workshop?

**Notes:** many transition from heavy installation to design, estimating, surveying, training, supervision, quality control.

### Q187 — Is there demand for skilled staircase workers in the UK?
- Are there jobs for stair specialists?

**Notes:** ongoing demand — new build, extensions, loft conversions, renovation, luxury projects. Specialist skills harder to find than general trades.
