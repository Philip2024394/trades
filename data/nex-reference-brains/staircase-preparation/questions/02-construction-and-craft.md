## B · Housing (routed grooves)

### Q050 — How deep is a housed string routed for treads and risers?
- What's the standard housing depth?

**Notes:** BWF · 12mm or 0.4 × string thickness, whichever is greater.
**Related:** Q051, Q052

### Q051 — Does housing depth scale with string thickness?
- If I use a 44mm string, is the housing deeper?

**Notes:** yes — 0.4 × 44 = 17.6mm.
**Related:** Q050

### Q052 — How do I work out tread length from clear width and housing depth?
- If my clear width is 800mm, how long is the tread blank?

**Notes:** tread length = clear width + 2 × housing depth. 800 + 24 = 824mm at 12mm each side.
**Related:** Q050, Q053

### Q053 — Are treads and risers the same length as each other?
- Do I cut the treads and risers together?

**Notes:** yes — both housed into the same string profile.
**Related:** Q052

## C · Wedges + joint verification

### Q054 — What's the purpose of a wedge in a housed staircase joint?
- Why does the tread have a wedge behind it?

**Notes:** wedges lock the tread and riser into the tapered housing; glued for rigid joint.
**Related:** Q055, Q056

### Q055 — Is a wedge that won't drive any further proof the tread is fully home?
- If I can't move the wedge, is the joint tight?

**Notes:** **NO** — the stopped-wedge principle (Philip's workshop observation). A wedge can lock on friction before the tread has fully seated against the housing shoulder.
**Related:** Q054, Q056

### Q056 — What happens if I keep hammering after a wedge has stopped?
- Should I keep going or stop?

**Notes:** risks split wedge · crushed fibres · damaged housing · bruised string. Objective is a fully seated joint, NOT the tightest wedge.
**Related:** Q055

### Q057 — What kind of glue is used with staircase wedges?
- Do you glue the wedge in?

**Notes:** BWF specifies wedges fitted with adhesive to form rigid joint.
**Related:** Q054

### Q058 — What angle should the housing taper be for the wedge?
- How steep is the wedge angle?

**Notes:** BWF publication states housings are tapered — specific angle typically 6-8° in practice; verification needed.

## D · Newel post connections

### Q059 — How is the string connected to the newel post?
- What joint holds the string to the newel?

**Notes:** tenoned connection — string carries tenon into newel mortice.
**Related:** Q060

### Q060 — Is a double tenon better than a single tenon for the newel joint?
- Should I use two tenons where the string meets the newel?

**Notes:** double tenon gives two bearing points, better resistance to twisting, cleaner haunch line, less breakout risk.
**Related:** Q059

### Q061 — Why does the top riser terminate against the trimmer, not the string?
- What's happening at the top of the flight — riser goes against what?

**Notes:** engineering invariant · a single flight terminates on a riser against the trimming joist. See top-tread machining evidence file.
**Related:** Q022

### Q062 — How should I mark out the haunch line before machining the newel mortice?
- Do I scribe the joint line first?

**Notes:** scribe first to define finished edge, cut fibres before machining, reduce tearing on hardwoods.
**Related:** Q059

## E · Timber selection + preparation

### Q063 — What's the difference between "wood" and "timber"?
- Are they the same thing?

**Notes:** wood = the natural material. Timber = wood prepared for construction (cut, seasoned, graded).

### Q064 — What timber species are used for UK staircases?
- Oak, pine, walnut — what's normal?

**Notes:** softwood (pine/redwood) for painted stairs · oak / ash for visible hardwood · walnut / sapele for premium.
**Related:** Q065

### Q065 — What timber should a beginner use for their first staircase?
- If I'm just learning, what wood do I start with?

**Notes:** pine — forgiving, affordable, easy to cut and shape.
**Related:** Q064

### Q066 — How should timber be stored before staircase manufacture?
- What's the right way to keep staircase timber?

**Notes:** flat and supported (bearers / stickers), airflow between boards, protected from moisture, allowed to acclimatise to workshop conditions.
**Related:** Q067

## F · Assembly + clamping

### Q070 — Why are staircases assembled upside down in the workshop?
- Why do stair makers turn the flight over?

**Notes:** access to housings, wedges, glue application, and joint verification — all invisible once the stair is right way up.

### Q071 — Do you need a hydraulic staircase press to make a good staircase?
- Can I build one without a factory press?

**Notes:** no — manual clamping works if pressure is controlled and applied evenly. The press adds speed and consistency, not accuracy.
**Related:** Q072

### Q072 — How do you apply clamping pressure evenly across a staircase?
- Where do you put the clamps first?

**Notes:** first and last riser establish the reference points; work progressively through the flight; check square before increasing pressure.
