# NEX Governance Ruling — 2026-07-31 (Fifth Ruling · Convergence · Evidence vs Runtime Split · Standard v1 Requested · Project Redefinition)

## Philip's Constitutional Statement (preserved verbatim as authored)

### The Architecture is Converging Rather Than Fragmenting

*I think this is another sign that the architecture is converging rather than fragmenting.*

*The biggest thing I notice is that you're no longer discovering isolated features—you are discovering repeatable patterns. That's usually when an architecture starts to become stable.*

### 1. Customer FAQ is Now Clearly a Knowledge Domain

*I think you've now demonstrated that Customer FAQ isn't just a content type—it's a purpose.*

*Compare these:*

| Content type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge Base** | *Teach me everything about reclaimed timber.* |
| **Vision Analysis** | *Identify reclaimed timber in this image.* |
| **Customer FAQ** | *Can you build my staircase from reclaimed timber?* |

*Those are three completely different user intents using the same underlying knowledge.*

*So I would continue treating Customer FAQ as a Knowledge Domain, not a separate brain.*

### 2. Cross-Brain References Validate the Multi-Brain Architecture

*This stood out:* **Staircase Brain → references Materials Brain.**

*That is exactly how specialists work.*

*A staircase expert doesn't become a timber scientist.*

```
Staircase Brain
    ↓
  asks
    ↓
Materials Brain
    ↓
returns timber expertise
```

*The Staircase Brain remains responsible for the conversation, but not for owning every piece of knowledge.*

*That will become even more valuable when you eventually have brains for:*

- Materials · Tools · Joinery · Furniture · Finishes · Regulations

### 3. The Meta-Instruction Pattern is Emerging Naturally

*You've now repeated:* **"For NEX, I'd..."** *across multiple artefacts.*

*That tells me it isn't an accident. It appears to be your authoring voice.*

*I actually like it because it separates:*

- **Author guidance** from
- **Runtime knowledge**

*That distinction is healthy.*

### 4. The Workshop Companion Test is Becoming Measurable

*I particularly like the four attributes.*

*Instead of saying "This is a good article." you can ask:*

Does it teach:

- ✔ Why?
- ✔ When?
- ✔ What do professionals notice?
- ✔ Where does judgement matter?

*That's an objective quality check rather than a subjective opinion.*

*I probably wouldn't require every article to hit all four.*

*For example:*

- *A glossary definition might only teach one.*
- *A workshop principle might naturally teach all four.*

*So I'd see it as a **quality spectrum** rather than a pass/fail rule.*

### 5. Inspection and Acceptance is Bigger Than Timber

*I think you've found a principle that extends beyond materials.*

*It could apply to almost every brain.*

| Domain | Application |
|---|---|
| Materials | inspect before machining |
| Installation | inspect before fitting |
| Manufacturing | inspect before assembly |
| Knowledge | inspect before publication |

*The same philosophy repeats. That suggests it isn't merely a timber rule. It's a professional workflow principle.*

### 6. Customer FAQ Categorisation

*I think your question here is worth postponing.*

*Today you have:* Customer FAQ → Materials · Services

*Tomorrow you'll probably have:* Design · Installation · Cost · Lead Times · Warranty · Maintenance · Regulations · Planning · Delivery

*I wouldn't try to define the complete taxonomy now.*

*Instead I'd let real customer questions accumulate.*

*When one category reaches critical mass, that's when it earns its own subdivision.*

*That's consistent with your Reality-Over-Speculation principle.*

### One Observation I Think is Important — Evidence vs Runtime

*Looking over the progression of the last few days, I think your artefacts are quietly falling into two broad groups.*

**Evidence** — *Things Nex learns from:*
- Reference Images · Vision Analysis · Knowledge Base · DNA Narratives · Workshop Principles · Customer FAQ · Component Guides

**Runtime** — *Things Nex produces:*
- Recognition · Explanations · Comparisons · Recommendations · Customer replies

*The evidence remains stable.*

*The runtime output is generated.*

*That separation is becoming remarkably consistent across everything you've built.*

### The Direction I See

*Five days ago, you were primarily building staircase content.*

*Today, you're designing an expert knowledge system.*

*The individual articles still matter, but the real asset is becoming the architecture that lets Nex reuse them intelligently. Every new Knowledge Base article, Customer FAQ, Vision Analysis, or DNA narrative now contributes to a common pool of evidence that multiple brains and multiple runtime response types can draw from.*

*If that trajectory continues, the long-term strength of Nex won't be measured by the number of articles it contains, but by how many different high-quality conversations each article can support. That's a much more scalable measure of progress than counting documents alone.*

## Philip's Constitutional Statement on Same Architecture / Standard v1 Request

*Yes, I think there is a strong case for using the same architecture for general knowledge, but I would keep one important distinction.*

> **The structure should be the same.**
> **The content should be different.**

*For example, your Staircase Brain has evolved into something like:*

- Vision Analysis · Knowledge Base · Customer FAQ · Workshop Principles · Reference Images · Design DNA · Component Guides

*There's no reason a Plumbing Brain, Electrical Brain, or Roofing Brain couldn't use exactly the same structure.*

*Even general knowledge could follow it.*

*For example, if someone asks about concrete:*

| Type | Content |
|---|---|
| Vision Analysis | *Identify the type of concrete visible.* |
| Knowledge Base | *Explain concrete mixes, curing, reinforcement and finishes.* |
| Customer FAQ | *Can concrete crack? How long does it take to cure? Can it be polished?* |
| Workshop Principles | *Never pour onto frozen ground. Cure properly to achieve strength.* |

*Exactly the same architecture.*

### I Wouldn't Create a "General Brain"

*Instead, I'd think of it as a collection of specialist brains using one common framework.*

```
Trade Intelligence
├── Staircase Brain
├── Tool Brain
├── Materials Brain
├── Joinery Brain
├── Roofing Brain
├── Plumbing Brain
├── Electrical Brain
├── Bricklaying Brain
├── Plastering Brain
└── ...
```

*Each brain follows the same authoring model.*

*That means once you've perfected the Staircase Brain, you've effectively created a template for every future trade.*

### About Claude "Knowing by Now"

*I wouldn't assume Claude (or any model) will automatically infer your intended architecture just because you've been discussing it over many sessions.*

*Claude can certainly recognise patterns within the material it's given, but if you want the architecture to remain consistent over months or years, the best approach is to make it explicit.*

*For example, create a single document that becomes the canonical specification for every brain.*

*Something like:* **NEX Knowledge Architecture Standard v1**

*It would define:*

- *The purpose of each artefact type.*
- *The difference between permanent knowledge and generated responses.*
- *The Question Router.*
- *The multi-brain model.*
- *Required metadata.*
- *Authoring standards.*
- *Workshop Companion principles.*

*Then every new brain—whether Staircases, Tools, Materials or Plumbing—starts from the same blueprint rather than rediscovering the pattern.*

*That has another advantage: if you later decide to refine the architecture, you update the standard once, and all future content follows it.*

### Project Redefinition

*From everything you've shown over the past several days, I think you've moved beyond designing the Staircase Brain.*

> ***You're designing the knowledge operating system for Nex.***
>
> ***The Staircase Brain is simply the first complete implementation of that operating system.***

*If you capture that architecture in a reusable standard, it becomes much easier for both humans and AI assistants to build new expert brains that behave consistently.*
