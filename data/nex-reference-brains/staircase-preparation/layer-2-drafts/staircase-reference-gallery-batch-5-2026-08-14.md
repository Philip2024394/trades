## Questions and Answers (informed by this batch)

### Q · Balustrade taxonomy extensions

**Q4.b5.001** · What is a "barley-sugar" or "twisted" turned spindle?
A · A classical turned spindle where the shaft carries a continuous spiral twist along its length (resembling a barley-sugar sweet). Distinguishes from standard bulb-and-urn turned spindles which have discrete decorative sections. In batch 5, img-31 shows an all-twisted set (Georgian/Regency); img-37, 38, 44 show barley-sugar twisted alternating with plain turned (patterned rhythm). Rarely seen in modern construction · signifies craftsman heritage.

**Q4.b5.002** · What is an "ornate scroll" wrought-iron baluster?
A · A metal baluster whose body carries multiple decorative scroll, loop, or S-curve elements (as distinct from a plain straight rod or a simple twisted rod). In batch 5 · img-42, 43, 48, 49 show variants ranging from multi-loop scroll to elongated S-curve. Always paired with light oak framework in this batch and always appearing in modern-traditional settings.

**Q4.b5.003** · What is a "bi-colour" newel post?
A · A newel post using two different finishes on the same post — commonly a dark stained outer with white painted field panels inset into the recessed faces. img-11 is the clearest example: dark walnut newel with white inset field panels. Reads as more decorative than a monotone newel and helps a two-tone (walnut + white) staircase visually anchor.

**Q4.b5.004** · What is a "serpentine" handrail profile?
A · A handrail whose profile forms an S-curve (as distinct from a straight profile or a simple sweeping curve). Very unusual · signals Federal / high-end Colonial styling. img-10 is the batch example.

**Q4.b5.005** · What is a "pipe handrail" in the industrial loft aesthetic?
A · A handrail constructed from actual black-painted galvanised plumbing pipe (typically ½"–1") with matching elbow fittings and flange mounting brackets. Signals industrial-warehouse conversion aesthetic. img-34 and img-41 both show this treatment with reclaimed weathered timber treads. Never seen in traditional or modern-luxury contexts in this batch.

**Q4.b5.006** · What is a "woven metal mesh" balustrade?
A · A balustrade whose infill is a bronze / copper / darkened diamond-woven wire mesh panel held between wood or metal frame stiles. Very rare. img-30 is the sole example — industrial-rustic aesthetic with dark rustic timber frame.

**Q4.b5.007** · What is a "horizontal timber slat" balustrade?
A · Parallel light-oak / timber slats running horizontally (not vertically) between newel posts, spaced approximately 30-40mm apart with the top capped by a matching timber handrail. Reads as modern minimal · used more often on landings than on flights. img-46 is the batch example, from an upper landing view.

### Q · Riser and tread material extensions

**Q4.b5.008** · What is a "metal-faced" riser?
A · A tread whose riser face is clad in a metal panel (usually brushed stainless steel) rather than painted timber. Industrial-luxury statement. img-25 shows this with stainless button LEDs inset into each metal riser face.

**Q4.b5.009** · What are "stainless button LEDs inset in risers"?
A · Small round stainless-steel LED puck lights (10-16mm dia) pressed into the front face of each riser at intervals. Creates a rhythm of bright dots across the flight (distinct from continuous under-nosing LED strips). img-24 and img-25 show this treatment.

### Q · Understair storage extensions

**Q4.b5.010** · What is an "understair LED-lit display shelf" unit?
A · A built-in cabinetry unit occupying the triangular void under the stringer, featuring open display cubbies + pull-out drawers, with LED strips illuminating each compartment interior (museum-style display). More sophisticated than plain cabinet doors. img-22 (natural oak with LED) and img-27 (black stained with LED-lit shelves) and img-28 (white) all show variants.

### Q · Colour × identity separation

**Q4.b5.011** · Why does batch 5 include so many colour variations of the same staircase?
A · Deliberate coverage. The same natural-light-oak-with-turned-spindles construction appears with sage stringer (img-14), navy stringer (img-13), sage feature wall (img-44), navy accent wall (img-48), yellow accent wall (img-49), charcoal accent wall (img-47), olive landing wall (img-46), and standard cream walls (img-39, 40, 45). **Q4.b5.012** · Do wall/accent colour choices affect staircase classification in the Brain?
A · No. Brain classification is based on **construction type + balustrade type + material + stringer/step-unit family**. Wall paint / accent colour / carpet colour are stored as descriptive tags (`context:navy_wall`, `context:sage_wall`, etc.) but do NOT change the staircase's identity or brain assignment. Two identical staircases in different colour rooms are the same design.

### Q · Historical / statement staircases

**Q4.b5.013** · What is a "Victorian/Edwardian grand hall" staircase?
A · Rich dark stained mahogany or oak throughout · turned spindles with elaborate detailing · massive turned newel with vase/acorn finial · full-height wood panelling on wall · patterned carpet runner with brass stair rods · classical chandelier · often accompanied by leaded window and ornate plaster crown moulding. img-2 is a batch 5 example.

**Q4.b5.014** · What distinguishes a "Jacobean/Tudor manor hall" staircase?
A · Rich dark stained oak/walnut with **Tudor-style raised panel** wall panelling (heavier and more architectural than plain wainscot) · massive newel with acorn finial · classical chandelier + arched window with heavy curtain · often accompanied by fireplace + framed portrait. img-7 is the batch example.

**Q4.b5.015** · What is a "country manor grand sweep" staircase?
A · A curved sweeping staircase with dramatic outward-curling flared base, mid-tone oak treads + rich dark turned spindles, MASSIVE turned newel with square panelled base + turned upper + acorn finial. Typically found in stone-clad or exposed-beam settings. img-9 is the batch example.

**Q4.b5.016** · What defines an "industrial loft" staircase?
A · Reclaimed weathered barn timber treads + apron · black iron plumbing pipe handrail with elbow fittings and flange mounting brackets · exposed brick + charcoal walls · often accompanied by industrial pendant lighting, framed text art, and vintage bar-scene furnishings. img-34 and img-41 both show this aesthetic.

Signals to future advisor conversations that these are attainable through human joinery, not just AI renders.
