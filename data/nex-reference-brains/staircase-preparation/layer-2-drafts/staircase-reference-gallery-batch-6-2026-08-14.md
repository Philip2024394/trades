## Questions and Answers (informed by batch 6)

**Q4.b6.001** · What is a "sculptural fretwork" balustrade?
A · A balustrade whose infill is a panel of solid timber (typically light oak) with a cut-out fretwork pattern — organic (branch/vine img-02, 08), angular (img-06), diamond lattice (img-07) or geometric. When backlit with an LED strip behind, the cutouts glow warm. Never seen in traditional English classical settings in batch 6; always in modern British luxury contexts with dark accent walls.

**Q4.b6.002** · What is an "LED-inset newel post strip"?
A · A vertical LED strip channelled into the face of a chunky newel post, glowing warm amber to signal the stair edge. Often paired with black metal accent plates on the newel corners. Seven examples in batch 6 (img-01, 03, 05, 09, 10, 11, 12) — this is now the dominant grand-entry lighting pattern in the Brain.

**Q4.b6.003** · What is a "threaded / ridged stainless steel spindle"?
A · A cylindrical brushed stainless steel spindle whose shaft carries a visible thread pattern (like a large screw thread) with square metal end caps at top and bottom. Very industrial-luxury. img-34, 35 show this — always paired with rich dark walnut framework.

**Q4.b6.004** · What are "piano-wire" horizontal balustrade rods?
A · Multiple parallel horizontal stainless steel rods, much thinner (approx 4-6mm) than typical horizontal-rod balustrades (which are usually 8-12mm). Reads visually lighter. img-36 is the batch example.

**Q4.b6.005** · What is a "chunky stainless steel vertical spindle"?
A · A brushed stainless cylindrical spindle wider (approx 25-35mm) than a slim rod (10-16mm) with square end caps top and bottom. Larger visual presence than a slim rod. img-39, 40 show this design.

**Q4.b6.006** · What is a "cane / rattan webbing" balustrade?
A · A traditional natural cane (rattan) webbing panel (typically hexagonal cane web) used as balustrade infill between light oak framing. Rare use of a traditional bag/chair material in a staircase context. img-30 is the only batch example.

**Q4.b6.007** · What is a "zigzag / sawtooth" visible stringer?
A · A cantilever open-riser stair where the mounting stringer (usually hidden behind treads) is instead exposed as a solid timber panel whose profile follows every tread edge, creating a visible sawtooth silhouette from the side. img-16 is the batch example — sculptural rather than functional.

**Q4.b6.008** · What is a "grand split staircase"?
A · A staircase that ascends from a single central landing at the bottom and then splits into two separate flights going up in opposite directions (or in a curved sweep). Very grand · Georgian classical proportion. img-32 is the first example in NEX Brain.

**Q4.b6.009** · What is a "grand double-height entry with symmetrical upper split landings (galleria)"?
A · A grand staircase ascending straight through a double-height entrance hall with the upper landing splitting symmetrically to left and right (forming a galleria overlooking the entry below). img-05, 11, 12 all show this configuration — Georgian classical.

**Q4.b6.010** · What is a "boxed-in cottage space-saver"?
A · A straight-flight staircase where both sides are enclosed with solid timber cladding (no visible spindle balustrade from the front) — the handrail is wall-mounted. Purely functional, minimal footprint. img-13 shows this cottage/farmhouse style.

**Q4.b6.011** · What is a Corinthian-column carved balustrade?
A · An extremely ornate hand-carved balustrade where each baluster is a full classical Corinthian column with a fluted shaft and a carved capital featuring acanthus leaves, often crowned by a decorative scroll pediment above. Renaissance Revival masterwork. img-49 is the only batch example.

**Q4.b6.012** · What is a "carved scroll / foliate" balustrade?
A · A heavily carved dark walnut balustrade whose panels feature curling foliate scrollwork (leaves, vines, flowers) as decoration between plain balusters, usually paired with a carved acanthus cartouche at the newel base of a curved sweeping staircase. Victorian/Edwardian revival masterwork. img-50 is the batch example.

**Q4.b6.013** · What is an "LED riser inset" lighting pattern?
A · An LED strip channelled into the front face of each riser (not under the nosing above), creating a dramatic backlit stripe pattern up the flight. Different from under-nosing LED (which lights the tread from above). img-28 shows this pattern paired with symmetric composition.

**Q4.b6.014** · What is an "under-stair seating nook"?
A · A built-in seating area occupying the triangular void under the stringer, typically featuring an upholstered chair or bench, an accent lamp or pendant, and a decorative wall panel (vertical slat, framed art). Reads as intentional lifestyle detail rather than storage. img-33 shows a sophisticated version.

**Q4.b6.015** · What is an "integrated under-stair radiator cover"?
A · A joinery unit that houses a radiator under the stair void, typically with a decorative lattice or grille front (diamond cross-hatch, vertical slats) painted white or matching the stair. Practical + decorative. img-26 shows a diamond-lattice example.

**Q4.b6.016** · What is a "carpet showroom display staircase"?
A · A staircase built inside a retail carpet/flooring store as a live display fixture for customers to see how carpet runners look on stairs. Not a home staircase but a retail example — img-48 shows one with "carpet warehouse" and "Lifestyle Floors" branding visible. Useful reference for NEX Brain to distinguish retail-context vs residential.

**Q4.b6.017** · Why does batch 6 include so many companion / near-duplicate images (img-11/12, img-37/38 area, img-41/42/43, img-46/51, etc.)?
A · Deliberate rotation of angle and finish to teach NEX that the same construction reads differently from different vantage points, and that construction identity is separate from viewing angle. Same as the img-20/21 (batch 4) and img-13/14 (batch 5) lessons at scale.

**Q4.b6.019** · How does batch 6 change what NEX knows compared to batches 4 and 5?
A · Batch 6 adds two entire new categories: **carved masterwork** (img-49 Corinthian columns + img-50 curved scroll foliate) and **sculptural fretwork balustrades** (branch/geometric/diamond/vine variants + cane webbing). Also adds first grand split staircase, first galleria configuration, first zigzag visible stringer, first LED-riser-inset pattern. The Brain is now much stronger on statement + ornate + grand + sculptural where batches 4/5 leaned modern + traditional.

**Q4.b6.020** · What is a "Renaissance Revival" carved balustrade?
A · Balustrade design inspired by 16th-century Italian Renaissance classical architecture, featuring hand-carved fluted columns, carved acanthus-leaf capitals (Corinthian order), and decorative scroll pediments or friezes above the columns. Very rare in modern construction · signals master joiner craftsmanship. img-49 is the batch example.

**Q4.b6.021** · What is a "Victorian revival" carved balustrade?
A · Balustrade design inspired by 19th-century Victorian ornate carving, featuring heavily carved foliate scrollwork (leaves, vines, flowers) as balustrade infill panels, usually in dark stained walnut or mahogany, typically on a curved sweeping staircase with a carved acanthus scroll cartouche at the newel base. img-50 is the batch example.
