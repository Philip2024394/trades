# Staircase Step Mats

**Step mats** (also called stair tread mats, tread covers, per-tread rugs or stair grip mats) are individual soft coverings that sit on each stair tread — typically one mat per tread — as an alternative to a continuous carpet runner or full carpet.

They provide grip, sound-dampening, warmth underfoot, and visual softening for hardwood or hard-surface staircases without covering the timber underneath.

---

## 1 · Step mats vs continuous carpet runner vs full carpet

- **Full carpet** — the whole staircase (treads + risers + edges) is carpeted. No timber visible.
- **Continuous carpet runner** — a single length of carpet runs down the centre of the staircase; timber margins remain visible on both sides.
- **Per-tread step mats** — individual mats sit on each tread; the risers and margins remain fully exposed timber. Each mat can be lifted independently.

Step mats sit at the **lightest-touch end** of that spectrum — the underlying staircase joinery remains fully visible, while soft coverage is added only where feet land.

---

## 2 · When to use step mats

- **Timber staircase where the joinery is the design feature** — mats add grip without hiding the treads/risers/nosing/newels
- **Refurbishment where the customer wants softness but not a full carpet commitment** — mats are removable/replaceable/washable
- **Slippery-tread staircase** (polished hardwood, waxed treads, laminate) where a full carpet is not wanted — mats add traction only on the tread walking surface
- **Homes with pets or children** — sound-dampening plus grip
- **Where the starting step is a curved / bullnose feature** — a per-tread mat can be shaped for the bullnose tread without carpeting a difficult curve. Sits well with the **carpet-and-starting-step tendency** (see `starting-steps-types-carpet-and-design-2026-08-14.md` Part 3): timber starting step remains exposed as the feature, but step mats can still be added above.
- **Where a customer wants seasonal / interchangeable style** — mats can be swapped for different colours or textures

Step mats are not normally used where a full continuous look is desired — a runner or full carpet suits that better.

Simple dark rectangular / D-shaped mats fitted per tread. Slight overhang beyond the tread nosing. Reads as a subtle grip-and-sound layer that keeps the oak visible on all four sides of each mat.

### 3.2 · Charcoal narrower / smaller mats
Same dark colour, smaller footprint — more of the oak tread showing around each mat. Reads as an even lighter touch.

### 3.3 · Charcoal mats including the bullnose starting step
Rectangular / bullnose-shaped mats sit even on the projecting starting step, showing that step mats can be shaped for curved treads that a carpet runner would struggle with.

### 3.4 · Brown / beige leaf-motif step mats
Warmer brown mats with a small cream leaf motif printed / woven into each mat. Coordinated with a matching brown floor rug at the base. Reads as a decorative rather than utilitarian choice — the mats become part of the room's textile scheme.

### 3.5 · Natural sisal / seagrass per-tread mats (batch 7 evidence)
Woven natural fibre mats per tread — warm neutral tone, textural, no printed motif. Common on modern British staircases where the mat visually connects with a natural jute or sisal hall rug.

## 4 · Sizing and fitting considerations

- **Mat depth** must not exceed tread depth minus the recommended clearance at the nosing (customer/installer verifies against actual tread size — never a generic rule).
- **Mat width** may be full tread width or centred with visible timber margins on both sides.
- **Adhesion/anti-slip backing** — non-slip rubberised backing, adhesive pads, or friction-only fitting depending on product. Adhesive-backed mats are more secure but harder to lift/replace.
- **Bullnose / curved starting step** — needs a curved-shape mat or a rectangular mat set back from the curve; a straight mat overhanging a bullnose will look wrong.
- **Two-sided cut-string staircase** — mats normally sit centrally on each tread; the exposed timber wraps on both sides.
- **Wall-fixed staircase** — mats may be positioned toward the free side or centred; the wall skirting is visible along the wall edge.
- **Compatibility with LED under-nosing** — mats should not obstruct or cast shadows over the LED strip below the nosing.

---

## 5 · How step mats relate to the wider staircase design language

- **Traditional / period staircases** — sisal, jute, wool weave, or subtle patterned mats. Coordinate with wool carpet elsewhere.
- **English cottage / farmhouse** — natural fibre or striped mats. Coordinate with hall rug.
- **Modern British** — solid neutral (charcoal, oatmeal, taupe) mats. Coordinate with runner rugs.
- **Architect-modern / minimalist** — very slim mats or none at all; the exposed timber tread is the feature.
- **Family / practical** — printed motif or coloured mats; sound-dampening becomes primary.

The mat is a **soft-layer addition** to the joinery — not a replacement for it. The staircase joinery is chosen first; the mat is chosen last, and can be swapped seasonally or replaced without touching the staircase.

## 6 · Step-mats Q&A (NEX advisor evidence)

**Q · What are step mats?**
A · Individual soft covers that sit on each stair tread — one mat per tread. They provide grip, sound-dampening, warmth and visual softening for hardwood staircases without covering the timber underneath.

**Q · What is the difference between step mats and a stair runner?**
A · A runner is a single continuous length of carpet running down the centre of the staircase. Step mats are separate per-tread covers that leave the risers and margins fully exposed timber. Runners create a continuous visual line; step mats are individually removable.

**Q · Are step mats a good option for a bullnose starting step?**
A · Yes — a per-tread mat can be shaped for the curved bullnose without the difficulty of terminating a continuous carpet runner around a curve. The bullnose remains visible as a feature; the mat sits above it.

**Q · Do step mats work on painted or laminate staircases?**
A · Yes — step mats work on any hard-surface stair tread. They are particularly useful on slippery treads (polished hardwood, waxed, laminate) where a full carpet is not wanted but grip is needed.

**Q · Can step mats have an adhesive back?**
A · Some do. Adhesive-backed mats are more secure but harder to lift and replace. Non-adhesive mats with rubberised anti-slip backing are lifted more easily and can be washed / rotated / replaced.

**Q · How do step mats interact with LED under-nosing lighting?**
A · They must not obstruct or cast shadows over the LED strip below the tread nosing. Mat placement should leave the nosing lip clear so the LED wash spills correctly onto the tread below.

**Q · Can step mats be different from each other on the same staircase?**
A · Usually no — a coordinated set of matching mats reads intentionally. Occasionally the starting-step mat is shaped differently (bullnose) while the rest of the flight uses rectangular mats, but the material and colour normally match.

**Q · Are step mats compatible with a hall rug at the base of the staircase?**
A · Yes — often coordinated. The mats can share the material / weave / motif of a hall rug at the foot of the flight, so the two textile elements read as one scheme.

**Q · Can I have step mats plus an exposed bullnose starting step?**
A · Yes. The bullnose starting step can remain exposed timber (see the carpet-and-starting-step tendency in `starting-steps-types-carpet-and-design-2026-08-14.md`) while step mats begin on the second tread up. Both the timber feature step AND the sound-dampened grip on the rest of the flight can coexist.

**Q · What sizes do step mats come in?**
A · Depth is typically less than the full tread depth (a small strip of timber remains visible behind the mat and in front of the riser); width can be full or reduced to leave timber margins on the sides. Customer / installer sizes against the actual staircase.
