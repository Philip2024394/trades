### Locked observation captured in this teaching series

- **Both-sided rounded bullnoses require both sides to be open** — the D-shape / oval / half-round / bullnose profile projects outward, and the wall side of a wall-fixed staircase blocks such projection.
- **Square platform starting steps work on both wall-fixed and two-sided cut-string staircases** — they do not require outward projection, only forward projection.
- **No projecting starting step is a valid choice** — the first tread starts flush at floor level. Common where floor space or minimalism dictates.

## Cross-cutting principles reinforced by batch 9

1. **Component families must match staircase style** — turned newels + volute + ball finial + moulded handrail = traditional; square newels + plain returns + matt-black caps + square handrail = modern.
2. **Timber choice sets the cost tier and reads the style** — pine (low, cottage/paint-grade), ash/beech (medium, modern-neutral), oak/maple (medium-high, English/Scandi), walnut/cherry/mahogany (high, formal).
3. **Starting-step geometry is governed by construction type** — two-sided cut-string can round on both sides; wall-fixed can round only on the free side.
4. **Newel caps are refurbishment-friendly** — a staircase can be transformed by replacing caps alone without changing the newel post.
5. **The staircase is a system of coordinated components** — treads, risers, string, base rail, spindles, handrail, newel, newel cap, starting step, volute, wedges, scroll brackets — each chosen from the compatible family for the overall design.
