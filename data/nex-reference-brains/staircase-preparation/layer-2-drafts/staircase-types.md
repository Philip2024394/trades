- A staircase manufacturer classifies staircases into five distinct product levels. *(L16 · adjusted from the perspective statement "A good staircase manufacturer sees..." per Philip 2026-07-30)*
  - **type:** classification
- Each level has its own manufacturing complexity, skill requirements, materials mix, and price positioning. *(L16)*
  - **type:** classification
- **Confusing levels leads to bad estimates, bad customer conversations, and unfair comparisons.** *(L16)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
  - **verification note (v2 restored):** three-consequence atom silently dropped in v1 — this is the WHY the taxonomy exists
- **Level 1** is a standard straight staircase: straight flight with 13–15 risers, standard oak or pine. Baseline complexity. *(L22)*
  - **type:** classification
- **Level 2** is winders and feature starts: a straight flight with kite winders at the bottom, or a bullnose/curtail feature start. Moderate complexity. *(L23)*
  - **type:** classification
- **Level 3** is a quarter-turn or half-turn staircase: L-shaped (quarter-turn) or U-shaped (half-turn) with an intermediate landing. Higher complexity from geometry planning and two coordinated flights. *(L24)*
  - **type:** classification
- **Level 4** is a sweeping radius staircase: a curved sweeping flight, often a feature staircase in a large entrance. High complexity from curved strings, curved handrails, CNC or specialist lamination. *(L25)*
  - **type:** classification
- **Level 5** is an architectural grand staircase: bespoke elliptical, helical, double-return, or double-height entrance feature. Highest complexity — full custom design, engineering, specialist craftsmanship. *(L26)*
  - **type:** classification
- A higher-level staircase is not a marked-up Level 1. It is a different product with different labour, different materials sourcing, different risk. *(L47)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- Sweeping stairs go by many trade names: sweeping staircases, curved staircases, radius staircases, grand curved stairs, elliptical staircases, helical staircases. *(L53–L57)*
  - **type:** factual
- Helical staircases rise continuously without a central landing. *(L58)*
  - **type:** factual
- Many high-end UK staircase manufacturers make sweeping / curved staircases. *(L70)*
  - **type:** factual
- Sweeping stairs require specialist skills at every stage: design (radius, rise, going, headroom, handrail geometry, structural support), manufacturing (curved strings, curved handrails), installation. *(L70)*
  - **type:** factual
- A sweeping staircase string is curved and can be produced from: laminated timber layers, CNC-cut engineered sections, curved plywood formers, or specialist bending techniques. *(L74–L79)*
  - **type:** factual
  - **verification note (FLAG 1 fixed):** restored source verb *"can be produced from"* (possibility) · removed the added phrase *"one of four techniques"* (closed-set fabrication) · exhaustiveness of the list is not claimed by the source
- **Laminated bent handrail** — multiple thin strips glued around a former. *(L83)*
  - Advantages (✓): very stable · accurate curves · repeatable production. *(L85)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Solid curved handrail** — made from selected timber sections. *(L87)*
  - Advantages (✓): premium appearance · natural grain. *(L89)*
  - Disadvantages (✗): very difficult · expensive · requires exceptional timber. *(L90)*
  - **type:** factual
  - **verification note (FLAG 2 fixed):** restored the source's explicit ✓ advantages and ✗ disadvantages structure · previously flattened into flat prose which removed the pro/con framing the expert provided
- Premium sweeping stairs often use: *(L94)*
  - Oak — most popular (European Oak · American White Oak). *(L96)*
  - Walnut — luxury interiors. *(L97)*
  - Mahogany — traditional high-end work. *(L98)*
  - Painted hardwood — for classic American styles. *(L99)*
  - **type:** factual
  - **verification note (FLAG 3 fixed):** restored source verb *"often"* (previously changed to *"commonly"* — subtle strengthening) · restored per-material qualifiers *"most popular"* · *"luxury interiors"* · *"traditional high-end work"* · *"for classic American styles"* — these carry the expert reasoning for each material and are not removable connecting words
- Manufacturing sequence for a **Level 1** staircase: cut parts → assemble → install. *(L103–L111)*
  - **type:** process
- Manufacturing sequence for a **Level 4** sweeping staircase: survey → 3D design → radius calculations → custom templates → special timber selection → curved components manufactured → trial assembly → finishing → installation. *(L113–L133)*
  - **type:** process
- **A single curved handrail can represent many hours of skilled work.** *(L135)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
  - **verification note (v2 restored):** concrete expert-perspective atom about handrail labour intensity · silently dropped in v1
- The cost differential between levels is not margin. It is labour + specialist material sourcing + longer manufacturing cycles + higher risk. *(L135)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
Why sweeping staircases are more common in America than in the UK — three separate expert reasons, preserved individually (FLAG 4 fixed):

- **Larger house layouts.** Many American custom luxury homes have large entrance halls, double-height ceilings, open foyers — a sweeping staircase needs space, and a small UK hallway usually cannot accommodate the radius. *(L62)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- **Architectural style.** American luxury homes often use the staircase as a centrepiece: curved oak handrails, large newel posts, decorative balusters, carpet runners, feature lighting. The staircase is designed almost like a piece of furniture. *(L64)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- **Custom home market.** In the USA there is a large custom-build market where homeowners may spend significantly more on architectural features. *(L66)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
