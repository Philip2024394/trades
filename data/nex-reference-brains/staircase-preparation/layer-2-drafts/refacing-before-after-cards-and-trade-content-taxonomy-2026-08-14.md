## 2 · How the trade card system uses these categories

A refacing trade card is not a single image — it is a **composed template** that pulls images from multiple categories:

- **Hero slot** — usually a TRUE BEFORE/AFTER (category 1.1) or a PRODUCT RENDER (1.3)
- **Process slot** — CRAFTSMANSHIP / IN-PROGRESS (1.2)
- **Materials tile row** — ISOLATED COMPONENT PRODUCT SHOTS (1.5)
- **Case-study strip** — UNDER-STAIR FEATURE (1.7) if the trade does bespoke transformations
- **Trust / capability row** — TRADE CENTRE / RETAIL / SUPPLY-CHAIN (1.10) or MATERIALS / QC (1.9)
- **Diagnostic add-on** — CONDITION SURVEY / PEST (1.8) if the trade also offers surveys
- **Design conversation opener** — DESIGN-SKETCH COMPOSITION (1.4) for aspirational conversations
- **Tools cue** — TOOLING (1.6) for craft credibility

The **card retrieval system** does not need a single "perfect" image per trade — it composes the card by pulling one image from each relevant category, filtered by:
- Timber species preference (customer / trade default)
- Staircase style (traditional / modern / minimalist / hybrid)
- Balustrade material (turned timber / matt black metal / stainless / glass)

## 3 · Composition principles for trade cards

- **Before / After is the strongest single-image type** for refacing cards because it demonstrates the transformation the customer is buying. Use where evidence is available; do not fabricate an "after" from a rendered image and pass it off as photographed evidence.
- **In-progress scenes build trust** — real trades on real projects with branded workwear read more credibly than sterile product renders.
- **Isolated component shots** are only useful when the customer is at the specification stage — do not lead the card with a component shot.
- **Under-stair feature scenes** are valuable case studies but should not lead the card unless the trade explicitly specialises in bespoke transformations.
- **Condition survey + pest imagery** is niche — surface it only where the trade offers surveys, or in advisor answers about "how do I know if my timber is sound before refacing?"
- **Trade centre / supply chain** imagery reads as capability signalling — best for trades that emphasise scale or specific supplier relationships.

## 4 · Trade-content-card Q&A (NEX advisor evidence)

**Q · What images should a refacing trade card include?**
A · At minimum a hero image (before/after or product render) and a proof-of-work in-progress image. Composed cards can also include isolated component tiles for materials, case-study under-stair transformations, tooling/QC credibility images, and design-conversation imagery.

**Q · What is a "before/after" refacing card?**
A · A split-panel composition with a raw/unfinished staircase in the "BEFORE" panel and the completed refacing result in the "AFTER" panel. Usually labelled with black-pill BEFORE/AFTER tags. The single strongest image type for demonstrating refacing value.

**Q · Can I use a rendered staircase image as a before/after "after"?**
A · No — this would mislead the customer. Renders can be used for aspirational / concept content (design-sketch category) but not as photographed after-evidence for a specific trade's completed work.

**Q · Do NEX trade cards use different image categories for different trade specialisms?**
A · Yes — the card retrieval system composes the card by pulling one image from each category relevant to the trade's declared capabilities. A trade offering surveys will surface condition-survey imagery; a trade offering bespoke under-stair transformations will surface those case studies.

**Q · What if a trade doesn't have their own photography?**
A · The card can be composed from the shared NEX Image Brain (product renders, materials tiles, trade-activity stock scenes) filtered to match the trade's declared specialisms and timber preferences. The trade's own photography, when supplied, always takes priority.
