### Group A · Before you order

- **A1 · The biggest buying mistake is choosing on price alone.** *(L37)*
  - **type:** warning
- Better comparison: timber quality · design · warranty · installation · after-sales support · reputation · survey process. *(L37)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- **The lowest quote is rarely the best value if problems follow.** *(L37)*
  - **type:** warning

- **A2 · Ask up front who takes the measurements.** *(L40)*
  - **type:** directive/rule
- If the staircase company measures, the survey error is normally their responsibility. If the customer supplies measurements, responsibility shifts. *(L40)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Get this in writing.** *(L40)*
  - **type:** directive/rule

- **A3 · Yes — always. Customers can review drawings before manufacture.** *(L43)*
  - **type:** factual
- Approval should cover: overall width · staircase direction · number of treads · newel locations · handrail style · balustrade type. *(L43)*
  - **type:** process
- **Approving drawings doesn't make the customer responsible for every technical measurement, but it's the stage that catches misunderstandings.** *(L43)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

- **A4 · If possible, visit the factory or showroom.** *(L46)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- A factory visit lets you see machinery · timber storage · sanding quality · finished staircases · attention to detail. *(L46)*
  - **type:** factual
- A showroom visit lets you compare timber species · handrail profiles · finishes · balusters · newel posts. *(L46)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Seeing and touching materials makes decisions easier.** *(L46)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

- **A5 · Yes — ask to see previous work.** *(L48)*
  - **type:** directive/rule
- Good companies show completed projects · photographs · testimonials · examples of different timber species. *(L48)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Real work beats brochures.** *(L48)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

- **A6 · Fair questions about where the timber comes from:** which species · is it kiln dried · is it certified from responsibly managed forests · is it solid or engineered/laminated. *(L52)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- **Customers increasingly value origin transparency.** *(L52)*
  - **type:** customer perception

- **A7 · The single best question to ask any staircase company:** *"If this staircase were going into your own home, what would you change and why?"* *(L54–L56)*

- **The answer tells you far more than a price list.** It reveals whether they're thinking about long-term quality · appearance · durability · customer satisfaction — not just completing another order. *(L58)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

### Group B · Design and materials

- **B1 · Oak is not always better than pine.** *(L62)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- Oak = harder · more durable · premium appearance. Pine = more affordable · easier to paint · suitable for many homes. *(L62)*
  - **type:** classification
- The best material depends on budget · design · finish. *(L62)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- **DEFER note:** cross-references wood-intelligence Principle 2 (application-specific quality) and Principle 9 (species characteristics · deferred in Materials v2).

- **B2 · The staircase does not always have to match the flooring.** *(L64)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- Great interiors often contrast — oak floor + painted staircase · walnut floor + oak staircase · stone floor + oak staircase. *(L64)*
  - **type:** classification
- **Colours and styles should complement, not necessarily match.** *(L64)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

- **B3 · Internal doors should generally match the staircase.** *(L66)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- Three architectural family examples: **Traditional** (4-panel doors + turned staircase) · **Modern** (Shaker doors + square newels + black balusters) · **Contemporary** (flush doors + glass balustrade). *(L66)*
  - **type:** classification
- **DEFER note:** cross-references staircase-design-terminology Principle G for the full door-style pairings table.

- **B4 · Neither timber nor glass balustrade is universally better — depends on the design.** *(L68)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- Timber = warm · traditional · easier to repair. Glass = modern · brighter · open feel. *(L68)*
  - **type:** classification

- **B5 · Children can safely use glass staircases when designed and installed to appropriate safety standards with suitable toughened or laminated glass where required.** *(L70)*

- **B6 · Increasingly yes to LED lighting.** *(L72)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

  - **type:** classification
- **Lighting can transform a staircase visually.** *(L72)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

- **B7 · Yes, mixing wood species is possible.** *(L74)*
  - **type:** factual

  - **type:** classification
- **Mixing done thoughtfully creates distinctive designs.** *(L74)*

### Group D · Fit and delivery disputes

- **D1 · "Doesn't fit" has three distinct situations:** *(L102–L106)*
  - **type:** classification
- **Manufacturing error** — staircase differs from approved drawing. **Company normally corrects.** *(L104)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Measuring error** — surveyor got dimensions wrong. **If company measured, they normally resolve.** *(L105)*
  - **type:** factual
- **The building changed** — builder moved a wall · floor level changed · concrete screed added · opening changed · steel beam installed later. **Often requires builder+staircase-company cooperation to decide responsibility.** *(L106)*
  - **type:** factual

- **D2 · Usually do not refuse delivery unless there's an obvious major issue.** *(L108)*
  - **type:** directive/rule
- If there's a problem: tell the driver/installer · note the issue · photograph it · contact the company promptly. *(L108)*
  - **type:** process
- **Accepting delivery does not necessarily mean accepting that everything is correct.** *(L108)*
  - **type:** factual

- **D3 · If there's a dispute, photograph:** whole staircase · close-up of the issue · tape measure showing dimensions · landing · floor opening · any damaged components. *(L110)*
  - **type:** process

- **D4 · Keep for a dispute:** quotation · drawings · approval emails · invoices · delivery note. *(L112)*
  - **type:** process
- **Good documentation resolves problems much faster.** *(L112)*
  - **type:** expert perspective

- **D5 · Whether the company can charge for a remake depends on why.** *(L114)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Company error** → they normally bear the cost. *(L114)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Customer changes their mind** (*"I want it to turn the other way"*) → different treatment, because the staircase may already have been manufactured to the agreed design. *(L114)*
  - **type:** factual

- **D6 · Ownership before payment depends on the contract.** *(L116)*
  - **type:** factual
- Many companies include a **retention of title clause** — company retains ownership until payment is made in full, even if delivered or installed **(subject to contract and applicable law).** *(L116)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Read the quotation and terms before ordering.** *(L116)*
  - **type:** directive/rule
- **BOUNDARY:** specific legal advice on retention of title clauses — NEX cannot answer (customer needs a solicitor for specific contract interpretation).

- **D7 · A colour dispute is different from a fault.** *(L118)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
- Ask: was the agreed species supplied · was the agreed stain/finish used · did you approve a sample beforehand. *(L118)*
  - **type:** process
- **Natural timber varies — two oak staircases from the same species will not be identical** (see B10). *(L118)*
  - **type:** factual

- **D8 · Damage after installation** (dropping a washing machine · moving furniture · pet damage · deep scratches) is **normally treated as accidental damage, not manufacturing fault.** *(L120)*
  - **type:** factual
- **Good news: timber often repairs more easily than many materials.** *(L120)*
  - **type:** expert perspective
