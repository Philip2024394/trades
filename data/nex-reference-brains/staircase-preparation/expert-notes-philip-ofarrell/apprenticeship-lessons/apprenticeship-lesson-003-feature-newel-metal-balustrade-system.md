## 5 · WHAT CAN I UNDERSTAND?

*(Understanding register. Every WHY qualified with "may have" · design language reading.)*

- **Why a feature newel at the lower end?** — a designer may have chosen a feature newel to provide a visual anchor and termination for the balustrade + handrail at the bottom of the flight. Feature newels often serve both a structural role (terminating the handrail and balustrade loads) and a visual role (marking the entry to the flight).
- **Why the material contrast between darker treads/newel and metallic-appearing balusters?** — a designer may have chosen the contrast to introduce a mixed-material design language. Darker warm-toned materials on the horizontal and vertical structural forms produce a traditional or grounded reading · while the metallic-appearing balusters introduce a contemporary or crafted-detail reading. The combination may aim at a *"traditional + contemporary"* or *"warm + cool"* design conversation rather than a single stylistic register.
- **Why closed risers with this balustrade style?** — closed risers may have been chosen to preserve a resolved appearance on the tread face · avoiding the more industrial or minimalist reading of open risers · consistent with a mixed-material premium feature reading.
- **Why a continuous handrail line?** — a designer may have preferred a continuous handrail for visual coherence and user experience. Continuous rails read as more refined than segmented rails.
- **Why balustrade rather than half-wall enclosure?** — the choice of an open balustrade system (rather than a solid or half-height wall) may have been made to allow visual openness across the flight while still meeting the safety requirement.

*(A visual intent indicator is present · per Lesson 001 review guidance: "the visible design includes contrasting material elements intended to create visual emphasis." This is a visually-supported observation about intent · not an unsupported claim about quality or price tier.)*
