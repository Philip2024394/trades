# Apprenticeship Lesson #002 · Concealed Structural Support

## 5 · WHAT CAN I UNDERSTAND?

*(Understanding register. Every WHY traces to observation or authored fact. "May have" qualifier applied to intent.)*

- **Why conceal the structural support?** — a designer may have chosen concealed support so that the staircase reads as an architectural element of the space rather than a constructed object sitting within it. Concealment produces the appearance that the staircase is emerging directly from the surrounding walls, even though the underlying construction remains conventional timber joinery.
- **Why enclose the staircase between two walls?** — the enclosure may have been chosen to reinforce the minimalist reading (walls carry the visual weight · staircase reads as inserted rather than free-standing).
- **Why closed risers on a concealed-support staircase?** — closed risers may have been selected to remove the through-view to the underside · which would otherwise expose the structural approach.
- **Why concealed fixings rather than visible ones?** — visible fixings would compete with the minimalist appearance · concealment supports the architectural reading of the staircase as a finished object.
- **Why keep the structural strings as timber rather than switching to steel?** — the source specification indicates conventional premium staircase manufacturing techniques. A designer may retain timber strings when they are hidden because the load requirement is met by timber engineering and switching materials would introduce additional interfaces (timber-to-steel connection · differential expansion · alternative fixing hardware).
- **Why does the design read as "emerging from the walls"?** — this is a specific visual intent indicator (per Lesson 001 review guidance): the enclosed configuration + concealed strings + closed risers + wall-mounted handrail together produce a reading where no staircase element visually sits separately from the wall.

## 6 · WHAT CAN I NOT KNOW?

*(The hardest section for this lesson · because the concealed support system is exactly what NEX must NOT invent.)*

### I CANNOT KNOW

- The exact material of the concealed structural strings (timber species · engineered timber variant · laminated construction) beyond the spec-attested category "timber structural strings"
- The section dimensions of the concealed strings
- The fixing method between the strings and the treads/risers (housed joint · glued · screwed · wedged · combination)
- The fixing method between the strings and the surrounding building structure (bolted to wall studs · sat on ledgers · rebated into masonry · other)
- Whether any steel reinforcement is present within or alongside the timber strings
- The load rating of the staircase
- The deflection under load
- Whether the concealed underside includes plasterboard · timber cladding · void space · services routing
- The exact rise · going · nosing profile · overall length
- Regulatory compliance for any building code (UK · IRE · US · EU · etc.)
- Manufacturer identity · country of installation · date of manufacture
- Cost · warranty · durability record
- Whether this is a finished installation photograph or a design visualisation

### I MAY UNDERSTAND

*(Interpretations qualified with "appears".)*

- The concealed support approach appears to preserve a minimalist architectural reading of the staircase
- The concealed support approach appears to require careful coordination between the staircase manufacturer and the surrounding wall construction (so that no fixing or seam becomes visible)
- The concealed support approach appears to add manufacturing complexity relative to an exposed-string configuration
- The concealed support approach appears to constrain later maintenance access

### I KNOW

*(Only backed by direct authored evidence in the source specification.)*

- Structural approach: traditional timber structural strings located directly beneath the treads and risers, forming the load-bearing structure of the flight (source: Spec 3 · Structural Construction section)
- The strings are integrated into the staircase assembly and are not visible from this viewpoint (source: Spec 3)
- The enclosing walls are architectural elements · NOT the stair strings (source: Spec 3 · explicit clarification)
- The staircase remains a conventionally manufactured timber staircase despite the enclosed appearance (source: Spec 3 · Manufacturing Intelligence section)

## 8 · TEACHING VALUE

**What can future NEX honestly TEACH from this lesson?**

- The concept of concealed structural support as a design pattern
- The distinction between what the eye sees (result) and what the specification attests (method)
- The relationship between concealment discipline and the surrounding design decisions (closed risers · enclosed configuration · concealed fixings · wall-mounted handrail)
- The three purposes concealment may serve (minimalist reading · architectural integration · staircase-emerging-from-walls illusion)
- The maintenance / manufacturing / coordination cost of concealment (I MAY UNDERSTAND · qualified)
- The specific I CANNOT KNOW list around any concealed system: material · dimension · fixing method · engineering · load rating · code compliance
- The anti-hallucination discipline: *"the appearance suggests a concealed approach, but the actual method cannot be known from the image alone unless provided by specification"*

**What SUBJECTS are unlocked by having this lesson in memory?**

- *"concealed structural support"* as a first-class subject with authored cannot-know boundaries
- *"hidden construction"* as a design language / intent indicator
- *"structural strings"* as a subject that can appear visibly (Lesson 001 · concealed under the treads) OR invisibly (Lesson 002 · same but as the framed subject)
- *"between-walls staircase"* as a first-class subject (count: 2 · one step further toward earning claim status)
- The distinction *"I can see the result"* vs *"I know the method"* as a NEX-wide teaching principle

**What follow-up questions can the user progressively ask?**

- How would concealed steel support differ from concealed timber support?
- What manufacturing sequence produces this concealment?
- What are the maintenance considerations for a concealed-support staircase?
- What visible cues indicate concealed support has been used?
- What are the alternative structural approaches for a straight flight between walls?

**What does this lesson add that prior lessons did not?**

- Lesson 001 tested a VISIBLE specialist relationship (brushed steel riser strip). Lesson 002 tests a HIDDEN system boundary (concealed structure).
- Lesson 002 is the first lesson where CANNOT KNOW carries the primary teaching weight rather than sitting as a discipline behind visible content.
- Lesson 002 introduces the "I can see the result vs I know the method" distinction as a teachable principle.
- Lesson 002 extends the *"enclosed straight flight"* subject count (Lesson 001 + Lesson 002 = 2 authored instances · still below frequency-claim threshold).
