- **Why the contrast between the warm-toned treads/newel and the very-dark balusters?** — a designer may have chosen the contrast to introduce a mixed-appearance design language. WHETHER the contrast represents two DIFFERENT materials OR one material with different finishes is UNKNOWN from image alone (per Lesson 003 strengthening).
- **Why an open-side balustrade rather than an enclosed wall?** — the open-side configuration may have been chosen to allow visual openness across the flight. Consistent with Lesson 003's open-side pattern (but three-lesson set is still not enough for frequency claims).
- **Why the concentrated glow beneath the tread nosings?** — a designer may have chosen to illuminate the tread edges to improve visibility of the step transitions AND to create an architectural feature effect. WHETHER the light source is integrated into the staircase OR concealed elsewhere is UNKNOWN.
- **Why closed risers with visible under-tread glow?** — closed risers preserve the visual closure of each step face WHILE allowing the glow effect to emphasise each tread edge. The two design decisions serve complementary readings.
- **Why a feature newel at the base?** — a designer may have chosen a feature newel to anchor the balustrade termination and provide a visual entry marker for the flight. Consistent with Lesson 003 pattern.
- **Why a handrail on the enclosed side as well as the open side?** — the presence of a wall-mounted handrail alongside a balustrade-mounted handrail may indicate user-support redundancy · accessibility consideration · or design symmetry preference. The reason cannot be verified from image alone.
