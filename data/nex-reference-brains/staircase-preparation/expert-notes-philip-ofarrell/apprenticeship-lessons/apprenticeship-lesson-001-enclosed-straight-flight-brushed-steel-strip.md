## 5 · WHAT CAN I UNDERSTAND?

*(Understanding register. Every WHY traces back to an observed pattern or an authored fact.)*

- **Why enclose the staircase between two walls?** — a designer may have chosen this configuration to maximise the appearance of the staircase as an architectural element of the space rather than a separate object. The enclosure produces clean geometric lines and removes the need for balustrade design.
- **Why closed risers rather than open?** — a designer may have chosen closed risers to reinforce the premium contemporary character (open risers read as more minimalist/industrial · closed reads as more resolved/premium).
- **Why the brushed stainless steel strip on each riser?** — the authored specification names three purposes: architectural detail, edge protection from foot scuffs, and visual definition between steps. The strip may also serve as a subtle reflective element that catches the architectural lighting.
- **Why wall-mounted handrail on one side only?** — with enclosure on both sides, only one handrail is required for user support; single-side mounting produces a simpler, more architectural appearance.
- **Why concealed structural strings?** — a designer may have chosen concealed strings to preserve the appearance that the staircase is "emerging directly from the walls" rather than sitting as a separate constructed object between them.
- **Why porcelain tile at the ground floor?** — the ground-floor material may have been chosen to provide a durable contrast to the warm oak · large-format tiles read as premium and reduce grout lines that would visually compete with the staircase.
- **Why recessed and cove lighting rather than tread lighting?** — a designer may have preferred to treat the staircase as a finished architectural object rather than a lit feature · lighting is placed AROUND the staircase to illuminate it rather than integrated INTO it.
