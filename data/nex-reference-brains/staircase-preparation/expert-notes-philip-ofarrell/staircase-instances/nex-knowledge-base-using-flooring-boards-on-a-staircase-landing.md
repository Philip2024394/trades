# Using Flooring Boards on a Staircase Landing

Can I use my flooring boards on the staircase landing? Yes, in many cases you can — but it's important to confirm this with your staircase manufacturer or installer before ordering or fitting the flooring.

The landing floor and staircase are designed to work together, so the flooring thickness can affect how the staircase connects to the landing.

## What your staircase manufacturer normally wants to know

Your staircase manufacturer will normally want to know:

- The finished flooring thickness
- Whether the flooring is engineered timber · solid timber · laminate · or LVT
- Whether an underlay will be used
- The finished floor height above the structural floor
- Whether the flooring will finish flush with the top tread
- How the flooring meets the second flight on quarter-turn or split-level staircases

Providing these details allows the staircase to be manufactured or adjusted so the top landing transition is correct and complies with building requirements.

## Why is the flooring thickness important?

The finished flooring thickness determines the final height of the landing.

If the staircase is built for a 20 mm engineered oak floor, but you later install a 14 mm floor, the top step may finish slightly proud of the landing.

Likewise, if a thicker floor is installed than originally planned, the top riser height may change. Stair designers aim to keep all riser heights as consistent as possible, as required by building regulations in many countries.

## Should I buy finished or unfinished flooring?

If your staircase is also being supplied unfinished, many staircase manufacturers recommend choosing unfinished timber flooring where practical.

This allows:

- The staircase and landing floor to be sanded and finished together
- The same stain or clear varnish to be applied to both
- A closer colour match
- A more consistent sheen across the landing and staircase

If you purchase pre-finished flooring and an unfinished staircase, it can be difficult to achieve an exact colour and finish match afterwards.

## Will the timber match exactly?

Not always.

Even if both products are described as European Oak, American White Oak, Walnut or another timber species, natural wood varies.

You may notice differences in:

- Grain pattern
- Knot size and quantity
- Colour variation
- Growth rings
- Natural mineral streaks
- Character markings

This is completely normal because every piece of timber is unique.

Some homeowners prefer a uniform appearance, while others enjoy the natural variation as a feature that adds character and authenticity.

## Before ordering your flooring

Confirm the following with your staircase manufacturer:

- Finished flooring thickness
- Underlay thickness (if applicable)
- Landing construction details
- Top tread thickness
- Whether the staircase will be supplied finished or unfinished
- Expansion gaps around timber flooring
- Direction of the flooring boards across the landing
- Suitable stair nosings or transition trims if required

## Why plan flooring and staircase together

By planning your flooring and staircase together from the beginning, you can achieve a cleaner transition, a more consistent finish, and reduce the likelihood of costly alterations later in the project.
