# Newel Posts · Phase 16 · Customer Buying Decisions, Specification and Quotation Knowledge

This phase covers what happens when a customer moves from learning about newels to choosing, ordering and approving a staircase. A professional staircase advisor needs to understand not only what a newel is, but how it affects the final project decision.

## 1. Choosing the Right Newel Post for the Customer

Customers rarely ask *"What newel post do I need?"* They usually ask:

- *"Why does this staircase look different from another one?"*
- *"Why is this quote more expensive?"*
- *"Can I make it more modern?"*
- *"Can I match my existing stairs?"*
- *"Why do I need such a large post?"*

The answer is usually connected to newel selection.

## 2. Newel Post Size vs Staircase Scale

A common mistake is choosing the post size only by price. The post must match the staircase proportion.

**Small domestic staircase** — typical 80–90mm square newel. Suitable for narrow hallways · simple straight flights · smaller homes.

**Standard premium staircase** — typical 90–100mm square newel. Suitable for oak staircases · modern homes · open-plan designs.

**Large feature staircase** — typical 120mm+ · custom sizes. Suitable for entrances · luxury homes · commercial spaces.

**A small newel on a large staircase looks weak. An oversized newel on a small staircase looks heavy.**

## 3. How Newels Affect Staircase Price

The newel post is one of many components affecting cost.

**Timber species** — pine → oak → walnut → exotic hardwood.

**Construction** — solid timber is more expensive · premium appearance · better repair options. Engineered is stable · efficient · often more cost controlled.

**Machining** — simple square: less machining. Turned: more labour. CNC carved: specialist production.

**Finish** — raw timber: lowest cost. Painted: more preparation. Hand-finished: highest cost.

## 4. Matching Newels to Existing Homes

Customers often replace only part of a staircase. Important questions:

- What timber is existing?
- What stain colour?
- What handrail profile?
- What baluster design?
- What age is the staircase?

A new oak newel beside old pine may look different even after staining.

## 5. Newel Post and Handrail Compatibility

The newel and handrail must work together.

**Traditional** — turned newel + rounded handrail + turned spindles.
**Contemporary** — square newel + square handrail + metal balusters.
**Luxury modern** — oak feature post + glass panels + LED detail.

## 6. Starting Newel Decisions

The first newel creates the first impression.

**Standard start** — simple and practical. Used when space is limited · budget is controlled.

**Feature start** — larger decorative post. Used when entrance is visible · staircase is a design feature.

**Extended newel** — post continues above the handrail. Used for traditional architecture · dramatic appearance.

## 7. Newel Posts and Staircase Layout

Different layouts create different requirements.

**Straight staircase** — usually bottom newel + top newel.
**Quarter turn** — corner newel + landing connection.
**Half turn** — multiple structural posts + landing coordination.
**Curved staircase** — specialist newel design + curved handrail coordination.

## 8. Customer Questions Nex Should Answer

**"Can I make my staircase look more expensive?"** — A larger feature newel, better timber selection and premium handrail profile can create a stronger entrance statement.

**"Why are oak newels expensive?"** — Oak costs more because of timber quality, machining requirements, finishing and durability.

**"Can I replace just the newel?"** — Sometimes yes, but it depends on how the existing balustrade and staircase structure are connected.

**"Can I paint oak newels?"** — Yes, but many customers choose oak because they want the natural grain visible.

## 9. Newel Post Specification Sheet

A professional quote should identify:

- **Location** — bottom flight · landing · top floor · feature position
- **Material** — oak · pine · walnut · painted hardwood
- **Size** — e.g. 90mm x 90mm
- **Height** — measured from finished floor level to handrail termination
- **Style** — square · turned · chamfered · decorative
- **Finish** — natural oil · lacquer · stain · paint

## 10. Nex Knowledge Rule

When a customer asks about a newel post, Nex should understand the hidden intent.

Customer: *"How big should my newel be?"* — possible meanings: design question · safety question · price question · comparison question. The answer should adapt.
