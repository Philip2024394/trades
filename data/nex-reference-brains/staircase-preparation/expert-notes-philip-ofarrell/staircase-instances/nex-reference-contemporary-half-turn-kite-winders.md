# Contemporary Half-Turn Staircase with Kite Winders — Overhead View

*This image shows a contemporary half-turn timber staircase viewed from the first-floor gallery looking down into the stairwell. The staircase combines a straight flight with kite winder steps to achieve a smooth 180-degree change in direction without using a full intermediate landing.*

*This is an excellent teaching image because it demonstrates how winders can replace a landing, reducing the amount of floor space required while maintaining a comfortable walking route.*

Unlike a staircase with a rectangular landing, this design changes direction using specially shaped treads.

## Walking Line

The walking line begins at the centre of the entrance.

The user walks:

1. Up the lower straight flight.
2. Across the three kite winders.
3. Through a smooth 180-degree turn.
4. Continues up the upper straight flight.
5. Arrives at the first-floor landing.

The walking line follows the widest part of the winders, providing the safest and most comfortable route through the turn.

## Winder Steps

**This staircase uses three kite winders.**

**Purpose:**

- Changes direction by approximately 180°
- Eliminates the need for a full landing
- Saves floor space
- Creates a smooth flowing staircase

**Characteristics:**

- Treads become wider on the outside of the turn.
- Treads become narrower towards the inside.
- Each winder has a different geometric shape.
- The centre winder forms the main turning point.

*The winders are carefully designed so the walking line provides consistent footing.*

## Teaching Notes for Nex

*This image is an excellent reference for teaching the difference between landing staircases and winder staircases.*

Users should first identify the overall layout, which is a half-turn (U-shaped) staircase. They should then observe how the direction changes. In this example, the staircase does not use a rectangular landing. Instead, it uses three kite winder steps, allowing the staircase to turn approximately 180 degrees within a smaller footprint.

This image also demonstrates closed string construction. The side strings remain smooth and continuous, concealing the ends of the treads and risers. Unlike a cut string staircase, there are no exposed tread ends or decorative stepped profiles.

*By comparing this staircase with a half-turn staircase that uses a full landing, Nex can teach users that the overall layout may be the same, but the method of changing direction is different. A landing provides a flat platform between flights, whereas winders use specially shaped treads to create the turn while saving space. This distinction is one of the key concepts in understanding staircase design.*
