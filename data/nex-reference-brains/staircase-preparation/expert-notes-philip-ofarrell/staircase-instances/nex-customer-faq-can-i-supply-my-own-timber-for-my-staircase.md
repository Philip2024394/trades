## Customer Question

> ***Can I supply my own timber for my staircase?***

*The simple answer is yes, but there are several important considerations before any staircase manufacturer agrees to use customer-supplied timber.*

## Why Staircase Makers May Be Reluctant

*Although reclaimed timber can produce stunning staircases with unique character, it introduces manufacturing risks that are not normally present with professionally sourced kiln-dried timber.*

### Hidden Metal

*One of the biggest concerns is concealed metal inside reclaimed wood.*

Examples include:

- Old nails
- Screws
- Bolts
- Wire
- Fence staples
- Broken drill bits
- Brackets
- Shot pellets or fragments from agricultural use

*Even after metal detection, small pieces can remain hidden.*

These can:

- Damage expensive CNC cutters.
- Destroy planer knives.
- Chip saw blades.
- Increase manufacturing costs.
- Cause production delays.

### Hidden Stones and Grit

Reclaimed beams often contain:

- Sand
- Gravel
- Mortar
- Concrete
- Brick dust
- Embedded stones

*These abrasive materials quickly dull cutting tools and may damage precision machinery.*

### Unknown Moisture Content

*Many reclaimed timbers have unknown storage histories.*

If the timber has excessive moisture, it may:

- Warp after machining.
- Twist during drying.
- Shrink after installation.
- Develop movement or cracking over time.

*Professional staircase manufacturers typically work with timber that has been kiln dried to a controlled moisture content suitable for interior joinery.*

### Internal Defects

The outside of an old beam may look perfect while hiding:

- Deep shakes.
- Internal splits.
- Rot pockets.
- Insect damage.
- Previous repairs.
- Structural weakness.

*These defects often only become visible after machining begins.*

## What About New Timber?

*New timber is not perfect either.*

Even high-quality hardwoods can present challenges such as:

- Natural knots.
- Colour variation.
- Grain changes.
- Mineral streaks.
- Sapwood.
- Limited board lengths.
- Matching multiple boards to create long strings or wide landings.
- Natural movement as timber acclimatises.

*Manufacturers minimise these issues by carefully grading, matching and selecting boards before production.*

## Why Manufacturers Prefer to Supply the Timber

When a staircase company supplies the timber, they can:

- Source the correct species.
- Verify moisture content.
- Ensure structural quality.
- Match colour and grain.
- Replace defective boards.
- Guarantee consistency throughout the staircase.
- Stand behind the finished product with greater confidence.

*Using customer-supplied timber can limit these quality controls and may affect the manufacturer's warranty or guarantee.*

## NEX Summary

*Customers can often supply their own timber for a bespoke staircase, particularly reclaimed wood with sentimental or architectural value. However, reclaimed timber carries additional manufacturing risks, including hidden metal, embedded stones, unknown moisture levels, internal defects and inconsistent quality. These issues can damage specialist machinery, increase production time and make it difficult for a manufacturer to guarantee the finished staircase. Professionally sourced timber is generally kiln dried, graded and selected specifically for staircase construction, providing greater consistency, reliability and long-term performance. While reclaimed timber can create a unique staircase with exceptional character, every piece should be thoroughly inspected and accepted by the manufacturer before production begins.*
