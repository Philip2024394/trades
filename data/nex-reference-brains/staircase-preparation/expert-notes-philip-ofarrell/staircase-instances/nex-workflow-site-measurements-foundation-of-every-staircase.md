# Site Measurements – The Foundation of Every Staircase

*This image represents the planning and measurement stage before a staircase enters production. It shows a timber workshop environment with:*

- Architectural drawings/rolled plans ready for review.
- Tape measures and measuring tools, symbolising accurate site surveying.
- Plywood sheets and timber samples used for design and material selection.
- Hand tools associated with manufacturing preparation.
- A professional joinery workshop in the background, indicating where bespoke staircases are manufactured.

## Why This Matters

*Every bespoke staircase begins with accurate site measurements. Before a single piece of timber is cut, the following must be confirmed:*

- Finished floor-to-floor height.
- Stairwell opening dimensions.
- Overall staircase width.
- Landing positions.
- Wall locations and structural obstructions.
- Headroom clearances.
- Handrail and newel post positions.
- String layouts and fixing points.

*Even a small measurement error can result in:*

- Components that don't fit.
- Incorrect rise and going calculations.
- Delays in production.
- Costly remanufacturing.
- Installation issues on site.

## NEX Explanation

*Accurate site measurements are the first and most important stage of the staircase manufacturing process. Every staircase is manufactured specifically for the property it is being installed into. Once measurements have been verified, the design can be engineered, production drawings created, and manufacturing can begin with confidence that every component will fit correctly during installation.*

## Suggested Caption

> ***"Measure Once. Build Once. Install Perfectly."***

*Every successful staircase starts with precise site measurements before entering production. Accurate dimensions ensure every string, tread, riser, handrail and newel is manufactured to fit the property exactly, reducing errors, delays and costly remakes.*
