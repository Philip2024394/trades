# Handrails · Phase 2 · Sizes, Heights, Profiles & Grip Dimensions

## 1. Why Handrail Size Matters

A handrail is designed around the human hand. The correct size must balance comfortable grip · visual proportion · staircase width · newel size · baluster design · building requirements.

**A handrail that is too small may feel weak. A handrail that is too large may feel uncomfortable.**

## 2. Common Timber Handrail Sizes

**41mm–45mm** — lighter traditional staircases · smaller residential designs. Slim and refined.

**50mm–60mm** — very common premium residential range. Oak staircases · modern homes · square newels. Balanced and comfortable.

**65mm–75mm** — larger staircases · luxury designs · commercial applications. More substantial.

## 3. Square Handrail Sizes

Common examples: 40×40mm · 50×50mm · 60×40mm · custom profiles. Used with square newels · glass balustrades · contemporary interiors.

## 4. Round Handrail Sizes

Common diameters: 40mm · 45mm · 50mm · 55mm. Used for wall-mounted handrails · commercial staircases · modern minimalist designs.

## 5. Handrail Height Principles

The handrail height is measured from the staircase walking line. Depends on country regulations · staircase type · building use · design requirements.

**Nex should always ask: "Where is the staircase being installed?" because requirements vary by location.**

## 6. Bottom and Top Handrail Position

A handrail should create a continuous comfortable path.

**Bottom Starting Point** — first step position · newel location · handrail return.

**Top Landing** — landing edge · continuation · safe transition.

## 7. Handrail Returns

**Returned End** — the handrail curves back. Neat finish · reduces snagging risk.

**Newel Termination** — handrail connects directly into a newel. Common in traditional staircases.

**Wall Return** — handrail turns into a wall. Common for enclosed stairs.

**Continuous Flowing Handrail** — used in luxury staircases. Smooth bends · long uninterrupted lines.

## 8. Handrail Profiles

**Oval** — traditional · comfortable · elegant. Used with turned newels · classic balusters.

**Round** — simple · universal · easy grip. Used with modern · minimalist designs.

**Square** — architectural · contemporary. Used with square oak newels · glass systems.

**Stop Chamfer** — combines square and softened edges. Used with shaker staircases · painted timber designs.

## 9. Matching Handrail to Newel Size

**90mm Square Oak Newel** — 50–60mm oak handrail.

**120mm Feature Newel** — larger handrail profile · custom design.

**Stainless Steel Newel System** — 40–50mm stainless handrail.

## 10. Material and Size Relationship

The same size feels different depending on material.

**Oak** — 55mm oak handrail feels warm · solid · traditional.

**Stainless Steel** — 50mm stainless handrail feels precise · modern · lightweight.

**Brass** — smaller profile can still feel luxurious.

## 11. Handrail for Glass Staircases

**Timber Top Rail** — oak · walnut. Warmth · contrast.

**Stainless Steel Top Rail** — modern appearance · seamless design.

**Frameless Glass** — minimal top rail · specially engineered systems.

## 12. Handrail Manufacturing

**Timber** — machining · sanding · profiling · finishing.

**Curved Timber** — laminating layers · steam bending · CNC shaping.

**Metal** — cutting · bending · welding · polishing.

## 13. Common Handrail Mistakes

**Too Small** — poor visual balance · uncomfortable grip.

**Wrong Profile** — traditional staircase with ultra-modern handrail.

**Poor Jointing** — visible joints reduce quality.

**Incorrect Alignment** — handrail should flow smoothly.

## 14. Nex Questions Before Recommendation

1. What country is the staircase installed in?
2. Residential or commercial?
3. Staircase material?
4. New or replacement handrail?
5. Open or closed staircase?
6. Do you already have newels?
7. Do you want modern or traditional?

## 15. Premium Design Rule

**A great handrail should feel like it belongs to the staircase.** It should match newels · balusters · materials · architecture · user's hand.

The handrail is the point where the person and the staircase connect.
