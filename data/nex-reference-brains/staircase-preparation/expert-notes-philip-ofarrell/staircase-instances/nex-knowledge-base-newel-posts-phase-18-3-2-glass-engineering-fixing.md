# Newel Posts · Phase 18.3.2 · Glass Newel Systems · Engineering, Fixing Systems, Glass Thickness and Installation Methods

## 1. The Engineering Behind Glass Staircase Systems

A premium glass staircase may look simple, but the engineering behind it is highly controlled.

Visible: Glass panel → Minimal hardware → Hidden support system → Stair structure → Building structure.

**The glass is not simply "standing there".** The system must control vertical loads · horizontal pushing forces · movement · vibration · impact resistance · long-term stability.

A professional installer does not only fit glass — they fit a complete structural system.

## 2. How Glass Transfers Loads

When a person walks beside a staircase, they naturally apply pressure — leaning on a handrail · pushing against a balustrade · carrying furniture · children holding the glass.

Load path: Human force → Handrail/glass → Fixings → Channel/posts → Floor or staircase structure → Building structure.

A weak fixing point can cause movement · rattling · glass stress · failure over time.

## 3. Main Glass Fixing Systems

### System 1 · Base Channel Glass System

One of the most popular modern systems. Glass sits inside a metal channel.

Materials: aluminium channel · stainless steel channel · concealed structural channels.

Advantages: clean appearance · no visible posts · premium architectural look. Used for floating stairs · modern homes · commercial interiors.

### System 2 · Stainless Steel Glass Posts

Traditional modern balustrade approach. Components: vertical stainless posts · glass clamps · handrail.

Advantages: strong · adjustable · easier installation · suitable for renovations.

Common finishes: brushed stainless · polished stainless · black powder coated steel.

### System 3 · Point Fixed Glass

A high-end architectural system. Glass is attached using individual fixing points.

Advantages: almost invisible support · luxury appearance · maximum transparency.

Used in designer homes · hotels · architectural staircases.

### System 4 · Side Mounted Glass

Glass fixed to the side of the staircase instead of sitting on top.

Benefits: increases walking width · creates floating appearance · cleaner edges.

Popular with mono-stringer stairs · open riser stairs · luxury floating stairs.

## 4. Glass Thickness Selection

Glass thickness depends on system design · support method · panel size · building requirements · manufacturer specification.

**10mm Glass** — lighter balustrade systems · supported frames.

**12mm Glass** — very common for residential glass balustrades · premium staircase systems. Balance of strength · appearance · weight.

**15mm Glass** — larger panels · premium frameless systems · higher visual impact.

**Laminated Glass Systems** — glass layer + safety interlayer + glass layer. If damaged: fragments remain attached · reduces falling risk.

## 5. Glass Edges and Finishing

**A professional glass staircase does not use unfinished glass edges.**

**Polished Edge** — smooth luxury appearance · visible glass edges look clean.
**Arrissed Edge** — removes sharp corners · safer handling.
**Rounded Corners** — used for premium designs · family homes.

## 6. Fixing Into Different Stair Structures

### Timber Staircase

Possible fixing points: solid timber treads · structural newels · reinforced areas. **Glass cannot simply be screwed into weak timber.**

### Steel Staircase

Common with mono stringers · fabricated frames. Fixings: welded plates · threaded inserts · engineered brackets.

### Concrete Staircase

Common in commercial buildings · modern architecture. Requires correct anchors · suitable fixing depth · verified substrate strength.

## 7. Installation Sequence

**Step 1 · Site Measurement** — finished floor levels · staircase angle · openings · wall positions.

**Step 2 · Structural Confirmation** — fixing points · support strength · alignment.

**Step 3 · Manufacture** — glass is produced after final measurements. **Glass is not normally cut on site.**

**Step 4 · Delivery Protection** — edge protection · secure transport · controlled handling.

**Step 5 · Installation** — vertical alignment · glass spacing · fixings · handrail position · movement checks.

## 8. Common Installation Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Using incorrect glass thickness.** Result: unsafe system · excessive movement.

**Mistake 2: Fixing into weak materials.** Result: loose panels · vibration.

**Mistake 3: Incorrect measurements before ordering.** Result: expensive glass replacement.

**Mistake 4: Poor handling during installation.** Result: chipped edges · scratches · damaged panels.
