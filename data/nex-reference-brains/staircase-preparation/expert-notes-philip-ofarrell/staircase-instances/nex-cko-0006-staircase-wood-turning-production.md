# NEX CKO 0006 · Staircase Wood Turning Production

**First Manufacturing Process CKO.** Category anchor for future Process CKOs (installation · fitting · machining · finishing · surveying · etc.).

## What the image shows

This image shows a specialist staircase wood turning production workshop where timber components such as newel posts, balusters, handrail fittings, and decorative turned parts are manufactured using professional wood-turning lathes.

Multiple industrial lathes are operating simultaneously, with partially machined timber pieces mounted between centres. Around the workshop are completed staircase components waiting for finishing, inspection, or assembly.

Large dust extraction systems, timber storage, and production benches indicate a dedicated manufacturing environment rather than a general joinery workshop.

## What this means

A staircase turning workshop allows manufacturers to produce both standard catalogue stairparts and completely bespoke designs.

Because every component is machined from solid timber, virtually any traditional or custom turning profile can be recreated provided suitable timber is available.

## Customer understanding

Many homeowners believe that if a staircase design is old or discontinued it cannot be reproduced.

In reality, experienced staircase turning companies can often manufacture replacement parts by copying an existing component, an old sample, a drawing, or even photographs combined with measurements.

This means historic, heritage, or sentimental staircase designs can frequently be preserved rather than replaced.

## Engineering understanding

The production process normally involves:

1. Selecting suitable timber.
2. Preparing square blanks.
3. Mounting the blank on a wood-turning lathe.
4. Cutting the required profile.
5. Sanding while rotating.
6. Final inspection.
7. Finishing or painting.
8. Delivery to the staircase manufacturer for assembly.

## Key customer message

If your staircase contains an older style of turned newel post or baluster that is no longer available from standard suppliers, it is often possible to have identical replacements manufactured in almost any suitable timber species.

## Suitable timber species

Examples include:

- Oak
- Ash
- Sapele
- Walnut
- Maple
- Pine
- Tulipwood
- Beech
- Iroko
- Accoya
- Douglas Fir

The final choice depends on strength requirements, appearance, finishing method, and whether the staircase is for internal or external use.

## Working with staircase makers

Professional turning companies regularly work alongside staircase manufacturers, joiners, architects, and restoration specialists.

The process usually involves:

- reviewing the customer's design,
- confirming dimensions,
- producing prototypes if required,
- manufacturing matching components,
- supplying finished parts ready for installation.

This collaborative approach allows completely bespoke staircases to be produced while maintaining structural accuracy and visual consistency.

## Common customer questions

**Can you copy my existing staircase?**

Yes. In many cases an existing newel post, baluster, or turning can be copied from a physical sample, detailed measurements, or accurate drawings.

**Can I choose another timber?**

Usually yes. Many turning profiles can be produced in different timber species provided the timber is suitable for the application.

**Can you reproduce discontinued stairparts?**

Often yes. Even if a design has been discontinued commercially, a specialist turning company may still be able to manufacture matching replacements.

### Layer 1 · Understanding

**What is it?** A specialist manufacturing facility that produces turned timber staircase components using precision wood lathes.

Typical products:
- Newel posts
- Balusters
- Handrail fittings
- Decorative columns
- Spindles
- Feature posts
- Restoration replicas

**Homeowner Explanation:** This is where decorative wooden staircase parts are manufactured. Skilled machinists use wood lathes to shape timber into beautiful staircase components.

**Trade Explanation:** Production facility using copy lathes, CNC turning centres and manual lathes to manufacture staircase components from hardwood and softwood blanks.

### Layer 2 · Engineering

**Engineering Purpose:** Convert rough timber into dimensionally accurate architectural components.

Goals: repeatability · symmetry · strength · finish quality · matching existing profiles.

**Engineering Rationale:** Wood turning allows rotationally symmetrical profiles to be reproduced with very high precision. This makes it possible to:
- replace damaged stairparts
- manufacture obsolete designs
- create bespoke components
- maintain historical staircases

### Layer 3 · Decision Knowledge

**WHEN to use:**
- heritage restoration
- replacing discontinued parts
- matching existing staircase
- bespoke luxury staircase
- listed buildings
- architectural reproduction

**WHEN NOT:** Avoid turning when:
- component is better machined flat
- structural replacement required
- severe timber instability exists

**Trade-offs:**
- Advantages: exact profile matching · unlimited customisation · premium appearance
- Disadvantages: slower · higher cost · skilled labour required

### Layer 4 · Relationships

- **Produces:** Newels · Balusters · Columns · Decorative posts · Handrail fittings
- **Depends On:** Timber quality · Moisture content · Machine accuracy · Tool sharpness
- **Supports:** Stair manufacturers · Restoration companies · Joinery workshops

### Layer 5 · Experience

**Customer often asks:** *"Can you copy my existing staircase?"*

**Correct answer:** Yes. If an original sample exists, specialist woodturning companies can reproduce almost any profile in almost any timber species.

**Common misconception:** *"If it's discontinued I have to replace the whole staircase."*

**Reality:** Often only one part needs reproducing.
