## Architectural Style

*This staircase blends premium hardwood craftsmanship with industrial steel detailing. The dark timber strings and walnut treads create warmth, while the exposed steel posts, horizontal rods, and architectural bolt details give the staircase a bold engineered character. It is well suited to luxury homes, loft apartments, boutique offices, and modern industrial interiors.*

## NEX Material Specification

- **Treads:** Solid walnut hardwood
- **Risers:** Solid walnut hardwood
- **Strings:** Dark stained hardwood
- **Handrail:** Solid walnut hardwood
- **Newel Posts:** Structural blackened steel with exposed bolts
- **Balustrade:** Horizontal powder-coated steel rods
- **Fixings:** Exposed stainless steel architectural hardware
- **Finish:** Premium matte oil timber finish with powder-coated steel components

## Overall Description

*The NEX Premium Industrial Straight Flight Timber Staircase combines luxury timber construction with exposed steel engineering. Rich walnut treads and risers contrast against dark stained hardwood strings, while the steel newel posts, horizontal rod balustrades, and visible architectural fixings create a refined industrial aesthetic. The result is a clean, modern staircase with exceptional structural presence, premium materials, and timeless architectural appeal.*

## Governance observations (Gatekeeper note)

**Pair partner to the diamond-plate variant** — same industrial contemporary design language · different riser and newel material choices:

| Element | Diamond-plate variant | Timber-riser variant (this file) |
|---|---|---|
| Risers | Diamond plate steel (powder-coated black) | Matching walnut timber |
| Newels | Heavy square timber posts | Structural blackened steel with exposed bolts |
| Balusters | Horizontal steel rails | Horizontal powder-coated steel rods |
| Fixings | Exposed stainless steel architectural bolts | Exposed stainless steel architectural bolts (same) |
| Design language | Industrial contemporary | Industrial contemporary (same) |

**First authored non-timber (steel) newel posts** in the dataset — previously all newels were timber (square oak · turned oak · square walnut · etc.).

**Together with the diamond-plate variant, this pair demonstrates** that the SAME design language can be expressed through different material choices while retaining the same architectural identity. Confirms Vision Scan #08 governance: *"Classification is additive rather than singular"* — industrial contemporary is one classification · riser material and newel material are additional independent classifications.
