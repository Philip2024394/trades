# NEX Knowledge Base

## Measuring Tapes for Staircase Manufacturing

### Purpose

*A tape measure is one of the most important tools in staircase manufacture and installation. Every staircase component—strings, treads, risers, newel posts, handrails, balusters and landing rails—is produced from measurements. An inaccurate tape can result in cumulative errors that prevent parts fitting correctly.*

### Why Quality Matters

A professional-quality tape measure should:

- Produce consistent measurements every time.
- Match other quality tape measures used within the workshop.
- Maintain accuracy over years of use.
- Have clear metric and imperial graduations.
- Resist wear from daily site and workshop use.

*Consistency between tape measures is essential. If one employee measures with one tape and another manufactures using a different tape that reads differently, components may not fit together correctly.*

### Common Tape Lengths Used in Staircase Work

| Length | Typical Use |
|---|---|
| 3 m (10 ft) | Small internal measurements, stair parts, workshop use |
| 5 m (16 ft) | General staircase manufacture |
| 8 m (26 ft) | Most common professional size for staircases |
| 10 m (33 ft) | Large stairwells, commercial projects |
| 30 m / 50 m | Site setting-out and long structural measurements |

*For most staircase manufacturers, an 8 m (26 ft) tape offers the best balance between reach, portability, and durability.*

### Measurement Units

Professional staircase work commonly requires:

- **Millimetres (mm)** — primary manufacturing unit in the UK.
- **Centimetres (cm)** — occasionally referenced but rarely used for machining.
- **Metres (m)** — overall stairwell dimensions.
- **Inches (in)** — useful when working from older drawings or imported components.
- **Feet (ft)** — building dimensions and legacy plans.

*Most professional tape measures display both metric and imperial scales.*

### Accuracy

A quality tape should:

- Have clearly printed graduations.
- Maintain accurate spacing between markings.
- Use durable ink or etched markings that resist wear.
- Produce repeatable measurements throughout its length.

*Professional workshops should periodically compare all tape measures against a calibrated steel rule or certified reference to ensure consistency.*

### End Hook (Tip) Movement

Many users mistakenly believe a loose hook indicates poor quality.

**In fact, a small amount of movement is intentional.**

The hook is designed to slide by approximately the thickness of the hook material. This compensates for the difference between:

- Inside measurements (hook pushed against an internal face).
- Outside measurements (hook pulled against an edge).

*This movement allows both measuring methods to remain accurate.*

However, problems occur when:

- the rivets become worn,
- the hook bends,
- the movement becomes excessive,
- the hook no longer returns to its correct position.

*A damaged or excessively loose hook can introduce measurement errors and should be repaired or the tape replaced.*

### Blade Quality

A good tape measure should have:

- High-contrast markings.
- Easy-to-read numbers.
- Strong blade coating to reduce wear.
- Good blade standout (distance the blade extends unsupported).
- Resistance to permanent kinks and bends.

### Locking Mechanism

Professional tapes should feature:

- Positive blade lock.
- Smooth blade retraction.
- Controlled recoil to reduce injury and blade damage.

### Magnetic Hook

Many professional tape measures include a magnetic end hook.

Benefits include:

- Measuring steel stair strings.
- Holding onto steel beams.
- Easier one-person measurements.
- Improved safety when measuring metal structures.

### Durability

A staircase workshop tape should withstand:

- Timber dust.
- Site debris.
- Drops onto concrete floors.
- Moisture.
- Daily transport in tool belts or vans.

*Rubber over-moulded cases improve impact resistance.*

### Workshop Best Practice

Professional staircase manufacturers often follow these practices:

- Assign one calibrated tape measure to each installer or machinist.
- Compare all workshop tapes against a master reference at regular intervals.
- Replace damaged tapes immediately.
- Never rely on a tape with a bent hook or unreadable graduations.
- Record critical dimensions in millimetres to avoid conversion errors.

### NEX Workshop Principle

> ***Measure once with confidence, manufacture once with precision.***

*A staircase can only fit as accurately as it is measured. High-quality, well-maintained tape measures—and consistent calibration across the workshop—are fundamental to producing staircases that assemble correctly on site, minimise rework, and meet the precision expected in professional staircase manufacturing.*
