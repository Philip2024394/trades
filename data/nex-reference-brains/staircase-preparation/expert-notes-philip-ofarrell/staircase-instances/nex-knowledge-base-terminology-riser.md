# Riser

The riser is the vertical board of a staircase step — the part between one tread and the next.

## Top-edge tongue joint into tread above

The riser has a **tongue on its top edge** that fits up into a groove cut into the underside of the tread above.

- Groove (in tread underside) depth: ~12mm
- Riser thickness: typically 18mm (groove width matches)

**Why:** Without this joint, the riser top simply butts against the tread body, and any tiny gap at manufacture (even 0.5mm) lets light show through from behind. The tongue-and-groove overlap seals the joint so no light path exists.

## String housing (both sides)

Both treads and risers slot into the string on both sides:

- Housing depth: **12mm to 15mm** into the string interior face
- The housing is dado-cut (rectangular groove) to receive the tread/riser end
- Combined with wedges, this creates a rigid mechanical joint

## Wedging (housed-string construction)

- **1 wedge upward at the riser** — driven upward behind the riser to lock the riser firmly in its housing.
- Wedge length: 9 to 12 inches (~230mm to ~305mm), cut to suit the specific rise and going.
- Wedge material: pine.
