# Tread

The tread is the horizontal board of a staircase step — the surface a person stands on when climbing or descending.

## Underside groove for riser tongue

The tread has a **groove cut into its underside at the back edge** to receive the top-edge tongue of the riser below.

- Groove depth: **~12mm** (into the tread body from underside)
- Groove width: matches the riser thickness (typically 18mm)
- The riser has a tongue on its top edge that fits up into this groove.

**Why:** Without this joint, the riser top simply butts against the tread body, and any tiny gap at manufacture (even 0.5mm) lets light show through from behind. The tongue-and-groove overlap seals the joint so no light path exists.

## Step-to-string housing (both sides)

Both **treads and risers slot into the string** on both sides of a housed-string staircase:

- Housing depth: **12mm to 15mm** into the string interior face
- The housing is dado-cut (rectangular groove) to receive the tread/riser end
- Combined with wedges, this creates a rigid mechanical joint

## Wedging (housed-string construction)

- **1 wedge under the step** — driven upward from below to lock the tread firmly in its housing.
- Wedge length: 9 to 12 inches (~230mm to ~305mm), cut to suit the specific rise and going of the staircase.
- Thick end: ~12mm tapering to thin end: ~3mm.
- Wedge material: pine (softwood, easier to drive, absorbs shock).
