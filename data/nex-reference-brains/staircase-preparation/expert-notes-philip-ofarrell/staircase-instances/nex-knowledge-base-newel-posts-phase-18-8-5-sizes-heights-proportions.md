# Newel Posts · Phase 18.8.5 · Sizes, Heights, Proportions & Choosing the Correct Scale

## 1. Why Newel Size Matters

A newel post is one of the strongest visual elements of a staircase. The correct size must balance staircase width · ceiling height · handrail size · baluster proportions · architectural style · material choice.

**A newel that is too small can make a staircase look weak. A newel that is too large can make a staircase feel heavy.**

## 2. Common Timber Newel Post Sizes

There is no single universal size because staircases vary by country, manufacturer and design.

**70 × 70mm** — smaller staircases · lighter designs · traditional replacement work. Appearance: slim and delicate.

**90 × 90mm** — one of the most common premium residential sizes. Oak staircases · shaker designs · contemporary homes. Appearance: balanced proportion.

**100 × 100mm** — larger homes · stronger visual presence · wider staircases. Appearance: more substantial.

**120 × 120mm** — luxury staircases · entrance halls · feature designs. Appearance: architectural and grand.

## 3. Oversized Feature Newels

Premium designers sometimes increase newel size intentionally.

Examples: 140 × 140mm · 150 × 150mm · custom sizes.

Used for statement entrances · grand staircases · open-plan architectural homes.

**Purpose:** the newel becomes a design feature rather than only a support element.

## 4. Turned Newel Sizes

Traditional turned newels are usually described by overall height · base diameter · decorative profile. The widest point often controls the visual weight.

A large turned newel may include base block · shaft · collar · cap.

## 5. Bottom Newel Post Height

The bottom newel is usually taller than the balusters because it creates the staircase starting point.

Height depends on floor-to-floor height · handrail height · first tread design · newel cap style.

**The important measurement is the final handrail position.**

## 6. Landing Newel Height

Landing newels often continue the same handrail line. They may appear equal height to stair newels · slightly different due to floor level · extended for architectural effect.

## 7. Why Some Newels Are Taller

**Grand Entrance Design** — stronger arrival point · luxury appearance.

**High Ceilings** — a small newel can look lost in a large room.

**Feature Staircases** — the post becomes part of the architecture.

## 8. Matching Newel Size to Staircase Width

**Narrow Staircase** — often suits 70–90mm newels because large posts can reduce visual space.

**Standard Residential Staircase** — often suits 90–100mm newels. Balanced appearance.

**Wide Staircase** — may suit 100–120mm+ to maintain proportion.

## 9. Matching Newel Size to Balusters

**Slim balusters + oversized newel** — creates luxury contrast.

**Large square balusters + small newel** — may look unbalanced.

## 10. Matching Newel Size to Handrail

The handrail and newel should feel connected.

**Modern Design** — 90mm square oak newel + square oak handrail + glass balustrade.

**Traditional Design** — turned newel + rounded handrail + decorative balusters.

**Luxury Design** — oversized timber newel + premium handrail profile + metal/glass details.

## 11. Material Changes Size Perception

**Oak** — 90mm oak feels warm · solid · natural.

**Stainless Steel** — 50mm stainless feels modern · precise · lightweight.

**Brass** — smaller profiles can still appear luxurious because of the finish.

## 12. Newel Size Mistakes

**Choosing Only by Cost** — a smaller newel may save money but weaken the design.

**Ignoring Room Scale** — a staircase in a large entrance hall needs different proportions.

**Mixing Sizes Incorrectly** — example: large bottom newel + very thin landing posts without design intention.

## 13. Nex Design Questions

Before recommending a newel size, Nex should ask:

1. What country is the staircase located in?
2. Is it a new staircase or replacement?
3. What material?
4. Stair width?
5. Open or closed staircase?
6. Modern or traditional style?
7. Is it a feature staircase?

## 14. Premium Design Rule

**The correct newel size is not the biggest one.** It is the size that creates the correct relationship between staircase · room · materials · human scale · architectural style.
