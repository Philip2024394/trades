# Newel Posts · Phase 19 · Sizes, Timber Dimensions, Heights and Selection Guide

## Typical timber newel post sizes

For residential staircases, timber newels are commonly manufactured in these sizes.

### Square timber newels

| Size | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 70 × 70 mm | Light domestic / smaller staircases |
| 90 × 90 mm | Most common premium residential size |
| 100 × 100 mm | Larger traditional or luxury stairs |
| 115 × 115 mm | Grand staircases / statement designs |
| 140 × 140 mm+ | Bespoke architectural staircases |

### Oak newel posts

Oak newels are commonly:

- **90 × 90 mm** — modern oak staircase standard
- **100 × 100 mm** — traditional oak staircase
- **115 × 115 mm** — premium bespoke work

The larger the newel:

- the heavier the visual appearance
- the stronger the architectural presence
- the more suitable for wide staircases

## Newel height

Typical finished heights:

**Bottom starting newel** — around 900–1100 mm above finished floor.

**Landing newel** — around 1000–1100 mm above finished landing.

**Continuous handrail newels** — sized to match balustrade height requirements.

## Where sizes are used

**70 × 70 mm** — compact homes · modern minimalist designs · narrow staircases.

**90 × 90 mm** — most oak straight flights · cut string staircases · closed string staircases · contemporary homes.

**100–115 mm** — large hallways · luxury homes · curved staircases · feature staircases.

**140 mm+** — hotel entrances · heritage restoration · architectural statement staircases.

## Material affects size

**Oak** — usually larger because the grain supports heavy profiles. Premium appearance.

**Pine** — often 70–90 mm. Painted staircases.

**Walnut** — commonly 90–115 mm. Luxury interiors.

**Metal** — usually not called a newel post in the same way. Fabricated from steel box sections, stainless steel tubes, or plates.

**Glass** — often uses hidden steel supports with minimal visible posts.

## Selection Rule

The correct newel size depends on:

- Staircase width
- Ceiling height
- Room size
- Tread thickness
- Handrail proportion
- Architectural style
- Material choice

A small newel on a large staircase looks weak. An oversized newel on a small staircase looks heavy.
