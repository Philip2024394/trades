# Baluster

Balusters are the vertical members of a staircase balustrade that fit between the handrail and the baserail (or between handrail and string where no baserail is present).

## Standard sizes (UK / Ireland)

| Baluster width | Corresponding handrail / baserail | Newel post size |
|---|---|---|
| 32mm | ~32mm (slim balustrade — modern minimalist) | Standard (90mm) |
| **41mm** | **41mm — current UK/Ireland standard** | Standard (90mm) |
| 44mm | 44mm — traditional/older, standard before mass production | Standard (90mm-100mm) |
| 50mm | Larger (~50mm) | Sized up |
| 60mm | Largest (~60mm) | Sized up (110-140mm) |

## Full-chamfer 41mm profile spec

- **Size:** 41mm × 41mm × 900mm long (standard UK)
- **Profile:** square cross-section with chamfered edges on all 4 long corners
- **Cut to fit:** top and bottom cut to the angle of the staircase pitch (so ends fit flush against the underside of the handrail groove and the top of the baserail groove)
- **Rail tenon depth:** 10mm inside the baserail at bottom + 10mm inside the handrail at top. Actual installed length = visible length + 20mm.
- **Corners hidden:** because top and bottom are cut to pitch angle AND embedded 10mm into each rail groove, the baluster corners are never visible — you only see the shaft between the rails, cleanly meeting each rail with no visible end-cut.
- **Installation:** slots into groove on top of baserail + groove on underside of handrail; slips (spacer strips) cut between balusters lock them in place.

## Grain direction (hard rule)

**Baluster grain always runs up-down along the length of the baluster. Never across.**

- Structural reason: grain running along the length gives the baluster its strength (wood is much stronger along the grain than across).
- Visual reason: grain running up-down reads correctly at a glance; grain across the baluster reads as amateur/wrong.

## Count rule

- **2 balusters per step** as the standard rule.
- Where a newel post is at a step: allow **1 baluster only** (the newel replaces the second baluster on that step).

## Attic-spare rule

When calculating baluster count, if the calc gives 1 or 2 leftover balusters, buy them anyway and store them. Baluster designs change with interior design trends — what's available today might be discontinued tomorrow. Having spares means if a baluster gets damaged years later, the replacement matches perfectly.

## Building regulation gap (Doc K) — measured horizontally, not along pitch

**Critical rule:** the maximum baluster gap for Building Regulations and child safety is measured **horizontally (at eye level)** — NOT along the diagonal pitch of the staircase.

- Maximum gap between balusters: **100mm** (a 100mm sphere must not pass through horizontally at any height)
- For 41mm balusters at 110mm horizontal centre-to-centre spacing: gap = 110 − 41 = 69mm — passes Doc K ✓

For wider balusters or wider spacing, verify the horizontal gap doesn't exceed 100mm.
