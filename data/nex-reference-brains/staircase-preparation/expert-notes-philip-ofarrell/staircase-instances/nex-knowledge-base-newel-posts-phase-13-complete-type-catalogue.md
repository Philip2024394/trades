# Newel Posts · Phase 13 · Every Type of Newel Post Used in Staircase Construction (Complete Reference)

This phase is a catalogue of the different types of newel posts used throughout the staircase industry. A professional staircase designer or manufacturer should be able to identify each type by name, know where it is used, and understand why it has been chosen.

## What Defines a Newel Type?

A newel can be classified by position · function · shape · construction · material · installation method · decorative style. The same staircase may contain several different types of newels.

## Classification by Position

**Starting Newel** — the first newel at the bottom of the staircase. Usually the largest and most decorative because it is the first thing people see. Supports the first handrail · baserail · first balusters · creates the visual entrance to the staircase. Typical sizes: 90×90mm · 100×100mm · 120×120mm.

**Bottom Newel** — sometimes called the starting newel. Located at the first step. Usually carries the greatest structural loading.

**Top Newel** — located at the landing. Supports landing handrail · stair handrail · landing balustrade. Almost every staircase has one.

**Landing Newel** — located wherever a landing exists. One staircase may have several.

**Intermediate Newel** — used between the bottom and top. Common on quarter-turn stairs · half-turn stairs · split-level staircases.

**Corner Newel** — positioned where the handrail changes direction. Normally found at 90° · 135° · 180° turns.

**Gallery Newel** — used around gallery landings. Supports long balustrades overlooking entrance halls. Large homes often have multiple gallery newels.

**Balcony Newel** — supports balustrades overlooking open spaces. Common in luxury houses.

**Feature Newel** — chosen primarily for appearance. Often oversized. May include carvings · lighting · decorative caps · steel inserts · glass.

## Classification by Shape

**Square Newel** — the modern standard. Most common today. Easy machining · contemporary appearance · suits glass · suits oak · suits painted finishes.

**Turned Newel** — manufactured on a lathe. Circular decorative profile. Traditional appearance. Often used with spindles · traditional handrails · cut-string staircases.

**Stop-Chamfered Newel** — square post with corners removed with decorative stopped chamfers. Popular in Arts & Crafts · Modern Traditional · Heritage homes.

**Octagonal Newel** — eight-sided. Less common today. Often found in period properties.

**Hexagonal Newel** — six-sided. Mostly decorative. Rare.

**Box Newel** — large hollow square construction. Very common in North America. Can exceed 200mm · 300mm · 400mm square. Often contains hidden structural framing.

**Monument Newel** — very large decorative newel. Used in grand staircases. Sometimes over 1500mm tall.

**Pedestal Newel** — looks more like furniture. Heavy proportions. Often supports elaborate staircases.

## Classification by Construction

**Solid Timber** — one piece. Traditional.

**Laminated Timber** — multiple laminations glued together. Today's premium manufacturing standard.

**Veneered Engineered Core** — stable engineered timber with decorative hardwood veneer outside. Common in commercial manufacturing.

**Steel-Core Timber** — timber outside with steel reinforcement inside. Provides exceptional rigidity.

**Hollow Newel** — decorative shell around hidden framework. Reduces weight. Often easier to install.

**Built-Up Newel** — constructed from multiple machined pieces. Allows complex detailing.

## Classification by Material

**Oak** — most common premium timber. Excellent durability. Excellent appearance.

**White Oak** — modern. Light colour. Stable.

**European Oak** — traditional UK favourite. Warm tones.

**Walnut** — luxury. Dark colour. Premium homes.

**Ash** — strong. Straight grain. Excellent machining.

**Beech** — hard. Smooth finish. Common in Europe.

**Maple** — fine grain. Excellent paint finish.

**Pine** — economical. Usually painted.

**Tulipwood** — paint-grade. Excellent stability.

**Steel** — commercial. Industrial. Contemporary.

**Stainless Steel** — marine. Luxury. Outdoor.

**Aluminium** — lightweight. Corrosion resistant.

**Glass** — some modern staircases replace traditional timber posts with structural glass fins.

## Classification by Installation

**Through Newel** — passes through floor. Maximum strength.

**Half Newel** — wall-mounted. Decorative. Space saving.

**Surface Fixed** — bolted directly onto floor. Modern construction.

**Concealed Fixed** — fixings hidden internally. Premium appearance.

**Resin Fixed** — anchored using chemical resin. Usually commercial.

## Classification by Stair Style

**Straight Flight** — normally bottom and top.
**Quarter Turn** — Bottom → Corner → Top.
**Half Turn** — Bottom → Landing → Landing → Top.
**Curved Staircase** — several specially positioned newels. Each supports changing handrail geometry.
**Spiral Staircase** — normally no traditional newels. Central column replaces them.
**Floating Staircase** — minimal newels. Sometimes none.
**Mono String Stair** — steel posts often replace timber.
**Glass Staircase** — timber, steel or no visible newels depending on design.

## Decorative Caps

The cap dramatically changes appearance. Common types include:

- **Flat Cap** — simple. Modern.
- **Pyramid Cap** — traditional. Widely used.
- **Acorn Cap** — decorative. Victorian influence.
- **Ball Cap** — rounded. Classic appearance.
- **Mushroom Cap** — soft rounded profile.
- **Urn Cap** — luxury staircases.
- **Custom CNC Caps** — contemporary bespoke homes.

## Decorative Details

Possible machining includes fluting · reeds · stop chamfers · beads · coves · lamb's tongue · ogee mouldings · carved panels · rope twist · barley twist · geometric carving · CNC engraving · family crests · initials · LED recesses.

## Modern Designer Newels

Current trends include oversized square oak · black stained oak · concealed steel reinforcement · hidden fixings · integrated LED lighting · glass grooves · flush caps · shadow gaps · minimalist detailing.

## Commercial Newels

Often replaced entirely by SHS steel posts · RHS steel posts · stainless tube · aluminium extrusions. Designed primarily for structural performance.

## Heritage Restoration

Historic staircases may contain hand-carved newels · mahogany · walnut · oak · pitch pine · elm. Each restoration requires specialist conservation methods.

## Choosing the Correct Newel

A professional staircase designer considers staircase geometry · structural requirements · building regulations · timber species · interior style · balustrade system · handrail profile · maintenance · budget · manufacturing method · installation method · long-term durability.

The "best" newel is not necessarily the most decorative or the largest. It is the one that matches the structural demands of the staircase, complements the architectural style of the building, and integrates correctly with the handrails, balustrades, and surrounding joinery. Understanding these different classifications allows a designer, manufacturer, or installer to select the right newel for both function and appearance.
