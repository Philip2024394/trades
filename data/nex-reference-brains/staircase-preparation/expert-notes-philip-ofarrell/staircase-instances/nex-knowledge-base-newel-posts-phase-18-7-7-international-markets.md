# Newel Posts · Phase 18.7.7 · International Markets, Terminology, Regional Styles & Global Applications

## 1. Newels Are Global, But Staircases Are Regional

A staircase component may have different names · sizes · materials · design expectations · regulations · installation methods depending on the country.

A premium staircase company serving international customers must understand that **the same customer question can mean different things in different markets.**

Customer asks: *"What size newel do I need?"* — the correct answer depends on country · staircase style · building type · local construction practice · material availability.

**Nex should avoid assuming one country's standard applies everywhere.**

## 2. Terminology Differences Around the World

### Newel Post

**UK / Ireland** — newel post · newel · starting newel · landing newel.

**USA / Canada** — newel post · starting post · landing post · stair post.

**Australia / New Zealand** — newel post · stair post.

**Europe** — terminology varies by language and local construction traditions.

## 3. Baluster Terminology Differences

Customer may ask *"How many spindles do I need?"* or *"How many balusters?"* — they may mean the same component.

**UK / Ireland** — spindle · baluster.
**USA** — baluster · spindle (less common).

### Professional Communication Rule

Nex should recognise: **baluster = spindle = vertical infill member** but answer using the customer's language.

Example: *"Balusters, also commonly called spindles in some regions, are the vertical members between the handrail and base rail."*

## 4. Newel Sizes Around Different Markets

**There is no single global newel size.** Size depends on staircase scale · timber availability · architectural style · engineering requirements.

**Traditional Residential** — smaller square profiles · decorative turned posts · proportioned to the staircase.

**Premium Residential** — larger square posts · stronger visual presence · feature proportions. Examples: 90mm · 100mm · 120mm square. Actual selection depends on design and engineering.

## 5. United States Staircase Market

Often features larger open staircases · hardwood floors · decorative balusters · larger entry halls.

**Popular materials** — red oak · white oak · maple · walnut · iron balusters.

**Craftsman** — chunky timber posts · square profiles · natural wood.
**Modern** — metal posts · glass · floating designs.
**Traditional Colonial** — turned newels · decorative caps · detailed woodwork.

## 6. Canada Staircase Market

Canadian designs often consider colder climate · timber movement · humidity changes.

Important considerations: correctly dried timber · stable finishes · seasonal movement allowance.

Popular materials: oak · maple · walnut.

## 7. Ireland Staircase Market

Irish staircase design often shares similarities with nearby European and UK traditions but has its own preferences.

Common: timber staircases · oak finishes · painted newels · glass upgrades.

**Nex should not automatically label components as "UK parts" when speaking with Irish customers.**

Better: *"This is a common style used across Ireland and the UK market."* or *"This style is available in many European staircase systems."*

## 8. Australia & New Zealand Market

**Important considerations** — open-plan homes · modern architecture · coastal environments.

**Popular** — glass balustrades · stainless steel · hardwood timber.

**Special attention** — corrosion resistance near coastal areas · UV exposure · outdoor applications.

## 9. European Premium Staircases

**European designs often emphasise** minimalist architecture · precision engineering · innovative materials.

**Common** — floating stairs · glass systems · metal structures · concealed fixing.

**Materials** — oak · steel · glass · stone combinations.

## 10. Coastal and Humid Locations

**Coastal Home** — stainless steel grade · protective finishes · moisture-resistant materials.

**Tropical Climate** — humidity · timber movement · ventilation · finishing systems.

**Cold Climate** — expansion and contraction · heating effects · moisture changes.

## 11. How Nex Should Handle Country Detection

**If Nex knows the customer's location:** use regional knowledge.

- Customer location: USA → Nex considers US terminology · common dimensions · local suppliers.
- Customer location: Ireland → Nex considers Irish terminology · local trade practices.

**If Nex does not know:** ask naturally — *"Which country is the staircase being installed in? Sizes and terminology can vary between regions."*

## 12. International Supplier Connection

Example customer: *"I am in America and I want UK staircase parts."*

Nex should not immediately send a supplier. First collect country · city/region · staircase type · parts required · quantities · materials · sizes · delivery requirements.

Then explain: *"Having these details ready helps the supplier understand your project quickly and gives you a more accurate response."*

## 13. Nex Global Rule

**Never assume "staircase = UK standard".**

Instead Nex should understand customer location · project location · terminology · material availability · local expectations.

## 14. International Customer Experience

The goal: a customer in any country should feel *"Nex understands my staircase market"* — not *"Nex is giving me information from another country."*
