# I Need a Staircase

## You're in the right place.

Whether you're building a new home, renovating, replacing an existing staircase, or converting a loft, we'll help you understand your options before you make any decisions.

Every staircase is made to suit the property, the available space, your style, and your budget.

---

## The first things to consider

To recommend the right staircase, we'll usually ask a few simple questions:

* Is this for a new build, renovation, extension or loft conversion?
* Is the staircase straight, quarter turn, half turn or another layout?
* Do you prefer a traditional or modern design?
* Would you like timber, glass, metal, or a combination?
* Will the staircase be painted, oak, walnut or another timber?
* Do you already have measurements or architectural drawings?

If you don't know the answers yet, that's perfectly fine. We can guide you through them one step at a time.

---

## Popular staircase styles

Some of the most common staircase types include:

* Straight flight
* Quarter turn
* Half turn
* Winder staircases
* Open riser staircases
* Closed string staircases
* Cut string staircases
* Space saver staircases

Each has advantages depending on the available space, appearance and intended use.

---

## Material choices

Many staircases can be manufactured in:

* European Oak
* American White Oak
* Walnut
* Pine
* Engineered softwood
* Painted hardwood and softwood combinations

The right material depends on appearance, durability, budget and the rest of your interior.

---

## Balustrade options

You can choose from many balustrade styles, including:

* Square timber balusters
* Turned balusters
* Glass balustrades
* Stainless steel balusters
* Painted balustrades
* Contemporary mixed-material designs

## If you're not sure where to start

Simply tell us what you know.

For example:

* "I need a staircase for my new house."
* "I'm replacing an old staircase."
* "I need a loft staircase."
* "I only have the floor-to-floor height."
* "I have my architect's drawings."
* "I want an oak staircase with glass."

From there, we'll guide you through the next steps.

---

## Typical information we'll eventually need

* Floor-to-floor height
* Staircase location
* Available space
* Desired style
* Timber preference
* Balustrade preference
* Whether the staircase will be painted or fully timber
* Any drawings or measurements available

Don't worry if you don't have everything yet. We can work through it together.
