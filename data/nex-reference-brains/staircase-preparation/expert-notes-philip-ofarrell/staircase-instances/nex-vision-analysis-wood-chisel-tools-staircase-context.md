## What Is a Wood Chisel?

*A wood chisel is one of the most important hand tools used in staircase manufacturing and installation. It is designed to remove wood accurately, clean joints, trim timber, fit components, and produce precise hand-finished work that machines cannot always achieve.*

*Even with modern CNC machines, routers, and spindle moulders, professional staircase makers still rely on chisels every day.*

- Flat ground cutting edge
- Suitable for joinery and staircase work

## Types of Wood Chisels Used in Staircase Manufacturing

### Bevel Edge Chisel

The most common staircase chisel.

**Used for:**

- Mortise clean-up
- Joint fitting
- Housed string cleaning
- Trimming tenons
- Fitting newel posts
- General joinery

**Common sizes:**

- 6 mm (1/4")
- 10 mm (3/8")
- 12 mm (1/2")
- 16 mm (5/8")
- 19 mm (3/4")
- 25 mm (1")
- 32 mm (1-1/4")
- 38 mm (1-1/2")

### Mortise Chisel

Heavy-duty chisel designed for cutting deep mortises.

**Used for:**

- Newel post mortises
- Handrail joints
- Structural timber joints
- Traditional staircase construction

**Common sizes:**

- 6 mm · 8 mm · 10 mm · 12 mm · 16 mm

### Firmer Chisel

Thicker blade than a bevel-edge chisel.

**Used for:**

- Heavy timber removal
- Large oak staircases
- Hardwood fitting
- Traditional carpentry

### Paring Chisel

Long thin blade.

**Used for:**

- Fine trimming
- Finish fitting
- Veneers
- Delicate joinery
- Final adjustments

**Lengths often:**

- 250–400 mm

### Butt Chisel

Short blade.

**Ideal for:**

- Tight areas
- Staircase installation
- Door frames
- Cupboards beneath stairs
- Confined working spaces

### Corner Chisel

Cuts perfect square internal corners.

**Used for:**

- Mortises
- Routed housings
- Hinge recesses
- String housings

### Slick Chisel

Large two-handed timber chisel.

**Used by:**

- Traditional staircase makers
- Timber framers

**Sizes:**

- 50 mm · 60 mm · 75 mm · 100 mm

### Skew Chisel

Angled cutting edge.

**Used for:**

- Difficult grain
- Tight corners
- Decorative work
- Curved staircase components

Typical bevels:

- **25°** — General woodworking
- **30°** — Hardwood staircase work
- **35°** — Heavy chopping
- **38°** — Oak and hardwood mortising

## Sharpening Equipment

Professional staircase makers often use:

- Diamond stones · Water stones · Oil stones

## Common Chisel Brands

Professional workshop favourites include:

- Ashley Iles · Robert Sorby · Narex · Lie-Nielsen · Veritas
- Two Cherries · Pfeil · Stanley Sweetheart · Marples · Crown Hand Tools

## Workshop Safety

When using chisels:

- Keep blades razor sharp.
- Always cut away from your body.
- Secure the workpiece before chiselling.
- Use a wooden or polyurethane mallet—avoid striking timber-handled chisels with a steel hammer unless they are designed for it.
- Protect the cutting edge with blade guards during storage and transport.
- Wear eye protection when chopping joints.
