# Fire Escape Staircases – Metals, Steel Sizes, and Anti-Slip Treads

For an external fire escape staircase, steel is almost always the preferred material because it is strong, durable, and can withstand exposure to the weather.

## What Metal Is Used?

The most common materials are:

| Material | Typical Use |
|---|---|
| Mild steel (hot-dip galvanised) | Most common choice for external fire escapes |
| Stainless Steel 304 | Internal or sheltered environments |
| Stainless Steel 316 | Coastal or highly corrosive environments |
| Structural steel (S275 or S355) | Main structural stringers and landings |

For most projects in the UK, a mild steel staircase that is hot-dip galvanised after fabrication is the standard solution. Galvanising coats the steel with zinc to protect it from rust.

## Typical Steel Sizes

The exact section sizes are designed by a structural engineer, but common examples are:

**Stringers**

- 200 × 100 mm rectangular hollow section (RHS).
- 250 × 150 mm RHS.
- 10–15 mm folded steel plate.
- 200–300 mm steel channel.

**Treads**

- 3–6 mm steel chequer plate.
- 25–40 mm steel grating.
- Serrated bar grating.

**Handrails**

- 42.4 mm or 48.3 mm diameter steel tube.

**Balustrade Posts**

- 40 × 40 mm.
- 50 × 50 mm.
- 60 × 60 mm square hollow section (SHS).

## What Stops People Slipping?

A fire escape staircase must provide good slip resistance, especially outdoors.

Common anti-slip tread options include:

- Serrated steel grating (most common).
- Open steel mesh grating.
- Perforated steel treads.
- Raised anti-slip nosings.
- Carborundum (abrasive) anti-slip strips.
- Aluminium anti-slip nosings.
- Drainage openings to prevent standing water.

Serrated open grating is often considered one of the best choices because:

- Rainwater drains through.
- Snow and mud don't build up as easily.
- The serrated edges improve grip.
- It requires relatively little maintenance.

## Can You Use Timber Treads?

Yes, but it depends on the application.

### Internal Feature Staircase

✅ Timber is very common.

### External Fire Escape

⚠️ Timber is generally not the preferred choice for a dedicated external fire escape because it:

- Can become slippery when wet.
- Requires ongoing maintenance.
- Can deteriorate over time if exposed to weather.
- Must satisfy fire performance requirements where applicable.

If timber is used, designers often specify:

- Durable hardwoods suitable for external use.
- Anti-slip inserts or abrasive strips.
- Grooved tread surfaces.
- Protective finishes and regular maintenance.

For most commercial external fire escapes, steel grating remains the standard solution.
