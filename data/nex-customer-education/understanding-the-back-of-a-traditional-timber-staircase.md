# Understanding the Back of a Traditional Timber Staircase

Most people only ever see the finished staircase, but hidden underneath is a carefully engineered structure that has been refined over centuries. Every component has an important purpose, working together to create a staircase that is strong, stable, and built to last.

Understanding these hidden parts gives you a greater appreciation for the craftsmanship involved in producing a quality timber staircase.

## Stair Strings

The stair strings are the main structural members that run along each side of the staircase.

Their job is to support the treads and risers while carrying the weight of everyone using the staircase. A well-manufactured string keeps the staircase straight, secure, and structurally sound throughout its life.

## Treads

The treads are the horizontal boards that you walk on.

Each tread transfers weight into the stair strings while providing a safe and comfortable walking surface. The accuracy of the joints and the quality of the timber play a major role in how solid a staircase feels underfoot.

## Risers

The risers are the vertical boards positioned between each tread.

As well as creating the appearance of a closed staircase, they help strengthen the overall structure by connecting the treads together and reducing movement.

## Timber Wedges

One of the most important hidden components is the timber wedge.

These wedges are driven tightly into specially machined housings where the treads and risers fit into the stair strings.

Their purpose is to:

- Lock the treads and risers securely into the strings.
- Create a tight, rigid joint.
- Eliminate movement between components.
- Help prevent squeaks from developing over time.
- Increase the overall strength of the staircase.

Although they are rarely seen once the staircase is installed, wedges are essential to the long-term performance of a traditional timber staircase.

## Angle Blocks (Glue Blocks)

The small triangular timber blocks fitted beneath each tread are known as angle blocks or glue blocks.

These simple components perform an important structural function by reinforcing the joint between the tread and riser.

They help to:

- Strengthen the connection between the tread and riser.
- Reduce flexing when the staircase is in use.
- Increase the overall rigidity of the staircase.
- Minimise movement that can lead to creaks or squeaks.
- Distribute weight more evenly throughout the structure.

Working together with the wedges, angle blocks help create the solid feel associated with a well-built timber staircase.

## Why These Hidden Parts Matter

While wedges and angle blocks may appear to be small details, they are fundamental to the strength of the staircase.

Without these components, joints could loosen over time, movement would increase, and unwanted noise would become far more likely.

Much of a staircase's quality comes from the parts that are never seen once installation is complete.

## Traditional Craftsmanship That Still Works Today

Many of the construction methods used in traditional timber staircases remain virtually unchanged because they continue to provide excellent long-term performance.

Modern manufacturing has improved machining accuracy and consistency, but the basic principles remain the same:

- Precision-cut joints
- Secure timber wedges
- Reinforcing angle blocks
- Strong stair strings
- Quality timber
- Careful assembly

When all of these elements are combined correctly, the result is a staircase that feels solid, performs quietly, and provides reliable service for many years.

## The Hidden Engineering Behind Every Staircase

A beautifully finished staircase is only part of the story. The true quality lies beneath the surface, where carefully designed joints, wedges, angle blocks, and structural components all work together to support every step.

Although these features are hidden from view, they are what give a traditional timber staircase its strength, stability, and longevity. They are a reminder that the finest craftsmanship is often found in the details that most people never see.

## Material Choices Behind the Staircase

The internal build can use different material combinations depending on budget and specification. Two common choices for the back of a traditional staircase are:

- **Pine lamwood strings, treads and risers** — the traditional all-timber build.
- **Pine strings paired with MDF treads and risers** — a widely used cost-conscious alternative.

**From behind, both look effectively the same.** Same wedges. Same angle blocks. Same structural joinery. The engineering carrying the staircase is identical, and the visual result from the back is identical too.
