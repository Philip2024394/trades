# Why Your Hallway Flooring Should Work with Your Staircase

When planning a new staircase, many homeowners focus on the timber, balustrades, and handrails — but one important design detail is often overlooked.

The width and direction of the hallway flooring boards should complement the proportions of the staircase treads.

NEX design intelligence treats flooring and staircases as one design, not two separate projects.

## Balance Is Everything

The eye naturally compares the flooring with the staircase.

If the flooring boards are significantly wider or narrower than the staircase treads, the proportions can feel unbalanced.

For example, installing very wide plank flooring leading into a staircase with narrow-looking treads can make the staircase appear smaller than it actually is.

Likewise, if the flooring boards are very narrow and the staircase has deep, wide treads, the flooring can make the staircase appear oversized.

Neither product is wrong on its own — but together, the proportions may not feel harmonious.

## Create a Natural Transition

The flooring should lead naturally towards the staircase.

When the board widths are in proportion with the tread dimensions, the eye moves smoothly from the hallway onto the staircase, creating a sense of continuity and flow.

This subtle relationship between the two elements can make the entire entrance feel more spacious, balanced, and professionally designed.

## Consider the Scale of the Home

Every home is different.

A large, open hallway may suit wider flooring planks paired with generous staircase treads, creating a bold, contemporary feel.

A smaller entrance may benefit from more modest board widths that complement the scale of the staircase and prevent either feature from dominating the space.

The goal isn't for the flooring boards and treads to be exactly the same width — but for them to feel visually balanced.

## Think About the Whole Space

When choosing flooring, consider how it works with:

- The width of the staircase treads
- The timber species and colour
- The grain direction
- The hallway size
- The landing flooring
- The skirting boards
- The door frames and architraves

When these elements work together, the entrance feels carefully planned rather than a collection of separate finishes.

## Landing Flooring: Match the Width, Not Always the Material

Landing flooring — the boards at the top of the staircase — sits in direct visual line with the downstairs hallway flooring, framed by the staircase between them.

There is no set-in-stone rule on board size. But two design norms carried by NEX design intelligence are worth knowing:

- **Larger boards look more appealing with wide staircase steps.** Wide treads and wide flooring boards reinforce each other and make the whole entrance feel generous.
- **Landing board width should match downstairs board width.** When both landing and downstairs flooring are visible with the staircase, they should share the same plank width even if the material differs. Species can change floor-to-floor; finish can change floor-to-floor; colour can change floor-to-floor — **width is what the eye catches**. A width mismatch is noticed instantly; a material change reads as intentional.

This is one of those quiet design decisions that separates a professionally-planned staircase from a cluster of finishes that happen to meet at the landing.

## The NEX Design Tip

Homeowners should view their staircase and flooring as one complete design.

Before choosing flooring, take a step back and imagine how it will meet the first tread. Ask whether the proportions feel balanced and whether the materials complement one another.

Small design decisions like board width can have a surprisingly large impact on the overall appearance of a home.

A well-designed staircase doesn't stand alone — it should flow naturally from the floor beneath it. When the flooring and staircase are in harmony, the entire entrance feels more elegant, more spacious, and beautifully connected.
