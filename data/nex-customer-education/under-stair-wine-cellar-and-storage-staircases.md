# Under-Stair Wine Cellars and Bespoke Storage Staircases

Instead of leaving the triangular void beneath a staircase unused, the space can be transformed into a bespoke architectural feature — a wine cellar, a display cabinet, a storage panel, or a combination. Designed as one continuous piece of joinery, the staircase and the cabinetry become a single architectural composition.

## The Straight-Flight Wine-Cellar Variant

A straight-flight closed-string staircase provides the cleanest side profile for integrating a full wine cellar underneath.

- **Type:** Straight-flight staircase.
- **Construction:** Closed-string staircase.
- **Style:** Contemporary with traditional proportions.
- **Balustrade:** Slim black metal balusters with hardwood handrail.
- **Timber:** Walnut or dark oak finish.
- **Under-stair feature:** Climate-controlled wine display and storage cabinet with diamond racks, horizontal bottle storage, glassware storage, lower storage drawers, integrated timber shelving, warm LED lighting throughout.

## The L-Shape + Wine-Cabinet Variant

The same wine-cellar treatment translates to an L-shaped quarter-landing staircase. The change of direction at the landing gives more usable floor space around the entrance while the wine cabinet still sits under the lower flight.

- **Type:** L-shaped quarter-landing staircase.
- **Starting step:** Double bullnose.
- **Construction:** Closed-string staircase.
- **Under-stair feature:** Wine cabinet with diamond racks, glass display, base cupboards, warm LED lighting.

## The L-Shape + Tongue-and-Groove Storage Variant

For homeowners who prefer hidden practical storage over a display cellar, the same L-shape double-bullnose staircase can be finished with a tongue-and-groove panelled under-stair enclosure with a flush access door.

- **Type:** L-shaped quarter-landing staircase.
- **Starting step:** Double bullnose.
- **Construction:** Closed-string staircase.
- **Finish:** Classic two-tone — oak treads, handrails and newel posts with painted white strings, risers and balusters.
- **Under-stair feature:** Full-height tongue-and-groove panelled enclosure with a hidden access door integrated into the panelling. The angled panelling follows the line of the staircase for a bespoke fitted appearance.

## Feature Newel Posts

All three variants use substantial square newel posts. Larger posts create a more substantial and traditional appearance; smaller posts produce a lighter, more contemporary feel. Their generous size complements the scale of the fitted furniture and prevents the staircase from appearing too delicate.

Newel posts anchor the staircase at:

- The starting step.
- The quarter landing (L-shape variants).
- The upper landing.
- The gallery balustrade.

## Why Closed-String Works Best Here

Closed-string construction is ideal when integrating fitted furniture beneath the staircase because it provides:

- A clean, uninterrupted side profile.
- Simple integration with cabinetry.
- A modern architectural appearance.
- Greater flexibility for bespoke joinery.

Cut-string construction, by contrast, exposes the stepped tread profile and complicates the interface between staircase and cabinetry.

## Bespoke Joinery Alignment

The cabinetry is manufactured as one continuous piece of furniture with the staircase. Notice how:

- The timber grain matches the staircase.
- The shelving aligns with the stair pitch.
- Drawer fronts maintain consistent proportions.
- Vertical divisions mirror the rhythm of the balusters above.

This creates visual harmony between the staircase and the cabinetry — the two elements read as a single architectural feature.

## Integrated Lighting

Warm LED strip lighting recessed into each compartment serves two purposes:

- Illuminates the wine collection or displayed items.
- Highlights the craftsmanship of the timber joinery.

The indirect lighting also creates depth, making the cabinetry appear more luxurious, particularly in the evening.

## Double Bullnose Starting Step

The double bullnose starting step (on the L-shape variants) creates an inviting entrance and softens the appearance of the staircase with flowing curves. Two extended curved treads project beyond the line of the staircase, providing a bespoke handcrafted look and a natural visual transition between the hallway and the staircase.

## Design Philosophy

These variants demonstrate how modern staircase design can maximise every square metre of available space. By transforming the under-stair area into a bespoke wine cellar, wine cabinet, or panelled storage enclosure, the design combines functionality, storage or display, and architectural beauty.

Each variant serves the same design goal — the staircase is not simply a circulation route between floors, it is a premium interior feature — while offering the homeowner the choice of layout (straight vs L-shape) and under-stair treatment (open display cellar vs closed panelled storage) that best suits their home.
