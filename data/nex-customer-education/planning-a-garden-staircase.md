# Planning a Garden Staircase – Creating a Safe, Beautiful Outdoor Space

A garden staircase is much more than a way to reach your decking or lower garden. It should become a natural extension of your outdoor living space, combining safety, durability, and attractive design.

Unlike an internal staircase, an outdoor staircase must perform in all weather conditions, from hot summer sunshine to heavy rain, frost, and even snow. Good planning at the beginning will ensure your staircase remains safe and enjoyable for many years.

## Start With How You Use Your Garden

Before choosing a staircase, think about how you use your outdoor space.

Ask yourself:

- Is this the main entrance to the garden?
- Will children regularly use the stairs?
- Will elderly family members need easy access?
- Do you entertain guests on your decking?
- Will furniture, barbecues, or plant pots be carried up and down?

The answers will help determine the best width, number of steps, handrail design, and landing sizes.

## Wide Steps Feel More Comfortable

If space allows, choose a staircase that is wider than the minimum required.

A wider staircase:

- Feels more welcoming.
- Makes carrying furniture easier.
- Allows two people to pass comfortably.
- Creates a more premium appearance.
- Provides extra safety.

Sometimes increasing the width by just a few hundred millimetres can completely change the look and feel of the staircase.

## Include a Landing Where Possible

If the staircase is long, consider adding a landing.

Landings:

- Break up long flights.
- Provide a resting point.
- Improve safety.
- Make the staircase feel less steep.
- Create opportunities for feature planters or lighting.

They can also improve the overall appearance by giving the staircase a more balanced design.

## Should Decking Boards Be Slip Resistant?

Yes.

One of the most important considerations for any outdoor staircase is the walking surface.

When timber becomes wet, it can become slippery. Moss, algae, leaves, and frost can make this even worse.

For this reason, many homeowners choose decking boards with slip-resistant properties or a textured finish.

Options include:

- Grooved timber decking.
- Slip-resistant composite decking.
- Anti-slip inserts fitted into timber boards.
- Non-slip grip strips.
- Textured hardwood decking.

The best choice depends on the location and how exposed the staircase is to the weather.

## Consider Drainage

Water should never be allowed to sit on the steps.

A well-designed staircase allows rainwater to drain quickly away from the walking surface.

Good drainage helps:

- Reduce slippery conditions.
- Minimise timber movement.
- Reduce algae growth.
- Extend the life of the staircase.

## Choose Outdoor Materials

Internal staircase materials are not always suitable outdoors.

Garden staircases should use materials designed for external conditions, such as:

- Pressure-treated structural timber.
- Cedar.
- Larch.
- Durable hardwoods.
- Composite decking boards.
- Stainless steel fixings.
- Hot-dip galvanised brackets and bolts.

Using the correct materials from the beginning will greatly reduce maintenance.

## Think About Lighting

Outdoor lighting makes a significant difference to both safety and appearance.

Popular options include:

- LED lights built into each step.
- Post cap lights.
- Handrail lighting.
- Low-level garden lighting.
- Motion-sensor lighting.

A well-lit staircase is easier to use after dark and creates an attractive feature in the garden.

## Handrails Are Just as Important

A handrail should feel solid and comfortable to hold.

Depending on the style of your home, you could choose:

- Timber handrails.
- Stainless steel handrails.
- Glass balustrades.
- Powder-coated aluminium systems.
- Composite balustrades.

The handrail should complement both the staircase and the decking.

## Make the Staircase Part of the Garden

Rather than treating the staircase as a separate structure, think about how it connects with the rest of your landscape.

You could include:

- Raised flower beds alongside the stairs.
- Built-in seating near the landing.
- Planters integrated into the newel posts.
- Decorative stone edging.
- Matching fencing and balustrades.
- Climbing plants or feature lighting.

When the staircase blends naturally into the garden, it becomes an attractive feature rather than simply a practical route.

## Think About Future Maintenance

Every outdoor staircase requires some level of maintenance.

Ask yourself:

- How often will the timber need treating?
- Are replacement boards easy to source?
- Can damaged components be replaced individually?
- Will the fixings resist corrosion?
- Is the staircase easy to clean?

Choosing quality materials and thoughtful construction can significantly reduce maintenance over the years.

## A Garden Staircase Should Be Built for the Future

A well-planned garden staircase should do more than connect two levels — it should improve how you enjoy your outdoor space. Safe, slip-resistant decking boards, durable outdoor materials, good drainage, comfortable handrails, and attractive landscaping all contribute to a staircase that is both practical and beautiful.

With careful planning, your garden staircase can become one of the standout features of your home, providing safe access while enhancing the overall look and value of your outdoor living area.
