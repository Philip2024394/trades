# Fire Escape Staircases – What They Are and When You Need One

A fire escape staircase (also called a means of escape staircase or escape stair) is a staircase designed to allow people to leave a building safely during a fire or other emergency. Unlike a decorative feature staircase, its primary purpose is safe evacuation.

## When Do You Need a Fire Escape Staircase?

Whether one is required depends on the building's use, size, height, occupancy, and local building regulations.

Typical examples include:

- Apartment buildings with multiple floors.
- Office buildings.
- Schools and colleges.
- Hotels.
- Hospitals and care homes.
- Factories and warehouses.
- Large retail units.
- Public buildings.

For a typical UK single-family house, you normally do not need a dedicated external fire escape staircase. Escape is usually provided through the protected internal staircase and escape windows where permitted.

## Typical Widths

The required width depends on how many people the staircase serves. Common values are:

| Building Use | Typical Clear Width |
|---|---|
| Single dwelling | 800–900 mm |
| Small office or shop | 1000 mm |
| Medium commercial | 1100–1200 mm |
| High occupancy | 1400 mm or more |

The clear width is the usable space between handrails or walls — not the overall steel width.

## Typical Construction

Fire escape stairs are commonly made from:

- Galvanised steel.
- Hot-dip galvanised structural steel.
- Stainless steel (coastal or corrosive environments).
- Powder-coated steel (where appearance is important).

Treads are often:

- Open steel grating.
- Serrated steel grating (anti-slip).
- Perforated steel plate.
- Chequer plate (less common for primary escape routes).

Open grating is popular because it allows water, snow, and debris to fall through, reducing slip hazards.

## Design Features

A fire escape staircase typically includes:

- Non-slip treads.
- Steel handrails on both sides (where required).
- Landings at changes of direction.
- High-load structural design.
- Corrosion-resistant finish.
- Adequate headroom.
- Emergency lighting (inside buildings).
- Suitable guarding and balustrades.

## External Fire Escape Stairs

These are usually installed on the outside of buildings and provide an alternative escape route if the internal stair cannot be used.

Common layouts include:

- Straight flights.
- Quarter-turn stairs.
- Dog-leg stairs.
- Multi-storey fire escape towers.

## Important — Regulatory Context

In the UK, fire escape stair requirements are governed by Building Regulations (Approved Document B), as well as standards such as BS 9991 or BS 9999 depending on the building type. The required number of escape stairs, their width, travel distances, and fire resistance must be designed to comply with these regulations, so a qualified designer or building control body should review the final design.
