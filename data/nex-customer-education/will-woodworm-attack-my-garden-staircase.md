# Will Woodworm Attack My Garden Staircase?

Woodworm is a concern for many homeowners, but the good news is that a properly built and maintained garden staircase is much less likely to suffer serious woodworm problems, especially if it has been constructed from pressure-treated timber.

## What Is Woodworm?

"Woodworm" is the common name for the larvae of several species of wood-boring beetles.

The adult beetle lays its eggs on or in timber. When the eggs hatch, the tiny larvae tunnel through the wood, feeding on it for months or even years before emerging as adult beetles through the small round holes often seen in damaged timber.

It is actually the larvae, not the adult beetles, that cause the damage.

## Why Does Woodworm Appear?

Wood-boring beetles are attracted to timber that provides suitable conditions for their larvae.

The most common reasons include:

- Damp timber.
- Poor ventilation.
- Timber that stays wet for long periods.
- Untreated wood.
- Timber already affected by fungal decay.

Moisture is one of the biggest factors. Timber that remains dry is generally far less attractive to many wood-boring insects.

## Can Pressure-Treated Timber Be Attacked?

Pressure-treated timber is designed to resist fungal decay and many insects, making it much more durable outdoors than untreated timber.

While no timber is completely immune under every circumstance, pressure treatment greatly reduces the risk compared with untreated wood.

This is one reason why pressure-treated timber is widely used for:

- Garden staircases.
- Decking.
- Fencing.
- Pergolas.
- Outdoor structures.

## How Can I Reduce the Risk?

A well-designed staircase helps protect itself.

Good practice includes:

- Keeping the staircase clean.
- Removing leaves that trap moisture.
- Allowing air to circulate around the structure.
- Preventing water from collecting around the base.
- Repairing damaged boards quickly.
- Reapplying protective finishes where recommended.

The drier the timber remains between periods of rain, the lower the likelihood of insect and fungal problems.

## Signs of Woodworm

If you suspect woodworm, look for:

- Small round exit holes in the timber.
- Fine wood dust (called frass) beneath the holes.
- Weak or crumbling timber.
- Fresh holes appearing over time.

It's worth remembering that old exit holes don't necessarily mean there is an active infestation. The damage may have occurred many years earlier. Fresh frass and new holes are stronger indicators that beetles may still be present.

## Is Hardwood Better?

Some hardwoods are naturally more durable than softwoods and can be less attractive to certain insects. However, any timber can deteriorate if it remains damp or is not suitable for outdoor use.

Choosing timber that is appropriate for external conditions is just as important as choosing between hardwood and softwood.

## The Best Defence Is Good Design

A garden staircase that is:

- Built from suitable outdoor timber.
- Properly pressure treated where appropriate.
- Well ventilated.
- Designed with good drainage.
- Regularly inspected and maintained.

…is much less likely to experience problems with woodworm or decay.

The aim is to keep the timber healthy by allowing it to dry after rain and preventing long-term moisture build-up. Combined with quality materials and routine maintenance, this gives your garden staircase the best chance of remaining strong, safe, and attractive for many years.
