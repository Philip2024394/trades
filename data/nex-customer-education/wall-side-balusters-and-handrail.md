# Should I Install Balusters and a Handrail on the Wall Side of My Staircase?

This is one of the most common questions asked when designing a bespoke staircase.

**The NEX Design Recommendation is usually: No — in most cases, installing balusters and a handrail against a wall is not recommended.**

There are several practical reasons why.

## 1. Cleaning Becomes Difficult

One of the biggest disadvantages is maintenance.

The small gap between the wall and the balusters makes cleaning extremely awkward. Dust naturally collects behind the balusters, and reaching between them without rubbing against the wall is almost impossible.

Over time, this often leads to scuffed paint, dirty marks, and scratched walls.

## 2. Decorating Is Much Harder

If you ever need to repaint your hallway or staircase walls, balusters fixed against the wall become a real obstacle.

Painting neatly around dozens of individual spindles is time-consuming and frustrating. Even professional decorators find it more difficult, which can increase decorating costs and make future maintenance more of a challenge.

A clear wall is much easier to prepare, paint, wallpaper, or redecorate.

## 3. They Don't Add Much Value

Balusters are primarily designed to provide safety where there is an open side to a staircase.

When fitted against a solid wall, they don't provide additional protection because the wall already serves that purpose.

In most situations, they simply add visual clutter rather than improving the overall design.

## 4. A Cleaner, More Modern Appearance

Leaving the wall side free from balusters creates a cleaner and more spacious appearance.

The staircase feels wider, the wall becomes a feature in its own right, and the overall design is simpler and more contemporary. This approach also gives you the freedom to add feature lighting, artwork, timber wall panels, or decorative finishes without the balusters getting in the way.

## What If I Need a Handrail?

If additional support is required, there are many excellent alternatives.

**A wall-mounted handrail is usually the best solution.**

These are securely fixed using wall brackets and come in a wide variety of styles and finishes, including:

- Timber handrails
- Oak, walnut, mahogany, or painted finishes
- Brushed stainless steel
- Black matte steel
- Round or square profiles
- Contemporary minimalist designs
- Traditional classic styles

A wall-mounted handrail provides the support you need while keeping the staircase easy to clean, easy to decorate, and visually uncluttered.

## The NEX Recommendation

NEX design intelligence recommends keeping the wall side free from balusters unless there is a specific design or structural reason to include them.

This approach offers several long-term benefits:

- Easier cleaning and maintenance
- Simpler decorating and repainting
- Fewer scuffs and wall marks
- A cleaner, more spacious appearance
- Greater flexibility for future design changes
- The option to install a stylish wall-mounted handrail whenever additional support is needed

A staircase should not only look beautiful on installation day — it should remain practical, easy to maintain, and enjoyable to live with for many years to come. That's why, in most modern staircase designs, less really is more.
