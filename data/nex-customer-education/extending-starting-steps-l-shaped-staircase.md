# Extending the Starting Steps of an L-Shaped Staircase

An L-shaped staircase is one of the most popular staircase layouts, offering an attractive change in direction while making efficient use of space. However, not every home provides enough room for the shorter section of the "L", which can leave the staircase feeling tight at the entrance.

Fortunately, there is an excellent design solution.

## Extend the Starting Steps

When the lower section of the staircase is restricted by a wall or limited hallway space, the starting step or first few steps can be extended beyond the face of the wall.

Rather than finishing flush with the wall line, these wider steps project into the room, creating additional walking space while introducing an elegant architectural feature.

This simple design change can dramatically improve both the appearance and functionality of the staircase.

## More Room Where You Need It Most

By extending the first one, two, or even three steps, you gain valuable extra space exactly where people begin using the staircase.

This creates:

- A more comfortable first step.
- Better flow into the staircase.
- Improved access when carrying furniture or shopping.
- A more welcoming entrance.
- A staircase that feels larger than the available space suggests.

Even a modest extension can make a noticeable difference.

## A Stronger Design Feature

Extended starting steps don't just improve practicality — they also create a beautiful focal point.

They provide an opportunity to introduce features such as:

- Wider statement treads.
- Bullnose or curved starting steps.
- Feature newel posts.
- Decorative balustrades.
- Glass panels.
- Timber or metal detailing.
- LED feature lighting.

The staircase immediately feels more bespoke and luxurious.

## A Better Finish to the Staircase Side

Another important advantage is how the staircase meets the surrounding wall.

Without extending the starting steps, the side of the staircase can sometimes appear abrupt or unfinished, particularly where the hallway is narrow.

By projecting the lower steps beyond the wall line, the side of the staircase gains a more balanced and intentional finish. The staircase appears properly grounded within the room rather than simply emerging from the wall.

This creates cleaner proportions and a more professional architectural appearance.

## Ideal for Compact Hallways

This design approach works particularly well in:

- Narrow entrance halls.
- Hallways with limited depth.
- Renovation projects.
- Victorian and Edwardian homes.
- Modern extensions.
- Homes where structural walls restrict the staircase layout.

Instead of compromising the staircase design, extending the lower steps makes better use of the available space.

## A Small Change with a Big Impact

One of the advantages of bespoke staircase design is that small alterations can completely transform the finished result.

Extending the starting steps is one of those details that often goes unnoticed at first — but once complete, it can make the staircase feel more spacious, more elegant, and better connected to the room around it.

The result is a staircase that not only works within the available space but also becomes a standout architectural feature in its own right.
