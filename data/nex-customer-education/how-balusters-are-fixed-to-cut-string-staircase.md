# How Are Balusters Fixed to a Cut String Staircase?

One of the defining features of a cut string staircase is the way the balusters are fixed directly onto the treads. While this may appear straightforward from above, there is a great deal of craftsmanship involved in creating a strong, long-lasting joint.

The methods used today have evolved over centuries while still respecting many traditional joinery techniques.

## Traditional Hardwood Staircases

During the 19th century and earlier, most quality staircases were manufactured from hardwoods such as oak, elm, ash, and mahogany.

These timbers were carefully seasoned and dried before construction, making them very stable with minimal movement after installation.

The traditional method was simple but highly effective.

Each baluster was manufactured with a round dowel at its base.

A matching round hole was bored into the tread, exactly the same diameter as the dowel. The baluster was then glued and driven into position, creating a strong mechanical joint.

Because the hardwood was stable and unlikely to shrink significantly, this method proved extremely reliable and became the standard for generations of staircase makers.

## Modern Staircase Construction

Today, this traditional dowel method is still widely used, particularly on hardwood staircases.

However, many modern staircases also use softwood components, especially where the staircase will later be painted.

Unlike hardwood, softwood can experience small amounts of seasonal movement as it adjusts to changes in temperature and humidity. Although this movement is usually very slight, it can sometimes create tiny gaps around the base of a baluster if no allowance has been made.

## The Introduction of a Haunched Joint

To overcome this, many skilled staircase manufacturers and carpenters introduced an additional detail known as a haunch.

Rather than simply drilling a round hole into the tread, a small square or rectangular recess is carefully formed around the baluster location.

The base of the baluster is then shaped to match this recess before being fitted.

This extra piece of craftsmanship provides several advantages:

- It hides any minor timber movement.
- It helps prevent small gaps appearing around the base of the baluster.
- It creates a cleaner finished appearance.
- It increases the bearing surface between the baluster and the tread.
- It provides an additional mechanical location for the baluster.

Although the difference may only be a few millimetres, it demonstrates the attention to detail that distinguishes quality joinery.

## Why Small Details Matter

Most homeowners will never notice whether a baluster has been simply dowelled or carefully haunched.

However, over many years, these hidden details can make a noticeable difference to the finished appearance of the staircase.

Quality staircase construction is often defined by preventing problems before they occur, rather than repairing them later.

## Traditional Skills That Continue Today

Many of the finest staircase construction methods have changed very little over the centuries.

While modern machinery has improved accuracy and consistency, the principles of good joinery remain the same:

- Carefully seasoned timber.
- Precision-machined joints.
- Strong dowel fixings.
- Thoughtfully designed haunched details where appropriate.
- Skilled hand finishing.

These techniques ensure that the balusters remain secure while maintaining a clean, professional appearance for many years.

## The Craft Behind Every Baluster

A baluster may seem like a simple component, but the way it is fixed is an important part of the staircase's overall quality.

From the traditional hardwood dowelled joints used by craftsmen centuries ago to the carefully haunched methods used on many modern softwood staircases, these construction techniques demonstrate how good joinery combines strength, precision, and long-term durability.

The best staircase details are often the ones you never notice — but they are the reason a well-built staircase continues to look and perform beautifully for generations.
