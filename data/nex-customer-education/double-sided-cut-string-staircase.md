# Double-Sided Cut String Staircase – Clean Lines with Bold Craftsmanship

A double-sided cut string staircase is a timeless design that exposes the profile of each tread while creating a bold architectural feature. Unlike a closed-string staircase, the decorative cut strings follow the shape of every step, giving the staircase greater depth and visual character.

This style has remained popular for generations because it combines traditional craftsmanship with a clean, elegant appearance.

## What Is a Double-Sided Cut String?

A cut string staircase has shaped side strings that follow the outline of each tread and riser. When both sides of the staircase are cut strings, the staircase presents the same detailed appearance from either side, making it ideal for open hallways or feature staircases where both sides are visible.

The stepped profile creates a handcrafted look while highlighting the construction of the staircase itself.

## Mitred String Edges

One of the finest details found on a quality cut string staircase is the mitred string edge.

Rather than leaving a square exposed edge, the top of the string is mitred to form a crisp, clean line that follows the staircase from bottom to top.

This small detail produces:

- Cleaner joins.
- Sharper shadow lines.
- A premium handcrafted appearance.
- Greater attention to detail.
- A more refined finished edge.

Although subtle, a mitred string edge is one of the features that distinguishes high-quality staircase craftsmanship.

## Heavy Feature Treads

This staircase uses deeper, heavier treads than those found on many standard staircases.

The increased thickness gives the staircase a stronger visual presence while creating a feeling of quality and durability.

Heavier treads can help achieve:

- A more substantial appearance.
- A luxury bespoke feel.
- Greater visual depth.
- Strong architectural proportions.
- A modern interpretation of a traditional staircase.

They are particularly effective in larger homes where a standard tread can sometimes appear too light.

## A Clean Design Without Scroll Brackets

Traditionally, many cut string staircases include decorative scroll brackets fitted beneath each tread.

These carved scrolls add classic detailing and are often associated with Victorian, Edwardian, and Georgian staircase styles.

However, this design deliberately leaves the cut strings plain.

Without scroll brackets, the staircase has:

- Cleaner lines.
- A more contemporary appearance.
- Less visual clutter.
- Greater emphasis on the timber and craftsmanship.
- A versatile style that suits both traditional and modern homes.

The simplicity of the design allows the quality of the timber and the precision of the joinery to take centre stage.

## Scroll Brackets Can Always Be Added

Choosing a plain cut string doesn't limit future possibilities.

Decorative scroll brackets can often be added later if a more traditional appearance is desired.

This gives homeowners the flexibility to begin with a simple, elegant staircase and enhance it over time as their interior style evolves.

## A Design That Complements Any Interior

A double-sided cut string staircase can be manufactured in a wide range of materials and finishes, including:

- European Oak
- American Black Walnut
- Mahogany
- Pine
- Painted timber
- Black matte finishes
- Natural clear lacquer
- Light or dark wood stains

It also works beautifully with timber balusters, glass panels, wrought iron, brushed stainless steel, or modern black metal balusters.

## Traditional Craftsmanship with Contemporary Styling

The double-sided cut string staircase demonstrates how traditional joinery can be adapted for modern homes. By combining mitred string edges, heavier feature treads, and clean, unadorned cut strings, the design achieves a balance between timeless craftsmanship and contemporary simplicity.

Whether installed in a period property or a modern home, this style offers an elegant staircase that showcases quality materials, precision joinery, and thoughtful design. It proves that sometimes, removing decorative elements rather than adding them allows the natural beauty of the timber and the craftsmanship to become the true focal point.
