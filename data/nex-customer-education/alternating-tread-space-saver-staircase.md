# Alternating Tread Staircases (Space Saver Staircases)

An alternating tread staircase, often known as a space saver staircase, is a clever solution for homes and buildings where space is limited.

Unlike a traditional staircase, the treads are specially shaped so that they alternate from left to right as you climb. One tread provides a full surface for your left foot, the next for your right foot, and so on all the way to the top.

This unique design allows the staircase to be much steeper while remaining more comfortable to use than a conventional steep stair or ladder.

## What Is It Called?

Depending on the manufacturer or country, you may hear it referred to as:

- Space Saver Staircase
- Alternating Tread Staircase
- Paddle Staircase
- Paddle Steps
- Alternating Step Staircase
- Loft Staircase (in some applications)

Although the names vary, they all describe the same principle of alternating left and right treads.

## How Does It Work?

Each tread has a cut-away section that allows extra foot space.

As you climb:

- Left foot on the first tread
- Right foot on the second tread
- Left foot on the third tread
- Right foot on the fourth tread

This alternating pattern allows each foot to land fully on its tread, even though the staircase occupies much less floor space than a traditional staircase.

## Where Are They Used?

Space saver staircases are commonly installed in:

- Loft conversions
- Mezzanine floors
- Tiny homes
- Garden offices
- Home studios
- Cabins
- Storage lofts
- Spaces where a conventional staircase will not fit

They are designed for situations where maximising available floor space is a priority.

## Advantages

A space saver staircase offers several benefits:

- Uses significantly less floor space.
- Ideal for compact homes and small rooms.
- More comfortable than climbing a ladder.
- Available in timber, steel, or mixed materials.
- Modern and visually interesting design.
- Can be manufactured in a range of styles and finishes.

## Things to Consider

Although highly practical, alternating tread staircases are not suitable for every situation.

Because the treads are designed to be used in a specific left-right pattern, users need to pay a little more attention when climbing or descending compared with a conventional staircase.

They are generally best suited to areas that are used less frequently rather than as the main staircase in a busy family home. In many countries, building regulations also restrict where they can be installed, so it's important to check local requirements before choosing this type of staircase.

## A Smart Solution for Small Spaces

When designed and installed correctly, an alternating tread staircase can transform a space that would otherwise be inaccessible. It combines clever engineering with efficient use of floor area, making it a popular choice for lofts, compact homes, and modern architectural designs.

If you're looking to maximise every square metre of your home, a space saver staircase could be the perfect solution — offering safe access while occupying a fraction of the space required by a traditional staircase.
