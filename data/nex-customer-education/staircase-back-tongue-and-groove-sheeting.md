# Tongue & Groove (T&G) Sheeting for the Back of a Straight Flight Staircase

Tongue and Groove (T&G) sheeting is a popular choice for enclosing the rear of a straight flight staircase, creating a clean, finished appearance while adding warmth and character to the space. The interlocking tongue-and-groove profile allows each board to fit tightly together, producing a seamless timber finish with minimal visible gaps.

The sheeting is typically fixed to a timber framework behind the staircase string or directly to supporting battens, depending on the staircase design. Once installed, the panels conceal the underside of the stairs, providing an attractive feature wall that can complement both traditional and contemporary interiors.

## Benefits

- **Clean, professional finish** — hides the structural components beneath the staircase.
- **Natural timber appearance** — adds texture and warmth to the room.
- **Strong interlocking joints** — reduces movement and helps maintain straight, even lines.
- **Versatile finish options** — can be left natural, stained, lacquered, or painted to match the staircase.
- **Ideal for feature walls** — creates an elegant backdrop in hallways, entrances, or open-plan living areas.

## Installation

T&G boards are installed vertically or horizontally depending on the desired design. Each board locks into the next using the tongue-and-groove joint before being securely fixed with concealed nails or screws. Care should be taken to maintain consistent spacing and alignment, ensuring a high-quality finish across the entire staircase.

## Materials

T&G sheeting is available in a range of timber species, including:

- Yellow Pine
- American Oak
- Tasmanian Oak
- Walnut
- Cedar
- Engineered timber products

The timber should be acclimatised before installation and finished with an appropriate coating to protect against moisture, wear, and everyday use.

## Design Tip

Vertical T&G sheeting, like the example shown, enhances the perception of ceiling height and gives the staircase a modern architectural look. When matched with the same timber used for the staircase treads or handrail, it creates a cohesive, premium finish throughout the home.
