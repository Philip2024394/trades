# Finishing the Side of a Cut String Staircase

One of the unique features of a cut string staircase is the opportunity to personalise its appearance. While the overall staircase structure may remain the same, the finish beneath each tread can completely change its character — from highly traditional to clean and contemporary.

The side of the staircase is often one of the first details people notice, making it an important design decision.

## Decorative Scroll Brackets

The most recognisable finish for a traditional cut string staircase is the addition of decorative scroll brackets, sometimes referred to as cut string brackets or gingerbread brackets.

These carved timber pieces are fitted beneath each tread, following the profile of the cut string.

Scroll brackets have been used for centuries and remain a hallmark of traditional staircase craftsmanship.

They add:

- Elegance and detail.
- A handcrafted appearance.
- Greater visual depth.
- A feature that enhances period properties.
- Character and individuality.

Many staircase manufacturers offer a selection of standard scroll designs, ranging from simple curves to highly decorative carvings.

## Bespoke Scroll Designs

If you're looking for something unique, many bespoke staircase makers can create custom scroll brackets.

This allows you to choose a design that reflects your personal style or complements the architecture of your home.

Options might include:

- Traditional Victorian scrolls.
- Georgian-inspired designs.
- Contemporary curved profiles.
- Simple geometric shapes.
- Completely bespoke carvings.

These custom details can transform a staircase into a true one-of-a-kind feature.

## Rosettes and Decorative Blocks

Another option is to incorporate rosettes or decorative blocks into the cut string design.

These smaller decorative elements can add subtle interest without the more elaborate appearance of full scroll brackets.

They are often chosen where a balance between traditional and modern styling is desired.

## Flush Face Cut Strings

At the opposite end of the design spectrum is the flush face cut string.

Instead of decorative scrolls beneath each tread, the string is finished with smooth, square profiles that create clean, uninterrupted lines.

This style has become increasingly popular in contemporary homes, particularly where the staircase is to be painted.

The flush design offers:

- A minimalist appearance.
- Clean architectural lines.
- Easier preparation for painting.
- Less visual clutter.
- A refined modern finish.

Without decorative carving, the staircase relies on its proportions and craftsmanship rather than ornamentation. Where a flush face is chosen, the tread and riser are often extended slightly beyond the cut string to offer subtle detail, and the top of the string is finished with a mitred edge — the normal choice for a clean contemporary reading.

## Traditional Structure, Modern Character

One of the most interesting aspects of staircase design is how a few simple changes can completely alter its appearance.

A traditional cut string staircase fitted with:

- Turned balusters
- Decorative scroll brackets
- Classic newel posts
- Moulded handrails

creates a timeless, heritage-style staircase.

However, replace those elements with:

- Square balusters
- Flush cut strings
- Square newel posts
- Straight contemporary handrails

and the same staircase immediately takes on a completely different personality.

The underlying construction remains traditional, but the finished appearance becomes clean, understated, and perfectly suited to a modern or semi-modern home.

## Blending Traditional and Contemporary Design

This combination has become increasingly popular because it offers the best of both worlds.

Homeowners can enjoy the proven strength and craftsmanship of a traditional cut string staircase while achieving a more contemporary look through simplified detailing.

The result is a staircase that feels timeless rather than tied to a particular era.

## Choosing the Right Finish

Whether you choose carved scroll brackets, decorative rosettes, or a completely flush design, the finish of the cut string plays a major role in defining the staircase's character.

Decorative details celebrate traditional craftsmanship and create a striking feature, while flush designs offer simplicity, clean lines, and a more modern aesthetic.

The beauty of a cut string staircase is its versatility. With just a few design changes, it can become a grand period centrepiece, a transitional design that blends old and new, or a sleek architectural feature that complements today's modern interiors.
