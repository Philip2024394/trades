# Will Timber Garden Stairs Become Slippery?

Yes, they can.

Any outdoor walking surface — including timber, composite decking, concrete, and stone — can become slippery when wet or when covered with moss, algae, leaves, or ice.

The goal is not to find a material that never becomes slippery, but to choose one that provides good grip and to keep it well maintained.

## Is Concrete Better Than Timber?

Not necessarily.

Concrete offers a durable surface, but smooth concrete can become slippery in wet weather. Many outdoor concrete steps are therefore finished with a brushed, textured, or exposed aggregate surface to improve grip.

Timber stairs can also provide excellent grip when designed correctly, especially if they use:

- Slip-resistant decking boards.
- Anti-slip inserts or strips.
- Grooved treads (although grooves alone are not a guarantee against slipping).
- A non-slip coating where appropriate.
- Good drainage to prevent standing water.

The choice depends on the look you want, the maintenance you're willing to do, and the conditions in your garden.

## Should Timber Stairs Have Slip-Resistant Treads?

For many homes, yes.

If your staircase is likely to be used:

- During winter.
- In rainy weather.
- By children.
- By elderly family members.
- In shaded areas where moss is more likely to grow.

…then choosing a slip-resistant tread surface is a sensible investment.

## Why Is Pressure-Treated Timber Green or Brown?

Many people think the colour is just decorative, but it actually serves a purpose.

Pressure-treated timber is placed inside a treatment vessel where preservatives are forced deep into the wood under pressure. These preservatives help protect the timber against:

- Rot.
- Fungal decay.
- Wood-boring insects.

The treatment itself often gives the timber a greenish tint. Manufacturers may also add a brown dye so the timber has a more natural appearance that blends into gardens.

The brown colour is not what protects the timber — the preservative does. The dye simply changes the appearance.

Over time, the brown colour will gradually fade with exposure to sunlight and weather.

## Can You Paint or Stain Treated Timber?

Yes.

After the timber has dried sufficiently following treatment (the recommended waiting time varies by manufacturer), it can usually be finished with products suitable for exterior timber, such as:

- Exterior wood stains.
- Decking oils.
- Timber paints designed for outdoor use.

These finishes can improve appearance and provide additional weather protection, but they do not replace the preservative treatment already inside the timber.

## Keeping Timber Stairs Safe

To reduce the risk of slipping:

- Sweep away leaves regularly.
- Remove moss and algae before they build up.
- Wash the staircase occasionally.
- Ensure water drains away freely.
- Repair damaged boards promptly.
- Consider adding anti-slip strips in high-risk areas.

## Which Material Is Best?

There is no single "best" material.

- Pressure-treated timber offers a warm, natural appearance and is usually the most affordable.
- Composite decking generally requires less maintenance and many products include textured, slip-resistant surfaces.
- Concrete is strong and durable but should have a textured finish for outdoor steps.
- Natural stone looks beautiful but some polished stones can become slippery when wet.

The most important factor is not just the material itself, but how the staircase is designed, maintained, and finished. A well-built timber staircase with slip-resistant treads and good drainage can be safer than a smooth concrete staircase that becomes wet and covered with algae.
