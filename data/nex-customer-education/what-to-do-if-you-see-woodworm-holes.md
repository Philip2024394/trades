# What to Do If You See Woodworm Holes in Your Staircase, Doors, or Floors

If you see woodworm holes in your staircase, doors, or wooden floors, you do not need to panic, but you should check it properly because it can sometimes spread and weaken timber if it is active.

The most important question is:

**Is it active woodworm or old damage?**

Many older wooden items have holes from woodworm that died years ago. The holes can remain visible for decades.

## Signs You Should Take Seriously

Look for:

- ✅ Fresh light-coloured wood dust (like fine powder) underneath holes.
- ✅ New holes appearing.
- ✅ Small beetles coming out of timber.
- ✅ Timber becoming soft, weak, or crumbly.
- ✅ Damage in several areas (stairs + doors + floors).

If you see these signs, arrange an inspection.

## Who Should You Call?

### 1. Timber Pest Specialist / Woodworm Treatment Company

They:

- Inspect timber.
- Identify the beetle type.
- Check if it is active.
- Apply treatment if needed.

Search for:

- "woodworm treatment specialist"
- "timber pest survey"
- "wood boring insect specialist"

### 2. Building Surveyor (especially for older houses)

Useful if you have structural concerns about:

- Staircase stringers.
- Floor joists.
- Roof timbers.

### 3. Carpenter or Joiner

Good for repairing damaged wood, but they may not be the best person to diagnose active woodworm.

## What Will the Specialist Do?

Usually they will:

- Inspect the holes and timber condition.
- Look for fresh frass (powder).
- Test the wood strength.
- Identify the insect.
- Recommend:
  - No treatment (if inactive).
  - Surface treatment.
  - Deeper treatment.
  - Replacement of damaged sections.

## For a Staircase Specifically

Pay extra attention to:

- The bottom of newel posts.
- Stringers (side supports).
- Underneath steps.
- Areas near walls.
- Hidden backs of risers.

A few small holes in a hardwood staircase does not automatically mean the staircase is unsafe.
