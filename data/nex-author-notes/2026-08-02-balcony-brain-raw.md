## Philip's Balcony Golden Rules (verbatim)

1. The top newel is the bridge between staircase and balcony.
2. The balcony handrail should flow naturally from the staircase.
3. Newels provide the main structural support.
4. Balusters provide protection, spacing, and additional stiffness.
5. Thin decorative sections must not compromise strength.
6. A railing can look strong while having weak hidden fixings.
7. Always follow the load path: handrail → baluster → newel → structure.
8. Commercial balconies require stronger thinking than private balconies.

## Core Nex Principles (verbatim)

- *"The staircase carries the person upward. The balcony protects the person after arrival."*
- *"A balcony balustrade is a safety structure first and a design feature second."*
- *"The staircase does not finish at the top step. The staircase becomes part of the building."*
- *"The staircase handrail carries the user upward. The balcony handrail protects the user after arrival. The top newel post is the structural and visual bridge between these two worlds."*
- *"A staircase balcony is not an independent railing. It is the continuation of the staircase."*
- *"The glass is visible. The engineering is hidden."*
- *"The balcony should look like it belongs to the staircase, not like it was added afterwards."*

## 90% material continuity rule

Approximately 90% of staircases use the same or complementary materials
from the stair flight into the balcony because the balcony is considered
part of the staircase system. The 10% exception is intentional design
contrast (e.g. traditional oak stairs + modern frameless glass gallery).
