## Golden principles preserved (verbatim)

- *"A staircase is not finished when the parts are manufactured. The installation process determines whether the staircase performs correctly for decades."*
- *"The symptom is not always the fault."*
- *"Follow the movement. If a handrail moves, ask what supports it. If a baluster moves, ask what it is fixed into."*
- *"Never adjust the newel before finding the cause."*
- *"Manufacturing creates the staircase. Installation connects the staircase to the building."*
- *"Perfect buildings do not always exist."* (installation tolerance)
- *"The factory should solve manufacturing problems. The site should solve installation problems."*
- *"Measure twice, machine once, install once."*
- *"A staircase should not be tested for the first time inside the customer's home."* (dry-fitting rule)
- *"The staircase is not finished when the last screw is installed. It is finished when the customer understands the product."*
- *"The reinforced concrete carries the structural loads. The oak cladding provides the visible finish. Together they form one complete staircase system."* (structure vs finish)
- *"The best staircase companies do not start by repairing a problem. They start by diagnosing why the problem exists."*
- *"Never assume two concrete staircases in neighbouring houses are identical."*
- *"Concrete provides strength. Timber provides warmth. Glass provides light. Steel provides modern appearance."*

## Concrete Staircase 13-Step Build Process (verbatim)

Design → Set Out → Formwork → Reinforcement → Inspection → Concrete Placement → Compaction → Surface Finish → Curing → Formwork Removal → Inspection → Final Finishes

## Terminology introduced

- **Concrete shell** — the structural concrete staircase before overlay
- **Cast in-situ** — poured on site
- **Precast** — manufactured in factory
- **Waist slab** — reinforced concrete beneath the steps (the "backbone")
- **Formwork / shuttering** — temporary mould for wet concrete
- **Monolithic construction** — poured as one piece
- **Cladding** — timber/stone/porcelain overlay onto concrete structure
- **Shadow gap** — designed gap for clean visual lines, floating appearance
- **Concrete cover** — depth of concrete over reinforcement bars
- **Snagging** — process of identifying and correcting small issues before final completion
- **Overlay / cladding / lining** — all names for the same finishing process

## Nex balanced-language rule (Philip verbatim)

> *"Nex should avoid statements such as 'All premium staircase companies...' or 'Every staircase manufacturer...' or 'Concrete staircases are always...'"*
>
> Instead use balanced language: *"Many premium staircase companies..."* · *"It is common practice..."* · *"A typical workflow is..."* · *"Best practice is often..."* · *"Depending on the project..."*
>
> This makes Nex more technically accurate because manufacturing methods vary between companies, countries, regulations and project specifications.

## Structure-vs-Finish rule (Philip verbatim)

> *"The customer often believes they have purchased an 'oak staircase.' In reality, they have purchased an oak staircase FINISH installed onto a reinforced concrete staircase STRUCTURE. This distinction is important."*

Nex must distinguish:
- The **structural staircase** (timber · steel · concrete)
- The **architectural finish** (oak · walnut · glass · painted MDF · stone · tile · polished concrete)

A customer describing an "oak staircase" or "glass staircase" may actually mean an oak-clad or glass-balustraded concrete staircase. Both are correct customer language; Nex understands the layered reality behind it.
