## Golden principles preserved (verbatim)

- *"A stringer can be structural, decorative, or both. Its role depends on the staircase construction method."*
- *"String dimensions follow staircase geometry, not the other way around."*
- *"Removing components does not always mean less material. Sometimes the remaining components must become stronger."* (open riser rule)
- *"The stringer is the hidden language of the staircase."*
- *"Every staircase has a starting point and an arrival point. The staircase direction is determined by the journey between these two points."*
- *"A staircase plan is the DNA of the staircase."*
- *"Traditional staircase language was created by craftsmen who described what they saw: the movement of animals, shapes in nature, and the way materials flowed."*

## Common Stringer Dimensions (Philip's industry practice)

**Closed strings** — commonly 8.5" or 9" wide (traditional balance between support, appearance and material efficiency).

**Cut strings** — commonly 14"–17" wide (need extra depth below the cut profile to maintain strength, proportion and fixing area).

**Double-side cut strings + open-riser strings** — thickness often increases ~60% or up to double the closed-riser equivalent because the string carries more visual and structural responsibility with fewer companion components.

**Rule:** these are common quality standards, NOT universal building rules. Final sizing depends on the individual staircase design, span and material behaviour.

## Plan-type family taxonomy (new IDs to add)

- `quarter-turn` — 90° change of direction. L-shape.
- `half-turn` — 180° change of direction. Includes dog-leg + U-shape variants.
- `dog-leg` — Two straight flights, opposite directions, half landing between them. Side profile resembles a dog's rear leg.
- `bifurcated` — Bifurcate = "to divide into two branches". Grand staircase splits into two directions.
- `winder` — Direction change using tapered/kite-shaped steps instead of a landing.

## Terminology origins captured (verbatim from Philip)

- **Dog-leg** — side profile resembles a dog's rear leg
- **Monkey volute** — decorative spiral resembling a curled monkey's tail
- **Swan neck / gooseneck** — curve resembling a swan or goose neck; connects lower stair handrail to higher landing handrail
- **Volute** — Latin *voluta* = "to turn / to roll"
- **Bullnose** — front edge rounded like a bull's nose
- **Winder** — the step "winds" around a corner
- **Kite winder** — tapered tread forms a kite-like shape
- **Spiral** — follows a spiral curve like natural coils/shells
- **Helical** — three-dimensional continuous curve, like DNA
- **Bifurcated** — Latin *bi* + *furca* = "two forks"
- **Newel** — main vertical post supporting the handrail and balustrade transitions
- **Baluster** — Italian *balaustra* = "wild pomegranate flower" (early balusters resembled the flower)
- **Balustrade** — the complete assembly of balusters + handrail + newels
- **Stringer** — "strings" the staircase together, connecting the steps into one continuous structure
- **Monkey tail** — curled handrail ending resembling a monkey's tail
- **Snake rail** — flowing curved path resembling a snake
- **Lamb's tongue** — decorative handrail ending shaped like a split-curve lamb's tongue
- **Ogee** — architectural moulding term describing an S-shaped curve
- **Torus** — Latin = "a swelling or rounded moulding"
- **Astragal** — rounded bead-like architectural moulding
- **Dado rail** — dado = lower section of a wall; the rail protected/decorated it
- **Housed string** — treads and risers are housed into grooves cut into the string
- **Wreathed handrail** — built around a curve or turn (wreath = curved form)
- **Blocking / glue blocks** — timber blocks added underneath joints to reduce movement and prevent squeaks
- **Tread** — from "to tread" = to step or walk on
- **Riser** — from "to rise"
- **Going** — from "going forward" = horizontal movement of the foot
- **Pitch line** — imaginary line showing angle of ascent
- **Headroom** — the room available above the head
- **Stairwell / well hole** — the vertical opening containing the staircase
